# Problem and pain point

## Tier key

P: a government or regulator text read in full, a company's own filing, official statistics, a peer-reviewed article read in full, or a primary database. S: any other source, including press, trade portals, consultancy pages, company web pages that are not filings, handbooks reproduced online, and search snippets. SOURCED: the page text was captured and read (the capture file is named). UNVERIFIED: not captured, blocked, unreadable, or only a snippet was read. DERIVED: our arithmetic, with the inputs and their tiers shown. PROXY: a stand-in for what is not measured. ASSUMPTION: a value chosen for the model, not sourced.

## Problem statement

The need is a lime drink made in the glass, quick to make and fair in price per serve, for Thai adults who already drink bottled non-alcoholic drinks. 18.2% of people aged 25 to 44 drink them every day, against 16.0% of those aged 45 to 59 [P, UNVERIFIED; the 2021 survey table is not captured]. No Thai source tests a sparkling lime form, so it is a hypothesis.

The named target is men aged 25 to 34, compared with men aged 35 to 44; men aged 45 to 59 are a later volume segment. Men aged 25 to 44 report current alcohol drinking at 68.6%, against 65.4% for men aged 45 to 59 [P, UNVERIFIED; the captured page did not extract these values reliably]. This is a PROXY for drinking occasions, not a sugar measure, and no source splits the 25 to 34 and 35 to 44 bands.

The problem is one of form, not flavour. The competition brief asks for new ingredients or production, or a new form of consumption. Singha Lemon Soda, sold in five flavours in 330 ml cans, names sucralose and acesulfame potassium as sweeteners [S, SOURCED]. A trade article of 11 March 2026 reports a Singha lemon sparkling water in a 330 ml can at a suggested retail price of 15 baht [S, SOURCED]. A new flavour alone would not be new. The central idea is a new form of consumption: a dissolvable cube, in a pouch, that makes the drink in the glass. Scope is domestic Thailand only.

## What is not known

- **No consumer test exists.** No panel, sales data or Thai retail audit exists, so nothing shows what Thai buyers would pay, drink, try or repeat. Taste is untested; the target pH of 3.5 is a working anchor. No bench test of fizz has been run.

- **The lead is a model result.** The model puts men aged 25 to 34 ahead in about 94% of draws [DERIVED from NSO 2021 age bands (UNVERIFIED) and an ASSUMPTION elasticity, uniform 0.5 to 1.0]. The median gap is 0.5 to 0.7 points across baselines of 20 to 50%; a five-point lead has probability zero [DERIVED, same inputs]. The per-year odds-ratio interval, 0.991 to 1.001, includes 1.0 [DERIVED; table not located, UNVERIFIED].

- **The proxy may not transfer.** The age pattern is a sugar-based proxy, and the cube uses an intensive sweetener. Drinks with sugar substitutes run the other way: everyday use is 0.6% at 25 to 44 and 1.0% at 45 to 59 [P, UNVERIFIED].

- **Occasions are unverified.** Of 83 public statements, 35 name a Singha product; 29 of those 35 fall into eight segments, and 15 of the 29 (52%) come from one platform [S, UNVERIFIED; DERIVED: 15 ÷ 29].

- **Novelty is untested.** The Thai patent register has not been searched. A European application published on 5 March 2025 (EP 4,516,115 A2) has a claim 1 headline, in machine translation, for a method of making a carbonate effervescent powder or tablet without sodium carbonate, with citric acid added afterwards [P, SOURCED]. Its legal status and dependent claims were not checked, so any overlap with potassium bicarbonate is unknown [UNVERIFIED]. Freedom to operate is not assessed.

- **The dose is 0.55 volume of CO2 per 330 mL** [ASSUMPTION: design value]. The gate for this dose is for R&D to define; none is set here. Dissolved CO2 is 0.592 volume at 35 °C [S, SOURCED], so the dose is within solubility at every serve from 5 to 35 °C [DERIVED]. An earlier, higher dose is not used.

- **Recipe per 330 mL serve.** Potassium bicarbonate 0.81 g (317 mg potassium); citric acid 0.52 g at the stoichiometric minimum, or 2.10 g at pH 3.5 (3.70 g at pH 3.0); sucralose 33 mg, about 100 mg per kg [DERIVED from the dose, standard molar masses and the pH anchor]. No can or cube mass is sourced, so carrying weight is untested.

- **Sweetener permission rests on the level; classification is open.** The Thai annex for flavoured drinks (category 14.1.4) sets sucralose at 300 mg per kg under condition 127, which applies the level to the drink as consumed [P, UNVERIFIED; annex not yet captured for this proposal]. A Thai FDA clarification confirms that a category level extends to its sub-categories [P, SOURCED]. Potassium bicarbonate and citric acid are quantum satis in the category [P, UNVERIFIED]. Condition TH3 applies only to electrolyte drinks; whether the product is a beverage, edible ice or an electrolyte drink is an open regulatory question.

- **Units.** One cube per pouch is inferred, not confirmed with the organiser. The form wording may refer to three components; the design uses one, an unchecked limit.

- **Cost is a model.** No Thai price or quote was found. The model gives about THB 3.84 per cube in a 12-cube carton [DERIVED; pouch base, print and processing costs are ASSUMPTIONS]. A THB 15 shelf price per serve is an ASSUMPTION.

- **Dates and intentions.** Launch dates of Singha's citrus sparkling lines are not established. A Mintel release of 16 February 2026 gives 67% for sweets and desserts and 61% for ready-to-drink sugary drinks; its sample and wording are not on the page [S, SOURCED]. These are survey results, not purchase data.
