# Success and fail case studies for the Lemon Soda

Date: 20 October 2026. Desk research only; no consumer, bench or Thai shelf test has been run. No domestic case of a dissolvable drink was found, so overseas analogues appear only as labelled evidence.

## Tiers

Tiers are defined once, in Appendix A.

## Analogues that worked (evidence)

Overseas unless marked Thailand; labelled evidence only.

- **Waterdrop drink cubes** (Austria, 2016; US, 2021). A trade article reports 20 markets (only Austria and the US named), over 25,000 retail doors, $700,000 first-year revenue (year not stated) and over $100m in 2023 [1] (S, SOURCED), figures not checked here. A 2023 trade note reports sales at Walmart and Target in California and New York [2] (S, SOURCED).

## Analogues that failed (evidence)

- **Fizzies drink tablets** (US; dry drink tablets), the closest format match. Regional launch in 1956; eight nationwide flavours from 1962 [3] (S, SOURCED). Discontinued in the late 1960s over cyclamate concerns; a federal cyclamate ban, announced in 1969, took effect on 1 February 1970 [3] (S, SOURCED). Revived in 1996 and 2005; gone by 2016, reason not given [3] (S, SOURCED). An encyclopaedia's early-1970s end date is uncited [4] (S, UNVERIFIED).
- **Coca-Cola Life** (UK; sparkling-cola match only). Withdrawn in the UK in June (year not stated) to focus on three main cola variants. Sales fell 58%, base not given, yet it stays on sale in more than 30 other markets, including the US [5] (S, SOURCED).
- **Aha sparkling water** (Coca-Cola; sparkling match only). Coca-Cola cited portfolio realignment. First-quarter 2023 sales fell 42%, with a 1.9% category share, both second-hand [6] (S, SOURCED). From 2024, sales were to be limited to focused channels [6] (S, SOURCED).

Launched, outcome not evidenced:

- **Santo Cubes** (coffee cubes; crowdfunding). Kickstarter launch on 16 October 2018; over 5,000 samples, 78% positive; retail only a target [7] (S, SOURCED). The campaign pledged $83,592 from 1,749 backers against a $30,000 goal [8] (S, SOURCED), about 2.8 times the goal (DERIVED). Pledges are not sales.
- **Ricola Drink Cubes** (company release, 5 June 2025). Each cube is stated to flavour 400 to 600 ml of water [9] (P, SOURCED).
- **1,2,CUBE** (Coca-Cola Japan; freeze-dried tea and coffee cubes). About 15 cubes per JPY 600 pouch, sold on Amazon Japan from May or June [10] (S, SOURCED; year implied). No sales figure.
- **Simply Pop** (Coca-Cola prebiotic soda). Launch from late February 2025 in select regions, at $2.49 per 12-ounce unit [11] (S, SOURCED). No withdrawal found.
- **Incrediballs** (New Zealand; effervescent drink tablets). Online orders from 16 February; company claims and targets only [12] (S, SOURCED).
- **Singha Sparkling Water** (a different product from Lemon Soda; Thailand; lime flavour; 330 ml can). The company release gives the lime flavour, the can and 15 baht [13] (P, SOURCED); a trade report gives the same price [14] (S, SOURCED). The company calls that can sugar-free with 0% calories; that is its claim, not checked here (COMPANY CLAIM).

## What the analogues imply for the cube

1. **Overlap on flavour.** The comparison product is the Singha Lemon Soda can, a 330 ml can listed on the company product page (S, SOURCED). Source: https://www.singhacorporation.com/singhalemonsoda/th/, undated. A PR-tagged article on the trade site Brand Buffet, dated 19 August 2020, reports the can sold at 330 ml and THB 16 [25] (S, SOURCED). Two later articles say the first launch was in late 2020 [26][27] (S, SOURCED), so the launch month is not settled. On 20 October 2026 Lotus's Shop Online listed a single can at THB 17.00 [28] (S, SOURCED; online price). 24-can packs read THB 330 (THB 13.75 a can) at 7-Eleven ALL Online and Lotus's, and THB 325 (THB 13.54 a can) at Makro PRO. Six-can packs read THB 82 (THB 13.67 a can) at Makro PRO and THB 85 (THB 14.17 a can) at Lotus's [29] (pack prices S, SOURCED, except that the Lotus's pack prices are DERIVED because those pages print only Each; per-can prices DERIVED). The six retail rows behind the band are the Lotus's single can, the three 24-can packs and the two six-can packs. They give a band of THB 13.54 to 17.00 a can, with a median of THB 13.75. A seventh listing, a 7-Eleven ALL Online six-can pack at THB 172 (THB 28.67 a can), is left out of the band as an outlier, because it is about twice that retailer's own 24-can price per can and its page gives no cause [29] (S, SOURCED; per-can price DERIVED). These are online prices; no store shelf price was read. The company page describes the can as 0% sugar with no calories (COMPANY CLAIM). The products overlap on flavour and differ in form and pack: a ready-to-drink can, against a dissolvable Lemon Soda that makes the drink in the glass, one cube per pouch. The can is the taste reference for the blind test (Gate 4). The company's sparkling lime water is a different product and is not the comparison.
2. **Novelty.** Dissolvable drink cubes and tablets exist overseas, but none was found in Thailand. A Thai patent record for soluble coffee tablets does not show its legal status [15] (S, UNVERIFIED). The case rests on the consumption form and the fizz mechanism, neither tested on Thai shelves.
3. **Sweetener and composition.** The cube is made with potassium bicarbonate, citric acid, sucralose, 0.80 g of mannitol (tableting grade, INS 421; isomalt is the fallback) as binder and filler, and 0.05 g of natural lemon flavour, and no sodium carbonate. The recipe specifies no sodium salt. The binder and flavour amounts are assumptions until a cube is weighed, and the flavour carrier is not yet specified. The model recipe uses 55 mg of sucralose a serve, about 167 mg per kg of drink (DERIVED), chosen to match the sweetness of about 10 g of sugar per 100 mL (ASSUMPTION for the sweetness target). The Thai annex sets 300 mg/kg for sucralose in flavoured drinks under condition 127, which applies the level to the drink as ready to drink; only its flavoured-drink rows were read [16] (P, SOURCED). Potassium bicarbonate, citric acid and mannitol are quantum satis for flavoured drinks in the rows read [16] (P, SOURCED; flavoured-drink rows only); the lemon flavour is not yet checked against that rule. Condition TH3, which comes with the mannitol listing, applies only if the product is classed as an electrolyte drink. A Thai FDA clarification carries a major-category permission into sub-categories, not upward [17] (P, SOURCED; pages 1 to 3 read; our translation). The mannitol price is a proxy from import values, THB 0.08 to 1.27 per cube; the lemon flavour is not priced.
   - **Class and potassium.** The proposed class is flavoured drink (category 14.1.4). The written Thai regulatory opinion at Gate 1 confirms the class and tests whether the cube is a mineral-salt beverage under Notification 195, clause 3. Where potassium is present, clause 4(1.3) of that notification sets it at no more than 195 mg per litre. The cube carries about 317 mg of potassium a serve (DERIVED), which is 9% of the 3,500 mg Thai reference [32] (P), and about 960 mg per litre (DERIVED), about 4.9 times that ceiling. If the opinion classed the cube as a mineral-salt beverage, the formula would have to change. The clause text is unchanged from the Gazette original through the Thai FDA compilation of about 29 March 2023 [30] (P, read from page images). No later amending notification was found by title on the Thai FDA list to 20 October 2026 [31] (P, negative finding), but a post-March-2023 amendment inside a general notification is not excluded. A medical review of the potassium is required at Gate 2 before any sale.
   - **Sodium.** Sodium from the recipe is 0 to about 2 mg a serve (DERIVED). The water adds its own: 0 to about 7 mg at the WHO typical level of 20 mg per litre [33] (P). At the 100 to 200 mg per litre reported for Thai tap water [34] (S, a news report; not measured), the serve holds about 33 to 68 mg in total. These are calculations, not measurements, and no sodium-free or low-sodium claim is made.
4. **Carbonation.** The design dose is 0.55 volume of CO2 per 330 ml, the amount the reaction releases at 0 °C; how much stays dissolved at serve is not measured. Solubility falls as serves warm: about 0.655 volume at 30 °C and 0.592 at 35 °C [18] (S, SOURCED). The desk model keeps 0.55 volume within solubility from 5 to 35 °C (DERIVED), with about 0.04 volume to spare at 35 °C. A 2021 abstract gives an average detection threshold of 0.44 g CO2 per litre for sparkling mineral waters [19] (S, abstract only; PROXY for a sweetened soft drink). Against that threshold, the desk model at 20 °C estimates three things (DERIVED on ASSUMPTION inputs; not measured). Surface loss alone keeps the dissolved carbon dioxide above the threshold for about 1.7 to 3.4 hours if the full dose dissolves at serve. Bubble formation could cut this to about 15 minutes (champagne proxy, PROXY). A drink with less than 41% of the dose dissolved at serve starts below the threshold. The model puts dissolution at 25 °C at about 6 minutes stirred and about 11 minutes not stirred for a central set of assumed inputs, and at 3 to 22 and 5 to 74 minutes across the assumed ranges. Gate 3 (dissolved carbon dioxide) restates its pass rule before the bench run, and no fizz claim is made until a measured test exists.
5. **Taste.** Untested. The desk model gives a pH of about 3.4 to 3.5, and the working pH of 3.5 is an anchor (ASSUMPTION). A 2025 abstract on four effervescent supplements reports bitterness, astringency and a metallic quality, with no numbers [20] (S, SOURCED; abstract only).
6. **Demand.** Santo's backers and the 1,2,CUBE launch are not retail evidence. Nearest domestic data: an August 2025 nationwide survey of 5,467 respondents ranked quality (24.55%), low price (24.12%) and convenience (22.06%), base not stated [21] (P, SOURCED). None covers dissolvable drinks.
7. **Risk.** US prebiotic soda market figures in trade press are undated or rest on an unread analyst note, so none is used [11] (S, SOURCED). A news headline dated 28 July 2026 says a new study questioned prebiotic soda claims; only the headline was readable [22] (S, UNVERIFIED). The proposal makes no claim of this kind.
8. **Patent.** EP 4,516,115 A2, published 5 March 2025, has a machine-translated claim 1 headline for a carbonate effervescent powder or tablet made with no sodium carbonate [23] (P, SOURCED for publication data and the headline). Its legal status is not checked; its pending status is a mirror's assumption, and a freedom-to-operate review is needed before any launch.

## Limits

- The desk model is not a measurement. The pH, the dissolution time and the fizz time are model estimates on assumed inputs, and nothing has been tasted.
- Outcomes are not evidenced for the six launched analogues; absence of withdrawal is not success.
- Fizzies is the only dissolvable drink tablet in this set with a recorded withdrawal. This is a limit, not a filled gap.
- The 35 °C value rests on one reproduced handbook table; a standards database gives 0.839 at 25 °C (Ostwald basis) against 0.759 in that table [24] (P, SOURCED). R&D should confirm before any carbonation wording.
- Check the 300 mg/kg level against the official annex before any labelling or filing.
- The can prices are online listings read on 20 October 2026, not shelf prices. The 7-Eleven 24-can price of THB 330 is a reduced price (THB 408 is printed beside it) inside a promotion window that ends on the read date. The Lotus's pack prices are read from pages that print only Each. The 7-Eleven six-can listing at THB 172 is left out of the band as an outlier whose cause is unknown.
- The sources differ on the can's launch month, and no original company launch release was found. The launch facts rest on press articles, one of them PR-tagged.
- The sodium figures are calculations, and the Thai tap-water level is a press report, not a measurement.
- Patent statuses are unresolved. The product's class is open until the written Thai regulatory opinion (Gate 1), including whether Notification 195 for mineral-salt beverages applies to the cube.
