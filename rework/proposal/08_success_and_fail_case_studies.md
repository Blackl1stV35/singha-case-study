# Success and fail case studies for the lime cube

Date: 19 October 2026. Desk research only; no consumer, bench or Thai shelf test has been run. No domestic case of a dissolvable drink was found, so overseas analogues appear only as labelled evidence.

## Tier key

P: a government or regulator text read in full, a company's own filing, official statistics, a peer-reviewed article read in full, or a primary database. S: any other source, including press, trade portals, consultancy pages, company web pages that are not filings, handbooks reproduced online, and search snippets. SOURCED: the text was captured and read. UNVERIFIED: not captured, blocked, unreadable, or only a snippet was read. DERIVED: our arithmetic, inputs and tiers shown. PROXY: a stand-in for what is not measured. ASSUMPTION: a value chosen for the model, not sourced.

## Analogues that worked (evidence)

Overseas unless marked Thailand; labelled evidence only.

- **Waterdrop drink cubes** (Austria, 2016; US, 2021). Trade press reports 20 markets, over 25,000 retail doors, first-year revenue of $700,000 (year not stated) and over $100m revenue in 2023 [1] (S, SOURCED). The article names no source for these figures. A May 2023 trade note reports sales at Walmart and Target locations in California and New York [2] (S, SOURCED).

## Analogues that failed (evidence)

- **Fizzies drink tablets** (US; dry tablets that fizz into a drink), the closest format match. Regional launch in 1956; national flavours from 1962 [3] (S, SOURCED). Discontinued in the late 1960s over cyclamate concerns; a US federal ban on cyclamate in consumer products, ordered in 1969, took effect on 1 February 1970 [3] (S, SOURCED). Revived in 1996 and 2005; gone by 2016, reason not given [3] (S, SOURCED). An encyclopaedia entry gives an early-1970s end date that it tags as needing a citation [4] (S, UNVERIFIED).
- **Coca-Cola Life** (UK; sparkling-cola match only). Withdrawn in the UK in June (year not stated), to focus on its three main cola variants and its other drinks. Sales fell 58% over a year, base not given; the drink was still sold in more than 30 other markets, including the US [5] (S, SOURCED). The stated reason is focus, not sales.
- **Aha sparkling water** (Coca-Cola; sparkling match only). Q1 2023 sales fell 42% and category share was 1.9%, both attributed to an unread trade data firm [6] (S, SOURCED). From 2024, sales were to be limited to focused channels [6] (S, SOURCED).

Launched, outcome not evidenced:

- **Santo Cubes** (coffee cubes in an instant-coffee format; crowdfunding campaign). Kickstarter launch 16 October 2018; over 5,000 samples, 78% positive; retail only a target [7] (S, SOURCED). Campaign (currency code and year not shown on the page): $83,592 from 1,749 backers against a $30,000 goal [8] (S, SOURCED), about 2.8 times the goal (DERIVED: $83,592 divided by $30,000; inputs [8] and [7], S, SOURCED). Pledges are not sales.
- **Ricola Drink Cubes** (company release, 5 June 2025). Each cube flavours 400 to 600 ml of water [9] (S, SOURCED; not a filing). No markets, price or sales.
- **1,2,CUBE** (Coca-Cola Japan; freeze-dried tea and coffee cubes). About 15 cubes per pouch at JPY 600, first sold on Amazon Japan in May or June, with the year implied by the June 2021 article date [10] (S, SOURCED). No sales figure.
- **Simply Pop** (Coca-Cola prebiotic soda). Launch from late February 2025 in selected regions, at $2.49 per 12-ounce unit [11] (S, SOURCED). No withdrawal found.

## What the analogues imply for the cube

1. **Novelty.** Dissolvable drink tablets and cubes exist overseas; none was found in Thailand. A Thai patent record for soluble coffee tablets does not show a legal status [14] (S, UNVERIFIED). The case rests on the consumption form and the fizz mechanism, neither tested on Thai shelves.
2. **Sweetener and composition.** The cube uses potassium bicarbonate (not sodium carbonate) and sucralose. Per 330 mL the model recipe uses 0.81 g of bicarbonate (DERIVED from the dose; recipe ASSUMPTION) and 33 mg of sucralose, about 100 mg/kg (DERIVED: 33 mg divided by 0.33 kg). Thai permission for flavoured drinks (category 14.1.4) is 300 mg/kg for sucralose, under condition 127, which applies the level to the drink as consumed [15] (P, SOURCED). Potassium bicarbonate and citric acid are quantum satis, with no numerical limit. Condition TH3 applies only to electrolyte drinks; the product's classification is open. A Thai FDA clarification carries a major-category permission, such as 14.1.4, into its sub-categories, but a sub-category permission does not extend upward [16] (P, SOURCED; unofficial translation).
3. **Carbonation.** The recommended dose is 0.55 volume of CO₂ per 330 mL. Solubility falls as serves warm: about 0.655 volume at 30 °C and 0.592 at 35 °C [17] (S, SOURCED). The desk model keeps 0.55 volume within solubility at every serve from 5 to 35 °C (DERIVED); the 35 °C margin is about 0.04 volume (DERIVED: 0.592 from [17], S, SOURCED, less the 0.55 dose, ASSUMPTION). No gate is set (R&D to define one); no fizz claim is made until a measured retention test exists.
4. **Taste.** Untested: no sensory test has been run. The target pH of 3.5 is a working anchor (ASSUMPTION). A 2025 abstract on four effervescent supplements reports bitterness, astringency and a metallic quality; it gives no numbers [18] (S, SOURCED).
5. **Demand.** Santo's backers and the 1,2,CUBE launch are not retail evidence. The nearest domestic data are general purchase drivers: an August 2025 survey of 5,467 respondents ranked quality (24.55%), low price (24.12%) and convenience (22.06%), base not stated [19] (P, SOURCED). None covers dissolvable drinks.
6. **Risk.** Trade-press market sizes are undated or rest on an unread analyst note; none is used [11] (S, SOURCED). A news headline dated 28 July 2026 says claims for prebiotic soda were questioned in a new study; only the headline was readable [21] (S, UNVERIFIED). The proposal makes no claim of this kind.
7. **Patent.** EP 4,516,115 A2 (published 5 March 2025) covers a method of making carbonate effervescent powders, granules or tablets, per a machine-translated claim headline [20] (P, SOURCED). Its pending status is a mirror's assumption; a freedom-to-operate review is needed before any launch.

## Limits

- The desk model is not a measurement.
- Outcomes are not evidenced for the six launched analogues; an absence of withdrawal in search is not success.
- Fizzies is the only dissolvable drink tablet among the analogues; no modern dissolvable carbonated cube failure was found. This is a limit, not a filled gap.
- The 35 °C solubility value rests on one reproduced handbook table. A standards database gives 0.839 at 25 °C on an Ostwald basis, against 0.759 in the table; the rows read from it run from 0 to 25 °C, with no 30 or 35 °C row [22] (P, SOURCED). R&D should confirm before any carbonation wording.
- The 300 mg/kg sucralose level is read from a downloaded copy; check it against the official text before any labelling or filing.
- Patent statuses and the product's classification are unresolved. Several page texts were read through an extraction tool; check figures against live pages.
