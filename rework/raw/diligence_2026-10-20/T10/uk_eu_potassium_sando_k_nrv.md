# UK Sando-K SmPC, EU reference intake and EFSA adequate intake, saved extracts (T10)

Date read: 20 October 2026.

## 1. Sando-K effervescent tablets (potassium chloride 600 mg and potassium bicarbonate 400 mg), UK SmPC
- URL: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/959/smpc (electronic medicines compendium; Stirling Anglian Pharmaceuticals; last revised 22 May 2024). Tier P (regulator-approved SmPC text on the UK emc).
- Section 4.2: 2 to 4 tablets daily "(24 to 48 mmol K+)", so about 12 mmol (about 469 mg) potassium per tablet by our arithmetic (12 x 39.098); the page does not give mg per tablet. Administration: dissolve in water; volume not stated.
- Section 4.3 contraindications: severe renal impairment with oliguria; inadequately treated Addison's disease; hyperkalaemia from any cause; crush injuries; acute dehydration.
- Section 4.4: care to avoid dosage above requirements in impaired renal function; periodic serum electrolytes and ECG; caution with potassium-sparing diuretics, ACE inhibitors and acidosis.
- Section 4.5: "If co-administered with potassium-sparing diuretics and ACE-inhibitors, the risk of hyperkalaemia must be considered."

## 2. EU reference intake and "significant amount" rule (Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011, Annex XIII)
- Copy read: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/1169/annex/XIII (UK-retained version of the EU Regulation, marked latest revised; the EUR-Lex page returned no text). Tier S for the copy; content is the Annex text.
- Part A point 1: potassium reference value 2 000 mg (adults). Part A point 2: significant amount is 15% of the reference value per 100 g or 100 ml (non-beverages), 7.5% per 100 ml for beverages, and 15% per portion for single-portion packages. Our arithmetic: 300 mg per 100 g or ml, 150 mg per 100 ml for beverages, 300 mg per single portion.
- Our reading for the cube: 316.6 mg per 330 ml is 96 mg per 100 ml, under the beverage line of 150 mg per 100 ml, but 316.6 mg is above the single-portion line of 300 mg for a single-portion pack. Which basis applies to a dissolvable cube is not settled; it is a reason to keep potassium claims off the pack.

## 3. EFSA adequate intake for potassium
- URL: https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/press/news/161025 (EFSA press release, 25 October 2016). Tier P (EU agency).
- Adults including pregnant women 3,500 mg a day; adolescents 15 to 17 years 3,500; lactating women 4,000; children 11 to 14 years 2,700. The page does not mention upper intake levels or supplements.

## 4. US NIH Office of Dietary Supplements and Harvard
- ODS potassium pages (professional and consumer) returned HTTP 403; not read.
- Harvard T.H. Chan "The Nutrition Source: Potassium", reviewed March 2023, https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/potassium/ . Tier S. Adequate intakes (US): women 19 and over 2,600 mg, men 19 and over 3,400 mg. Warns that people with compromised kidneys eating a high-potassium diet or using potassium-based salt substitutes can accumulate potassium, and that medicines that retain potassium, including NSAIDs, can raise it; advises checking with a doctor before using potassium salt substitutes if medicines raise potassium or excretion is poor. No mg per food values on the page.
