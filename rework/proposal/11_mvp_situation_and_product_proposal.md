# MVP situational analysis and product proposal: Singha Lemon Soda and the Cube

Date: 19 October 2026. Scope: the Singha Lemon Soda product line, consumed from the 330 mL can, and the addition form (the Cube, one dissolvable cube dropped into 330 mL of water). No other product is in scope.

Tiers: P (regulator text, company filing or release, official statistics), S (press, trade or consultancy page, company claim relayed by press, search snippet opened), DERIVED (our arithmetic, inputs shown), ASSUMPTION (chosen value, not sourced), UNVERIFIED (not opened, blocked or not found). Every sourced figure carries its date and its source in section 9.

## 0. Reading of the brief

- The MVP product is Singha Lemon Soda, a ready-to-drink lemon soda in a 330 mL can. We read "addition way of consumption" as the Cube: one cube dropped into 330 mL of water to make the same lemon drink in the glass.
- Only lemon is in scope. Other Singha flavours appear only as options in the proposal, not as a plan.
- Product-level figures for Singha Lemon Soda are mostly launch-era or press figures. No audited product figure is public. Where a figure is missing, section 4 says so and no figure is estimated.

## 2. Situational analysis

### 2.1 Customer base

What is known:

- The company has described its target buyers in three ways over time: healthy refreshment (2020 launch, S), younger and health-conscious buyers (2021 CEO statement, S), and Gen Z (2024 release, S). These are company claims, tagged COMPANY CLAIM.
- The only category profile is a Bangkok study of Gen X and Gen Y carbonated-drink buyers, n = 400, S, 31 August 2025. It gives a modal profile only, a man aged 27 to 44. The sampling method is not stated and no percentages are given.
- Purchase pattern (Kantar, S, 9 April 2024): 90% of urban consumers drink carbonated drinks out of home, more than 22 purchases a year, about THB 19 per trip. Thai press of 11 January 2024 reports the same figures.
- Preference (Mintel, S, 27 December 2023): 37% of Gen Z prefer sweet-flavoured non-alcoholic drinks, against 30% of the total sample; 43% of consumers aged 45 and over prefer low, no or reduced sugar, against 33% of Gen Z.
- Sugar and sweetness (Mintel, S, 16 February 2026): 44% say they strictly try to reduce sugar; 27% are open to sugar or salt alternatives; sweetness preference is 40% full, 36% less sweet, 24% undecided.
- Population context (NSO sample, S): the 2021 Health Behaviour of Population Survey base is 57% female and 53% rural. This is the adult population, not Singha buyers.

What is not known: age, sex, income, generation and purchase channel for Singha Lemon Soda buyers; any generation split of lemon-soda buyers. These are gaps (section 4).

Persona (proposed, ASSUMPTION): urban adults aged 25 to 44 who buy sparkling soft drinks out of home and want less sugar without losing taste. The plan's lead band, men aged 25 to 34, is an ASSUMPTION. The only confirmed band is men aged 27 to 44.

### 2.4 What consumers like, and why

- Refreshment is the category reason most often cited (Thai press, 11 January 2024, S; StrategyHelix, 2026, S).
- Taste matters: about two in five consumers name taste as a decision factor for soft drinks (Innova, 31 October 2025, S).
- Cost can be decisive, and soft drinks are price sensitive (Innova, S; Euromonitor summary, November 2025, S).
- Carbonated drinks are bought on impulse and paired with meals (Euromonitor summary, S).
- Sugar: consumers expect taste to match zero sugar (Coca-Cola Thailand, June 2021, S; Mintel, February 2026, S).
- No lemon-specific survey on reasons to choose was found. These are category reasons, not lemon reasons.

## 3. Product proposal

### 3.1 Target customer

- Does the base still hold? Not measurable. No volume or buyer data after 2021 is confirmed, so the base trend cannot be shown. This is a limit.
- Segments to convert: people who want less sugar and still want the taste. Mintel (2026, S) puts the pool at about a quarter of its sample (27% open to sugar or salt alternatives; sample not stated). Use 27% as an ASSUMPTION base; the sample is not stated.
- Percentage of base lost: no sourced figure. Cannibalisation of the can is carried as an ASSUMPTION in the volume model; the plan's review threshold is 50% of buyers saying they switched.
- Lead segment: the plan leads with men aged 25 to 34 (ASSUMPTION). The confirmed band is men aged 27 to 44 from the Bangkok category sample. We recommend keeping the confirmed band as the lead and treating 25 to 34 as a labelled test.

### 3.2 Competitive advantage

- Form: a dissolvable cube in a single-serve pouch replaces a 330 mL can. It is lighter and smaller to carry. Per serve, the can costs THB 16 to 17 (S, 2020 to 2023) against a Cube planning price of THB 9.00 ex-factory and a THB 15 base shelf price (ASSUMPTION). Any per-serve comparison is assumption-driven.
- Sugar: a sugar-free claim requires less than 0.5 g of sugar per reference serving (Notification 445, P). The Cube's sugar-free status is not yet tested against that threshold.
- Not confirmed: taste, carbonation and consumer preference against cans. These need a blind test (plan, Gate 4).

### 3.3 Value-chain change

- Cost minimisation: the Cube's cost is dominated by pouch and print (THB 2.80 of THB 4.09 per cube, ASSUMPTION inputs). The plan's figure is not verified against a quote; THB 0.12 per cube is not itemised.
- Asset turnover: the Cube has no asset base yet. It avoids can filling and cold chain, but no co-packer line is verified. Peer asset turnover is in section 2.2 as context only.
- Scale: at 200,000 cubes a year (ASSUMPTION base), fixed cost is THB 1.25 per cube at THB 250,000 and THB 2.50 at THB 500,000 (DERIVED). At 50,000 cubes, THB 5.00 and THB 10.00.
- Carton: THB 0.67 per cube in a six-cube carton at THB 4 (ASSUMPTION), THB 0.42 in a twelve-cube carton at THB 5 (ASSUMPTION, scaled).
- Co-packer scale: one Thai dry-powder maker states a minimum of 500,000 units per SKU (S, company statement). That is 2.5 years of the 200,000-cube base, so the base is below the minimum unless the minimum is negotiated.
- Can scale (desk model, DERIVED): with THB 300 million of fixed cost a year, cost per can falls from THB 5.54 at 500 million cans to THB 5.12 at 1,676 million. With THB 600 million, it falls from THB 6.14 to THB 5.29. The variable floor is THB 4.94 per can.

### 3.4 Regulation and trademark

Regulation (Thai FDA, P):

- Notification 468 on food additives is in force from 12 December 2025, with a clarification signed 6 January 2026. Existing products have until 12 December 2027 to comply. A new launch such as the Cube is not in that grace period (research inference, S).
- Sweeteners are a defined functional class. Notification 450 (19 July 2024) requires the sweetener to be declared by functional class and name.
- The maximum level of an intense sweetener for flavoured drinks is not verified. The annex is too large to read in the research run.
- Sugar-free: less than 0.5 g sugar per reference serving; sodium-free: less than 5 mg sodium per reference serving (Notification 445, 2023).
- Classification: a food (flavoured-drink preparation), ASSUMPTION. It is a supplement only if marketed as one.
- Label: food serial number and net weight are required (Notification 450).

Trademark:

- The Department of Intellectual Property register was not reachable on 19 October 2026 (HTTP 403). No Thai status is confirmed for SINGHA LEMON SODA or SINGHA LEMON SODA (CUBE).
- Owner: an aggregator lists 12 SINGHA marks owned by Boon Rawd Brewery Co., Ltd. (S), none labelled for Thailand. The brief names Singha Corporation. The owner of the Thai SINGHA mark is not confirmed.
- A US application for SINGHA lemon soda goods (serial 98124128, filed 9 August 2023) was seen in a snippet only (UNVERIFIED).
- Thai trademark term is 10 years, first to file (original 1991 Act, P). The amended text was not read.
- Name conflict: the repo's working name is Lime Cube (no SINGHA). The brief uses SINGHA LEMON SODA (CUBE). The company's flavour list includes "Lemon Soda" as a name (capture 48), which a judge may read as close to the product line.

Registrations to resolve before pack print:

1. Search and file the Cube name at the DIP in class 32 (non-alcoholic beverages) and in any food class. Class 30 was not checked.
2. Confirm which entity owns the SINGHA mark in Thailand (Boon Rawd or Singha Corporation) before any licence is drafted.
3. Confirm with the Thai FDA whether the Cube needs a product notification or is a food under Notification 468, and check the sweetener limit in the annex.
4. Obtain the food serial number and label approval before pack print.

## 4. Limits and gaps

- No product-level gross margin, net margin, contribution cost, revenue share or unit share is disclosed for Singha Lemon Soda.
- No volume after 2021 and no current shelf price (the latest verified price is THB 17 for Ume in 2023).
- No promotion data for the lemon soda or for any soda competitor.
- No age, sex, income, generation or purchase channel for Singha Lemon Soda buyers, and no switching or loyalty data for lemon soda.
- Consumer reasons are category-level. No lemon-specific survey was found.
- The sweetener is named only in the company page extract (capture 48). The 2020 launch release and the press do not name it. The extract is not a raw save, and no raw save is held. This is a limit before judge use.
- Sugar-free wording is a company claim (2020 and 2022 releases, S). It is not checked against a label.
- The DIP register was not reachable. The trademark owner is unresolved. Class 30 is not checked.
- The annex limits for intense sweeteners in flavoured drinks are not verified.
- The plan's Cube inputs are ASSUMPTIONS. The pouch band in the plan runs from THB 0.67 to 2.70; the listed pouch is THB 1.30. THB 0.12 per cube is not itemised.
- Competitor prices are undated listings or 2022 figures. No current price has been verified for any competitor.
- Quotes are limited to one verbatim fragment per response; the research paraphrased the rest.
- No raw page saves were made to rework/raw in this pass. CLAUDE.md section 3 requires saved page text before a SOURCED figure is cited in a record. Until then, the company-page figures are extract-based.
- The Singha financial statements were not read; the press figures are entity-level and used only as context.
- Team-only: the financial projection in rework/56 and the Boon Rawd and Singha group proxy inputs are internal and are not used here.

## 5. Recommendation

- Keep the MVP to the lemon can and the Cube, as the brief says.
- Lead the consumer test with men aged 27 to 44 (confirmed band) and keep 25 to 34 as a labelled test.
- Before any judge-facing use, obtain raw saves of the company product page and the 2026 retail listing, confirm the trademark owner, and search the DIP register.
- Treat every unit-economics figure as assumption-driven until a co-packer quote and a shelf-price check are in the file.
