# Success and fail case studies for the Lemon Soda

Date: 20 October 2026. Desk research only; no consumer, bench or Thai shelf test has been run. No domestic case of a dissolvable drink was found, so overseas analogues appear only as labelled evidence.

## Tiers

Tiers are defined once, in Appendix A.

## Analogues that worked (evidence)

- **Waterdrop drink cubes** (Austria, 2016; US, 2021). A trade article reports 20 markets (only Austria and the US named), over 25,000 retail doors, $700,000 first-year revenue (year not stated) and over $100m in 2023 [1] (S, SOURCED), figures not checked here. A 2023 trade note reports sales at Walmart and Target in California and New York [2] (S, SOURCED).

## Analogues that failed (evidence)

- **Fizzies drink tablets** (US; dry drink tablets), the closest format match. Regional launch in 1956; eight nationwide flavours from 1962. Discontinued in the late 1960s over cyclamate concerns; a federal cyclamate ban, announced in 1969, took effect on 1 February 1970. Revived in 1996 and 2005; gone by 2016, reason not given [3] (S, SOURCED). An encyclopaedia's early-1970s end date is uncited [4] (S, UNVERIFIED).
- **Coca-Cola Life** (UK; sparkling-cola match only). Withdrawn in the UK in June (year not stated) to focus on three main cola variants. Sales fell 58%, base not given, yet it stays on sale in more than 30 other markets, including the US [5] (S, SOURCED).
- **Aha sparkling water** (Coca-Cola; sparkling match only). Coca-Cola cited portfolio realignment. First-quarter 2023 sales fell 42%, with a 1.9% category share, both second-hand. From 2024, sales were to be limited to focused channels [6] (S, SOURCED).

Launched, outcome not evidenced:

- **Santo Cubes** (coffee cubes; crowdfunding). Kickstarter launch on 16 October 2018; over 5,000 samples, 78% positive; retail only a target [7] (S, SOURCED). The campaign pledged $83,592 from 1,749 backers against a $30,000 goal [8] (S, SOURCED), about 2.8 times the goal (DERIVED). Pledges are not sales.
- **Ricola Drink Cubes** (company release, 5 June 2025). Each cube is stated to flavour 400 to 600 ml of water [9] (P, SOURCED).
- **1,2,CUBE** (Coca-Cola Japan; freeze-dried tea and coffee cubes). About 15 cubes per JPY 600 pouch, sold on Amazon Japan from May or June [10] (S, SOURCED; year implied). No sales figure.
- **Simply Pop** (Coca-Cola prebiotic soda). Launch from late February 2025 in select regions, at $2.49 per 12-ounce unit [11] (S, SOURCED). No withdrawal found.
- **Incrediballs** (New Zealand; effervescent drink tablets). Online orders from 16 February; company claims and targets only [12] (S, SOURCED).
- **Singha Sparkling Water** (a different product from Lemon Soda; Thailand; lime flavour; 330 ml can). The company release gives the lime flavour, the can and 15 baht [13] (P, SOURCED); a news reprint matches it [14] (S, SOURCED). The company calls that can sugar-free with 0% calories; that is its claim, not checked here.

## What the analogues imply for the cube

1. **Overlap on flavour.** The comparison product is the Singha Lemon Soda can, a 330 ml can on the company product page [15] (S, SOURCED). A launch release in the press reports an August 2020 launch, and two later articles say late 2020; the lemon can launched at THB 16 [16] (S, SOURCED). On 20 October 2026 Lotus's Shop Online listed a single can at THB 17.00, and 24-can packs were listed at THB 13.54 to 13.75 a can [17] (S, SOURCED; per-can figures DERIVED). Across six retail rows the band is THB 13.54 to 17.00, median 13.75 (DERIVED). These are online prices; no store shelf price was read. The products overlap on flavour and differ in form and pack: a ready-to-drink can, against a dissolvable cube made in the glass, one per pouch. The company's sparkling lime water is a different product and is not the comparison.
2. **Novelty.** Dissolvable drink cubes and tablets exist overseas, but none was found on sale in Thailand in desk searches. A Thai patent record for soluble coffee tablets does not show its legal status [18] (S, UNVERIFIED). The case rests on the consumption form and the fizz mechanism, neither tested on Thai shelves.
3. **Sweetener and composition.** The cube is made with potassium bicarbonate, citric acid, sucralose, mannitol as binder and filler (isomalt is the fallback) and natural lemon flavour, with no sodium salt and no sodium carbonate. Recipe sodium is 0 to about 2 mg a serve (DERIVED); water adds 0 to about 7 mg at the WHO typical level, or about 33 to 68 mg at the reported Thai tap-water level [19] (P; S, a news report), and no sodium-free or low-sodium claim is made. The binder and flavour amounts are ASSUMPTIONS until a cube is weighed. The model recipe uses 55 mg of sucralose per serve, about 167 mg/kg, equal in sweetness to about 10 g of sugar per 100 mL (DERIVED; sweetness an ASSUMPTION). The Thai annex sets 300 mg/kg for sucralose in flavoured drinks under condition 127, which applies the level to the drink as ready to drink, and lists potassium bicarbonate, citric acid and mannitol at quantum satis; only its flavoured-drink rows were read, and the flavour is not yet checked [20] (P, SOURCED). Condition TH3 covers electrolyte drinks only. A Thai FDA clarification carries a major-category permission into sub-categories, not upward [21] (P, SOURCED; pages 1 to 3 read; our translation). The team's class setting is flavoured drink (category 14.1.4); the Gate 1 opinion confirms it and tests whether the cube is a mineral-salt beverage under Notification 195, which sets potassium at no more than 195 mg per litre where present [22] (P, read from page images; no later amendment found by title to 20 October 2026; one inside a general notification is not excluded). The cube holds about 960 mg per litre (DERIVED), about 4.9 times that ceiling; a medical review at Gate 2 precedes any sale.
4. **Carbonation.** The working dose is 0.55 volume of CO2 per 330 mL. Solubility falls as serves warm: about 0.655 volume at 30 °C and 0.592 at 35 °C [23] (S, SOURCED). The desk model keeps 0.55 volume within solubility from 5 to 35 °C (DERIVED), with about 0.04 volume to spare at 35 °C. A 2021 abstract gives an average detection threshold of 0.44 g CO2 per litre for sparkling mineral waters [24] (S, abstract only; PROXY for a sweetened soft drink). The model keeps fizz above that threshold for about 1.7 to 3.4 hours at 20 °C by surface loss if the full dose dissolves at serve, or about 15 minutes with bubble loss (champagne proxy); under 41% dissolved, the drink starts below it (DERIVED on ASSUMPTION inputs). The R&D lead restates the pass rule before the bench run (Gate 3), and no fizz claim is made until a measured test exists.
5. **Taste.** Untested. The desk model gives a pH of about 3.4 to 3.5; the working anchor of 3.5 is an ASSUMPTION. A 2025 abstract on four effervescent supplements reports bitterness, astringency and a metallic quality, with no numbers [25] (S, SOURCED; abstract only).
6. **Demand.** Santo's backers and the 1,2,CUBE launch are not retail evidence. Nearest domestic data: an August 2025 nationwide survey of 5,467 respondents ranked quality (24.55%), low price (24.12%) and convenience (22.06%), base not stated [26] (P, SOURCED). None covers dissolvable drinks.
7. **Risk.** US trade-press prebiotic soda market sizes are undated or rest on an unread analyst note, so none is used [11] (S, SOURCED). A news headline dated 28 July 2026 says a new study questioned prebiotic soda claims; only the headline was readable [27] (S, UNVERIFIED). The proposal makes no claim of this kind.
8. **Patent.** EP 4,516,115 A2, published 5 March 2025, has a machine-translated claim 1 headline for a carbonate effervescent powder or tablet made with no sodium carbonate [28] (P, SOURCED for publication data and the headline). Its legal status is not checked; its pending status is a mirror's assumption, and a freedom-to-operate review is needed before any launch.

## Limits

- The desk model is not a measurement.
- Outcomes are not evidenced for the six launched analogues; absence of withdrawal is not success.
- Fizzies is the only dissolvable drink tablet in this set with a recorded withdrawal. This is a limit, not a filled gap.
- The 35 °C value rests on one reproduced handbook table; a standards database gives 0.839 at 25 °C (Ostwald basis) against 0.759 in that table [29] (P, SOURCED). R&D should confirm before any carbonation wording.
- Check the 300 mg/kg level against the official annex before any labelling or filing.
- Patent statuses are unresolved. The class is open until the Gate 1 opinion, including the Notification 195 question.
