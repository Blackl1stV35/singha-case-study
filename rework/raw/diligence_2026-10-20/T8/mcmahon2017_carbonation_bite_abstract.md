## Extracted facts
- Eleven sparkling wine treatments from 0 to 7.5 g CO2/L. A consumer panel (n = 48) compared 1.2, 2.0, 4.0, 5.8 or 7.5 g CO2/L against a 0 g/L control for carbonation and bite mouthfeel and sour taste.
- Differences from the control were significant (p <= 0.001) at 2.0, 4.0, 5.8 and 7.5 g/L. 1.2 g/L was NOT listed among the significant levels. The authors conclude that more than 1.2 g CO2/L was needed for consumers to detect mouthfeel differences.
- Reading for the cube: 1.2 g/L is the level at which no difference from the still control was found, not a level at which one was found. The cube's 1.08 g/L is below it (and below 2.0 g/L, the first level that differed).

## Limits
- Wine matrix with ethanol. The figures are detection-of-difference levels, not liking levels. See mcmahon2016_poster_bite_levels.md for the trained-panel levels of the same wine set.
