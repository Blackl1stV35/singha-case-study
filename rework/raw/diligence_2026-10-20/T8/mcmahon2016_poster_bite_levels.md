# McMahon, Culver, Castura and Ross (2016 poster): sparkling wines of varying carbonation, trained panel

M. McMahon, C. Culver, J. C. Castura, C. F. Ross)
- Date read: 2026-10-20
- Tier: S (conference poster, not peer reviewed in the poster form). Status: SOURCED, PROXY for a soft drink (wine matrix, trained panel).

## Extracted facts
- Treatments: 0, 1.2, 2.0, 2.8, 3.1, 4.0, 4.6, 4.9, 5.8, 6.7 and 7.5 g CO2/L, made by varying dextrose before second fermentation. Trained descriptive panel n = 11.
- Printed: "For burn, carbonation/bubble pain, and tingly, the lowest concentration at which there was a significant difference from the control wine was 2.0 g CO2/L. For bite, numbing, after-numbing, prickly, pressure, and foamy, the lowest concentration was 2.8 g CO2/L (p<0.05)."
- Table 1 of the poster: bite scores 1.9 (0 g/L), 2.1 (1.2 g/L) and 2.8 (2.0 g/L) share the letter a; 4.2 at 2.8 g/L is b. So 1.2 g/L does not differ from the control on any mouthfeel attribute.

## Limits
- Wine matrix with ethanol and trained assessors, so the levels are a PROXY for a consumer soft drink. They show how much CO2 gives a mouthfeel difference in wine, not what a consumer likes.
