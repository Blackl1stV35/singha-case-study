# Problem and pain point

## Problem statement

The need is a lemon drink made in the glass, quick to make and fair in price per serve, for Thai adults who already drink bottled non-alcoholic drinks. Lemon Soda, a dissolvable cube in a pouch, is the proposed answer. A national survey reports daily drinking of bottled non-alcoholic drinks for the 25 to 44 and 45 to 59 age groups [P, UNVERIFIED; the 2021 survey table was not read, so no figure is quoted here]. No Thai source tests a sparkling lemon form, so it is a hypothesis.

Lemon drinks are already sold ready to drink in cans in Thailand. No dissolvable drink cube was found on sale in Thailand in searches of Thai retail listings read on 20 October 2026, although drink cubes sell overseas [S, SOURCED]. The claim is that none was found in desk searches, not that none exists.

The flavour overlaps with a product the company already lists. The Singha Lemon Soda can is listed on the company product page, which describes it as 0% sugar with no calories [S, SOURCED; company page read 20 October 2026; the description is a COMPANY CLAIM]. It is also listed online at 7-Eleven, Lotus's and Makro PRO on 20 October 2026 [S, SOURCED]. A launch release carried in the trade press reports the launch in August 2020, and two later articles say late 2020 [S, SOURCED]; the lemon can launched at THB 16 in 2020 [S, SOURCED]. On 20 October 2026 a single can is listed at THB 17.00 at Lotus's Shop Online, and 24-can packs are listed at THB 13.54 to 13.75 a can [S, SOURCED for the listed pack prices; the per-can figures are DERIVED]. Across six retail rows the band is THB 13.54 to 17.00 a can, with a median of THB 13.75 [S, SOURCED; DERIVED]. These are online prices; no store shelf price was read. The assumed THB 15 shelf price per serve for Lemon Soda sits above the can's pack prices and below its single-can price [DERIVED comparison; the THB 15 price is an ASSUMPTION]. Lemon Soda differs in form and pack: one dissolvable cube per single-serve pouch, which makes the drink in the glass.

The problem is one of form, not flavour. The brief asks for new ingredients or production, or a new form of consumption; a lemon flavour alone would not be new, because the company already lists one. Scope is domestic Thailand only.

The named target is men aged 25 to 34, the proposed lead, compared with men aged 35 to 44; men aged 45 to 59 are a later volume segment, pended. The lead is a test, not a finding. Men aged 25 to 44 report current alcohol drinking more often than men aged 45 to 59 [P, UNVERIFIED; the 2021 table was not read, so no figure is quoted here]. This is a PROXY for drinking occasions, not a sugar measure, and no source splits the 25 to 34 and 35 to 44 bands. The only category profile found is a Bangkok study of carbonated-drink buyers (n = 400, 31 August 2025), which gives a modal buyer of a man aged 27 to 44 and no percentages [S, SOURCED]; the confirmed category band is therefore men aged 27 to 44.

## What is not known

- **No consumer test exists.** No panel, sales data or Thai retail audit exists. Taste is untested; the desk model gives a pH of about 3.4 to 3.5 (working pH 3.5), and no bench test of fizz has been run.

- **The size of the market is contested.** NielsenIQ values printed by the press are about THB 70,772 million for the 12 months to November 2024 and THB 66,000 million for the 12 months to January 2024 [S, SOURCED; press relays]. For 2025 the values found run from about THB 67,000 million (trade press, no source named) to THB 76,900 million (a figure credited to Kasikorn Research Center and relayed by Thansettakij on 8 September 2025, whose Kasikorn original was not found, and a Marketeer forecast of 9 May 2025, which names no source) [S, SOURCED]. The sources disagree on the direction of 2025, and none names NielsenIQ. No source says whether these values are retail or manufacturer values, and the paid datasets behind them were not opened, so no market is sized from them in this proposal.

- **The lead is a model result.** Per person within each age and sex group, the model ranks men aged 25 to 34 who drink alcohol first among six drinker groups in about 94% of 40,000 simulated outcomes (87% to 97% by month). Their margin over men aged 35 to 44 is about 3% on purchase intent (about 0.9 points at a 30 percent baseline) and about 6% after the potassium-caution exclusion, and both ranges include no difference. The survey power calculation uses a lead of about 1.2 points. By total reach no group leads clearly; the order depends on how the groups are defined. The heat effect (Bangkok's monthly temperature, 27 to 31 degrees Celsius) is an ASSUMPTION, because no Thai temperature-to-demand coefficient was found. No Singha buyer data exists, so every group is a market proxy [DERIVED from NSO 2021 age bands (UNVERIFIED) and an ASSUMPTION elasticity, uniform 0.5 to 1.0]. The per-year odds-ratio interval, 0.991 to 1.001, includes 1.0 [DERIVED; table not located, UNVERIFIED].

- **The proxy may not transfer.** The age pattern is a sugar-based proxy, and the cube uses an intensive sweetener. Drinks with sugar substitutes run the other way: everyday use is 0.6% at 25 to 44 and 1.0% at 45 to 59 [P, UNVERIFIED].

- **Occasions are unverified.** Of 83 public statements, 35 name a Singha product, and 15 of the 29 that fall into eight segments (52%) come from one platform [S, UNVERIFIED; DERIVED: 15 ÷ 29].

- **Novelty is untested.** The Thai patent register has not been searched. A European application published on 5 March 2025 (EP 4,516,115 A2) [P, SOURCED for publication data] has a claim 1 headline [S, SOURCED; machine translation] for a carbonate effervescent powder or tablet made with no sodium carbonate. Its legal status and dependent claims were not checked, so any overlap with potassium bicarbonate is unknown [UNVERIFIED].

- **The dose is 0.55 volume of CO2 per 330 ml** [ASSUMPTION: design value]. This is the carbon dioxide the reaction releases at 0 °C; how much stays dissolved at serve is not measured. The R&D lead restates the pass rule before the bench run (section 10, Gate 3, by M1); none is set here. Bench cubes are made by M2 and dissolved carbon dioxide is measured at 5, 20 and 35 °C. Dissolved CO2 is 0.592 volume at 35 °C [S, SOURCED], so the dose is within solubility at every serve from 5 to 35 °C [DERIVED].

- **Dissolution and fizz are model estimates, not measurements.** At 25 °C the desk model gives a dissolution time of about 6 minutes stirred and about 11 minutes not stirred for a central set of assumed inputs, and 3 to 22 minutes stirred and 5 to 74 minutes not stirred across the assumed ranges; this holds only if the cube breaks into fine pieces. At 20 °C, surface loss alone keeps the dissolved carbon dioxide above the 0.44 g/L sparkling threshold for about 1.7 to 3.4 hours if the full dose dissolves at serve; bubble formation could cut this to about 15 minutes (champagne proxy). If less than 41% of the dose is dissolved at serve, the drink starts below the threshold [DERIVED from literature solubility and diffusion values (S) on ASSUMPTION inputs; the 0.44 g/L threshold is a PROXY, S, abstract only]. Nothing has been measured.

- **Recipe per 330 ml serve (basis B, confirmed 19 October 2026; sucralose at 55 mg and the mannitol binder confirmed 20 October 2026).** Potassium bicarbonate 0.81 g (317 mg potassium); citric acid 2.10 g anhydrous at a working pH of about 3.5, of which 0.52 g neutralises the bicarbonate (the stoichiometric minimum) and 1.58 g is free acid; sucralose 55 mg, about 167 mg per kg of drink, equal in sweetness to about 10 g of sugar per 100 ml (ASSUMPTION for the sweetness target); a binder and filler of 0.80 g mannitol, tableting grade (INS 421), with isomalt as the fallback (ASSUMPTION for the amount; range 0.5 to 1.2 g); natural lemon flavour powder 0.05 g (ASSUMPTION; range 0.02 to 0.10 g; carrier not yet specified) [DERIVED from the dose, molar masses and the pH anchor; pKa inputs S, UNVERIFIED]. Potassium bicarbonate and citric acid together are 2.91 g. Central cube mass is 3.79 g (range 3.46 to 4.24 g, set by the binder and flavour allowances), DERIVED on the ASSUMPTION inputs; the listed amounts sum to 3.82 g, and the weighed cube settles the difference before print (Gate 9). The cube is 14 mm a side, density 1.38 g/cm3 (band 1.25 to 1.60 is an ASSUMPTION). With a 1.5 g pouch (ASSUMPTION) the packed unit is 5.29 g. Nothing is weighed, and the net weight on the label is a 3.8 g specification, verified by weighing before print (Gate 9). No can mass is sourced.

- **Sodium.** The recipe specifies no sodium salt and no lubricant; sodium from the recipe is 0 to about 2 mg a serve [DERIVED; the supplier declarations that would back the specification are not yet in hand, an ASSUMPTION until then, Gate 9]. Water adds its own sodium: 0 to about 7 mg a serve at the WHO typical level of 20 mg per litre [P; the WHO notes higher levels in some places], or about 33 to 68 mg at the 100 to 200 mg per litre reported for Thai tap water [S, a news report; not measured]. No sodium-free or low-sodium claim is made. These are calculations, not measurements.

- **Potassium and class are open until Gate 1 and Gate 2.** One serve holds about 317 mg of potassium, which is 9% of the 3,500 mg Thai reference [DERIVED; the reference is P, Notification 445 Annex 3, English translation published by the Thai FDA]; per litre it is about 960 mg [DERIVED]. The Thai notification for mineral-salt beverages (No. 195, clause 4(1.3)) sets potassium at no more than 195 mg per litre where it is present, so the cube would hold about 4.9 times that ceiling if it falls in that class [DERIVED]. The text of the clause is unchanged from the Gazette original through the Thai FDA compilation of about 29 March 2023 [P; read from page images]. No later amending notification was found by title on the Thai FDA list to 20 October 2026 [P, negative finding], but a post-March-2023 amendment inside a general notification is not excluded. The 2011 and 2020 amendments did not change clauses 3, 4(1) or 7. Whether the cube is a mineral-salt beverage under clause 3 is the Gate 1 question; the proposed class is flavoured drink. If it were classed as a mineral-salt beverage, the formula would have to change. A medical review of the potassium is required at Gate 2 before any sale.

- **Sweetener permission rests on the level; classification is open.** The Thai annex for flavoured drinks (category 14.1.4) sets sucralose at 300 mg per kg under condition 127, against about 167 mg per kg proposed; potassium bicarbonate and citric acid are quantum satis [P, SOURCED; only the flavoured-drink rows were read, not the full annex]. Mannitol is listed for flavoured drinks at quantum satis in Notification 468 [P, SOURCED]. The lemon flavour is not yet checked against this rule. A Thai FDA clarification confirms that a category permission extends to its sub-categories [P, SOURCED; read in part]. Condition TH3 applies to electrolyte drinks only, and this proposal makes no such claim. The team's setting for the class is flavoured drink (category 14.1.4); the written Thai regulatory opinion at Gate 1 confirms it and tests the mineral-salt rule (owner: regulatory affairs head, by M1).

- **Units.** The team's setting is one cube per single-serve pouch, sold in a six-cube carton (190 x 150 x 50 mm, an ASSUMPTION), owned by the marketing lead with finance, by M2. The form wording may imply three components, an unchecked limit.

- **Cost is a model.** No Thai price or quote was sought. The model gives about THB 4.09 per cube in the recommended 6-cube carton, and THB 3.84 in the 12-cube carton, the larger option [DERIVED; pouch, print and processing costs are ASSUMPTIONS or non-Thai listings]. These figures exclude the binder and the lemon flavour. The binder is priced only as a proxy from import unit values, THB 0.08 to 1.27 per cube, which takes the 6-cube cost to THB 4.17 to 5.36 [DERIVED; the import values are S, PROXY]; the lemon flavour is not priced, and no price is invented for it. The 6-cube cost includes a carton share of THB 0.67 per cube (THB 4 per 6-cube carton, an ASSUMPTION scaled from the 12-cube price). The 12-cube cost includes a carton share of THB 0.42 per cube (THB 5 per 12-cube carton, from a retail box listing used as a PROXY). A THB 15 shelf price per serve is an ASSUMPTION. Through the price chain (shelf price THB 15; invoice THB 9.34 after VAT and margins; excise THB 1.31; net THB 8.03) the contribution is THB 3.94 per cube after excise on the cost of THB 4.09. Before excise, at the planning price of THB 9.00 (60% of the shelf price, an ASSUMPTION), the contribution is THB 4.91. The two bases are shown separately, and the valuation uses THB 3.94. Both are before the binder and flavour are priced, so they are upper bounds until those are quoted (Gate 5) [DERIVED on PROXY and ASSUMPTION inputs]. The margins in that chain are not sourced Thai product margins. The 21.6% retailer margin is a group all-category proxy; CP ALL's 2025 group gross margin is 22.7% [P, a group figure covering all categories]. No sourced Thai distributor margin was found; the closest sourced value is the National Statistical Office's 2023 wholesale value-added share for non-alcoholic beverages, 16.2% [P, PROXY; a value-added measure, not a margin], and the 10% to 20% band used in the model is an ASSUMPTION. Section 05 compares the cube cost with the Singha Lemon Soda can, whose variable cost is about THB 3.3 before aluminium (90% range 2.6 to 3.9) [DERIVED on PROXY and ASSUMPTION inputs; an estimate, not a Singha figure].

- **Dates and intentions.** The release and reprint give March 2026 as the launch month of the Singha lime sparkling water, a different product from Lemon Soda, with no launch day [P, SOURCED; S, SOURCED]. A Mintel release of 16 February 2026 gives 67% (sweets and desserts) and 61% (ready-to-drink sugary drinks) for planned sugar cuts; it also reports that 44% say they strictly try to reduce sugar and 27% are open to sugar or salt alternatives. Its sample is not on the page [S, SOURCED].
