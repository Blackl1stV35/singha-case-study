# 21 CFR 201.306, potassium salt preparations intended for oral ingestion by man (T10)

Date read: 20 October 2026. The text is that of the US Code of Federal Regulations, read here from the Cornell Legal Information Institute copy (tier S, a copy of a primary text). The primary text was read later the same day: the official GPO print (`us_21cfr201_306_govinfo_cfr2014.md`) and the eCFR current text through its API (`ecfr_21cfr201_306_current_2026-10-20.md`), both P and both giving the same 100 mg and 20 mg per mL lines. Currency date is not shown on the copy; its source note lists amendments to 1975, 1990 and 1 February 2002.
- URL read: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/201.306 (official: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-201.306, not opened).
- Title: "Potassium salt preparations intended for oral ingestion by man."
- Paragraph (a): coated tablets supplying "100 milligrams or more of potassium per tablet": FDA would not act against continued marketing if conditions met, including a prescription caution, a warning on small-bowel lesions and a new-drug application.
- Paragraph (b): uncoated tablets (100 mg or more per tablet) and liquids (20 mg or more per mL): FDA may begin regulatory proceedings unless the labelling includes the prescription statement and advice to dissolve tablets in liquid and dilute liquids.
- Warning text begins: "There have been several reports, published and unpublished, concerning nonspecific small-bowel lesions" (paraphrase of the start of the quoted warning).
- The section contains no "99 mg" figure and says nothing about foods or dietary supplements.

## The 99 mg figure (separate from the CFR text)
- The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements fact sheets (https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Potassium-HealthProfessional/ and .../Potassium-Consumer/) returned HTTP 403 on 20 October 2026 and were not opened. The 99 mg figure was therefore not read at its usual primary source.
- Secondary read on 20 October 2026: Wikipedia, "Potassium in biology", subsection on labelling under Supplements (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_in_biology). It says that, because of the risk of small-bowel lesions, the US FDA requires some potassium salts containing more than 99 mg to be labelled with a warning, and it cites the NIH ODS "Potassium Health Professional Fact Sheet" of 2 June 2022. Tier S. The same sentence gives the amount as "about 1.3 mEq", which does not match 99 mg of potassium (99 mg / 39.098 = 2.53 mEq), so the secondary copy has an internal inconsistency.
- What this does and does not support: a labelling line at 99 mg of potassium is reported by a secondary page. Later the same day the Council for Responsible Nutrition page (`crn_99mg_otc_drugs_2026-10-20.md`, S) said that the 99 mg per dose limit is specific to over-the-counter drugs, that FDA has not ruled on potassium in dietary supplements, and that manufacturers have followed the limit in practice. The 99 mg line reads as the convention just under the 100 mg line of 21 CFR 201.306.
- Ratios (script): 316.6 / 100 = 3.17 (sourced to the CFR text, S copy); 316.6 / 99 = 3.2 (rests on the secondary 99 mg statement).

## What it means for the cube (our arithmetic and reading)
- The cube is a solid unit with 316.6 mg potassium, above the 100 mg per unit line; the made-up drink holds 316.6 mg in 330 mL, about 0.96 mg per mL, far below the 20 mg per mL line for liquids.
- Section 201.306 governs drug-type potassium salt preparations. Whether a food cube falls under it is a question for a US regulatory reader; the US rule is a comparator here, not the applicable Thai law.
