# Sweet-sour interaction studies, abstract level (Consensus index records)

- Date read: 20 October 2026. Route: Consensus search tool (abstracts as returned; articles not opened).
- Tier: S (abstract level). Status: SOURCED at abstract level.

## Mao et al. 2022, npj Science of Food
- URL: https://consensus.app/papers/details/d8beb9d5ce1d572bade4d4a4b86ee2a2/
- Abstract states: citric acid raised the absolute threshold of sucrose (0.424 to 0.624 %) and the Weber fraction (20.5 to 33.0 %); sucrose raised the absolute threshold of citric acid (0.0057 to 0.0082 %) and lowered its Weber fraction (17.96 to 9.53 %). The percentages are as printed (percent by weight, read as g per 100 mL).
- Used: citric acid detection threshold about 0.006 to 0.008 g per 100 mL, that is 0.057 to 0.082 g/L, in sucrose-containing solutions.

## Mao et al. 2025, npj Science of Food
- URL: https://consensus.app/papers/details/be6205296a6552c98fe5006691190936/
- Abstract states: sweetness of 2 to 10 % sucrose and sourness of 0.006 to 0.024 % citric acid showed mutual inhibition in time-intensity tests (30 assessors); an optimal sweet-to-sour acceptance ratio was determined but its value is not in the abstract.

## McBride et al. 2007 (first author R. McBride), International Journal of Food Science and Technology
- URL: https://consensus.app/papers/details/0ff41d49d54b5a1aa397a891a828f24e/
- Abstract states: sixteen sucrose and citric acid combinations in low-acid lemon juice; sucrose clearly suppressed perceived acidity; only the highest acid level unequivocally suppressed sweetness; an optimum sugar-acid blend was found but its values are not in the abstract.

## Limits
- No sourced optimum sugar-to-acid ratio. The benchmark ratio in the model is the ratio of the Brix and citric acid measured in commercial sodas (ali2022 file) and in the patent example (us7572471 file), not a sensory optimum.
