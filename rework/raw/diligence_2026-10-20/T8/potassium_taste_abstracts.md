# Potassium taste, abstract-level records

- Date read: 20 October 2026. Tier: S (abstract level) for all rows. Status: SOURCED at abstract level.

## Ben Abu, Harries, Voet, Niv 2018, Food Chemistry 255, 165-173
- URL: https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-taste-of-kcl-what-a-difference-a-sugar-makes/ (opened with WebFetch)
- States that KCl substitution leaves a bitter-metallic off-taste, and that trehalose and sucrose reduced the bitter-metallic taste of KCl while enhancing or not changing saltiness; perceived sweetness of the sugar strongly influenced how KCl tasted. KCl concentrations and thresholds are not on the page.

## Sinopoli and Lawless 2012, Journal of Food Science (Consensus record)
- URL: https://consensus.app/papers/details/4f704524ebfd5e4bb44b803160c4f300/
- Check-all-that-apply study: adding NaCl to KCl reduced unpleasant side tastes of KCl (bitter, chemical, metallic). No thresholds in the abstract.

## Murphy, Cardello, Brand 1981, Physiology and Behavior (Consensus record)
- URL: https://consensus.app/papers/details/22738c461fcf57bea7adf973388a0d1f/
- Fifteen halide salts at several concentrations; heavier cations and anions gave more bitter-tasting salts; both cation and anion contribute to taste. No thresholds in the abstract.

## Andres et al. 2015, Food Chemistry (Consensus record)
- URL: https://consensus.app/papers/details/83414242d4ff5eaaa659f40000901092/
- Nineteen commercial milk- and soy-juice based beverages: potassium was the major macromineral at 148 to 941 mg/L.

## Hershenhouse et al. 2025, Journal of Endourology (Consensus record)
- URL: https://consensus.app/papers/details/0848f9dc507450bba959a5762d9d07cd/
- Palatability of four liquid potassium citrate products, 63 adults, blinded swish-and-spit: overall palatability scores from 1.7 +/- 0.7 (unflavoured bulk product) to 4.3 +/- 0.6 (flavour-masked product) on a five-point scale; flavour-masked products scored better.

## Torrico et al. 2015, Journal of Food Science 80(6), S1404-S1411 (abstract record; see torrico2015_bitterness_recognition_KCl_caffeine.md)
- Bitterness recognition thresholds of caffeine and KCl together in solution: 0.0242 to 0.0586 g per 100 mL (one combined range, not separable by compound).

## Limits
- No open source gives a metallic recognition threshold for K+, or a bitterness threshold for K+ alone, in a sweet-sour drink; the only KCl bitterness figure is the combined caffeine and KCl range above (water, not a drink). The only quantitative taste threshold is the detection threshold in schiffman1995_potassium_salt_detection.md.
