# Schiffman et al. 1995: detection thresholds of potassium salts

- URL: https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/767025 (publication record with abstract)
- Citation: S. S. Schiffman, M. P. Manning, Z. S. Warwick, A. L. Crumbliss, "Detection thresholds of potassium salts are related to the molar conductivity of the anion", Brain Research Bulletin 37(6), 623-626 (1995), doi 10.1016/0361-9230(95)00054-i
- Date read: 20 October 2026 (record page read twice; the second read, through WebFetch, listed all nine salts). The article itself was not read.
- Tier: S (abstract-level record page). Status: SOURCED at abstract level.

## Extracted facts: mean detection thresholds, nine potassium salts (molar, as listed on the record)
1. acetate 0.00311 M
2. carbonate 0.00286 M
3. chloride 0.00242 M
4. citrate 0.000300 M
5. phosphate 0.00196 M
6. sulfate 0.00090 M
7. tartrate 0.00164 M
8. glutamate 0.00153 M
9. ascorbate 0.00375 M
- Thresholds correlate linearly with the molar conductivity of the anion (r = -0.92). The rank-order correlation between the sodium and potassium salt thresholds is 0.88. The authors conclude that detection is governed by the charge mobility of the anion.
- The record does not state a taste quality (salty, bitter, metallic) and does not say which citrate salt was used. These are detection thresholds in water only.

## Use in rework/71
- The drink holds 24.5 mmol/L K+. Against potassium chloride (0.00242 M, one K per salt) that is 10.1 times. Against potassium citrate (0.000300 M): 27 times on a tripotassium basis (three K per salt, 0.9 mM K+), or 82 times if one K per salt is assumed; the abstract does not say which. Against potassium carbonate (0.00286 M, two K per salt): 4.3 times on a K+ basis.
