# C3: what the product looks like (conditional, not validated)

C3 is a premium 250 ml glass botanical-citrus sparkling served in Singapore hotels, restaurants and bars. It is the conditional alternative-format concept. The test that decides it is venue velocity, which has not been measured.

## Product

- **Name:** working name only, to be cleared on IPOS. It must not use SINGHA.
- **Format:** 250 ml clear glass bottle with a crown cap, sold on-premise and not in retail cans.
- **Base:** carbonated water.
- **Citrus:** yuzu and calamansi, as whole-fruit or natural flavour. Lemon and yuzu species on the SFA list still need confirming.
- **Botanical:** one named whole extract with a long food-use history, such as lemongrass, kaffir lime leaf, ginger or pandan. Thai culinary botanicals are the likely route to avoid the 9 to 12 month SFA novel-food review. The SFA's 20-year rule counts food use outside Singapore, but this must be confirmed.
- **Sweetening and sugar:** no added sugar. Whether a sweetener is used is a formulation decision; a sugar-free claim needs 0.5 g or less per 100 ml with an energy statement.
- **Label:** flavour and occasion wording only. No health, therapeutic or functional wording. Singapore bars health claims (Reg 9(2)).
- **Serve:** over ice with a citrus garnish. Positioned as an alcohol-free option at dinner.

## Price and channel

- **Menu price:** S$8 ex-GST and ex-service (a search excerpt of an undated PDF; not verified).
- **Outlet markup:** 3x to 4x for the supplier's wholesale price. A 6.7x benchmark from a 2021 Ontario study is the stress case.
- **Distributor margin:** 15 to 30 percent (judgement).
- **Channel:** a Singapore distributor, not a group importer. Target: 10 to 15 venues first.

## Regulatory

- **BCRS:** glass sits outside the deposit scheme per the NEA page (updated 10 April 2026). The dine-in clause may not cover imported bottles. The NEA text must be read verbatim.
- **Nutri-Grade:** a sugar-free drink without sweeteners can reach Grade A; a sweetened one is capped at B, with the mark optional for both. The grade must be calculated on the final recipe.
- **Water import permit:** whether a flavoured botanical sparkling in glass needs one is open. An SFA enquiry is needed.

## Economics (scan model, unvalidated)

- **Variable cost:** S$0.43 to S$0.87 per bottle, central S$0.59 (exworks plus freight; glass cost is an assumption of 1.5x to 3.0x a can).
- **Break-even menu price:** about S$2.8 at 3x markup and about S$3.7 at 4x.
- **Volume for the fixed cost:** S$450k a year needs about 292k bottles at 3x (about 270 venues selling three a day), or about 445k at 4x.

## Kill criteria before 22 October

1. **Velocity:** a venue audit of 10 to 15 Singapore venues. Kill if the velocity cannot reach about three bottles a day across roughly 270 venues at 3x (about 400 at 4x).
2. **Glass cost:** three glass suppliers quoting 250 ml sparkling-grade bottles. Kill if the FOB price exceeds USD 0.60 per bottle.
3. **Margin:** two or three Singapore distributors. Kill if the margin exceeds 30 percent.
4. **Regulatory:** the NEA text for the glass exclusion, and an SFA enquiry on the water permit.

## What C3 cannot show

- Whether a venue will list a new 250 ml botanical sparkling at S$8. No dated menu price was found.
- Glass cost, distributor margin and velocity. All are judgement.
- Whether the on-premise occasion is larger than the retail can occasion.
