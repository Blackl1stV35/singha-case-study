### Slide 2. Executive summary

- **Title:** Executive summary
- **Exhibit:** a mind map. Centre: "Lemon Soda: one cube, dissolved in one 330 ml serve, made in the glass."
  1. **Problem statement.** Lemon drinks are sold ready to drink in cans. No dissolvable drink cube was found on sale in Thailand in desk searches, although cubes sell overseas. The gap is a new way of drinking, not a new flavour.
  2. **Identity problems.** Singha already sells a lemon soda can, which its product page describes as sugar-free (company claim), so Lemon Soda overlaps its flavour. The name needs a register search before use (Gate 7). The lion and SINGHA are for this proposal only.
  3. **Proposal.** One cube, dissolved in one 330 ml serve. The desk case at 200,000 cubes a year and THB 500,000 fixed cost gives an NPV of THB 0.17 million after excise and tax. That value is small, and across the stated ranges the chance of a positive NPV is about one in four. The pilot tests the two inputs that decide it.
  4. **KPI and expected goal.** At least 3 units per store per week in the in-store route; repeat of at least 15% in both cohorts; a pilot capped at THB 84,686 with nine creators on commission-led offers, a costed in-store route and conditional paid social; a measured decision at M10 to M11 to scale one route, repeat once, or stop. All targets are assumptions until the pilot reads.
- **Ask bar:** a gated proof programme, not a launch. The executive sponsor signs the Gate 0 record, which names one owner per gate and the first budget ceiling.
- **Footnote:** Proposer: Singha Corporation. Boon Rawd Brewery is the holding company in the company's own description; press and company sources describe the group differently (Appendix C). Desk model on assumed volumes and costs, not a forecast.
- **Speaker note:** Read the four branches in order and end on the ask. Say plainly that the value case is small and uncertain, and that the pilot tests demand and switching, which decide it.

## Section 3: The product

### Slide 6. The cube in use

- **Title:** The consumer makes the drink in the glass: one cube in 330 ml of water. The chemistry is modelled, not yet tasted.
- **On the slide:** three steps: open the pouch; drop the cube into 330 ml of water; stir, then drink. The desk model gives a pH of about 3.4 to 3.5 (working pH 3.5). Taste is not yet tested.
- **Exhibit:** a three-stage transition strip: the cube, dropped into water, and ready to drink. The current stage is large; its neighbours are small.
  - **Stage 1, the cube:** shelf life not yet tested. The storage test (35 C for 8 weeks, assumption) starts by M2 (Gate 5).
  - **Stage 2, dropped into water:** model estimate at 25 C, not measured. About 6 minutes stirred and about 11 minutes not stirred for a central set of assumed inputs; 3 to 22 minutes stirred and 5 to 74 minutes not stirred across the assumed ranges. This holds only if the cube breaks into fine pieces.
  - **Stage 3, ready to drink:** model estimate at 20 C, not measured. Surface loss alone keeps the dissolved carbon dioxide above the 0.44 g/L sparkling threshold for about 1.7 to 3.4 hours if the full dose dissolves at serve. Bubble formation could cut this to about 15 minutes (champagne proxy). If less than 41% of the dose is dissolved at serve, the drink starts below the threshold.
- **Draft label wording box:** "Drop one cube into 330 ml of water and stir." "Serving: one cube made up to 330 ml." "Contains an intensive sweetener: sucralose (working choice; the Gate 1 opinion is to confirm it)." "Single-serve pouch." Net weight: 3.8 g (specification; verified by weighing before print, Gate 9).
- **Label note:** Wording only, not cleared. No sugar-free, zero-sugar, health, hydration, sodium-free or fizz claim is printed. Potassium appears only where the Thai panel requires it; any warning waits for Gate 2.
- **Tests box:** before any taste, dissolution or fizz claim, four tests run: (1) a blind sensory test against a lemon-lime benchmark at pH 3.5 and either side, with a check for a potassium off-note; (2) weighed dissolution in 330 ml at 20, 25 and 30 C, stirred and not stirred, with the break-up recorded; (3) dissolved carbon dioxide at serve and its decline in a glass; (4) the cube's break-up time.
- **Speaker note:** Say which numbers are derived from sources and models (pH, acid, sweetness, potassium) and which are model estimates that no one has measured (dissolution time, fizz time, taste). The first measurement is a weighed cube in 330 ml with the break-up recorded.
- **Source line:** Desk chemistry model: charge balance with citric acid pKa values at zero ionic strength (S); the 0.44 g/L threshold from an abstract-only mineral-water study (S, PROXY); dissolution model from literature solubility and diffusion values (S, ASSUMPTION inputs); Sprite benchmark from a Serbian titration and a Peshawar assay (S). Nothing has been measured.

- **Potassium and class:** about 317 mg a serve, which is 9% of the 3,500 mg Thai reference (P). Per litre it is about 960 mg. A Thai notification for mineral-salt beverages (No. 195, clause 4(1.3)) sets potassium at no more than 195 mg per litre where it is present. The text is unchanged from the Gazette original through the Thai FDA compilation of about 29 March 2023 (P; read from page images). No later amending notification was found by title on the Thai FDA list to 20 October 2026 (P, negative finding), but a post-March-2023 amendment inside a general notification is not excluded. The 2011 and 2020 amendments did not change clauses 3, 4(1) or 7. Whether the cube is a mineral-salt beverage under clause 3 is the Gate 1 question. The proposed class is flavoured drink. If the Gate 1 opinion classes the cube as a mineral-salt beverage, the potassium would be about 4.9 times the ceiling, and the formula would have to change.
- **Kidney and medicines:** the desk model does not cover people on potassium-retaining medicines or with kidney disease. About 1% of men aged 25 to 34 and about 13% of adults aged 25 and over are in the caution group (model bounds 0.1% to 5.5% and 5% to 25%; DERIVED). A medical review is required at Gate 2 before sale.

- **Pack precaution (draft for the Gate 2 reviewer):** "Contains about 320 mg of potassium per cube. Ask a doctor or pharmacist before drinking if you have kidney disease or take medicines that can raise blood potassium. Do not drink more than one cube a day." The daily limit is a team assumption; no guideline sets it.

A Thai nutrition guideline recommends 1,500 to 2,000 mg a day for kidney patients on a low-potassium diet; for them one serve is 16% to 21% of the day (S).

### Slide 10. Valuation and shareholder value

- **Title:** At base inputs the cube adds THB 0.17 million after excise and tax, but across the stated input ranges the chance of a positive NPV is about one in four.
- **Exhibit left:** NPV after excise and tax (THB million) at 200,000 cubes a year and THB 500,000 fixed cost: 50,000 cubes, minus 1.41; 184,325 cubes, 0 (break-even with no cannibalisation); 200,000 cubes, 0.17 (base case); 500,000 cubes, 3.32; 1,000,000 cubes, 8.59. The before-excise, pre-tax grid is in Appendix B.
- **Exhibit middle (stated uncertainty):** the chance of a positive NPV over 200,000 draws of every input is 0.26 after excise and tax (0.40 before excise and tax; 0.58 on a simpler structure). The test fixed in advance needs 0.95, and no basis reaches it. Every 90% range includes zero. The expected NPV after excise and tax is minus THB 0.88 million; its 90% range is minus THB 3.40 to plus THB 1.59 million.
- **Exhibit right (value drivers):**
  1. **Contribution:** THB 3.94 a cube after excise (THB 4.91 before excise, on the planning price).
  2. **Cannibalisation:** the NPV is zero when about 7.5% of cube buyers would otherwise buy a can, and negative above that. The review level is 7%, just under the break-even.
  3. **Volume:** the NPV is zero at 184,325 cubes a year with no cannibalisation (148,076 before excise). The base is 200,000. The expected NPV is zero at about 335,000 cubes across the ranges.
  4. **Fixed cost and shelf price:** these explain most of the spread. At 200,000 cubes the NPV stays positive up to THB 0.54 million a year of fixed cost.
  5. **Option:** the pilot reads demand after 13 weeks and can stop. In the model the demand read is worth THB 0.03 to 0.25 million. Pilot spend is not sized from sources.
- **How value reaches shareholders:**
  - The model books the profit in Singha Corporation. The booking entity is a stated limit.
  - If Boon Rawd Brewery held all of Singha Corporation and Singha paid out all of the profit, Boon Rawd would receive about THB 151,000 a year in years 2 to 5, or about THB 136,000 if only half of each dividend is excluded from its tax. Year 1 is a loss year and pays no dividend. Neither the holding nor the payout was found in the sources read.
  - Each 10% stake in Boon Rawd, if it passes the dividend through, would receive about THB 15,000 a year before holder tax.
  - Incremental profit equal to 0.1% of Singha Corporation's 2025 net profit needs about 1.1 million cubes a year with no cannibalisation.
- **Scale note:** Singha Corporation's 2025 net profit was about THB 3,085 million (MGR Online, 23 May 2026, S; a single-company press figure). The base-case after-tax NPV is about 0.005% of one year's profit. Boon Rawd Brewery's 2022 net profit was THB 5,793 million (Thaiger, 18 April 2025, S), so against a wider base the shares would be smaller.
- **Takeaway:** at the base volume the cube is a learning option, not a value driver. Its value is small on every basis and can be zero or negative.
- **Source line:** Desk model: five years, no terminal value; discount rate 10% (assumption: the Bank of Thailand policy rate of 1.00% from the decision of 26 August 2026, S, plus a 9-point premium); corporate income tax 20% (Revenue Department, S); setup cost depreciated over five years; year 0 carries one year of fixed cost and year 1 sells half the base volume (assumptions). 200,000 Monte Carlo draws with a fixed seed, conditional on the stated ranges; not market data. Payback about 3.3 years after excise and tax.
- **Speaker note:** Say that the base case is small and positive when every input sits at its middle value, and that the chance of a positive NPV does not reach the 0.95 set before the analysis. Fixed cost and shelf price explain most of the spread. The pilot reads demand and asks buyers about switching; the model prices the demand read but not the switching or repeat reads.

- **Ingredient list, as fact:** potassium bicarbonate, citric acid, sucralose, mannitol (binder and filler) and lemon flavour. - **Barred, no clearance route:** sugar-free, zero-sugar and no-added-sugar wording; sodium-free, low-sodium and very-low-sodium wording; health, function, electrolyte, hydration and potassium-benefit wording; calorie wording; certification and award wording, none of which is verified. The drink does not meet the Thai standard for an electrolyte drink (Notification 195) and is not presented as one.
- **Sodium, basis and tier:** the recipe specifies no sodium salt and no lubricant, and the specification excludes sodium-salt lubricants, preservatives, coated acids and a sodium-bearing pouch seal layer (ASSUMPTION until supplier declarations are in hand). Sodium from the recipe is 0 to about 2 mg a serve (DERIVED). Water adds its own sodium: 0 to about 7 mg a serve at the WHO typical level of 20 mg per litre (P; the WHO notes higher levels in some places), or about 33 to 68 mg at the 100 to 200 mg per litre reported for Thai tap water (S, a news report; not measured). The one-serve total is therefore about 0 to 9 mg at the WHO typical level, and about 33 to 68 mg with Thai tap water. These are calculations, not measurements.
- **Sodium claims:** Notification 445 Table 1 limits are per serving: sodium-free below 5 mg, very low below 35 mg, low below 140 mg. The result depends on whether the water is counted. With water counted at 20 mg per litre, sodium-free fails. Only low sodium passes at Thai tap-water levels. A claim would need sodium measured in the made-up drink from three production batches by an accredited laboratory, with the water named and its sodium reported separately, and the Gate 1 opinion.
- **Binder and filler:** mannitol, tableting grade (INS 421), 0.80 g per cube (amount an ASSUMPTION). Thai permission: listed for flavoured drinks (category 14.1.4) at quantum satis in Notification 468 (P), with condition TH3, which applies only if the product is classed as an electrolyte drink. Mannitol is a polyol, which the Thai nutrition definition does not count as sugar, and its total sugars are at most 1.0% (FAO and WHO specification), so it does not change the excise tier. Fallback: isomalt (INS 953), direct-compression grade, with the same permission and amount. Price is a proxy range from import unit values, THB 0.08 to 1.27 per cube, not a Thai quote.

Ask a doctor or pharmacist first if you have kidney disease, have been told to limit potassium, or take medicines that can raise blood potassium, such as ACE inhibitors, ARBs, spironolactone, eplerenone, amiloride or triamterene. Do not take it with potassium salt substitutes or potassium supplements unless a doctor advises this. Do not drink more than one cube a day."
