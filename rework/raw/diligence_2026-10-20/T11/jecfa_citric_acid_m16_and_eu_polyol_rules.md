## A. Citric acid, FAO JECFA Monographs 16 (2014), INS 330

- URL read: http://www.fao.org/fileadmin/user_upload/jecfa_additives/docs/monograph16/additive-135-m16.pdf (the https address redirects to this http address)
- Date read: 2026-10-20. Tier: P (FAO/WHO JECFA specification). Status: SOURCED for the sulphated ash limit.
- Text: "Prepared at the 79th JECFA (2014), published in FAO JECFA Monographs 16 (2014), superseding specifications prepared at the 53rd JECFA (1999)."
- Purity, as laid out in the text layer (the columns are scrambled in the layer, so the assignment of the lower limits is uncertain): "Anhydrous: Not more than 0.5% (Karl Fischer Method)"; "Monohydrate: Not less than 7.5% and not more than 8.8%"; "Sulfated ash (Vol. 4) ... Not more than 0.05% (Method I, use 20 g sample)"; "Oxalate ... Not more than 100 mg/kg" (line position uncertain); "Sulfates ... Not more than 150 mg/kg" (line position uncertain).
- No sodium impurity limit appears in the monograph text read. Group ADI "Not limited" for citric acid and its calcium, potassium, sodium and ammonium salts (17th JECFA, 1973).

Our bound (DERIVED): residue on sulphated ash is at most 0.05% (500 mg/kg). If all of it were sodium sulphate, sodium is at most 500 x 0.3238 = 162 mg/kg. For 2.10 g per serve, that is at most 0.34 mg per serve.

## B. EU rules on polyols (for context; EU rules, not Thai rules)

1. Commission Regulation (EU) 2021/1175 of 16 July 2021 (OJ L 256, 19.7.2021, p. 53), recitals (5) and (6). Read from a copy: https://www.wb6cif.eu/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/CELEX_32021R1175_EN_TXT.pdf (EUR-Lex page returned no text). Date read: 2026-10-20. Tier: P (OJ text, third-party copy).
   - Recital (5): "A limited use of polyols as sweeteners is considered acceptable in food provided that their laxative action is taken into account. Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008 does generally not allow for the use of polyols in beverages due to their laxative potential. However, when used in solid foods, ... polyols are unlikely to cause any undesirable laxative symptoms below a consumption of 20 g of polyols per day from all sources."
   - Recital (6): "the labelling of foods containing more than 10 % added polyols authorised pursuant to Annex II of Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008 must include the mandatory statement `excessive consumption may produce laxative effects' in accordance with Annex III to Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011."
2. Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011, Annex III, point 2.4. Read from: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/1169/annex/III (UK copy of the EU text). Date read: 2026-10-20. Tier: P (copy).
   - "Foods containing more than 10 % added polyols authorised pursuant to Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008." Required particular: "excessive consumption may produce laxative effects".

Our arithmetic (DERIVED): one serve carries 0.80 g of binder polyol, which is 0.24 g per 100 mL made up, and about 21 percent of the 3.79 g dry cube. The 20 g per day figure is far above one serve. The 10 percent trigger is set on the food as sold; applied to the cube as sold it would be exceeded (EU rule, not Thai).
