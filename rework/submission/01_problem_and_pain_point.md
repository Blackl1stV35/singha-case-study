# Problem and pain point

## Tiers

Tiers are defined once, in Appendix A.

## Problem statement

The need is a lemon drink made in the glass, quick to make and fair in price per serve, for Thai adults who already drink bottled non-alcoholic drinks. Lemon Soda, a dissolvable cube in a pouch, is the proposed answer. A national survey reports daily drinking of bottled non-alcoholic drinks for the 25 to 44 and 45 to 59 age groups [P, UNVERIFIED; the 2021 survey table was not read, so no figure is quoted here]. No Thai source tests a sparkling lemon form, so it is a hypothesis.

The flavour overlaps with a product the company already lists. The Singha Lemon Soda can is listed on the company product page (tier S); retail sale not dated [S, SOURCED]. Its price is not verified. Lemon Soda differs in form and pack: one dissolvable cube per single-serve pouch, which makes the drink in the glass.

The problem is one of form, not flavour. The brief asks for new ingredients or production, or a new form of consumption; a lemon flavour alone would not be new, because the company already lists one. Scope is domestic Thailand only.

The named target is men aged 25 to 34, compared with men aged 35 to 44; men aged 45 to 59 are a later volume segment. Men aged 25 to 44 report current alcohol drinking more often than men aged 45 to 59 [P, UNVERIFIED; the 2021 table was not read, so no figure is quoted here]. This is a PROXY for drinking occasions, not a sugar measure, and no source splits the 25 to 34 and 35 to 44 bands.

## What is not known

- **No consumer test exists.** No panel, sales data or Thai retail audit exists. Taste is untested; pH 3.5 is a working anchor, and no bench test of fizz has been run.

- **The lead is a model result.** The model puts men aged 25 to 34 ahead in about 94% of draws, with a median gap of 0.5 to 0.7 points [DERIVED from NSO 2021 age bands (UNVERIFIED) and an ASSUMPTION elasticity, uniform 0.5 to 1.0]. The per-year odds-ratio interval, 0.991 to 1.001, includes 1.0 [DERIVED; table not located, UNVERIFIED].

- **The proxy may not transfer.** The age pattern is a sugar-based proxy, and the cube uses an intensive sweetener. Drinks with sugar substitutes run the other way: everyday use is 0.6% at 25 to 44 and 1.0% at 45 to 59 [P, UNVERIFIED].

- **Occasions are unverified.** Of 83 public statements, 35 name a Singha product, and 15 of the 29 that fall into eight segments (52%) come from one platform [S, UNVERIFIED; DERIVED: 15 ÷ 29].

- **Novelty is untested.** The Thai patent register has not been searched. A European application published on 5 March 2025 (EP 4,516,115 A2) [P, SOURCED for publication data] has a claim 1 headline [S, SOURCED; machine translation] for a carbonate effervescent powder or tablet made with no sodium carbonate. Its legal status and dependent claims were not checked, so any overlap with potassium bicarbonate is unknown [UNVERIFIED].

- **The dose is 0.55 volume of CO2 per 330 ml** [ASSUMPTION: design value]. R&D restates it before the run (section 10, Gate 3) the gate; none is set here. Dissolved CO2 is 0.592 volume at 35 °C [S, SOURCED], so the dose is within solubility at every serve from 5 to 35 °C [DERIVED].

- **Recipe per 330 ml serve (basis B, confirmed 19 October 2026).** Potassium bicarbonate 0.81 g (317 mg potassium); citric acid 0.52 g at the stoichiometric minimum, or 2.10 g at pH 3.5 (1.58 g of it free acid); sucralose 33 mg, about 100 mg per kg; a binder and filler 0.80 g (ASSUMPTION; range 0.5 to 1.2 g; not yet named, sodium not checked; gate G3); natural lemon flavour powder 0.05 g (ASSUMPTION; range 0.02 to 0.10 g) [DERIVED from the dose, molar masses and the pH anchor; pKa inputs S, UNVERIFIED]. Reactants are 2.94 g. Central cube mass is 3.79 g (range 3.46 to 4.24 g), DERIVED on the ASSUMPTION inputs; the cube is 14 mm a side, density 1.38 g/cm3 (band 1.25 to 1.60 is an ASSUMPTION). With a 1.5 g pouch (ASSUMPTION) the packed unit is 5.29 g. Nothing is weighed, and the net weight on the label is a 3.8 g specification, verified by weighing before print (Gate 9). No can mass is sourced.

- **Sweetener permission rests on the level; classification is open.** The Thai annex for flavoured drinks (category 14.1.4) sets sucralose at 300 mg per kg under condition 127; potassium bicarbonate and citric acid are quantum satis [P, SOURCED; only the flavoured-drink rows were read, not the full annex]. The binder, filler and flavour are not yet checked against this rule. A Thai FDA clarification confirms that a category permission extends to its sub-categories [P, SOURCED; read in part]. Condition TH3 applies to electrolyte drinks only, and this proposal makes no such claim. Classification is an open question for a regulatory adviser.

- **Units.** One cube per pouch is inferred, not yet confirmed with Singha Corporation; the form wording may imply three components, an unchecked limit.

- **Cost is a model.** No Thai price or quote was sought. The model gives about THB 4.09 per cube in the recommended 6-cube carton, and THB 3.84 in the 12-cube carton, the larger option [DERIVED; pouch, print and processing costs are ASSUMPTIONS or non-Thai listings]. The binder and filler and the lemon flavour are not priced, so this figure excludes them; no price is invented for either. The 6-cube cost includes a carton share of THB 0.67 per cube (THB 4 per 6-cube carton, an ASSUMPTION scaled from the 12-cube price). The 12-cube cost includes a carton share of THB 0.42 per cube (THB 5 per 12-cube carton, from a retail box listing used as a PROXY). A THB 15 shelf price per serve is an ASSUMPTION. Section 05 compares the cube cost with the Singha Lemon Soda can, whose price is not verified.

- **Dates and intentions.** The release and reprint give March 2026 as the launch month of the Singha lime sparkling water, a different product from Lemon Soda, with no launch day [P, SOURCED; S, SOURCED]. A Mintel release of 16 February 2026 gives 67% (sweets and desserts) and 61% (ready-to-drink sugary drinks) for planned sugar cuts; its sample is not on the page [S, SOURCED].
