## Section 1: Opening

- **Speaker note:** One sentence: a lemon drink made in the glass from one cube; whether it matches the can's flavour is the Gate 4 test. Name the proposer.

### Slide 2. Executive summary

- **Title:** Executive summary
- **Exhibit:** a mind map. Centre: "Lemon Soda: one cube, dissolved in one 330 ml serve, made in the glass."
  1. **Problem statement.** Lemon drinks are sold ready to drink in cans. No domestic dissolvable drink cube was found in desk searches. The gap is a new form of drinking, not a new flavour.
  2. **Identity problems.** Singha already lists a lemon soda can, which its product page describes as having no sugar (company claim), so Lemon Soda overlaps its flavour. The name needs a register search (Gate 7). The lion and SINGHA are for this proposal only.
  3. **Proposal.** One cube, dissolved in one 330 ml serve; one cube per pouch, not yet confirmed by Singha Corporation. Desk case: THB 4.91 contribution per cube; NPV THB 0.88 million at 200,000 cubes a year and THB 500,000 fixed cost (desk model, assumptions).
  4. **KPI and expected goal.** At least 3 units per store per week; repeat 30%, floor 15%; at least 60% sell-through in 90 days; at least two listings in six months (all assumptions). A measured decision at M10 to M11: scale one route, repeat once, or stop.
- **Ask bar:** a gated proof programme, not a launch: twelve decisions for Singha Corporation, starting with the go decision and named gate owners.
- **Footnote:** Proposing entity: Singha Corporation; Boon Rawd Brewery is the holding company. Desk model on assumed volumes and fixed costs, not a forecast.
- **Speaker note:** Read the four branches in order and end on the ask.

## Section 2: The case

### Slide 3. The gap

- **Title:** Lemon drinks are sold in cans, and no dissolvable cube was found in Thailand in desk searches.
- **On the slide:**
  - Press figures for the Thai carbonated soft-drink market differ: over THB 66,000 million (2024 press, year not stated) and THB 76,900 million (2026 bottler data). Neither is checked at source, and no market is sized from them. Canned carbonated drinks were THB 13,172 million (year not stated; Marketeer, 16 August 2022).
  - Sugar is the concern: 44% say they strictly try to reduce sugar, and 27% are open to sugar or salt alternatives (Mintel, February 2026).
  - Drink cubes exist overseas (Ricola, 2025). None was found for sale in Thailand in desk searches.
  - The opportunity is the form of drinking: a pouch that makes the drink in the glass.
- **Exhibit:** two columns. Today: a 330 mL can. Proposed: one cube in 330 mL of water.
- **Source line:** Thansettakij, 16 March 2024 (S); market value figures as cited in Appendix B, section 2.3 (S); Marketeer, 16 August 2022 (S); Mintel, 16 February 2026 (S); Ricola release, 5 June 2025 (P).
- **Speaker note:** The claim is "not found in desk searches", not "none exists". The two market figures disagree, so the deck does not rely on either.

### Slide 4. Leverage the can

### Slide 5. Consumers and the drinking way

- **Title:** Consumers want less sugar without losing taste; the lead segment is a test, not a finding.
- **On the slide:**
  - Out of home, often: 90% of urban consumers drink carbonated drinks out of home, more than 22 purchases a year, about THB 19 per trip (Kantar, April 2024, S).
  - Taste matters: about two in five consumers name taste as a decision factor for soft drinks (Innova, October 2025, S). Consumers expect taste to match zero sugar (Mintel, February 2026; Coca-Cola Thailand, June 2021; both S).
  - Sweetness: 40% prefer full sweetness, 36% less sweet, 24% are undecided (Mintel, February 2026, S).
  - Lead segment: men aged 25 to 34 (assumption). The desk model puts the gap over men aged 35 to 44 at about 0.6 to 1.2 points at a 30% baseline, depending on the model scale, and its 90% interval includes zero. The confirmed category band is men aged 27 to 44 (Bangkok sample, n = 400, S).
  - Men aged 45 to 59 are a later volume segment, pended.
  - Limit: no published buyer profile exists for Singha Lemon Soda.
- **Exhibit:** three tiles: 90% | more than 22 purchases a year | about THB 19 per trip.
- **Source line:** Kantar, 9 April 2024; Mintel, 16 February 2026; Innova, 31 October 2025; Coca-Cola Thailand, June 2021; Bangkok study, 31 August 2025 (all S).
- **Speaker note:** Present the lead segment as the first thing the pilot tests.

## Section 3: The product

### Slide 6. The cube in use

- **Title:** The consumer makes the drink in the glass: one cube in 330 ml of water.
- **On the slide:** three steps: open the pouch; drop the cube into 330 ml of water; stir, then drink. Working pH 3.5 (assumption); taste is not yet tested.
- **Exhibit:** a three-stage transition strip: cube, dropped into water, ready to drink. The current stage is large; its neighbours are small.
  - Stage 1, the cube: shelf life not yet tested; the storage test (35 C for 8 weeks, assumption) is to start by M2 (Gate 5).
  - Stage 2, dropped into water: dissolution time not yet measured.
  - Stage 3, ready to drink: fizz above the 0.44 g/L threshold for up to about 96 minutes. This is a derived, surface-only upper bound, not measured; the threshold comes from an abstract-only sparkling-water study (proxy).
- **Draft label wording box:** "Drop one cube into 330 ml of water and stir." "Serving: one cube made up to 330 ml." "Contains an intensive sweetener: sucralose (working choice; to be confirmed at Gate 1)." "Single-serve pouch." Net weight: 3.8 g (specification; verified by weighing before print, Gate 9).
- **Label note:** Wording only, not cleared. No sugar-free, zero-sugar, health or hydration statement is printed.
- **Speaker note:** Name the stage durations and say which are derived and which are not yet measured.

## Section 5: The plan

### Slide 11. Pilot

- **Title:** The pilot tests direction and repeat before any paid media is scaled.
- **On the slide (three columns):**
  - **Concept pre-test, M3 to M5:** recruited online panel of men aged 25 to 59 who drank alcohol in the past year; one brand-blind concept signed off by M3; top-2-box purchase intent by age cell at one test price (THB 15, assumption); rule fixed in advance; it reads direction and a 5-point size rule, and it cannot confirm the modelled 1.2-point lead; no media, no sale.
  - **Pilot, two routes, M7 to M10 (13 weeks):** click-to-purchase read on non-paid traffic in the first four weeks; Route A in-store trial at 3 or more units per store per week; Route B paid social to one listing only after Singha Corporation sets the cost threshold, and at base inputs it does not recover its first-purchase cost in one serve (derived); repeat read at 8 and 12 weeks.
  - **Measured decision, M10 to M11:** scale one route, repeat once in the same stores, or stop; scale needs repeat of 15% or more on both cohorts and contribution of THB 3.51 or more per cube; national listing is read at about M13.
- **Survey box:** a screening test at 1,377 completes per cell for a 5-point lead at a 30% baseline. The power simulation's modelled lead is about 1.2 points, which would need about 22,166 per cell. At 1,377 per cell the test confirms the direction of the lead in 12.7% of simulated runs and rules out a 5-point lead in 56.4%. Fieldwork waits for Gate 1 and for approval of the panel provider and budget.
- **Source line:** Desk assumptions. Cost per click THB 11.5 (proxy, one agency); cost per thousand impressions THB 120 (assumption).
- **Speaker note:** Say that the survey is a screen, and that no market is sized from it.
