# Thai FDA Q&A on Notification 445: sugars, laboratory analysis, dry foods (read 20 October 2026)

- URL: https://food.fda.moph.go.th/media.php?id=666168280611954688&name=faq-nutrition-label.pdf (12 pages, Thai; listing page https://food.fda.moph.go.th/faqs/category/faq-nutrition-label ). Read 20 October 2026 from the PDF text layer.
- Tier: P (regulator guidance, Thai). Status: SOURCED. The English below is our translation.

## Entries used

- Section 7 (1): "Total sugars" means the sum of monosaccharides and disaccharides, for example glucose, fructose, lactose, sucrose, maltose. (Polyols are not in this list.)
- Section 7 (2): for a sugar alcohol not given in the notification, a conversion factor may be used if there is scientific evidence, but each sugar alcohol must be analysable.
- Section 6 (heading "การวิเคราะห์คุณค่าทางโภชนาการ", nutrition analysis) (3): the energy and nutrients shown in the nutrition panel must come from analysis of the final product by a government laboratory or an ISO 17025 accredited laboratory; calculation from the recipe cannot replace it ("ไม่สามารถใช้การคำนวณแทนได้").
- Section 6 (5): products of the same type with the same recipe that differ only in flavour may share one analysis, case by case; a different recipe needs a new analysis.
- Section 6 (6): accepted laboratories: (1) government laboratories in Thailand or abroad; (2) bodies appointed or accredited by the government of that country; (3) bodies accredited by an accreditation body to at least ISO/IEC 17025; a non-government body must hold that accreditation.
- Section 6 (4): no expiry is set for an analysis report; it depends on the maker's quality control, and a retained sample's result must agree with the label.
- Section 6 (11): the nutrient values in the nutrition panel must come from laboratory analysis ("ต้องได้จากการวิเคราะห์ของห้องปฏิบัติการ"). The question asked whether potassium could be shown as zero without analysis; the answer is no.
- Section 6 (8): a supplement taken by mixing before drinking may be analysed as a powder or as ready to drink, if the preparation (for example mixing with water) does not change the nutrient values.
- Section 8 (1): for a dry food that normally has water or a liquid of insignificant nutrient content added, the amount per serving follows the reference amount for preparing the food ready to eat, and the label must give preparation directions using plain water.
- Section 9 (1): "added sugars" includes monosaccharides, disaccharides, syrups, honey and concentrated juice, and names trehalose among the examples. (Polyols are not named.)

## Limits

- The Thai FDA's own criteria page for permitted differences between label and analysis (https://food.fda.moph.go.th/for-entrepreneurs/criteria-nutrients ) was named in the Q&A but not opened.
