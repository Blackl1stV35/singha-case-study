# Roche and de Preux (2024): extreme heat and soft drink purchases, US conference abstract

- Item: V5. Date read: 20 October 2026.
- Work: Maxime Roche and Laure de Preux (Imperial College London), "How is climate fuelling the thirst for sweetness? Exploring drivers and adaptation", oral presentation, Sustainable Food Systems Symposium, 17 September 2024.
- Page opened: https://events.gwdg.de/event/626/contributions/1962/ (WebFetch, 20 October 2026). This is a conference programme entry, not a journal article. No later journal version was found or checked.

## Statements (short fragments, our paraphrase around them)

- Data: NielsenIQ Consumer Panel 2011 to 2019 (3,933,041 household-month observations, 94,738 households, 11,954 zip codes, continental US) and the NielsenIQ Retail Scanner 2011 to 2012 (32,742 stores).
- Method: household-level Poisson pseudo-maximum-likelihood panel fixed-effects regressions of monthly per capita volume on 16 five-degree daily maximum temperature bins.
- Result 1: volume rises non-linearly, "with positive effects above 80F (27C)".
- Result 2: one extra day above 95F (35C) raises average monthly per capita purchased volume of sugary drinks by 0.3 percent (bottled water 0.6 percent).
- Result 3: "Temperatures do not affect diet drink purchases".
- Result 4: rural households are more sensitive to extreme heat; no difference by income.
- This is a per-extra-hot-day effect on a monthly total, so it cannot be converted to a per-degree slope without further assumptions.

## Tier and status

- Tier S (conference abstract page). Status PROXY (US, sugary versus diet drinks). Relevance: the Lemon Soda Cube is sweetened with sucralose (zero sugar), so a result that diet drinks show no temperature response is relevant, but it rests on one abstract.
