# Success and fail case studies for the lime cube

Date: 19 October 2026. Evidence base: desk research only. No consumer test or Thai shelf test has been run.

## Tier key

P: a government or regulator text read in full, a company's own filing, official statistics, a peer-reviewed article read in full, or a primary database. S: any other source, including press, trade portals, consultancy pages, company web pages that are not filings, handbooks reproduced online, and search snippets. SOURCED: the page text is saved in rework/raw and was read; this is the repository rule. UNVERIFIED: the page was not saved, or only a snippet or summary was read; a figure stays UNVERIFIED until its page is saved. DERIVED: our arithmetic, with the inputs and their tiers shown. PROXY: a stand-in for what is not measured. ASSUMPTION: a value chosen for the model, not sourced.

## Analogues that worked (evidence)

- **Waterdrop drink cubes** (Austria; Vienna 2016, US 2021). Cubes sold in 20 markets and over 25,000 retail doors (company figures). Year-one revenue about $0.7m, and over $100m in 2023, company-reported and unaudited. US listings at Walmart and Target were reported in May 2023. Sweetener not named. [1][2], S, UNVERIFIED. This is the only analogue here with reported commercial scale, and that scale is company-reported.

## Analogues that failed (evidence)

- **Fizzies drink tablets** (US; dry tablets that fizz into a drink). Launched 1956 or 1957 (sources differ); national from 1962. First ended in the late 1960s to early 1970s. A trade article links this to a 1969 cyclamate ban, effective 1 February 1970. An encyclopaedia entry says the maker could not make a product that was both legal to sell and acceptable to taste. Revived 1996 and 2005; ended February or March 2016, reason not given. [11][12], S, UNVERIFIED.
- **Coca-Cola Life** (stevia-and-sugar cola; sparkling match only). Coca-Cola GB announced in 2017 that it was phasing the drink out of the UK. UK sales fell 58% in a year to £8.8m (Nielsen, as reported in a trade article). It was still sold in more than 30 markets, including the US, in 2017. The global end date (2019 to 2020) is unresolved because sources conflict. [13], S, UNVERIFIED.
- **Aha sparkling water** (US; sweetness not stated; near match). Launched March 2020. Sales fell 42% in Q1 2023, as reported (baseline not stated), and its share was 1.9% against 14% for LaCroix. In October 2023 Coca-Cola said it would wind down distribution. [14], S, UNVERIFIED.

Fizzies is the closest format match, a dissolvable drink tablet from the 1950s to 2016. The other two are sparkling-category matches only.

Launched, outcome not evidenced:

- **Santo Cubes** (US; compressed coffee and cane-sugar cubes). US launch reported in October 2018 [3], S, UNVERIFIED. A Kickstarter campaign (16 October to 15 November 2018) raised $83,592 from 1,749 backers against a $30,000 goal, about 2.8 times the goal (DERIVED: 83,592 / 30,000 = 2.79; both inputs S, UNVERIFIED) [4]. Backer money is not sales. The founder reported over 5,000 samples, 78% positive (UNVERIFIED). No retail data found.
- **Ricola Drink Cubes** (co-developed with Waterdrop). Austria and France from August 2025. Switzerland is a company plan for spring 2026 (S, UNVERIFIED). No sales data. [6], S, UNVERIFIED.
- **1,2,CUBE** (Coca-Cola Japan; freeze-dried tea and coffee cubes). Amazon Japan only, in limited quantity, at JPY 600 before tax per 15 cubes, May to June 2021 (translation by the authors). No sales reported. A search summary claimed sales beat expectations; that is not used because no checked page supports it. [7][8], S, UNVERIFIED.
- **Simply Pop** (Coca-Cola US prebiotic soda). Launched February 2025 at US$2.49 per 12 oz can, with 6 g fibre (as reported). No withdrawal found. [9], S, UNVERIFIED.
- **Incrediballs** (New Zealand; effervescent drink tablets). Pre-launch per the article; online orders from 16 February 2026. No sales or price. Stabilisation is company-stated. [10], S, UNVERIFIED.

## What the analogues imply for the cube

1. **Novelty.** Dissolvable drink tablets and cubes exist abroad. A Thai patent describes a method for soluble compressed coffee tablets (filed 29 June 2012, published 26 April 2016; grant status not shown) [5], P, UNVERIFIED. The claim that the cube has never existed before is therefore unproven. The case rests on the new consumption form, which is the central claim, and on the fizz mechanism, neither tested on Thai shelves.
2. **Sweetener permission.** A trade article links Fizzies' first end to a 1969 cyclamate ban; that date is not checked against a primary text [11]. Sucralose is recommended, not chosen, and its Thai permission is open and would block sale until resolved. A steviol glycoside fallback is noted for the same reason. No analogue shows a sucralose problem.
3. **Carbonation is unproven.** No cube analogue is shown to hold carbonation, and Incrediballs' stability is company-stated [10]. The dose is 0.9 volume of CO₂ per 330 mL and is under review; the alternative is 0.55 volume. Solubility at 1 atm falls as the serve warms: 0.655 volumes at 30 °C and 0.592 at 35 °C (Lange-attributed table [16], S, UNVERIFIED). The same table gives 0.878 volumes at 20 °C and 1.194 at 10 °C (S, UNVERIFIED). So a 0.9-volume dose exceeds solubility at every serve above about 19 °C (DERIVED by linear interpolation between the 10 °C and 20 °C values). A 0.55-volume dose is below the 30 °C and 35 °C values. For 0.9 volume, at least 0.6 volume must remain dissolved at 10 minutes at serves of 30 °C or below (ASSUMPTION threshold). For 0.55 volume, gate not set; to be defined by R&D. A 0.55-volume dose cannot reach the 0.6-volume level, so it needs its own gate. A measured retention test is needed before any fizz wording. Incrediballs is the nearest precedent for holding fizz until use, but its article describes a sodium bicarbonate base, unlike the potassium system here (S, UNVERIFIED).
4. **Taste.** No taste evidence exists for the cube. A search lead on sensory work with effervescent supplements could not be retrieved, so no off-taste is claimed. The potassium system needs its own taste test, and the pH of 3.5 is an assumption (ASSUMPTION).
5. **Demand.** Santo's Kickstarter backers and the 1,2,CUBE limited online launch are not retail evidence. Neither says anything about Thai price or shelf acceptance.
6. **Risk.** A Citi analyst, as reported in one article, expects new drink sub-categories to fade within a few years [9] (UNVERIFIED). A HealthDay headline dated 28 July 2026 says health claims for prebiotic soda were questioned; only the headline was read, so any findings are UNVERIFIED [15]. Making no health claim avoids this route.
7. **Patent.** EP 4,516,115 A2 (a sodium-free effervescent carbonate; status shown as pending) is a separate freedom-to-operate question, not assessed here [17].

## Limits

- No Thai dissolvable-drink analogue was found, and Thai retail sites could not be read during the desk search.
- The failure search found three analogues. Only Fizzies is a dissolvable drink tablet. No modern dissolvable carbonated cube failure was found; this is a limit, not a filled gap.
- Outcomes are not evidenced for Ricola, 1,2,CUBE, Simply Pop, Incrediballs or Santo. An absence of withdrawal in search is not success.
- Not retrieved: the 1969 cyclamate notice, the full text of a 2025 sensory study on effervescent supplements, the Coca-Cola Life end date, and the Thai patent's grant status.
- The dose is under review. The 0.9-volume and 0.55-volume options both remain open until measured, and the 0.55-volume gate is not set.
- The page texts behind these sources have not yet been saved for checking, so every source is tagged UNVERIFIED.
- The proposal makes no health claim, and competitor nutrient claims are not reproduced.
