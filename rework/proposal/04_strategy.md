# Strategy: Lemon Soda (20 October 2026)

Scope: the Thai domestic market only.

## Tiers

Tiers are defined once, in Appendix A.

## 1. Target segment

The proposer is Singha Corporation. Boon Rawd Brewery is the holding company in the company's own description; press and company sources describe the group differently (see section 02). The lead segment is men aged 25 to 34, and the lead is a test, not a finding. Men aged 45 to 59 are a later volume segment, pended.

- Alcohol drinking is 68.6% of men aged 25 to 44 and 65.4% of men aged 45 to 59 (2024) [P, UNVERIFIED; source 1]. These drinkers are a PROXY for the non-alcoholic audience, and 25 to 44 stands in for 25 to 34. No Singha buyer data exists, so every group compared is a market proxy.
- The comparison varied every input across its range in 40,000 simulated outcomes, including Bangkok's monthly temperature (27 to 31 degrees Celsius). Compared per person within each age and sex group, men aged 25 to 34 who drink alcohol rank first among six drinker groups in 94% of simulated outcomes (87% to 97% by month). Their margin over men aged 35 to 44 is about 3% on purchase intent (about 0.9 points at a 30% baseline) and about 6% after the potassium-caution exclusion. Both ranges include no difference [DERIVED; team model on UNVERIFIED inputs and ASSUMPTIONS].
- Potency is 1.15 for men 25 to 34 against men 45 to 59 (90% interval 1.03 to 1.28), a reference ratio only [DERIVED; UNVERIFIED inputs; ASSUMPTION].
- By total reach no group leads clearly, and the order depends on how the groups are defined. The only confirmed category band is men aged 27 to 44 (Bangkok sample, n = 400, 31 August 2025) [S; source 27].
- Everyday sugar-substitute drink use, a PROXY for sweetener use, is higher at 45 to 59 (1.0%) than at 25 to 44 (0.6%) [P, UNVERIFIED; source 2]. It does not support the lead segment.
- The hot-season occasion is a hypothesis. The model uses Bangkok monthly mean temperatures of 27.3 to 30.7 degrees Celsius [P; Thai Meteorological Department climate normals 1991 to 2020, the Bangkok station, not a national mean; source 28]. How much demand responds to heat is an ASSUMPTION: no Thai or tropical temperature-to-demand value was found (Thai sources give the direction only; S), and no source gives a difference by age or sex. A sourced temperature value changes no leader; the heat effect would change the lead only if men aged 35 to 44 responded to heat much more strongly than men aged 25 to 34, which no source shows [DERIVED]. No mixing or chilled-use test has been run.
- Segment reading, a team setting fixed before the concept test (owner: research lead; rule fixed before fieldwork, about M3): men aged 25 to 34 keep the lead if the lower bound of the 95% interval on their intent over men aged 35 to 44 is above zero; the lead is reversed if the upper bound is below zero; otherwise it is undetermined.
- Limit: no published buyer profile exists for Singha Lemon Soda, so the comparison is a model result under stated assumptions, not a consumer finding.

## 2. Proposition, format and cost

**Strategic position: compete on form, not flavour.** The Singha Lemon Soda can is the competitor on flavour. It is listed on the company product page (source 18; S, SOURCED; the page is undated). It launched in a 330 mL can in August 2020 according to a launch release as printed by Brand Buffet on 19 August 2020 (S); two later articles say late 2020 (S); the lemon can's launch price was THB 16 (2020, S) [source 24]. The company page says the can has 0% sugar and no calories [COMPANY CLAIM; source 18]. Its online prices on 20 October 2026 are in section 3. The can range also names Yuzu in the press (launched June 2021; mentioned in April 2025) [S; source 24]. A Yuzu can is listed at Lotus's Shop Online at THB 17 on an aggregator page stamped 20 October 2026, and Makro PRO and Lazada list Yuzu packs [S; source 39]; whether a Yuzu can can be bought in October 2026 is not confirmed. Lemon Soda overlaps the can on flavour; the difference is form: one dissolvable cube per pouch that makes the drink in the glass, not a ready-to-drink can. Whether the cube matches the can's flavour is the Gate 4 blind taste test, with the can as the reference. Lemon Soda makes no sugar-free claim. The group's sparkling lime water, a different product, is a 330 mL can at THB 15, launched March 2026 [P, SOURCED; source 11; S for a news reprint, source 12]; the company claims sugar-free with 0% calories for that can [COMPANY CLAIM]. The group's own lemon soda can is the closest substitute, so cannibalisation is tracked as a KPI: if 7% or more of cube buyers say they would otherwise have bought a can, the value model is rerun before any scale [DERIVED on ASSUMPTION inputs].

**Market context.** The Thai carbonated soft-drink market is sized differently by each source. NielsenIQ values printed by the press are about THB 70,772 million for the 12 months to November 2024 and THB 66,000 million for the 12 months to January 2024 (press relays; S). For 2025 the values found run from about THB 67,000 million (trade press, no source named) to THB 76,900 million (a Kasikorn Research Center estimate relayed by Thansettakij on 8 September 2025, and a Marketeer forecast of 9 May 2025); none names NielsenIQ, and the sources disagree on the direction of 2025 (S) [source 25]. No source says whether these are retail or manufacturer values, and the paid datasets behind them were not opened, so no market is sized from them in this proposal. Brand shares have different bases: press shares of Coca-Cola 51%, Pepsi 30% and est 8.6% by volume carry no year or data owner (S), and ThaiNamthip, one of the two Coca-Cola bottlers, holds 51.4% of Thai carbonated soft drinks in 2024 (a Frost and Sullivan estimate quoted in its filing; basis not stated; S) [source 26].

The format is one dissolvable cube per 330 mL serve, which releases carbon dioxide and makes the drink [ASSUMPTION]. No dissolvable drink cube was found for sale in Thailand in searches of Thai retail listings read on 20 October 2026 (S); the finding is "not found in desk searches", not "none exists". Cubes sell overseas: waterdrop [S, SOURCED; source 16] and Ricola Drink Cubes, sold through waterdrop's EU store [P, SOURCED; source 15]. They are cited only because no domestic example was found.

At 0.55 volume of CO2 per 330 mL, the dose is about 8.1 mmol [DERIVED]. This is the carbon dioxide the reaction releases at 0 C; how much stays dissolved at serve is not measured. The recipe is: potassium bicarbonate 0.81 g; citric acid 2.10 g anhydrous, of which 0.52 g neutralises the bicarbonate and the rest sets a working pH of about 3.5 (the desk model gives a pH of about 3.4 to 3.5); sucralose 55 mg a serve, about 167 mg per kg of drink, equal in sweetness to about 10 g of sugar per 100 mL [ASSUMPTION for the sweetness target]; a binder and filler of 0.80 g mannitol, tableting grade (INS 421), with isomalt as the fallback [ASSUMPTION for the amount; permission P, source 5]; and natural lemon flavour 0.05 g, carrier not yet specified [ASSUMPTION] (ranges 0.5 to 1.2 g for the binder and 0.02 to 0.10 g for the flavour; basis B). The carbonating agent is potassium bicarbonate; no sodium carbonate is used. The central dry cube mass is 3.79 g (range 3.46 to 4.24 g, set by the binder and flavour allowances), in a 14 mm cube (density 1.38 g/cm3; band 1.25 to 1.60 an ASSUMPTION) [DERIVED on ASSUMPTION inputs, not weighed]. The five components at their central amounts add to about 3.8 g. The packed unit with a 1.5 g pouch (ASSUMPTION) is 5.29 g; the net weight is a 3.8 g specification, verified by weighing before print (Gate 9). Potassium is about 317 mg a serve [DERIVED], about 9% of the Thai reference of 3,500 mg a day [P; Thai FDA English translation of Notification 445, Annex 3; source 3], and about 960 mg per litre [DERIVED]. These are composition figures, not benefit claims, and a medical review is required at Gate 2 before sale. Citric acid, carbonic acid and water pKa values are S, UNVERIFIED [source 20]. The chemistry is a desk model, and taste is untested.

One cube per pouch. The recommended pack is a 6-cube carton, 190 x 150 x 50 mm outside, for portable sport and recreation use [ASSUMPTION]. The larger option is a 12-cube carton, 190 x 150 x 100 mm [ASSUMPTION]. A 20-foot container holds about 21,386 cartons and 128,316 cubes in 6-cube cartons (a volume estimate at 92% of the internal volume; payload 28.33 t) [DERIVED; container dimensions and payload S, source 10]; 12-cube cartons give about the same number of cubes on the same volume estimate.

Cost: THB 4.09 per cube in the recommended 6-cube carton before binder and flavour, and THB 3.84 in the 12-cube carton, the larger option [DERIVED]. The 6-cube cost includes a carton share of THB 0.67 per cube (THB 4 per 6-cube carton, an ASSUMPTION scaled from the 12-cube price; no listing). The 12-cube cost includes a carton share of THB 0.42 per cube (THB 5 per 12-cube carton, from a retail box listing used as a PROXY). Reactants THB 0.12 [DERIVED; source 7; PROXY]. Pouch THB 1.30, print THB 1.50 and processing THB 0.50 are ASSUMPTIONS. An overseas pouch listing (USD 0.03 to 0.05; S, PROXY, UNVERIFIED; source 8) is THB 1.01 to 1.68 at the 15 October 2026 rate of 33.55 THB per USD [P, SOURCED; source 6]. The pouch band of THB 0.67 to 2.70 is an ASSUMPTION, giving THB 3.46 to 5.49 per cube in the 6-cube carton (THB 3.21 to 5.24 in the 12-cube carton; DERIVED), before the binder and flavour. The binder is priced only as a PROXY from Thai import unit values for mannitol: THB 0.08 to 1.27 a cube, with a world average of THB 0.12 [S, PROXY; source 31]. With the binder, the 6-cube cost is THB 4.17 to 5.36 per cube [DERIVED]. The lemon flavour (0.05 g) is not priced. The contribution is THB 3.94 a cube after excise on the THB 4.09 cost, and THB 4.91 before excise on the planning price of THB 9.00 [DERIVED]. At the top of the binder range the contribution falls to about THB 2.67 after excise, close to the after-excise floor of THB 2.54 per cube (the before-excise floor of THB 3.51 less the excise per cube).

## 3. Price and channel

The pilot channel is the team's setting (Gate 8; owner: sales and channel head, by M4). The pilot tests demand and repeat before any paid media is scaled. It has two routes and a creator pilot, and every target is an assumption until the pilot reads.

- Route A is an in-store trial in 20 stores: three sampling days a store, in weeks 1, 5 and 9, with 20 samples a day at THB 15 a sample [ASSUMPTION]. Its cost is THB 18,000 (20 x 3 x 20 x THB 15; range THB 12,000 to 30,000 at THB 10 to 25 a sample) [DERIVED]; the promoter day rate is not priced.
- Route A is read at weeks 8 and 13: scale if both reads are at or above 3 units per store per week, stop if both are below, otherwise hold. Three units a store a week at an assumed 1,282 stores is 200,000 cubes a year; the store count is an ASSUMPTION. About 15,945 7-Eleven stores are listed at end-2025, read as Thailand-only by inference [P, SOURCED; source 13]. In simulated pilots under stated assumptions (not pilot data), a true 2.5 units is stopped in about 90% of runs and a true 3.5 is scaled in about 85%; three units is a target and a necessary condition, not a profit test.
- Route B is paid social to one online listing. It starts only if non-paid traffic in weeks 1 to 4 reaches a 1.0% click-to-first-purchase rate [ASSUMPTION]. A first purchase costs about THB 1,150 in paid clicks (THB 11.5 per click [S, SOURCED, PROXY; source 14]; 1% conversion [ASSUMPTION]), against one carton's contribution of THB 21 to 29, so Route B is a capped learning test. About 234 cubes recover it at THB 4.91 before excise (about 292 at THB 3.94 after excise) [DERIVED; contribution excludes the binder and flavour allowances]. Paid social stops above a cap of THB 27.38 per first purchase at a 30% repeat (THB 24.22 at 15%): the floor contribution of THB 21.06 per carton (THB 3.51 per cube times six) times 1.3 cartons per buyer [DERIVED on ASSUMPTION inputs].
- The creator pilot uses nine chosen creators (four mid-tier and five mega-tier) on commission-led offers: a product kit and a commission per carton sold through a unique code. No flat fee is committed. None has been contacted, and commission-only terms are an ASSUMPTION; if creators refuse them, the pilot runs Route A and the conditional paid social only.
- Budget: committed THB 24,686 (creator kits 442, commission cap 4,000 for 500 cartons at THB 8.00, Route A 18,000, contingency 10%). Conditional THB 60,000 for paid social, released in two tranches against measured results: a learning tranche of 20,000 after the week 4 read at or above 1.0%, and a scale tranche of 40,000 at week 8 if the cost per first purchase is at or below the cap. Ceiling THB 84,686 [DERIVED on ASSUMPTION inputs]. Flat fees are not recommended: at lower-bound benchmark fees (mid-tier THB 33,550 a post; mega-tier THB 671,000 a post; PROXY, S; source 37) the nine creators would cost THB 3,489,200 for one post each, and recovering that would take about 127,000 buyers, about one million cubes in the pilot, five times the planned annual volume.
- Measured decision (M10 to M11): scale one route, repeat once in the same stores, or stop. Scale needs repeat of 15% or more in both cohorts, a contribution of at least THB 3.51 per cube before excise (THB 2.54 after excise), and a cannibalisation share under the 7% review level.
- The Singha Lemon Soda can was priced online on 20 October 2026 at THB 17.00 for a single can at Lotus's Shop Online, at THB 330 for 24 cans at 7-Eleven and Lotus's (THB 13.75 a can), and at THB 325 at Makro PRO (THB 13.54 a can) [S, SOURCED for the pack prices; per-can prices DERIVED; source 23]. Across six retail rows the band is THB 13.54 to 17.00, with a median of THB 13.75. These are online prices, and no store shelf price was read. Singha Soda, a different product, is about THB 8 a can [DERIVED; source 19].
- Shelf price THB 10 to 20 (base THB 15), ex-factory share 60% [ASSUMPTION]. At THB 15, Lemon Soda sits above the sugar-free lemon and lime pack prices read at named sellers (THB 12.40 to 14.63 per 330 mL) and below the sugar-free single cans (THB 16.25 and THB 17.00) [S; online prices, 20 October 2026; source 38]. The proposal presents Lemon Soda as competing on form and on the Singha name, not on price. The THB 15 price is an ASSUMPTION and is not tied to a verified shelf price. Gross margin at the planning price of THB 9.00 is 54.6% on the 6-cube cost of THB 4.09 (40.4% to 53.7% with the binder at its import-value range), before excise, freight and overhead [DERIVED; the lemon flavour is not priced].
- Price chain at THB 15: invoice THB 9.34 after VAT and margins; excise THB 1.31; net THB 8.03; contribution THB 3.94 after the THB 4.09 cost [DERIVED on PROXY and ASSUMPTION inputs]. Before excise, at the planning price of THB 9.00 (60% of THB 15; ASSUMPTION), the contribution is THB 4.91. The two bases are shown separately, and the valuation uses THB 3.94. The 21.6% retailer margin is a group all-category PROXY; CP ALL's 2025 group gross margin is 22.7% (P, a group figure that includes all categories) and its net margin is 2.8% (DERIVED from two P figures) [source 13]. No sourced Thai distributor margin was found. The closest sourced value is the National Statistical Office's 2023 wholesale value-added share for non-alcoholic beverages, 16.2% (P, PROXY; a value-added measure, not a margin; source 29); the 10% to 20% band used in the model is an ASSUMPTION. The excise is a 14% ad valorem rate [P; source 30]; the model applies it to the invoice value [ASSUMPTION; the ad valorem base was not read], and whether a dissolvable cube falls under that item is not verified, so the value case uses it as an ASSUMPTION.
- Break-even shelf price is THB 6.82 at the 6-cube cost of THB 4.09 and THB 5.77 to 9.15 across the pouch band [DERIVED; before excise, on the 60% ex-factory share]. Registration, tooling, trademark and marketing are excluded, and so are the binder and the flavour.
- Value case [DERIVED; desk model on ASSUMPTION and PROXY inputs; see section 05]: at 200,000 cubes a year and THB 500,000 fixed cost, the NPV over five years at a 10% discount rate is THB 0.17 million after excise and tax at base inputs. Across the stated ranges the chance of a positive NPV is 0.26 (0.40 before excise and tax; 0.58 on a simpler structure), against a test of 0.95 fixed in advance. The expected NPV is minus THB 0.88 million, with a 90% range of minus THB 3.40 to plus THB 1.59 million. Break-even with no cannibalisation is 184,325 cubes a year after excise and tax (148,076 before excise), and the operating break-even (fixed cost divided by the after-excise contribution) is about 127,000 cubes. Cannibalisation break-even is about 7.5% of cube buyers, and the review level is 7%. At the base volume the cube is a learning option, not a value driver.

## 4. Decisions, limits and claims

The settings the plan needs before the pilot are the team's own, each with its basis, an owner role and a time (the full list is in section 10). Sale is blocked until Gates 1, 2, 7 and 9 pass; a pilot batch also needs Gates 3 to 6 and 8. The only ask is the executive sponsor's signature on the Gate 0 record, which names one owner for each gate and the first budget ceiling.

Settings:

- Go record and outside contact: the Gate 0 record names the executive sponsor, one owner for each gate and the first budget ceiling (owner: executive sponsor, by M0). The providers (regulatory opinion, medical review, tax adviser, laboratory, taste panel, concept panel) are named in that record, and no outside party is contacted before Gate 0 (owner: executive sponsor, by M1).
- Product class and sweetener: flavoured drink (category 14.1.4) is the team's setting. The Gate 1 written Thai regulatory opinion confirms it, together with condition 127, the annex edition, the sucralose level and the mineral-salt question below. Placement within 14.1.4 decides the sucralose limit. Owner: regulatory affairs head, by M1. Basis: sucralose at 55 mg a serve (about 167 mg per kg of drink) is within the 300 mg/kg limit for flavoured drinks in category 14.1.4 under condition 127 [P, SOURCED; source 5]; only the flavoured-drink rows were read, not the full annex or the sub-category rows. The amount rests on an untested sweetness target of about 10 g of sugar per 100 mL [ASSUMPTION]. Condition TH3 applies only to electrolyte drinks.
- Potassium and Notification 195 (Gate 1 and Gate 2): a Thai notification for mineral-salt beverages (No. 195, clause 4(1.3)) sets potassium at no more than 195 mg per litre where it is present. The cube holds about 960 mg of potassium per litre, about 4.9 times that ceiling [DERIVED]. The clause text is unchanged from the Gazette original through the Thai FDA compilation of about 29 March 2023 [P; read from page images; source 22]. No later amending notification was found by title on the Thai FDA list to 20 October 2026 [P, negative finding]; a post-March-2023 amendment inside a general notification is not excluded. The 2011 and 2020 amendments did not change clauses 3, 4(1) or 7. Whether the cube is a mineral-salt beverage under clause 3 is the Gate 1 question. The proposed class is flavoured drink; if the Gate 1 opinion classes the cube as a mineral-salt beverage, the formula would have to change. The medical review of the potassium is at Gate 2.
- Dose: 0.55 volume of CO2 per 330 mL. Solubility at 35 C is 0.592 volume [S, SOURCED, Lange-attributed table; source 4]; the desk model keeps the full dose dissolved from 5 to 35 C [DERIVED; no bench test]. Bench cubes are made at a laboratory by M2 and dissolved CO2 is measured at 5, 20 and 35 C; R&D restates the pass rule before the run, and the 0.55 volume dose is held after one rework. No release value is stated. Owner: R&D lead, by M1.
- Binder: 0.80 g mannitol, tableting grade (INS 421), with isomalt as the fallback; both are listed for flavoured drinks at quantum satis in Notification 468 [P], with condition TH3, which applies only if the product is classed as an electrolyte drink. The amount is an ASSUMPTION, and the price is the import-value proxy in section 2, not a Thai quote; the binder and flavour are quoted at Gate 5.
- Dry-solids route: a pilot batch at a dry-solids co-packer named at Gate 6. The co-packer's minimum run of 500,000 to 1,500,000 units per product and formula [COMPANY CLAIM; S; source 33] is the binding constraint, not line time: 200,000 cubes a year need about 79 hours of pouch-line time at a planning point of 42 pouches a minute (60 a minute, a mid-range maker figure for pre-made pouch machines, at an assumed 70% efficiency; both ASSUMPTIONS), a range of 45 to 152 hours, about 2% of a 4,000-hour year [DERIVED]. Pre-made pouch makers list about 15 to 80 pouches a minute as dry-cycle maximums [P, COMPANY CLAIM; source 32]. Owner: operations director, by M2.
- Sold unit and carton: one cube per single-serve pouch; six-cube carton, 190 x 150 x 50 mm [ASSUMPTION]. Owner: marketing lead, with finance, by M2.
- Brand and legal rulings: until a written ruling, no glassware, lemon imagery, bubbles or alcohol cues are used, and the SINGHA name and lion stay within this proposal. The SINGHA lion appears only as the SINGHA mark holder's brand; the product is named Lemon Soda. The lion emblem is authorised for this proposal only. The rulings are a dependency on the mark holder's brand and legal function (Gate 7), and the alcohol-cue question is unchecked. Owner: brand and legal function, by M2.
- Pilot routes, budget, paid-social threshold and traffic for the read are in section 3. Owners: sales and channel head (routes, by M4); finance lead (budget, and the paid-social threshold with the marketing lead running it, by M4); marketing lead (non-paid traffic to the product page or listing in weeks 1 to 4, by M4).

Limits:

- Desk model only: no bench, sensory or shelf test, and nothing has been measured. Dissolution at 25 C is a model estimate: about 6 minutes stirred and about 11 minutes not stirred for a central set of assumed inputs, and 3 to 22 and 5 to 74 minutes across the assumed ranges; this holds only if the cube breaks into fine pieces [DERIVED on ASSUMPTION inputs]. Fizz at 20 C is a model estimate: surface loss alone keeps the dissolved carbon dioxide above 0.44 g/L for about 1.7 to 3.4 hours if the full dose dissolves at serve, bubble formation could cut this to about 15 minutes (champagne proxy; S, PROXY; source 36), and if less than 41% of the dose is dissolved at serve the drink starts below the threshold. No decay curve is measured. The only threshold found, 0.44 g/L CO2 for sparkling water, is from an abstract [S, abstract only; source 21] and is a PROXY for a soft drink.
- One cube per pouch and the one-component reading (the brief's sense, not an ingredient count) are unconfirmed. No Thai quote for the reactants, binder or pouch was found: the reactant and binder prices are import-value proxies, and the pouch price is an overseas listing. No source gives a with-product powder rate or a line efficiency for a pouch line.
- The NSO inputs are UNVERIFIED, so the segment ranking is a model output.
- No store shelf price, no sales volume for the can after 2022 and no product margin for the can were found. The market values are press relays with no stated level.
- Two Singha Corporation pages conflict on the parent company, and the sources disagree on how the group is structured; the strategy does not depend on it [source 18].
- The name Lemon Soda is the same as the competitor's product name, Singha Lemon Soda, without the Singha word. A register and shopper-confusion check has not been run (Gate 7), and no legal view is given. The Thai trademark register was not reached, so the owner of the SINGHA mark in Thailand is unconfirmed.
- No desk figure answers the question for people on potassium-retaining medicines or with kidney disease. A medical review is required at Gate 2 before sale.
- The EP 4,516,115 A2 pending status is the register mirror's assumption [P, SOURCED; source 17]; US 5,254,355 A is unchecked, with no freedom-to-operate view.

Claims: no benefit, nutrition or function statement is made, and Lemon Soda carries no sugar-free status and no sodium-free or low-sodium claim. The composition is stated as a specification: potassium bicarbonate, citric acid, sucralose (a working choice until the Gate 1 opinion confirms it), mannitol as binder and filler, and natural lemon flavour. The recipe specifies no sodium salt, uses no sodium carbonate and carries no lubricant; a supplier declaration for each ingredient is required before pack print (Gate 9). Sodium from the recipe is 0 to about 2 mg a serve [DERIVED]. The water used adds its own sodium: 0 to about 7 mg a serve at the WHO typical level of 20 mg per litre [P; the WHO notes higher levels in some places; source 34], or about 33 to 68 mg at the 100 to 200 mg per litre reported for Thai tap water [S, a news report; not measured; source 35]. The binder and flavour amounts are assumptions until a cube is weighed. The fizz is a mechanism only, and no fizz, sparkling or bubble wording is printed until a measured test exists. No Thai novelty, consumer result, market size or sales forecast is claimed.
