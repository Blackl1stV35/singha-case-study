# Notification No. 356 B.E. 2556 and No. 465 B.E. 2568 (beverages in sealed containers): how the general beverage classes are drawn (read 20 October 2026)

Purpose here: to see whether the general beverage notification contains any rule that sends a flavoured drink into, or out of, the mineral-salt beverage class of Notification 195. It does not.

## No. 356 (English, FDA unofficial translation)

- Tier: S for the wording (unofficial translation by the Thai FDA; the Thai text prevails).
- URL: https://food.fda.moph.go.th/media.php?id=509377822071070720&name=P356_E.pdf
- File: 6 pages, 161,157 bytes, SHA-256 a9656eb70f9ddc44d5696bf565d80556cbeda620776ce7327272a0b9ba792f37 (text decoded and read in full).
- Published: "Government Gazette Vol. 130 Special Part 87 Ngor, dated 24th July 2013"; notified 26 June 2013.
- Clause 2: "Beverages in Sealed Containers is prescribed food to have qualities or standards."
- Clause 3 as first issued (five categories): (1) water with dissolved carbon dioxide or oxygen gas; (2) a beverage containing or made from fruits, plants or vegetables; (3) a beverage containing or made from other constituents, except fruits, plants or vegetables; (4) concentrated beverage as in (2) or (3); (5) beverage as in (2) or (3) in dried form.
- Clause 4(9) as first issued: "Artificial sweetener shall follow to Food Standard of Joint FAO/WHO, Codex, Re: Food additives, ... and may be used in single or combination with sugar." Clause 5(3): moisture of a dried beverage not more than 6 percent by weight. Clause 8: labels follow the Ministry notification on labels.
- No clause in the English text mentions electrolyte or mineral-salt drinks, excludes them, or defines the scope of "beverages in sealed containers" by reference to Notification 195.

## Reading for the classification question (DERIVED from the texts above)

- The general beverage notification, No. 356 as amended by Nos. 402, 465 and 473, divides beverages by ingredient origin (fruit, plant or vegetable, or other), form (liquid, concentrate or dried) and dissolved gas. It draws no line by mineral content. The types are read from the Thai text of No. 465 (tier P); No. 356 itself and No. 402 are read in English only (tier S). A dry lemon-flavoured drink would fall in category (4) of the amended clause 3 as the dried form of category (2), if it is a sealed-container beverage at all.
- The line between that general class and the mineral-salt beverage class is therefore drawn only by clause 3 of Notification 195 (mineral salts as the main ingredient), and by the Thai FDA's practice at product notification. No document opened here states how "main ingredient" is judged. Notification 416 Annex 1 (n195_amending_notifications_2554_2563.md, section C) lists the two classes as separate foods with separate notifications.
