## 1. Answer in plain words

1. **The notification is shown as in force by the Thai FDA.** Notification of the Ministry of Public Health No. 195 B.E. 2543 (2000), Re: mineral-salt beverages (English title on the Thai FDA site: Electrolyte Drinks), has not been repealed on the regulator's own list. On 20 October 2026 the Thai FDA's list of Ministry notifications shows it as "in force" with an effective date of 24 July 2001 and no repeal mark. Among the list rows for Nos. 333 to 473 no title is about electrolyte or mineral-salt beverages or names No. 195 or No. 332 (tier P for the list's fields; the list was read through the fetch tool's page extraction, in two passes). This is a status taken from the list. It is not a reading of the text after March 2023 (see point 3).
2. **Four notifications amended it, and none touched the provisions asked about.** They are Notification (No. 2) B.E. 2554 (Royal Gazette volume 128, special part 41 ง, 7 April 2011) and Notifications 413, 415 and 417 of B.E. 2563 (2020). Between them they changed or removed only clause 4(5)(c), 4(5)(e), 4(6), 4(7) and the dry-form paragraph of clause 4. I read each amending clause in the Gazette text (tier P). The Thai FDA compilation made about 29 March 2023 records the same five changes as its five footnotes.
3. **Potassium limit, clause 4(1.3).** In plain words: where potassium is present, a mineral-salt beverage may hold at most 195 mg of potassium per litre. Its status is stated in three parts so that a fact and a limit are not mixed.
   - Text: unchanged from the Gazette original of 24 January 2001 through the Thai FDA compilation made about 29 March 2023 (tier P; the same words in both, no footnote on the sub-clause).
   - Later period: no amending or repealing notification found by title on the Thai FDA list to 20 October 2026, and the list shows No. 195 in force (tier P; negative finding from page extraction, not a screenshot).
   - Not excluded: an amendment after March 2023 inside a general notification whose title does not name No. 195 (stated limit; to be settled by the Royal Gazette index or at Gate 1). The post-March-2023 notifications actually read for this record, Nos. 465 and 473 in Thai and the first two pages of the FDA summary of No. 468, do not name No. 195.
   - It is a composition standard for the class that clause 3 defines. It is not a limit on potassium in every drink. Provision: Notification 195 clause 4(1.3), with clause 4(1) as the head.
4. **Label statements, clause 7.** Clause 7(2) requires three statements in a red-bordered rectangle on a white ground, in letters at least 2 mm high: children and infants should not drink it; only for those who have lost sweat through exercise; not to be drunk beyond a stated number of units a day, with a total of at most 1 litre a day. The text is the same in the Gazette original and in the March 2023 compilation, and clause 7 carries no footnote of amendment (tier P). The same limit as in point 3 applies to the period after March 2023. Clause 7(1) sends the label to "the Ministry notification on labels", a generic cross-reference, so it reads through to the current labelling notification (the FDA list shows No. 450 B.E. 2567, "Labelling of food in containers", in force from 19 July 2024; DERIVED, to confirm at Gate 1).
5. **Does clause 4(1) apply to a flavoured drink that is not sold as a mineral-salt beverage?** On the text, clause 4(1) governs "mineral-salt beverages", and clause 3 defines that class as a drink that has mineral salts as its main ingredient, dry forms included. A drink that is outside the class is outside clause 4(1). The text gives no number or method for "main ingredient", and nothing opened gives the Thai FDA's method or a ruling. So whether the cube drink is inside the class is a classification question that this record cannot close. It is a Gate 1 item (section 7). The general beverage notification is No. 356 as amended by Nos. 402, 465 and 473. Nos. 465 and 473 were read in Thai (tier P) in this revision; No. 356 and No. 402 were read in the FDA's English translation (tier S). An earlier pass of this record knew No. 473 only from a secondary summary and had not opened its Thai text. The Thai text of No. 473 is now opened and read, and none of these texts draws a line by mineral content or mentions No. 195 (section 6).

## 6. Does clause 4(1) apply to a flavoured drink not sold as a mineral-salt beverage?

What the texts say (tier P unless marked):

1. Clause 4 opens "เครื่องดื่มเกลือแร่ต้องมีคุณภาพหรือมาตรฐาน ดังต่อไปนี้" (a mineral-salt beverage must have the following quality or standard). Sub-clause (1) is a per-litre composition of "เครื่องดื่มเกลือแร่", so it governs the defined class and not drinks in general.
2. Clause 3 defines the class by one test: a drink that has mineral salts as its main ingredient, including the dry form. The definition gives no percentage, no basis (weight, dissolved solids or function), no reference to the product name or claims, and no reference to potassium.
3. For a dry-form drink, the 2011 paragraph says the made-up drink, dissolved in the ratio on the label, must meet clause 4(1) and the other listed sub-clauses. So if a dry drink is in the class, clause 4(1) is tested on the drink as made up, per litre.
4. The general beverage notification, No. 356 as amended by Nos. 402, 465 and 473, classes beverages by ingredient origin, form and dissolved gas, and draws no line by mineral content. The four types in clause 3 as replaced by No. 465 (Thai Gazette text, tier P) are: (1) a drink that has or is made from fruit, plants or vegetables; (2) a drink that has or is made from ingredients that are not fruit, plants or vegetables; (3) a concentrate of (1) or (2) that must be diluted; (4) a dried form of (1) or (2). Each of (1) and (2) may or may not contain dissolved carbon dioxide. No. 473 (Thai Gazette text, Volume 143, special part 226 ง, 16 September 2026, in force 17 September 2026; tier P) re-states clauses 4(8), 5(1) to (3), 5(5), the first paragraph of 8 and clause 9 of No. 356 with the renumbered types, and does not change the types. No. 402 B.E. 2562 (English translation, tier S) changes labelling only: it repeals the last paragraph of clause 8 of No. 356 and adds a caffeine warning paragraph to clause 9. None of the four texts has a clause that excludes mineral-salt beverages, sends a mineral-containing drink into the class of No. 195, or names No. 195 (saved: n356_amendments_465_473_thai_and_402_english_2026-10-20.md; n356_n465_sealed_container_beverages.md). A dry lemon-flavoured drink would sit in type (4), the dried form of type (2), if it is a sealed-container beverage. As context only: for a dried beverage the replaced clause 5(3) of No. 356, as re-stated by No. 473 clause 4, allows at most 6 percent moisture by weight (the moisture of the cube is not known).
5. Notification 468 condition TH3 attaches a sugar-pairing duty to "mineral-salt beverage products" inside its flavoured-drinks category. Reading (DERIVED): for additive purposes the Ministry treats mineral-salt beverages as a kind of product that can sit among flavoured drinks, and it does not say that every flavoured drink holding a mineral salt is such a product.

What the texts do not say:

- No document opened states how "main ingredient" is judged, who decides it, or at what stage. No Thai FDA guidance, question-and-answer page or ruling on the point was found (search log: search_and_access_log_V6.md). Consumer and pharmacy pages that came up are not evidence of the FDA's practice and are not used.

## 7. What the Gate 1 opinion must settle (items for counsel; not legal advice)

1. **Class.** Whether the Thai FDA, applying clause 3 of No. 195 ("mineral salts as the main ingredient"), would class the dissolvable lemon cube drink as a mineral-salt beverage or as a beverage under No. 356 as amended by Nos. 402, 465 and 473 (the dried form of type (2)), and by what test: mass, function, composition, name, or claims.
2. **If outside the class.** Confirm that clause 4(1), including the potassium limit, clause 6 (the 250 mL dry-pack rule) and clause 7(2) do not apply to it, that condition TH3 of Notification 468 (sugar with the sweetener) does not apply to sucralose, and which dried-beverage limits of No. 356 as amended then apply (for example the 6 percent moisture limit).
3. **If inside the class.** The drink as designed meets none of clause 4(1.1) (sodium about 0 to 6 mg per litre against 460 to 920), 4(1.2) (no glucose, fructose or sucrose against at least 2 or 4 percent), 4(1.3) (potassium about 959 mg per litre against 195) or 4(1.5) (citric acid about 6.4 g per litre as added against a citrate limit of 819 mg; the basis of expression is not stated), and bicarbonate before reaction is about 1,496 mg per litre against 793 (rework/screen/t11_sodium_results.json). The paragraph after (1.5) lets the FDA approve other mineral salts or other sugars; it does not say it can relax the limits in (1.1) to (1.5). Clause 6 would require a dry drink to be packed for dissolving in 250 mL, and the cube is made up in 330 mL. Clause 7(2) would require the red-bordered statements, including "only for those who have lost sweat through exercise". The cube could not be sold in that class as designed.
4. **Evidence of the FDA's practice.** Whether the product notification (food serial number) route records the class, and whether the FDA has classed comparable dissolvable or tablet drinks.
5. **Changes after March 2023.** Confirm from the Royal Gazette index that no notification issued after the March 2023 compilation changed clause 3, 4(1) or 7 by name inside its body. The scan in this record is by title only.
6. **Clause 7(1) cross-reference.** Confirm which labelling notification clause 7(1) now reaches (the FDA list shows No. 450 B.E. 2567).
