# Workaround concept screen (2026-10-17)

Context: the competing candidate's zero-sugar botanical-citrus can (Singapore and Korea) is not presentable as a go case. Base cases are not validated, the contribution threshold is missed at realised prices, Singha's own zero-citrus cans compete directly in both markets, and the SINGHA name cannot be used. This screen tests four alternative concepts against the competition's three themes, using the same evidence rules and the same economic logic.

Themes: (1) Ingredients and Nutrition; (2) Alternative Formats; (3) New Trend-Driven Target. Registration closes 22 October 2026.

## Recommended concept: C3, conditional

- **Theme fit:** theme 2 is strongest (on-premise serve, glass bottle). Theme 3 is moderate: a design-led premium and willingness to pay for glass in hospitality (Lefebvre et al. 2018, abstract; Chitturi et al. 2021). Theme 1 is weak, since no health claim is made.
- **Regulation:** glass is outside BCRS per the NEA page (updated 10 April 2026). The dine-in clause is not confirmed to cover imported bottles. Botanical novel-food status (SFA, 9 to 12 months) and any water import permit remain open.
- **Economics (scanner model, unvalidated):** variable cost S$0.43 to S$0.87 per bottle (central S$0.59). A S$8 menu price, ex-GST and ex-service, clears 20 percent contribution at 3x to 4x markup. Break-even menu price is about S$2.8 at 3x and about S$3.7 at 4x.
- **Volume:** S$450k fixed cost needs about 292k bottles a year at 3x markup (about 270 venues selling three a day), or about 445k at 4x. The brief's per-can threshold does not transfer to glass and must be restated for the bottle.

## Why this is conditional, not presentable as validated

- The S$8 price is a search excerpt of an unreadable, undated PDF.
- Glass cost, the distributor margin (15 to 30 percent) and the outlet markup are judgement or assumed. The markup range runs from 3x (a vendor blog) to 6.7x (a 2021 Ontario benchmark).
- No velocity data exist for a 250 ml premium bottle in Singapore venues.
- The scan's falsifier (a S$5 price) does not match its own break-even: S$5 fails only above about a 5.4x markup.

## Open conflicts in the record

1. **Price basis.** The C1 scan finds brand net receipts 2.4 to 2.9 times the KRW 528 category value, which suggests the 528 figure may be a customs or importer average, not a price a premium brand receives. The judge asks the team to confirm whether the S$0.48 and KRW 528 values are importer purchase prices or customs values, and whether they include promotions. This affects every verdict in the submission.
2. **The 20 percent test.** The brief's definition is unit margin or after fixed-cost absorption. The judge reads it as unit margin. The volume answer depends on the reading.
3. **Malt-fibre.** The botanical-citrus memory says malt-fibre stays the live candidate. The limitations memory says the malt-fibre route stays not promising until a supplier states viscosity. The judge's workaround keeps malt-fibre as the live candidate, but it was not scanned in this screen. **The user needs to confirm which status applies.**

## Tests before the deadline (kill criteria)

1. Confirm the 20 percent definition and compute the bottles or cans needed for the fixed cost.
2. Establish whether the S$0.48 and KRW 528 values are purchase prices or customs values.
3. Venue checks: call or visit 10 to 15 Singapore hotels, restaurants and bars. Collect a dated menu photo for a 250 ml premium sparkling bottle and comparable weekly sales. **Kill if velocity cannot reach about three bottles a day across roughly 270 venues at 3x markup, or 400 at 4x.**
4. Supplier quotes: three glass suppliers for 250 ml and 330 ml sparkling-grade bottles (FOB price, MOQ), and one can supplier for the USD 0.20 can assumption. **Kill if glass exceeds USD 0.60 FOB per bottle.**
5. Distributor check: two or three Singapore on-trade distributors or importers on margin for a premium non-alcoholic bottle. **Kill if the margin exceeds 30 percent.**
6. Read the NEA BCRS text verbatim for the glass exclusion and the dine-in clause.
7. Written enquiries to SFA and HPB, now: water import permit for glass; novel-food status of each named botanical; whether a Nutri-Grade A display or sweetener-free wording counts as a health claim. These will not return by 22 October, so they are presented as gates.
8. Confirm Singha zero-citrus shelf and on-premise listings and prices in Singapore.

## What this screen cannot show

- The exporter's receivable after importer margin, duty and logistics in either market.
- Whether any consumer likes a sweetener-free or zero-alcohol botanical citrus sparkling (the nearest study is negative for unsweetened flavoured water).
- Current 2026 regular shelf prices for zero-alcohol cans in Korea and for 250 ml zero cans.
- Whether Terra Zero's company-reported volumes are real. No panel or retailer data checked them.
- Whether malt-fibre passes any test. It was not scanned here.
- Primary regulatory text. Several rules were read only as press, search or fetch summaries.

## Recommendation

Present C3 as a conditional on-premise alternative, with the tests and kill criteria above, and keep the can concepts as backups. Do not present C3 as validated. The verdict is conditional until venue velocity and glass cost are measured.
