## Extracted facts
- Panel: 70 young women. Citric acid stock solutions tested at 1.20 g/L (ISO group A, n = 15), 0.24 g/L (ISO/5, group B, n = 30) and 0.12 g/L (ISO/10, group C, n = 25), each in an eight-step dilution series (Table 2 of the paper; the series spans 0.06 to 2.86 mmol/L).
- At the ISO stock of 1.20 g/L (6.2 mmol/L as citric acid) all participants detected sourness at the lowest step and recognised it within the two lowest steps, so the ISO concentrations are too high for deionized water. Mean sour stimulus threshold in log(micromol) units: A 2.8, B 2.1, C 1.8; recognition: A 2.8, B 2.2, C 1.9.
- Table 1 (as printed): sucrose stock 24.00 g/L (0.98 to 35.06 mmol/L series), caffeine 0.54 g/L (bitter), FeSO4 heptahydrate 0.016 g/L (metallic).

## Limits
- The log(micromol) unit is per presented sample volume, which the page does not state in the extracted text, so a g/L threshold is not derived. Used only for the order of magnitude: sour detection is reached below 0.12 g/L (0.6 mmol/L) citric acid in deionized water.
