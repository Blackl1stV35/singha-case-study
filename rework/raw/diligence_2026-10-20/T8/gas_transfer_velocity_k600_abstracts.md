# Gas transfer velocity at calm water surfaces (k600), abstract level (Consensus index records)

- Date read: 20 October 2026. Route: Consensus search tool (abstracts as returned; articles not opened).
- Tier: S (abstract level). Status: SOURCED at abstract level. All are PROXIES for a 65 mm drinking glass: they are lake and estuary surfaces.

## Cole and Caraco 1998, Limnology and Oceanography 43(4)
- URL: https://consensus.app/papers/details/fa5377a4a839510da089a94420acbe29/
- SF6 added to a small low-wind softwater lake (15 ha) for 50 days: k was independent of wind speed and averaged 2.65 +/- 0.12 cm per hour (95 % CI), normalised to a Schmidt number of 600.

## Cole et al. 2010, Limnology and Oceanography: Methods
- URL: https://consensus.app/papers/details/ef4fd61e8c4f54bdbfd1dbeaf1744898/
- Four methods in small low-wind lakes (0.3 to 45 ha) converged on k600 between 0.35 and 0.74 m per day (1.5 to 3.1 cm per hour).

## Crusius and Wanninkhof 2003, Limnology and Oceanography
- URL: https://consensus.app/papers/details/984f253a520851b7ba3406342e877df3/
- At low wind speeds the transfer velocity predicted by the bilinear steady-wind relationship was "virtually identical to the theoretical predictions for transfer across a smooth surface"; convective cooling contributes at low wind.

## Rosentreter et al. 2017, Limnology and Oceanography
- URL: https://consensus.app/papers/details/fe72cbe1578a5ac98f0b1e0709eefa6c/
- Floating chambers in mangrove estuaries: k600 for CO2 and CH4 from 0.9 to 28.3 cm per hour, controlled mainly by current-generated turbulence.

## Xiao et al. 2014, Tellus B 66, 23795 (summary read; see xiao2014_pond_k600_floating_chamber.md)
- Floating chambers at a subtropical pond: CO2 k600 2.83 cm per hour (summer, site N) and 0.65 cm per hour (autumn, site M; 0.72 in the text). The lowest coefficient in the sources read; not a bound.

## Limits
- A lead of a reservoir study with k600 of 0.07 to 0.39 m per day (0.3 to 1.6 cm per hour) was reported by a reviewer; two searches did not find it and the search budget is used up, so it is NOT read and NOT used.
- k600 is the transfer velocity normalised to Sc = 600. For CO2 in water at 20 C Sc is about 600, so k(CO2) at 20 C is about equal to k600; the exponent -1/2 for other temperatures is the usual free-surface convention and was not opened here (ASSUMPTION).
- No source in this set measured CO2 loss from a drinking glass or beaker.
