## Recommendation 4.4.3, page 27 (Thai, then our translation)
- Heading (checked again 20 October 2026 in the extracted text): "Recommendation 4.4.3 Minerals in chronic kidney disease patients before kidney replacement therapy" (Thai: คำแนะนำที่ 4.4.3 เกลือแร่ในผู้ป่วยไตเรื้อรังก่อนได้รับการบำบัดทดแทนไต). The potassium bullet is the third bullet under it.
- Thai: "ผู้ป่วยโรคไตเรื้อรังควรได้รับการดูแลรักษาให้ระดับโพแทสเซียมในเลือดอยู่ในเกณฑ์ปกติ แนะนำให้บริโภคอาหารที่มีโพแทสเซียมต่ำ ไม่เกิน 1,500 ถึง 2,000 มก./วัน และควรหาสาเหตุในผู้ป่วยที่มีระดับโพแทสเซียมในเลือดสูง" (evidence quality 3, strength ++).
- Translation: CKD patients should be kept at a normal blood potassium; a low-potassium diet of not more than 1,500 to 2,000 mg a day is recommended, and the cause should be sought in patients with a high blood potassium.

## Explanation, page 28
- Thai: CKD patients who eat about 40 to 50 mg potassium per kg ideal body weight a day usually have normal blood potassium; blood potassium below 4 or above 5 mmol/L is linked to higher mortality; if blood potassium is high, look for causes such as ACE inhibitors, ARBs, beta-blockers and "black cough syrup" (ยาแก้ไอน้ำดำ), together with limiting dietary potassium to not more than 1,500 to 2,000 mg a day.
- Translation as above (our translation). The explanation also says that restricting potassium too much may cost patients foods that are good for the heart and blood vessels (cites reference 67), which is why it ties the 1,500 to 2,000 mg a day limit to a high blood potassium. The recommendation sentence itself is written without that condition: it advises the low-potassium diet and, separately, a search for the cause when blood potassium is high.

## Haemodialysis, page about recommendation on minerals
- Thai: HD patients should be advised to limit sodium 1.8 to 2.5 g/day, potassium 2,000 to 2,500 mg/day, phosphorus 800 to 1,000 mg/day.
- Peritoneal dialysis: dietary potassium advice depends on the patient's blood potassium (PD often causes low potassium).

## What this does and does not say
- It states a daily ceiling for diet in CKD with a high blood potassium. It does not set stage-specific ceilings and does not mention potassium additives or salt substitutes.
- The ceiling is per day of all food and drink; it is not a per-serving limit.
