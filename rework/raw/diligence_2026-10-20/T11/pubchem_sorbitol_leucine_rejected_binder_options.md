# PubChem entries opened for rejected binder options: sorbitol and leucine (saved reading, third pass)

- Source: PubChem PUG View, compound records read through a fetch tool (extractions of the JSON, not the verbatim pages). Date read: 2026-10-20. Tier: S (database that cites older primary sources and safety cards; the primaries were not opened).

## D-Sorbitol (CID 5780), https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/rest/pug_view/data/compound/5780/JSON?heading=Physical+Description and ?heading=Solubility

- Physical description as listed: "White hygroscopic powder, crystalline powder, flakes or granules" (EU Food Improvement Agents, E 420 (i)); "WHITE HYGROSCOPIC SOLID IN VARIOUS FORMS" (ILO-WHO International Chemical Safety Card); "Hygroscopic solid" (Haz-Map, citing the Merck Index).
- Solubility: very soluble in water; 2,750,000 mg/L at 25 C (Mullin, Crystallisation, 1972); 220 g per 100 mL at 20 C (ICSC).
- Use: sorbitol is described as hygroscopic in three listed sources, which supports the rejection on moisture beside an acid and bicarbonate pair. No Thai 468 row for sorbitol was read.

## L-Leucine (CID 6106), https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/rest/pug_view/data/compound/6106/JSON?heading=Solubility

- Water solubility: 21,500 mg/L at 25 C (DrugBank, citing Yalkowsky and Dannenfelser 1992); 24.26 g/L at 25 C (Merck Index, through HSDB); JECFA lists only "slightly soluble" in ethanol.
- Use: 0.80 g in 330 mL is 2.4 g/L, about one tenth of the 24 g/L solubility, so it would dissolve. This does not support leucine as a filler: no tableting, hygroscopicity or Thai 468 evidence was opened for it. Leucine stays "not assessed from sources" in the choice table.
