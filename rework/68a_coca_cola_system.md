## 0. What the system is, in six lines

1. The Coca-Cola system in Thailand has three layers. The Coca-Cola Company owns the brands and sells concentrate (P). Coca-Cola (Thailand) Limited runs brand marketing and is named as brand proprietor in the company's releases (S). Two bottlers make and distribute: ThaiNamthip Corporation PCL, which ranks first in Thai carbonated soft drinks with a 2024 estimate of 51.4% (filing P; the estimate is S; the province count of 63 is S, from Marketeer), and Haad Thip PCL, which serves 14 southern provinces (P).
2. Ownership changed in 2024. The Coca-Cola Company agreed on 9 February 2024 to divest its minority stake in ThaiNamthip to ThaiNamthip and Swire Coca-Cola (P, Swire Pacific release). Swire Coca-Cola took control of the Thai and Lao bottling operations under that alliance (P, Swire Pacific release). ThaiNamthip became a subsidiary of Swire Coca-Cola on 30 September 2024 (P, Swire Coca-Cola 2025 results page, re-opened 20 October 2026). The same page says the Thailand and Laos franchises became subsidiaries in 2024 but gives a date only for ThaiNamthip; no date is given for Laos. At 30 June 2025 Swire Beverages (South East Asia) Pte Ltd held 55.59% of ThaiNamthip Corporation PCL (P, Version 2 of the filing; 21 April 2025 in Version 1). After the offering the filing prints 50.03%, or 54.54% if the put option is exercised in full (P, Version 2, PDF page 9). Haad Thip is an independent listed company; its largest holder is Gutsche Family Investments Proprietary Limited at 24.40% (P, 10 September 2026).
3. ThaiNamthip holds 51.4% of Thai carbonated soft drinks in 2024 (S, a Frost and Sullivan estimate quoted in ThaiNamthip's own IPO filing). The filing attributes the figure to the company, not to the whole Coca-Cola system. A share for the whole system, with Haad Thip's 14 southern provinces, is not available in any source read.
4. The nearest product to Lemon Soda is already on the shelf. Sprite Lemon Plus is a sugar-free lemon soda, first sold in Thailand and Australia (release of 9 September 2022, S); its only Thai price read is THB 15 for a 325 mL can in 2022 (Thaiger review of 29 August 2022, S, dated). Sprite No Sugar has been sold in Thailand since a release of 1 April 2019 (S). Its 24-pack listings read on 20 October 2026 are THB 14.13 to 19.29 per 330 mL (four listings, DERIVED from S). The regular Sprite can is THB 16.00 for 325 mL at one retailer (16.25 per 330 mL, DERIVED from S). A sugar-free Sprite single-can price is not verified.
5. Margins are not comparable across the layers. The Coca-Cola Company is a brand owner with a 61.6% gross margin (FY2025, printed in its 10-K extract, P). The two Thai bottlers earn 38.5% (ThaiNamthip, FY2024, on sales of goods) and 42.2% (Haad Thip, FY2025, company page). Net margins are 22.6% for the owner in FY2024 (27.3% in FY2025), 10.6% for ThaiNamthip in FY2024 (on total revenue) and 6.90% for Haad Thip in FY2025 (printed by the company; its base is inferred as revenue from sales).
6. The bottler was under pressure in 2025. ThaiNamthip's total revenue for the six months to 30 June 2025 fell 14.9% and its net profit fell 53.7% (P, DERIVED). Volume fell 16.2% (P, DERIVED from 181 and 216 million unit cases). Revenue from the "other carbonated soft drinks" line, the line nearest the cube's segment, fell 20.2%, more than cola (14.7%) (P, DERIVED). The filing does not split this line by brand; the team reads it as covering Fanta, Sprite, Schweppes and A&W (section 6.2). The filing cites weather, the economy, weaker consumer spending and, in traditional trade, higher price competition.

How the ownership moved. The Coca-Cola Company's FY2024 net income includes a gain of USD 506 million on the sale of its interest in an equity method investee in Thailand (P, Form 10-K FY2025, other income discussion). The Swire Pacific release of 9 February 2024 (P) says The Coca-Cola Company would divest its minority ownership of ThaiNamthip to ThaiNamthip and Swire Coca-Cola. We read the two as the same transaction. Neither text names the other, so that link is DERIVED, not confirmed.

The planned offering. ThaiNamthip offers 612,451,687 new shares, 10.00% of the enlarged capital (P, filing Version 2, PDF page 9: 5,512,065,185 shares before the offering and 6,124,516,872 after it). The Securities and Exchange Commission approved the filing on 23 July 2026 (S, Marketeer, 25 July 2026). If the whole offering is sold, the filing prints that Swire Beverages falls to 50.03% and TNTL to 26.95% (P, page 9). The same page sets out a put option: existing Thai individual shareholders may sell a total of 276,342,760 shares to Swire Beverages, with the total not exceeding USD 250 million, at a price not below the IPO price, expected on the first trading day through a big-lot trade. If the whole put option is sold, Swire Beverages would hold 54.54% (P, page 9). The filing says the actual steps may change. The arithmetic matches: 3,064,110,570 plus 276,342,760 gives 3,340,453,330, which is 54.54% of 6,124,516,872 (checked in the script, section 8). No page read confirms a first trading date or an offer price.

### 6.5 How to read the margins

- A brand owner sells concentrate and licenses brands. Its gross margin is high (The Coca-Cola Company: 61.6%). Its net margin (27.3%) includes tax, financing and one-off items.
- A bottler buys concentrate, sugar, resin, cans and energy, fills, and distributes. Its gross margin is lower (ThaiNamthip 38.5%, Haad Thip 42.2%) and its net margin is far lower (10.6% and 6.9%).
- The gap between the owner and ThaiNamthip in FY2024 is 22.6 points of gross margin (61.1% against 38.5%, DERIVED). It reflects a difference of role, not of efficiency. Do not rank the two against each other. Do not compare either against Singha Corporation or against a beverage maker that owns its brand and its plant.
- The best Thai peer for a bottler margin is Haad Thip (listed, independent). The best reference for a brand-owner margin is the group figure of The Coca-Cola Company, with the caution that it is global.

## 9. Threat and response to a dissolvable cube

What the evidence shows.

- Coca-Cola Japan, which the team reads as the group's Japanese business unit (the capture names it and does not state the link), has made a cube-type product before. It sold "1,2,CUBE" freeze-dried tea and coffee cubes on Amazon Japan from May (tea) and June (coffee), in the year implied by the BeverageDaily article of 17 June 2021. A pouch of about 15 cubes sold at JPY 600, with no export plan stated (S, BeverageDaily 17 June 2021, saved as captures_2026-10-19/21). The company's own release page returned HTTP 403. No sales figure exists. The precedent is tea and coffee, not a fizzy lemon drink.
- The system sold a sugar-free lemon soda first in Thailand and Australia. Sprite Lemon Plus went to market in those two countries before any other (S, release of 9 September 2022). The release does not call it a test.
- Coca-Cola businesses in the UK and the US have withdrawn products that did not meet their goals: Coca-Cola Life in the UK (capture 20, which names Coca-Cola GB; S) and Aha sparkling water in the US (capture 27; S). The captures state no group-wide decision. That is context for how quickly a copy could be retired. It is not evidence of a plan.
- The proposer's own sugar-free lemon range is the strongest local comparison. Singha Lemon Soda was launched in August 2020 according to the company's launch release (Brand Buffet, 19 August 2020, S, a company release relayed by trade press; saved in rework/raw/diligence_2026-10-20/T6/09). Trade articles of June 2021 and September 2023 say it entered the market in late 2020 (T6/09, sections E and F; conflict listed in section 11.1). Singha Red Lemon Soda followed in August 2022 at THB 16 (Thansettakij, 15 August 2022, S, saved in T6/09). Five flavours are on the Singha product page (read 19 October 2026, capture 48, S): Lemon, Yuzu, Ume, Red and Pink, sweetened with sucralose and acesulfame potassium. The lemon can is THB 17.00 at Lotus's Shop Online (read 20 October 2026, S).

What the system could copy.

- The price. A THB 15 cube sits above the cheapest sugar-free 24-pack listing (THB 14.13) and the Schweppes six-pack (THB 14.00) and below the regular Sprite can (THB 16.00 for 325 mL). A price cut on cans is quicker for the system than a new format.
- The flavour. Sugar-free lemon is already on shelf as Sprite Lemon Plus (sucralose and acesulfame potassium), so the claim "sugar-free lemon soda" is not new in the market.
- The reach. A new format launched through ThaiNamthip would reach about 511,000 outlets (30 June 2025), and through Haad Thip the South. The two bottlers launch under one brand owner's marketing, as the Sprite Lemon Plus release shows.
- A cube-type format in principle, since Coca-Cola Japan has sold freeze-dried tea and coffee cubes (capture 21).

What the system cannot copy easily (the team's reading, an ASSUMPTION and not a sourced fact).

- The SINGHA name and the Singha family of lemon sodas (Singha Lemon Soda since 2020 according to the company's release).
- The economics of the bottlers. Their revenue is filled, water-based unit cases from five plants with 22 bottling lines and about 564 million unit cases of capacity. A cube moves water and carbon dioxide to the home and takes volume away from those lines. The filings read show no dry-goods line for the bottlers.
- A single decision-maker. A Coca-Cola branded cube would need the brand owner and, in Thailand, two separate bottler companies covering 63 and 14 provinces (the 63 count is S), under licence contracts. The filing names dependence on The Coca-Cola Company and Schweppes Holdings Limited for the brands and trademarks as a risk (P).
- The timing. ThaiNamthip is absorbing a first-half 2025 fall of 16.2% in volume, and it has the Swire integration and an IPO in progress.

Likely response, as the team reads it (ASSUMPTION). The system's first answer would be price and promotion on the can (it already does this), then flavour extensions, not a cube. The cube's case therefore rests on its form (no can, light to carry, one cube per glass) and on the Singha link. The proposal should not rely on a price advantage over Sprite.
