# Feasibility and Thai registration route

Status at 19 October 2026. Desk study only: no bench test, supplier quote or submission. One lime cube per pouch is inferred, not confirmed with the organiser.

## Tier key

P: a government or regulator text read in full, a company's own filing, official statistics, a peer-reviewed article read in full, or a primary database. S: any other source, including press, trade portals, consultancy pages, company web pages that are not filings, handbooks reproduced online, and search snippets. ## Technical feasibility (desk)

The design dose is 0.55 volumes of CO2 per 330 mL serve (ASSUMPTION: a design choice) at a working pH anchor of 3.5 (ASSUMPTION). Taste is untested. Potassium bicarbonate at 0.81 g releases about 8.1 mmol of CO2 (DERIVED). Citric acid is 0.52 g stoichiometrically but 2.10 g to reach pH 3.5, leaving about 1.58 g free (DERIVED). The free acid is the main taste risk.

CO2 solubility at 35 C is 0.592 volumes (S, SOURCED: CO2 table and CO2 35 C page; the second page flags its basis as ambiguous). The 0.55 dose is below it, and the desk model finds it within solubility at every modelled serve from 5 to 35 C (DERIVED). The NIST table (P, SOURCED) has no 35 C row and is not used.

The desk model gives a fizz duration of up to about 96 minutes above a sparkling-sensation threshold, and a mass-transfer time constant of up to about 106 minutes (DERIVED). This is a surface-only upper bound with no nucleation. The 0.44 g/L threshold comes from an abstract only (S, SOURCED: threshold abstract) and is a mineral-water value applied to a soft drink as a proxy (PROXY). It is a planning bound, not a product property.

One serve holds about 317 mg of potassium, 9.0% of the Thai RDI of 3,500 mg per day (DERIVED from S, SOURCED: RDI copy A and RDI copy B). The EFSA adequate intake for adults is also 3,500 mg per day (P, SOURCED: EFSA opinion summary). Both figures stay internal.

Question for R&D: what bench gate should confirm that 0.55 volumes is reached and kept at serve, and by which method? No value is set here.

Dry-cube shelf life is not tested. The 35 C storage temperature is an ASSUMPTION, because the climate normals could not be read (UNVERIFIED: climate normals PDF).

## Ingredients and supply

Potassium bicarbonate and citric acid are quantum satis in category 14.1.4. Sucralose is limited to 300 mg/kg there, under condition 127 (P, SOURCED: annex extract). Condition TH3 is not relevant unless the product is classed as an electrolyte drink. The design uses 33 mg per serve, about 100 mg/kg (DERIVED).

## Thai regulatory route

The additive limits come from Ministry of Public Health Notification No. 468, amendment No. 4, Annex 1, category 14.1.4 (flavoured drinks) (P, SOURCED: annex extract, read 19 October 2026). The full notification was too large to fetch, so the rows rest on the annex extract's reading (full text UNVERIFIED).

A Thai FDA clarification of Notification 468, in force from 12 December 2025 (B.E. 2568), says a permission at 14.1.4 also covers sub-categories 14.1.4.1 to 14.1.4.3 (S, SOURCED for pages 1 to 3 of 36; read in part).

Condition 127 applies the sucralose limit to the drink as consumed, which matches the cube (P, SOURCED: annex extract). Condition TH3 applies only to electrolyte (mineral) drinks. The annex extract reads a flavoured sparkling drink as not one; this is a regulatory question, not a settled point.

A Thai food registration or notification step applies to the product as classified. Its step, form, fees and timing are UNVERIFIED. No registration number or outcome exists, and none is claimed.

## Labelling and claims

The composition may be stated as fact: potassium bicarbonate, citric acid and sucralose, with no sodium carbonate.

Barred claims: sugar-free, zero-sugar, sodium-free, health, electrolyte, hydration, potassium-benefit and validated-fizz. The 9.0% reference figure and fizz duration stay internal and do not appear on pack.

The labelling notice to check is the food-labelling notification under the Food Act. Its number, date, and the rules for sweetener statements and nutrition declarations are UNVERIFIED. Label text stays provisional until that notice is read.

## Classification and excise

Excise item 02.02 covers sweetened or flavoured drinks, including those sweetened with an intensive sweetener. Its rate is 14% ad valorem in every tier (P; UNVERIFIED in this check: Gazette fetch blocked, page-image reading not re-checked).

Per-litre rates depend on sugar per 100 mL. From 1 October 2566 they are 0 baht up to 6 g, and 1, 3 and 5 baht above that (same status). The cube has no sugar, so it sits in the 0-baht band. The 6 g figure in the desk model is a sweetness anchor, not an excise input (ASSUMPTION).

Item 02.01 (no added sweetener, no flavouring) does not fit this product. Its rate is 0 baht per litre and 14% ad valorem (same status). The ad valorem base was not read, so the 14% cannot be converted to baht, and later schedules have not been searched.

The product's classification (beverage, edible ice or electrolyte drink) is open and is a question for a regulatory adviser. It decides the registration route, the excise line and whether TH3 applies.
