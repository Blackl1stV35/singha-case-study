# Asian and other temperature-to-beverage evidence reviewed for V5

- Item: V5. Date read: 20 October 2026. Every entry below was opened (or its abstract record was returned by a search service) and none gives a usable per-degree coefficient for soft drinks in Thailand or a tropical market. They are kept so the record shows what was checked and why each was set aside.

## A. Asian sources with a direction or a threshold but no soft-drink coefficient

1. Asia Business Daily, "Convenience Store Bestsellers That Change with Temperature", 17 June 2020. URL https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2020061711103640733 (WebFetch). South Korea, citing the convenience store industry, Seven Eleven and GS25. Fragments: carbonated drinks "sell well when the temperature exceeds 24 degrees"; bottled water and sports drinks have a higher threshold (29 C for both, per the extract); beer 26 C. The English page is an automated translation; the Korean original was not checked. Tier S. Use: a threshold, not a slope. All twelve Bangkok monthly means (27.3 to 30.7 C) are above 24 C.
2. Takumi Kato, "Impact of Weather Conditions by Time Zone on Soft Drink Purchases across Offline and Online Channels Considering the Effect of TV Advertising", 2021 5th International Conference on E-Business and Internet, pages 1 to 6, DOI 10.1145/3497701.3497702. First pass: abstract record returned by the Consensus search service only (the ResearchGate page returned HTTP 403). Revision of 20 October 2026: the bibliographic record was confirmed in Crossref and the abstract was read in the OpenAlex record; the ACM publisher page returned HTTP 403; the paper itself was NOT read. Japan, soft drinks, five channels, fixed-effects model on purchase amount; the abstract says mean temperature has a positive effect in every time zone and gives no magnitude. Tier S at abstract level, direction only. See kato_2021_icebi_japan_soft_drinks_abstract.md.
3. Japan Meteorological Agency with the Japan Soft Drink Association, "Survey on climate risk management for vending machine drink sales". URL https://www.data.jma.go.jp/risk/en/softdrink.html (WebFetch). Tokyo vending machines, hot coffee: higher volumes below 22 C, almost none above it. The page reports no percentage per degree. Tier S in this use (hot drinks, not soft drinks).
4. INTAGE Vietnam, "The impact of weather and seasonal factors on beverage purchasing behavior on iPanel INTAGE" (no author or date on the page). URL https://intage.com.vn/the-impact-of-weather-and-seasonal-factors-on-beverage-purchasing-behavior-on-ipanel-intage/ . Qualitative only: carbonated and soft drink revenue "often higher than in winter"; no panel size, no period, no number. Not usable (policy: no date and no author). Not cited for any figure.
5. Sangwoo Lee and Sang Hyeon Lee (2024), "Analyzing the Impact of Weather Conditions on Beer Sales", Asian Journal of Business Environment 14(3), 1-11. URL https://koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO202421143213552.do . South Korea, Nielsen Korea, weekly, January 2018 to August 2020, ARMAX. Beer only; the page states it has no soft-drink data. Set aside.
6. Min Goo Lee, Yong Kuk Park, Kyung Kwon Jung (2014), polynomial model of cafeteria beverage sales, Korean Institute of Information and Communication Sciences conference. URL https://koreascience.kr/article/CFKO201423965828332.pdf (text-extracted locally with PyMuPDF). A cafeteria of about 700 people, hot and iced coffee, February to May 2014, linear fit for iced Americano y = 1.9424x - 5.463 on daily maximum temperature. Coffee, one cafeteria, four months. Set aside.
7. Kochi University of Technology 2007 thesis on temperature and low-alcohol beverage sales per head (Japanese). Only metadata could be read. Alcohol. Set aside.

## B. Other markets with no coefficient in the text that was read

8. Mirasgedis and co-authors (2013 online, Business Strategy and the Environment), "The Impact of Climate Change on the Pattern of Demand for Bottled Water and Non-Alcoholic Beverages". Abstract page on MetaTOC opened (https://metatoc.com/papers/19487-the-impact-of-climate-change-on-the-pattern-of-demand-for-bottled-water-and-non-alcoholic-beverages): regression of sales on monthly temperature, historic climate 1961 to 1990 and projections 2021 to 2050; no number and no country on the page. The Wiley and ResearchGate pages returned HTTP 403. Set aside.
9. Cerovic and Horvat (2013), Croatian Adriatic hospitality, Tourism and Hospitality Management 19(2): a combined R-squared of 99 percent for overnight stays and air temperature; no coefficient on the page (https://hrcak.srce.hr/en/112340). Set aside.
10. Stulec, Petljak and Naletina (2019), Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services: abstract record (Consensus) says weather sensitivity of non-alcoholic beverage sales varies between months and that the drinks are bought on impulse; no coefficient in the abstract; full text not opened. Set aside.
11. Yoo and co-authors (2024), Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services, Seoul credit-card data: all retail sales rise 4 percent during heatwaves above 35 C (abstract record, Consensus). All retail, not beverages. Set aside.
12. Klevmarken and Fink (2025), KTH degree project "Factors affecting the demand for soft drinks" (https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1976663/FULLTEXT01.pdf, text-extracted locally). Sweden, weekly sales of one beverage maker, 2020 to 2024, company units not stated as a percentage, student thesis. Its temperature coefficient (about 1,855 units per unit of temperature in one appendix model) cannot be converted to a percentage. The abstract reports no clear difference between sugar-sweetened and non-sugar models. Set aside as a source of a coefficient. It does repeat the Keles et al. 0.21 and 2.1 percent figures (see keles_2018_ijpe_beverage_weather.md).
13. Hirche, Haensch and Lockshin (2021), International Journal of Wine Business Research 33(3), 432 to 455, US alcohol retail sales. First pass: cited as "Haensch and co-authors" from a Consensus abstract record that was not opened. Revision of 20 October 2026: the Emerald publisher abstract page was opened (full text not read); it says liquor and white wine sales do not respond to temperature in the warmest regions and do in the coolest. Alcohol, not soft drinks. Tier S, abstract level. Set aside as a source of any number; kept only as a hint that a response can vanish in the warmest regions, which the He et al. result also shows. See hirche_haensch_lockshin_2021_ijwbr_alcohol_temperature.md.
