# Thai FDA Q&A on Notification 445 (B.E. 2566): laboratory analysis, dissolving products, dry foods with water, sugars (saved excerpt, corrected 20 October 2026)

- Source: Thai FDA question-and-answer document on the nutrition label, 12 pages (Thai): https://food.fda.moph.go.th/media.php?id=666168280611954688&name=faq-nutrition-label.pdf (listing page: https://food.fda.moph.go.th/faqs/category/faq-nutrition-label ).
- Date read: 2026-10-20. Tier: P (regulator guidance, Thai). Status: SOURCED for the items below.
- Method: pages 6 and 8 were rendered as images (PyMuPDF, 110 dpi) and read by the reader, twice (first pass and again on the second pass). The Thai text layer does not decode. Our translation, marked as such. Item numbers are the document's own.

## Page 6, section 6 (nutrition analysis)

- Item (8). Question: a dietary supplement that is dissolved in water before taking must be analysed for its nutrient values as a powder or as ready to drink? Answer (our translation): if the preparation before taking has no effect on the nutrient values, for example dissolving in water, it may be analysed either as a powder or as ready to drink; the operator may ask the laboratory for test details. Note: this item is about dietary supplements, so it is a pointer only for a drink cube.
- Item (11). Question (our translation): if one wants to show the nutrition information under Notification 445 but does not want to send for additional potassium analysis, can potassium be shown as zero? Answer: the nutrient information shown in the panel must come from laboratory analysis ("ต้องได้จากการวิเคราะห์ของห้องปฏิบัติการ"). The answer is general, so it applies to sodium and every other panel value; the question itself is about potassium.
- Item (12): the same product made at different sites may use an additional potassium analysis from a single site. Item (13): if the analysis report used for the label has no potassium result, an additional potassium analysis may be sent to another laboratory that the FDA accepts. Our translation of the sense.

## Page 6, section 7 (calculation and conversion)

- Item 7 (1): "Total sugars" means the sum of monosaccharides and disaccharides, for example glucose, fructose, lactose, sucrose, maltose. Polyols are not in the list. Our translation.
- Item 7 (2): for a sugar alcohol not given in the notification, a conversion factor may be used if there is scientific evidence; each sugar alcohol must be analysed for its amount. Our translation of the sense.

## Page 8, section 8 (serving amount of dry foods)

- Item 8 (1), question: how is the serving amount set for a dry food or similar food that must have water added, for example a powdered sauce? Answer (our translation): where a dry or similar food needs water or a liquid of insignificant nutrient value added before eating, the amount per serving follows the amount recommended for preparing the food to the ready-to-eat state set in the reference serving table; the example is a powdered sour sauce, one serving of 1/2 teaspoon (2 g), which prepares 1 teaspoon of liquid sauce, and the label must give directions to dissolve the product in 1 teaspoon of plain water.
- Reading (DERIVED): plain water with insignificant nutrient content is the preparation liquid, not part of the product. The answer does not say whether that water is counted in a sodium claim calculation, and does not say whether the prepared state needs its own panel. Both are open (UNVERIFIED).

## Page 8, section 9 (added sugars)

- Item 9 (1): "added sugars" includes monosaccharides, disaccharides, syrups, honey and concentrated juice, and names trehalose among the examples. Polyols are not named. Our translation.

## Our reading (DERIVED)

- The panel sodium value for the cube must be a laboratory value, not a calculated one (page 6, item 11).
- Mannitol, isomalt, erythritol and other polyols are outside the Thai nutrition definitions of total sugars and added sugars in this Q&A. Maltodextrin is a glucose polymer; its free glucose and maltose would count (supplier saccharide profile needed).
