# Zeebe 2011: diffusion coefficients of dissolved CO2, HCO3- and CO3(2-)

- Source: R. E. Zeebe, On the molecular diffusion coefficients of dissolved CO2, HCO3- and CO3(2-) and their dependence on isotopic mass. Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta 75, 2483-2498 (2011). doi 10.1016/j.gca.2011.02.010
- URL opened: https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/oceanography/faculty/zeebe_files/Publications/ZeebeDiffGCA11.pdf (PDF saved by the fetch tool, text extracted with PyMuPDF, 16 pages; abstract, introduction passages, Table 2 and the discussion read)
- Date read: 2026-10-20
- Tier: P (peer-reviewed; the coefficients are molecular dynamics fits, not direct measurements). Status: SOURCED.

## Extracted facts
- The abstract states that very little experimental data is available on the bicarbonate and carbonate ion diffusion coefficients.
- Table 2 fit: D_i = D0_i [(T/Ti) - 1]^c_i (T in K, 273 to 373 K), D0 in 1e-9 m2/s: CO2 14.6836, Ti 217.2056, c 1.9970; HCO3- 7.0158, Ti 204.0282, c 2.3942; CO3(2-) 5.4468, Ti 210.2646, c 2.1929.
- DERIVED at 298.15 K from the fits: D(HCO3-) = 1.10e-9 m2/s; D(CO2) = 2.04e-9 m2/s (the experimental CO2 value used in rework/71 is 1.92e-9, Wikipedia, S; a +6 percent sensitivity).
- Printed in the introduction: from the limiting ionic conductivities, the self-diffusion coefficients of HCO3- and CO3(2-) at 25 C and infinite dilution may be estimated as 1.19e-9 and 0.92e-9 m2/s; the discussion says the conductivity-based values are higher than the few experimental values.

## Limits
- Force-field dependent; no direct measurement of HCO3- in the cube's solution (ionic strength and potassium present).
