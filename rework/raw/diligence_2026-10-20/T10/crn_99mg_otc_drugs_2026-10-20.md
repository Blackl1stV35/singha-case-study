## The question (paraphrased by the fetch tool)
A CRN member asked whether FDA sets an upper limit for potassium. They had seen wording about a 99 mg limit, could not find it in 21 CFR, and saw higher potassium amounts elsewhere in the CFR.

## The answer, in short quotes
- "The upper limit of 99 mg per dose for potassium is specific to OTC drugs"
- OTC drugs: "the requirement to label OTC products with a warning statement if they contain more than 100 mg potassium/serving"
- Supplements: "FDA has not actually ruled or commented on potassium in dietary supplements." and "FDA does not set maximums for supplements."
- Practice: "in practice, manufacturers have followed this limit due to the safety concerns expressed by the agency"
- Advice: "it would be prudent for manufacturers to stick with the less than 100 mg limit"
- The answer links to earlier CRN comments "back in 2014" and to an NIH Office of Dietary Supplements potassium fact sheet described as "recently updated" (no date given). That NIH page returned HTTP 403 when opened on 20 October 2026.

## What this settles for T10
- The 99 mg figure is a labelling convention for over-the-counter drugs, set just under the 100 mg warning line that matches 21 CFR 201.306 (P). It is not a line in 21 CFR 201.306 and not a rule for dietary supplements. Supplement makers follow it by convention, on CRN's account.
- The brief's phrase "99 mg per dose US limit for potassium in over-the-counter supplements" is therefore a convention followed by makers (S), not a regulatory limit for supplements. The comparator the record keeps as primary is the 100 mg line of 21 CFR 201.306.
- The cube serve is 316.6 mg, 3.2 times 99 mg and 3.17 times 100 mg.
