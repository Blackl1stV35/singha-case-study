## Implementation plan

The plan starts at M0, which the Gate 0 record sets. The executive sponsor signs that record, and it names one owner for each gate and the first budget ceiling. The evidence does not date M0, so every duration is an ASSUMPTION. No step, quote, test, sign-off or outside contact has happened. Each outside contact is named in the Gate 0 record first, and none happens before it. The plan follows the strategy in section 04 (the lead segment is men aged 25 to 34, with men aged 45 to 59 a later volume segment, and the proposition is form, not flavour) and the gates, KPIs and kill criteria in section 06.

## Marketing campaign

**Segment.** Men aged 25 to 34 are the proposed lead segment. That is a proposal, not a consumer finding. We compared the customer groups a Singha product would reach, varying every input across its range in 40,000 simulated outcomes, including Bangkok's monthly temperature (27 to 31 degrees Celsius); the heat effect is an assumption. No Singha buyer data exists, so every group is a market proxy. Compared per person within each age and sex group, men aged 25 to 34 who drink alcohol rank first among six drinker groups in 94 percent of simulated outcomes (87 to 97 percent by month). Their margin over men aged 35 to 44 is about 3 percent on purchase intent (about 0.9 points at a 30 percent baseline) and about 6 percent after the potassium-caution exclusion. Both ranges include no difference. The desk model puts the gap between men aged 25 to 34 and men aged 35 to 44 at a median of about 0.6 to 1.2 points at a 30 percent baseline, depending on the model scale (0.6 on the odds scale and 0.9 on the share scale in the age-band model; 1.2 in the survey power simulation). On the odds scale its 90 percent interval at a 30 percent baseline runs from minus 0.03 to 1.39 points, so it includes zero (DERIVED from a desk model with unsourced inputs). By total reach no group leads clearly, and the order depends on how the groups are defined. The confirmed category band is men aged 27 to 44 (Bangkok sample, n = 400, S). The model rests on drinking of sugar-sweetened drinks, and the cube contains no sugar, so transfer to a sweetener-only product is untested. The drinking shares for men aged 25 to 44 and 45 to 59 are 68.6 and 65.4 percent [R, P, UNVERIFIED]. Use of sugar-substitute drinks is 0.6 percent at ages 25 to 44 and 1.0 percent at ages 45 to 59 [S, P, UNVERIFIED; the survey's column definition is to be checked before any figure is printed]. The population counts and the age gradient in the model are UNVERIFIED. The survey does not split ages 25 to 34 from 35 to 44; that split is modelled. Men aged 45 to 59 are a later volume segment and are pended.

**Concept pre-test (Phase 2; no media, no sale).** A recruited online concept test, run only after the Gate 0 record names the panel provider and its budget (setting 3). Screen: men resident in Thailand aged 25 to 59 who drank alcohol in the past 12 months and had a drinking occasion in the past three months. Exclude people working in beverage, alcohol, food retail, advertising and research, and anyone in a beverage concept test in the past six months. Cells: ages 25 to 34 and 35 to 44, with 45 to 59 as reference. Stimulus: one brand-blind concept, text and image, showing the mechanism only, and signed off under Gate 7 by M3. Secondary: one price of 15 baht, against the Singha Lemon Soda can (single can THB 17.00 and 24-can packs THB 13.54 to 13.75 a can, online, 20 October 2026, S); occasion incidence; open-text comprehension, with health, diet and sugar mentions flagged; reaction to the sweetener disclosure; attention check.

Decision rule, fixed before the run (setting 12). The lead is supported if the lower bound of the 95 percent interval on men aged 25 to 34 minus men aged 35 to 44 is above zero. It is reversed if the upper bound is below zero. Otherwise it is undetermined. The pre-test reads direction and size under the interval rule set out in Appendix C, and the five-point rule is applied as stated there. About 1,377 respondents per cell give 80 percent power to detect a five-point gap against zero (DERIVED). Under the five-point rule, the lower bound must reach five points, which at that sample needs an observed gap of about 8 points (DERIVED from the design's interval).

**Message rules (all phases).** Allowed: "Drop one cube into 330 ml of water and stir"; "Serving: one cube made up to 330 ml"; "Single-serve pouch"; "Contains an intensive sweetener: sucralose (working choice; the Gate 1 regulatory opinion is to confirm it)". The working name is used only after Gate 7 clears it; until then a neutral placeholder is used. Potassium may appear as a composition fact; any amount or warning waits for Gate 2.

## Validation plan: concept survey

The survey is a brand-blind concept test of the lemon cube. Track 1 (brand-blind) can run first. Track 2 (brand-named) waits for Gate 7.
