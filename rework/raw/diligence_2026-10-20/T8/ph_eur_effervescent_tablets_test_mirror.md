## Extracted text (our wording of the passage, numbers as printed)
- Definition: effervescent tablets are uncoated tablets generally containing acid substances and carbonates or hydrogen carbonates, which react rapidly in the presence of water to release carbon dioxide; they are intended to be dissolved or dispersed in water before administration.
- Test (in the monograph Tablets, not in chapter 2.9.1): place 1 tablet in a beaker containing 200 mL of water at 15 to 25 C; numerous gas bubbles are evolved; when the evolution of gas around the tablet or its fragments ceases, the tablet has disintegrated, dissolved or dispersed so that no agglomerates remain. Repeat on 5 other tablets. The tablets comply if each of the 6 disintegrates in the manner prescribed within 5 min, unless otherwise justified and authorised.
- For comparison, the same monograph says soluble tablets disintegrate within 3 min in water at 15 to 25 C.

## Limits
- The lead for this task named "Ph. Eur. 2.9.1" for the 5 minute limit; in edition 7.0 the effervescent limit sits in the Tablets monograph and chapter 2.9.1 covers the basket test. The current edition (10 or 11) was not opened, so the wording may have changed.
- The limit is a pass criterion for 200 mL and one tablet, not a measured dissolution time.
