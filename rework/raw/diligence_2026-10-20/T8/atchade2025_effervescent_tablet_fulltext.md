## Extracted facts (Tables and Methods)
- Disintegration test (Methods): one tablet in a beaker with 200 ml of distilled water at 25 C; the tablet disintegrates when dissolved or dispersed with no particle agglomerate; six tablets must each disintegrate within 5 min (300 s), per the Ph. Eur. 10th edition. The abstract's 156 s is the F2 formulation (citric acid to sodium bicarbonate stoichiometric ratio 1:3.11).
- Table of results, disintegration time: F1 307, F2 156, F3 263, F4 209, F5 261 seconds (limit 300 s; n = 3, mean). Median of the five 209 s.
- Tablet properties: mass 513.2, 508.2, 451, 480.5 and 521.8 mg (F1 to F5); diameter 11.1 mm; thickness 4.28 mm; hardness 81.8 to 118 N.
- Composition (Table 1, percent): active ingredient 45.4; citric acid 8.56 to 9.32; sodium bicarbonate 10.68 to 11.44; PVP 4; wheat starch 8; lactose 21.6; magnesium stearate 1. Citric acid plus bicarbonate is about 20 percent of the tablet.
- The paper states that more effervescent mixture gives faster dispersion and dissolution, with carbon dioxide acting as a disintegrant.

## Comparison with the cube (DERIVED)
- The cube (3.79 g, 14 mm cube) is 7.3 to 8.4 times heavier, 3.3 times thicker (14 against 4.28 mm), and about 78 percent reactants (2.94 g of 3.79 g) against about 20 percent. The tablet times are therefore an order-of-magnitude anchor (about 2.5 to 5 minutes for a 0.5 g tablet), not a prediction for the cube.
