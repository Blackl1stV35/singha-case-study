# Liger-Belair et al. 2012: monitoring gaseous CO2 and ethanol above champagne glasses (PLoS ONE 7(2), e30628)

## Extracted facts
- Each glass received 100 +/- 2 mL of champagne. A standard 75 cl bottle holds about 9 g of dissolved CO2.
- For the flute the paper cites an earlier article: dissolved CO2 falls from about 7.4 g/L just after pouring to about 3 g/L about 15 minutes later. This gives a first-order loss constant of ln(7.4/3) / 900 s = 1.0e-3 per second for a flute of champagne at room temperature (DERIVED here; the paper gives no constant).
- The paper gives no equation for diffusion through the free surface and no bubble-versus-diffusion split.

## Limits
- The 7.4 to 3 g/L figure is a quote of a previous article seen through a summariser; it is a PROXY for nucleation-driven loss in an open glass at 4.5 bar-equivalent supersaturation, not for a 1 g/L drink. The temperature of that test is not stated in the extract.
