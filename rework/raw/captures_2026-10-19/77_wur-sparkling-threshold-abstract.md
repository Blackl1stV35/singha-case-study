## Extracted facts

- Title: "Is it still water? Relationships between sparkling sensitivity and consumption frequency of carbonated waters".
- Authors: Sharon Puleo, Maria Teresa Castillo, Rossella Di Monaco, Markus Stieger.
- Year: 2021 (early online 2 July 2021). Journal: Food Research International, vol. 147, article 110584.
- Carbonation detection threshold: 0.44 g/L CO2 concentration, on average, by the best estimate threshold (BET) method. Unit: g/L CO2 concentration. The abstract does not report CO2 volumes.
- Tested concentration range as stated in the abstract: 0.03 to 1.05 g/L.
- Sample size: 100 participants, grouped as non-consumers, infrequent consumers and frequent consumers of sparkling water.
- Stimuli: five sparkling mineral waters with CO2 levels from 0.21 to 4.92 g/L.
- Test as stated: thresholds by the BET method. Intensity and liking were rated for the five waters. The abstract does not name the rating scale or the test format. It does not mention a triangle test or a ranking test.
- Other results as stated in the abstract: detection thresholds did not differ by consumption frequency, PROP taster status or other consumer characteristics. Perceived intensity of sparkling sensation differed significantly by consumption frequency, with non-consumers rating it stronger. Non-consumers reported higher liking than infrequent or frequent consumers.
