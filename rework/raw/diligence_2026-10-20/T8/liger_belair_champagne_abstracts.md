# Liger-Belair and co-workers on CO2 loss from champagne glasses, abstract level

- Date read: 20 October 2026. Routes: Consensus index records (abstract as returned) and PubMed (via the PubMed connector; cite PubMed and the DOI). Articles not opened unless stated.
- Tier: S (abstract level). Status: SOURCED at abstract level.

| Paper | Source record | What the abstract states |
|---|---|---|
| CO2 volume fluxes outgassing from champagne glasses in tasting conditions: flute versus coupe. J Agric Food Chem 2009 | https://consensus.app/papers/details/1234cfa3be0c5032a4feb9e1c1afa5f7/ | Standard champagne with about 11.5 g/L dissolved CO2; first 10 min after pouring; the loss of dissolved CO2 is higher in a coupe than a flute; bubbles collapsing at the surface carry most of the flux; "invisible diffusion through the free surface" is discussed and compared for both glasses; no numbers for the split in the abstract. |
| Kinetics of CO2 fluxes outgassing from champagne glasses in tasting conditions: the role of temperature. J Agric Food Chem 2009 | https://consensus.app/papers/details/617880735f1b5b81b374a328897c7fbe/ | About 11 g/L dissolved CO2; flute; 4, 12 and 20 C; the lower the temperature the lower the outgassing flux; the temperature dependence of viscosity plays a major role; effervescence is modelled. |
| Monitoring the losses of dissolved CO2 from laser-etched champagne glasses. Food Res Int 2013 | https://consensus.app/papers/details/14871ba0f2d45e329e5c06d269605eda/ | More nucleation sites give faster loss of dissolved CO2 early on, but from about 3 min the highest gas flux is not from the glass with the most bubble trains; the flux is a balance between effervescence intensity and the dissolved CO2 concentration. |
| Modeling the losses of dissolved CO2 from laser-etched champagne glasses. J Phys Chem B 2016 (PubMed 27031022, doi 10.1021/acs.jpcb.6b01421) | https://consensus.app/papers/details/53b571368adc5ea18bdec178d604c89e/ | A global model of heterogeneous nucleation, bubble rise and mass transfer gives the progressive loss; temperature role corroborated experimentally. |
| On the losses of dissolved CO2 during champagne serving. J Agric Food Chem 2010 (PubMed 20681665, doi 10.1021/jf101239w) | PubMed | Bottle at 11.4 +/- 0.1 g/L; losses during pouring at 4, 12 and 18 C; the higher the temperature the larger the loss. |
| CO2 diffusion in champagne wines: a molecular dynamics study. J Phys Chem B 2014 (PubMed 24506162, doi 10.1021/jp410998f) | PubMed | Experimental CO2 diffusion coefficients of 1.4 to 1.5e-9 m2/s for champagne wines (the abstract cites the experimental range). |

## Limits
- None of the abstracts gives the split between free-surface diffusion and bubbling, or a mass-transfer coefficient. The only quantitative loss rate used in the fizz model is the 7.4 to 3 g/L in about 15 min flute figure in liger_belair_plos2012_PMC3275598.md.
