## Text read (quotes as printed, long passages shortened with "...")

Paragraph (a), coated tablets: "The Food and Drug Administration will initiate no regulatory action with respect to the continued marketing of coated tablets containing potassium chloride or other potassium salts which supply 100 milligrams or more of potassium per tablet provided all the following conditions are met:"

Paragraph (b), uncoated tablets and liquids: "The Food and Drug Administration may initiate regulatory proceedings after 30 days from the date of publication of this section, with respect to the marketing of uncoated tablets containing potassium chloride or other potassium salts which supply 100 milligrams or more of potassium per tablet or liquid preparations containing potassium chloride or other potassium salts which supply 20 milligrams or more of potassium per milliliter, labeled or intended for human use, unless all the following conditions are met:" The conditions are the prescription statement in section 503(b)(4) of the Act, and labelling with the full disclosure warning in paragraph (b)(2).

Warning statement (paragraph (b)(2)), opening and key sentences: "Warning. There have been several reports, published and unpublished, concerning non-specific small-bowel lesions consisting of stenosis, with or without ulceration, associated with the administration of enteric-coated thiazides with potassium salts. ... These small-bowel lesions have caused obstruction, hemorrhage, and perforation. ... Based on a large survey of physicians and hospitals, both United States and foreign, the incidence of these lesions is low, and a causal relationship in man has not been definitely established. ... Coated potassium tablets should be used only when adequate dietary supplementation is not practicable."

Scope parenthetical after the warning: "(Although the warning statement includes references to enteric-coated potassium salt preparations, it applies to any capsule or coated tablet of a potassium salt intended for oral ingestion without prior dilution with an adequate volume of liquid to preclude gastrointestinal injury.)"

## What the text does and does not contain
- It contains the 100 mg per tablet line and the 20 mg per mL line for liquids.
- It does not contain a 99 mg figure, and it says nothing about foods, electrolyte drinks or dietary supplements as such.
- The 99 mg figure used in the T10 brief is therefore not a line in this regulation. The Council for Responsible Nutrition (S) says 99 mg per dose is an over-the-counter drug labelling convention, followed by makers (see `crn_99mg_otc_drugs_2026-10-20.md` and `us_99mg_secondary_leads_2026-10-20.md`).
- Whether a dissolvable food cube falls within "tablets" or "capsule or coated tablet ... intended for oral ingestion without prior dilution" is a question for a U.S. regulatory reader. The cube is dissolved in water before drinking, which bears on the scope parenthetical.
