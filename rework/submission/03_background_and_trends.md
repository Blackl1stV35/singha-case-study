# Background and trends: Thailand, domestic market

Scope: Thailand, domestic market. Thai-language sources are summarised in English. Overseas analogues are labelled, because no domestic evidence for dissolvable cubes was found.

## Background: the group and its beverage business

In the company's own description, Boon Rawd Brewery became the holding company in a 2000 reorganisation, and Singha Corporation was set up in 2001 (Source 1; S; SOURCED). Singha Corporation is a subsidiary of Boon Rawd Brewery and manages more than 150 group companies (S; current English home page, undated, read 19 October 2026; the count of more than 150 is from the About Us page, Source 1). It reports directly to the Boon Rawd board (Source 1; S; SOURCED). Press and company sources describe the group differently, and the sources disagree on how it is structured; this proposal follows the company's own description.

The current English home page describes Singha Corporation as a subsidiary of Boon Rawd Brewery. An older affiliate wording, on a page with a 2014 footer, is superseded and is not used (S). The beverages category lists six products: Singha Water, Singha Soda, Singha Lemon Soda, Purra, Purra Vitamin Water and FIJI (Source 2). Treating that category as the nearest unit for a non-alcoholic product is an ASSUMPTION.

The group already sells a lime sparkling water, which is a different product from Lemon Soda. Its launch release, dated 13 March 2026, describes Singha Sparkling Water Lime Flavour, a 330 ml can priced at 15 baht, launched in March 2026 (Source 3; P; SOURCED). A news reprint posted 17 March 2026 gives the same flavour, can size and price (Source 4; S; SOURCED). The release calls the can sugar-free with 0% calories; this is the company's claim (COMPANY CLAIM), not checked here. It gives no ingredient list, and carbonation and canning capacity are not evidenced here.

The Singha Lemon Soda can overlaps Lemon Soda on flavour. The difference lies in form, pack and price. Lemon Soda is a dissolvable cube that makes the drink in the glass, one cube per pouch. The company's lemon soda is a ready-to-drink 330 mL can, listed on the company product page, which says the can has 0% sugar and no calories (Source 5; S; SOURCED; COMPANY CLAIM; read 20 October 2026; the page is undated).

Launch and range. The can was launched in August 2020 according to a PR-tagged trade press article dated 19 August 2020 (S), which prints no launch date, and two later articles say late 2020 (S), so the launch month is a stated limit (Source 23). The launch price of the lemon can was THB 16 (2020; S). Later flavours as reported in the press are Yuzu (June 2021), Red (August 2022), Ume (September 2023), Pink (June 2024) and Lemon Cream (November 2025) (S; Source 24). Yuzu is also named in the range in press mentions of April 2025 (S). Yuzu is listed online at Lotus's Shop Online at THB 17 on an aggregator page stamped 20 October 2026 (S), and Yuzu packs appear at Makro PRO and Lazada (Source 22). Against this, Lotus's own pages and the 7-Eleven shop pages opened list no Yuzu, and the Makro Yuzu pages carry a delivery-area notice (Source 22). Whether a Yuzu can can be bought in October 2026 is not confirmed.

Price and channels. On 20 October 2026, online listings gave a single lemon can at THB 17.00 at Lotus's Shop Online, and 24-can packs at THB 330 (THB 13.75 a can) at 7-Eleven ALL Online and Lotus's and at THB 325 (THB 13.54 a can) at Makro PRO (Source 21; S). The pack prices are SOURCED and the per-can prices are DERIVED. Across six retail rows the band is THB 13.54 to 17.00, with a median of THB 13.75. A seventh row, the 7-Eleven ALL Online six-can pack at THB 172 (THB 28.67 a can), is left out as an unreconciled outlier (Source 21). These are online prices; no store shelf price was read. Press reports from 2024 and 2025 name 7-Eleven, Lotus's, Makro, department stores, supermarkets and the Singha Online Shop (S; Source 24), and the lemon can was listed online at 7-Eleven, Lotus's and Makro PRO on 20 October 2026 (S; Source 21). Retail reach and the number of outlets that carry the can are not verified.

The price of the cube, THB 15 per serve, is an ASSUMPTION. It sits above the 24-can pack prices (THB 13.54 to 13.75 a can) and below the single can's THB 17.00 online price, so the proposal presents Lemon Soda as competing on form and on the Singha name, not on price. Lemon Soda is the working name, and it needs a trademark register search before use (Gate 7). The SINGHA name and the lion emblem are used in this proposal only, and any other use needs written sign-off at Gate 7.

Limit: no consolidated accounts, innovation budget or group R&D spend were available, and no group financial ratio is used here.

## Limits

Several pages were read through an automated summary; re-check their figures against the live page before any published use. No domestic study of dissolvable cubes has been found. Two Priceza price-comparison searches, for waterdrop and Ricola, were read on 20 October 2026 and no dissolvable drink cube was found on sale in the rows shown (Source 29). That was a partial read: 36 of 1,073 waterdrop matches and 24 Ricola rows were visible, the rows behind the show-more button were not opened, and some Thai retailer pages returned HTTP 403 and were not opened. No store shelf price was read, and no in-store shelf search has been run. Global market-size estimates are not used.

The carbon dioxide and pH figures are desk-model results (DERIVED on ASSUMPTION inputs). No bench or sensory test has been run, and taste is untested. The model gives a pH of about 3.4 to 3.5, and the working pH of 3.5 is an ASSUMPTION. At 25 C a cube is modelled to dissolve in about 6 minutes stirred and about 11 minutes not stirred for a central set of assumed inputs (3 to 22 and 5 to 74 minutes across the assumed ranges). At 20 C, surface loss alone keeps the dissolved carbon dioxide above the 0.44 g/L sparkling threshold for about 1.7 to 3.4 hours if the full dose dissolves at serve; bubble formation could cut this to about 15 minutes (champagne proxy). If less than 41% of the dose is dissolved at serve, the drink starts below the threshold. The threshold comes from an abstract-only mineral-water study (Source 30; S, PROXY). The dissolution inputs are assumed values and the 15-minute figure is a champagne proxy; the product section (section 11) states both as such and gives no separate source for either. None of these times has been measured. The R&D lead restates the pass rule for the 0.55-volume dose before the bench run (Gate 3); none is set here.

Sodium and potassium. The water used to make up the drink adds sodium: 0 to about 7 mg a serve at the WHO typical level of 20 mg per litre (Source 27; P; the WHO notes higher levels in some places), or about 33 to 66 mg at the 100 to 200 mg per litre reported for Thai tap water (Source 28; S; a news report, not measured). With the recipe's own 0 to about 2 mg, the one-serve total is about 0 to 9 mg at the WHO typical level and about 33 to 68 mg with Thai tap water. These are calculations, not measurements, and no sodium-free or low-sodium claim is made. The cube holds about 960 mg of potassium per litre (DERIVED). Thai Notification 195, on mineral-salt beverages, sets potassium at no more than 195 mg per litre where it is present (clause 4(1.3)). The text of that clause is unchanged from the Gazette original through the Thai FDA compilation of about 29 March 2023 (Source 26; P; read from page images). No later amending notification was found by title on the Thai FDA list to 20 October 2026 (P, a negative finding), but an amendment after March 2023 inside a general notification is not excluded. The 2011 and 2020 amendments did not change clauses 3, 4(1) or 7 (Source 26). Whether the cube is a mineral-salt beverage under clause 3 is the Gate 1 question; the proposed class is flavoured drink. If the Gate 1 opinion classes the cube as a mineral-salt beverage, its potassium would be about 4.9 times the ceiling and the formula would have to change.

Only the flavoured-drink rows of the Thai food-additive annex were read, not the full annex. Unverifiable figures, including two Thai consumer surveys and two trend percentages, are not used. The Singha Lemon Soda can's national retail reach and the number of outlets that carry it are not verified here, its launch month is contested (August 2020 in a PR-tagged trade press article, late 2020 in two later articles), and whether a Yuzu can can be bought in October 2026 is not confirmed.
