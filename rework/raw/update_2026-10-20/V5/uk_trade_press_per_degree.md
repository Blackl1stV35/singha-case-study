# UK trade press: soft drink sales per degree of temperature

- Item: V5. Date read: 20 October 2026. Both pages are trade press and are tier S. Neither gives a method, a sample or a country in the text that was read.

## 1. Scottish Grocer, "How hot days generate sales", 1 June 2014

- URL: https://www.scottishgrocer.co.uk/2014/06/how-hot-days-generate-sales/ (WebFetch).
- Standfirst, unattributed: demand for soft drinks can go up "by as much as 5%" for each degree the temperature rises.
- Body, attributed to Nielsen through a Lucozade Ribena Suntory soft drinks category director: "for every degree the temperature rises above 14C, there is a 2-5% increase in sales".
- Geography is not stated on the page (the publication is Scottish; the UK is the likely setting and is not confirmed).

## 2. Forecourt Trader, "Sales pour in", 6 May 2019

- URL: https://forecourttrader.co.uk/soft-drinks/sales-pour-in/643387.article (WebFetch).
- Attributed to IRI: "for every one degree increase in the temperature, soft drinks sales increase by 1.6%".
- Attributed to Nielsen: soft drink sales grew by 6 to 8 percent during the warm weather of the previous summer (no temperature stated).
- Geography is not stated on the page (UK forecourt trade title; the UK is the likely setting and is not confirmed).

## 3. Asian Trader, "Scorching weather provides 30 million pound boost for c-sector", 1 June 2026, byline Andy Marino

- Added in the V5 revision, 20 October 2026. URL: https://www.asiantrader.biz/hot-conditions-convenience-sales-rise (WebFetch).
- One sentence states that each one-degree rise in temperature lifts soft drinks sales by 1.8 percent, about 1.3 million pounds of extra sales. The sentence names no source; the preceding paragraph credits Talysis (its Q1 convenience report), and the extract says the figures appear to come from Talysis without saying so. The page says the effect is measured against the expected seasonal average temperature, gives no period for the per-degree figure and no method. The setting is the UK convenience sector.
- Two other pages carrying the same claim (kamcity.com, ?p=270985 and ?p=271979) returned HTTP 403 and were not read.
- As a log-scale beta: ln(1.018) is 0.0178.

## Tier and status

- Tier S. Status PROXY (temperate market, trade-press claims of unknown method, a range of 1.6 to 5 percent per degree, applied above 14 C in one case; the 2026 claim is from a single recent hot spell and its period is not stated).
