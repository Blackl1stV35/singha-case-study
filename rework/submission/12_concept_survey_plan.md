# Appendix C: concept survey plan

Status: Not run. No outside contact, quote or fieldwork has happened.

Tiers follow Appendix A. Each figure carries its tier where it first appears. DERIVED means arithmetic from the inputs shown. ASSUMPTION means a value chosen for the model. S means a secondary source that was read and opened. UNVERIFIED means the figure was not read, could not be read reliably, or has not been re-read for this plan.

## 1. Purpose

This plan sets out a concept survey that would test whether men aged 25 to 34 state a higher purchase intent than men aged 35 to 44 for a dissolvable lemon-flavoured drink cube. It fixes the design, sample size, decision rule and preconditions before any fieldwork. It reports no result.

The survey measures stated purchase intent, which is a proxy for conversion. None of the sources reviewed holds a conversion outcome. The conversion comparison in this proposal therefore rests on a model built from published inputs, with no respondents. This plan is the recruited design. It is deferred, and it runs only after Singha Corporation approves a fieldwork provider and a budget in writing.

## 2. Entity and product

- Proposer: Singha Corporation.
- Holding company: Boon Rawd Brewery, which holds the SINGHA marks as far as found (S).
- Product: a dissolvable lemon-flavoured drink cube in a pouch, presented in the proposal as the Singha Lemon Soda Cube (lemon). One cube is dropped into a glass of water. The product is sweetened with an intensive sweetener, which is disclosed in the ingredient list and not claimed.
- Name: the name Lemon Soda matches the printed flavour name on the Singha Lemon Soda product page (S). A register search and a shopper-confusion check are open under Gate 7. Track 1 therefore uses a neutral placeholder name.

## 3. Two tracks

- **Track 1, brand-blind.** The stimulus names no brand, shows no lion and uses no public product name. It makes no health, nutrition, sugar or sodium claim. Track 1 can be designed now. It runs only after the preconditions in section 11 are met.
- **Track 2, brand-named.** It runs only after Gate 7 clears the SINGHA name and lion, the product name and the copy. Gate 7 covers written sign-off on the lion and SINGHA, rulings on alcohol cues, and a register search for the name. Track 2 is not designed in this plan. It needs its own stimulus, cells and decision rule before fieldwork.

## 4. Universe and cells

Universe: men resident in Thailand, aged 25 to 59, who drank alcohol in the past 12 months and who pass the occasion screen in section 5. The sample comes from a national online panel. Bangkok is an optional stratum and is not funded in this plan.

| Cell | Population | Role |
|---|---|---|
| A | Men aged 25 to 34 | Lead cell |
| B | Men aged 35 to 44 | Comparison cell, the closest per-person cell |
| C | Men aged 45 to 59 | Descriptive only. Informs the pended volume question. Not a decision input. Runs only if funded |

**Universe size.** Registration counts for November 2568 give about 4.76 million men aged 25 to 34, 4.75 million aged 35 to 44 and 6.65 million aged 45 to 59 (UNVERIFIED). Mid-year counts from the Department of Disease Control give 4.71 million, 4.65 million and 7.13 million (UNVERIFIED). The two sources agree within about 2% for men aged 25 to 44 and differ by about 7% for men aged 45 to 59 (DERIVED from the UNVERIFIED counts). The source tables have not been re-read for this plan.

**Drinking share.** About 68.6% of men aged 25 to 44 are current drinkers in the 2024 survey (UNVERIFIED). The figure is cited to the NSO Social Indicators 2025, Table 4.6.2. The table could not be read reliably on 19 October 2026, and the 2024 definition of current drinking is inferred. Men aged 25 to 34 and 35 to 44 are not published separately, so both use this share.

**Screening load.** At 1,377 completes per cell, about 2,008 screened men per cell are needed if the occasion screen passes every drinker (DERIVED from the UNVERIFIED drinking share). At an illustrative occasion pass rate of 0.5 (ASSUMPTION), about 4,015 are needed (DERIVED). No source gives the occasion pass rate. The pilot in section 12 measures it.

**Bangkok.** In Bangkok, men aged 25 to 34 and 35 to 44 are about equal in size (DERIVED from UNVERIFIED mid-year counts of about 370,000 each). A Bangkok stratum would need its own sample size, which this plan does not derive.

**2021 counts.** No 2021 population count by age band exists in the sources reviewed. This is a stated limit.

## 6. Stimulus and questionnaire

### Stimulus rules (track 1)

- Describe the mechanism only. The cube dissolves in water and releases carbon dioxide. No fizz volume or level is stated. A physics simulation with stated limits supports the mechanism. It is a model, not a measurement, so no fizz level goes into the stimulus.
- No health, nutrition, sugar, sodium or brand claim. The following words are not used: "sugar-free", "no sugar", "zero", "sodium", "sodium-free", "low", "healthy", "natural", "for hydration", and any botanical health wording.
- No SINGHA name, lion or public product name.
- The ingredient list reads: potassium bicarbonate, citric acid, lemon flavour, and an intensive sweetener. The sweetener name is not yet set. The proposal's working choice is sucralose, pending the Gate 1 opinion (ASSUMPTION).
- Concept image: one cube dropped into water. The design draft adds a glass and a lemon wedge. Under the proposal, both wait for the Gate 7 rulings on glassware and lemon imagery. A claim check of the image and text runs before use. The image is not yet produced.

Draft stimulus text. Bracketed values are not yet set.

> [Working name] is a lemon-flavoured drink cube. Drop one cube into a glass of water. The cube dissolves and releases carbon dioxide into the water. Pack: [N] cubes. Price: [PRICE] THB per pack.
>
> Ingredients: potassium bicarbonate, citric acid, lemon flavour, [intensive sweetener, name not yet set].

### Questionnaire

Wording is exact. Options appear in order.

- **Q1 Exposure.** Show the stimulus once. The minimum dwell time is set by the proposer. The proposed minimum is 10 seconds (ASSUMPTION).
- **Q2 Primary measure.** "If you saw this product in a shop, how likely would you be to buy it?" 1 Definitely would not; 2 Probably would not; 3 Might or might not; 4 Probably would; 5 Definitely would. Top-2-box is codes 4 and 5.
- **Q3 Price.** "This product costs [PRICE] THB for a pack of [N] cubes. Would you buy it at this price?" Yes or No. The price is not yet set.
- **Q4 Occasions.** "When would you use this product? Select all that apply." (a) On a hot day, outdoors or at work; (b) At home, on its own; (c) Mixed with beer, spirits or another drink; (d) At a party or gathering; (e) Other (specify); (f) I would not use it. Occasion measures: (a) hot season, (b) drinking at home, (c) mixing.
- **Q5 Comprehension.** "In your own words, what kind of product is this?" Open text, coded for product type. Any mention of health, diet or sugar is flagged as a comprehension finding.
- **Q6 Disclosure.** "The ingredient list says this product is sweetened with [SWEETENER]. How does this affect your interest?" Much more interested; A little more; No change; A little less; Much less.
- **Q7 Attention.** "To show you are paying attention, select 'Probably would not'."

**Profile (not screened on).** P1 is exact age, taken from S2. P2 is province, for the optional Bangkok stratum. P3 is "In the past 7 days, how often did you drink a sweetened soft drink?" Never; 1 to 2 times; 3 to 6 times; once a day; more than once a day.

## 7. Primary outcome and secondary items

- **Primary outcome.** The share of respondents giving codes 4 or 5 at Q2, per person exposed. This is a PROXY for conversion. It measures stated intent, not a purchase.
- **Primary contrast.** Top-2-box share in cell A minus cell B. Two-sided alpha 0.05. The 95% confidence interval uses the Newcombe-Wilson method.
- **Secondary items.** Q3 Yes rate at the stated price by cell, with the same test. Q4 occasion incidence by cell, to check whether the occasion mix explains any age gap. Q6 disclosure reaction. Q5 comprehension. Cell C compared descriptively with A and B.

## 8. Sample and power

**Method (DERIVED).** Two independent cells, two-sided alpha 0.05, power 0.80. The formula is:

n per cell = [z(alpha/2) × sqrt(2 × pbar × qbar) + z(beta) × sqrt(p1 × q1 + p2 × q2)]^2 / (p1 - p2)^2

where cell A has proportion p1, cell B has proportion p2, pbar = (p1 + p2) / 2 and qbar = 1 - pbar. No continuity correction and no design effect are applied. Both would increase n. A one-sided alpha of 0.05 would reduce it. At a 30% baseline and a 5-point lead, one-sided alpha gives 1,084 per cell.

**Baseline (ASSUMPTION).** No source gives a top-2-box baseline for men aged 35 to 44. The grid covers 20%, 30%, 40% and 50%.

**Grid: n per cell for 80% power (DERIVED).**

| Baseline (cell B) | 5 points | 10 points | 15 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20% | 1,094 | 294 | 138 |
| 30% | **1,377** | 356 | 163 |
| 40% | 1,534 | 388 | 173 |
| 50% | 1,565 | 388 | 170 |

The 5-point test at a 30% baseline uses 1,377 per cell. A power check at that size gives about 0.80 for a fixed 5-point lead (DERIVED).

**Modelled lead.**

- Ratio A, men 25 to 34 over men 35 to 44: median 1.04, 90% interval 1.00 to 1.09, probability above 1 of 0.94 (ASSUMPTION). It comes from the age gradient alone. The per-year odds ratio for the age gradient has an interval of 0.991 to 1.001 that includes 1.0 (UNVERIFIED). Ratio A is a model assumption, not a finding.
- At a 30% baseline, ratio A is a lead of 1.23 percentage points (DERIVED). Eighty percent power on a lead that size needs about 22,166 per cell (DERIVED). The 1,377 per cell test is not sized for a lead of that size. Averaged over the uncertainty in ratio A, power at 22,166 per cell is about 66% (DERIVED).
- Ratio B, men 25 to 34 over men 45 to 59: median 1.15, 90% interval 1.03 to 1.28 (ASSUMPTION). Reference only. At a 30% baseline it is a 4.4-point lead, which needs about 1,751 per cell (DERIVED).

**Interval width (DERIVED, normal approximation).** At 1,377 per cell, the 95% interval on the difference is about plus or minus 3.4 points at a 30% baseline and about plus or minus 3.7 points at 50%.

## 9. Simulation of the test outcome

**Method (DERIVED).** In each simulated run, a person in cell B is top-2-box with probability p, and a person in cell A with probability p times R. R is drawn from a log-normal distribution fitted to the median and 90% interval of the ratio. A pooled two-proportion z-test runs in each of 20,000 draws per case, with a fixed seed. The outcomes are the shares of draws that confirm the lead, reverse it, or leave it inconclusive. The share of draws whose 95% upper bound is below 5 points is also reported. The inconclusive share is the remainder after the two rounded shares, so the three add to 100.

**Result at a 30% baseline and 1,377 per cell, ratio A (DERIVED).**

| Outcome | Share of runs |
|---|---|
| Confirms the lead (men 25 to 34 significantly higher) | 12.7% |
| Reverses the lead (men 35 to 44 significantly higher) | 0.8% |
| Inconclusive | 86.5% |
| Rules out a 5-point lead (95% upper bound below 5 points) | 56.4% |

The remainder after rounding is 86.5 percent; the results file reports 86.6 percent for the inconclusive share.

**Other checks (DERIVED, at a 30% baseline unless stated).**

- No lead (ratio 1): confirms about 2.5% of runs and reverses about 2.7%. It rules out a 5-point lead in 82%. The test behaves as a two-sided 5% test.
- Ratio A at a 10-point size (356 per cell): confirms 6.0%. At a 15-point size (163 per cell): confirms 4.5%.
- Ratio A at its own 80% power size (22,166 per cell): confirms about 66%, averaged over the uncertainty.
- Ratio B at 1,377 per cell: confirms 63% and reverses 0.3%.
- Ratio A confirms in 7.7% of runs at a 20% baseline (1,094 per cell), 12.7% at 30% (1,377 per cell), 18.6% at 40% (1,534 per cell) and 25.9% at 50% (1,565 per cell).

**Reading.**

- If the lead is as modelled (ratio A), the test confirms it in about 8% to 13% of runs at a 20% to 30% baseline, and about 26% of runs at a 50% baseline. It almost never reverses it.
- At a 30% baseline the test rejects a 5-point or larger lead in more than half of runs. That is the rejection the design can deliver.
- A result that shows no difference is not evidence against the lead. Under ratio A it is the most likely outcome.

## 10. Decision rules

Choose one rule before fieldwork and record the choice. The recommended rule is the interval rule.

**Recommended: the interval rule.** The primary contrast is the top-2-box share in cell A minus cell B, with its 95% Newcombe-Wilson interval.

- **Direction.** "Lead supported" if the lower bound is above 0. "Lead reversed" if the upper bound is below 0. Otherwise "direction undetermined."
- **Size.** "5-point lead supported" if the lower bound is at or above 5 percentage points. "5-point lead not supported" if the upper bound is below 5 percentage points. Otherwise "size undetermined."
- Both statements are reported together. A result can support the direction and still rule out a 5-point lead.

**Alternative: the probability rule.** It reports the posterior probability that cell A's share is higher than cell B's, under a prior stated before fieldwork, and it sets a decision threshold before fieldwork. The prior and the threshold are not set in this plan. The result depends on the prior, which here rests on ratio A (ASSUMPTION).

## 12. Fieldwork sequence

Recruitment is an outside contact. It starts only when item 2 in section 11 is met. The sequence is as follows.

1. Singha Corporation approves this design and the decision rule in section 10 before fieldwork.
2. The stimulus inputs are fixed, and a claim check runs on the image and the text.

## 13. Outputs and limits

**Outputs.** The primary contrast with its interval. The direction and size statements. The secondary items in section 7. Occasion mix by cell. Exclusion counts for speeders, duplicate IDs and short dwell times.

**Limits.**

- **Proxy outcome.** The survey measures stated purchase intent, a PROXY for conversion. It does not measure actual conversion, repeat purchase or sales. Intent is not behaviour. No conversion data exists in the sources reviewed.
- **Assumed baseline.** The 30% baseline is assumed (ASSUMPTION). No source gives it.
- **Assumed link.** The link between stated intent and per-person potency is assumed (ASSUMPTION). It is not evidenced.
- **Ratio A.** It rests on the age gradient alone (ASSUMPTION). The interval for the age gradient includes 1.0 (UNVERIFIED).
- **Price.** One stated price is tested. The price response curve is not measured.
- **Concept only.** Taste and fit are not tested. No sodium, sugar, health or nutrition effect is tested, and no such claim appears in the stimulus.
- **Bangkok.** Bangkok and upcountry differences are not tested unless the stratum is funded.
- **Brand.** Track 1 is brand-blind. Brand effects are not tested until track 2 runs after Gate 7.
- **Population and drinking figures.** The figures in section 4 are UNVERIFIED, and the 2024 definition of current drinking is inferred.
