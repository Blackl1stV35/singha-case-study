## 0. Answers in brief

1. **Binder and filler.** Primary: mannitol, tableting or direct-compression grade (INS 421), 0.80 g per cube. Fallback: isomalt (INS 953), direct-compression grade, 0.80 g per cube. The five specified components add to 3.793 g, so the formula carries no lubricant; sodium-salt lubricants and sodium-coated acids are excluded by specification (section 1.5). Thai permission is read in Notification 468 itself: mannitol and isomalt are listed for category 14.1.4 (flavoured drinks) at quantum satis, with condition TH3, and their function lists include bulking agent, stabilizer and thickener, so they are listed additives and not only processing aids (P, section 1.1). TH3 applies only if the product is classed as an electrolyte drink. Notification 195 defines that class and sets its standard (sodium 460 to 920 mg per litre, plus glucose, fructose or sucrose); the cube cannot meet it, so it must not be named or presented as an electrolyte drink, and the classification stays a Gate 1 question (section 1.1).
2. **Sugar and excise.** Mannitol and isomalt are polyols. The Thai nutrition FAQ defines total and added sugars as mono- and disaccharides and does not name polyols. The Excise Department notice names the test "Total Sugar" (AOAC) on the made-up drink and does not name polyols. The JECFA mannitol monograph limits total sugars to 1.0 percent as glucose, at most 8 mg per cube. Even if the whole 0.80 g were counted as sugar, the drink would hold 0.24 g per 100 mL, inside the lowest excise tier (up to 6 g per 100 mL, 0 baht per litre). The tier is read from the B.E. 2560 schedule extract and a press report of phase 4 (S, 31 March 2025); the later decree was not opened (limit, section 3.4).
3. **Sodium per serve.** Formula ingredients: 0 to about 2.0 mg per 330 mL serve (0.67 mg bounded by JECFA sulfated-ash limits for citric acid, sucralose and mannitol; 1.31 mg planning values for potassium bicarbonate and lemon flavour). The water used to make up the drink adds its own sodium and is the largest open term. For water at or below 20 mg per litre (the WHO typical value; the WHO text says concentrations can greatly exceed this in some countries) the water adds 0 to 6.6 mg, and the total is 0 to about 8.6 mg per serve, 0 to 2.6 mg per 100 mL. A Thai Department of Health nutritionist is reported in the press (S) to put tap water at 100 to 200 mg per litre, which would add 33 to 66 mg per serve and bring the total to about 33 to 68 mg. Thai water was not measured and nothing in the drink was measured.
4. **Thai claim thresholds.** Notification 445 Table 1 sets limits per serving: sodium-free below 5 mg, very low below 35 mg, low below 140 mg. They are tested on the consumed serving and, where the reference amount is not over 30 g, also on two-fold the reference serving (a 400 mL equivalent). Table 2 (per 100 mL: 5, 20 and 60 mg) is for foods without a prescribed reference serving; the cube has one, so Table 2 is a hypothetical only if a regulator ruled otherwise. Script results (formula at its high case): with the water not counted, all three claim limits are met on both bases (1.98 mg on the 330 mL basis, 2.40 mg on the 400 mL basis). With the water counted, the highest water sodium at which the test is still met is: sodium-free about 9.1 mg per litre on the consumed serving and about 6.5 mg per litre if the two-fold basis also applies (and 15.2 and 12.5 mg per litre if the formula held no sodium); very low about 100 and about 81 mg per litre; low about 418 and about 344 mg per litre. At the WHO typical 20 mg per litre the water alone (6.6 mg) exceeds the sodium-free limit when counted. At 100 to 200 mg per litre only the low-sodium limit is met on every basis (section 3.1).
5. **"No added sodium salt".** Supportable as an ingredient-list fact and a recipe specification (ASSUMPTION until supplier declarations; the lemon flavour carrier is unspecified). Codex clause 7.2 allows a non-addition claim on that basis. Thai "no salt added" wording has its own conditions and is held (section 4.1).
6. **Claims.** Sodium-free, low-sodium and very-low-sodium wording stays barred. The earlier phrase "no clearance route" is wrong: Notification 445 gives numeric routes, each needing a measured value for the made-up drink with the water named, and the Gate 1 Thai opinion (section 4.2).
7. **Label.** A dry beverage falls under Thai GDA labelling (Notification 394 clause 3 (6.3), as amended). Notification 446 replaced clause 5(1) of 394, so the nutrition panel follows Annex 1 of Notification 445, which prints sodium and also potassium (clause 2.3 marks both as mandatory). The potassium line (about 320 mg after rounding, about 9 percent of the 3,500 mg Thai reference) conflicts with the submission's "off pack" statement (section 6). Notification 394 clause 5, last paragraph, lets an individual pack with a front area under 65 sq.cm carry the panels on the wholesale package; the pouch front area is a Gate 1 and Gate 9 point (section 3.3). Notification 467 was read in full in English by the T10 record and changes only the panel's background colour and font sizes.
8. **Binder price.** No Thai supplier price was found. From the saved UN Comtrade rows (Thailand imports of mannitol, HS 290543, 2025) the unit value runs from the world average of THB 147 per kg (THB 0.12 per cube) through THB 410 to 433 per kg for France and Japan (THB 0.33 to 0.35 per cube) to THB 1,588 per kg for Germany (THB 1.27 per cube, 91 percent of the THB 1.40 headroom between contribution and the stop threshold). The rows do not say which grades the higher values are, so a tableting grade could sit anywhere in that spread, including near the binder-only break-even of about THB 1,750 per kg. The 1 to 3 times grade premium used in the first version is an ASSUMPTION and is not relied on. Isomalt price: UNVERIFIED (section 2.3).

### 1.1 Thai permission (P, Notification 468 read in this pass)

- Source: Thai original of Notification 468 (B.E. 2568), Annex 1, read from rendered pages of the local copy (839 pages), saved in thai_notification_468_binder_rows_14_1_4.md.
- Mannitol (INS 421), category 14.1.4: quantum satis, condition TH3, year 2566, printed page 417 (PDF page 418). Header functions (printed page 414): anticaking agent, bulking agent, humectant, stabilizer, sweetener, thickener, solvent or carrier.
- Isomalt (INS 953): quantum satis, TH3, 2566, printed page 328. Header (printed page 326): anticaking agent, bulking agent, flavour enhancer, glazing agent, stabilizer, sweetener, thickener.
- Maltodextrin: quantum satis, no condition, 2559, printed page 413. Header (printed page 411): solvent or carrier, emulsifier, stabilizer, thickener, bulking agent.
- Erythritol (INS 968): quantum satis, TH3, 2566, printed page 254; functions flavour enhancer, humectant, sweetener.
- TH3 (Annex 2, printed page 835): for electrolyte (mineral) drink products the additive must be used together with sugar of the type and amount set in the notification on electrolyte drinks. That notification is No. 195 B.E. 2543 (Thai original, FDA compilation copy, rendered pages, saved in thai_notification_195_electrolyte_drink_2543.md). Clause 3 defines an electrolyte drink as a drink with mineral salts as its main ingredient, dry form included. Clause 4 (1) sets, per litre: sodium 460 to 920 mg; glucose or fructose at least 2 percent by weight or sucrose at least 4 percent; potassium not more than 195 mg; bicarbonate not more than 793 mg; citrate not more than 819 mg. Clause 7 puts label statements on such a drink (not for children and infants; for those who lose sweat in exercise; a daily limit).
- Reading for the cube (DERIVED, script block electrolyte_drink_standard_check_notification_195): the made-up cube holds 0 to about 6 mg of sodium per litre from the formula (against at least 460), no sugar of the kind named, and about 959 mg of potassium per litre (against at most 195); its citric acid as added is about 6.4 g per litre (citrate limit 819 mg; the basis of expression is not stated). It cannot meet the electrolyte drink standard, so the recipe must not be named or presented as an electrolyte drink. This agrees with the barred electrolyte, hydration and potassium-benefit wording. TH3 does not apply if the product is a flavoured drink.
- The risk and the setting. The class turns on "mineral salt as the main ingredient". Potassium bicarbonate is the carbonating agent of the cube. The team proposes to present the product as a carbonated flavoured drink, with no mineral, electrolyte or hydration wording (basis: Notification 195 clause 3 and 4 (1) and the composition above; tier DERIVED from P). If a regulator nevertheless classed it as an electrolyte drink, it could not meet clause 4 (1), and TH3 would require sugar to be used with mannitol and isomalt; maltodextrin is the one binder row read with no condition and has no tableting or saccharide evidence read. That classification is part of the Gate 1 opinion and is a stated limit. Whether Notification 195 has been amended after the March 2023 compilation was not searched.
- The Thai FDA clarification of 468 defines bulking agent to include "filler" and stabilizer to include "binder" and "tablet binder" (P, saved earlier). A tableting binder and filler therefore sits in a listed class for mannitol and isomalt. The predecessor Notification 418 rows (rendered pages) give the same level and are corroboration only; its Annexes I and II are listed as cancelled.
- This closes the 468 limit of the first version.

### 1.2 Primary choice: mannitol, tableting grade, 0.80 g per cube

- **Sodium.** Sulfated ash at most 0.1 percent (JECFA monograph, 46th JECFA, P), so at most 0.26 mg per cube if all of it were sodium sulphate (DERIVED upper bound). A supplier tableting-grade sheet was not opened (UNVERIFIED); the JECFA limit is a primary bound.
- **Sugar and excise.** See answer 2. JECFA total sugars at most 1.0 percent as glucose, at most 8 mg per cube, 0.0024 g per 100 mL (DERIVED from P). The excise notice names "Total Sugar" and not polyols (P, saved).
- **Moisture.** The acid and bicarbonate pair is moisture-sensitive. Rosch et al. (peer-reviewed; the first 100,000 of 133,725 characters of the full text were read) report that anhydrous citric acid begins to take up water at about 62 percent relative humidity at 25 C, that a mannitol-based granule mix (with a monosodium-citrate-coated citric acid, not our recipe) showed no mass change up to 70 percent RH and a large uptake from 80 percent, and that mannitol with adipic acid tablets stored 8 weeks at 25 C and 60 percent RH gained 2.0 percent in weight and lost hydrogen yield (97.9 to 87.3 percent). An abstract (Ansar 2011, read by the earlier T11 record, not re-opened here) records a fruit effervescent tablet whose reaction began before dissolution at 85.5 percent RH and whose bicarbonate was lost at 35 C. Moisture protection is therefore a pack and test matter whichever binder is used. Mannitol's loss on drying limit of 0.3 percent is far below isomalt's water limit of 7.0 percent, the only other binder specification read.
- **Tablet strength.** In the same study mannitol gave the weakest tablets of the fillers compared and no friability pass at that setting. The cube is 14 mm with a central dry mass of 3.79 g. Hardness and friability must be tested. This is the main reason for the fallback.
- **Dissolution.** 0.80 g in 330 mL is 2.4 g per litre, about 1.1 percent of the 216 g per litre solubility at 25 C (PubChem, citing a 1992 database; S). JECFA says only "soluble in water". Clarity is expected, not measured. The earlier 0.25 kg per kg figure came from a conference abstract that was not opened and is dropped.
- **Tolerance.** JECFA ADI "not specified". One serve carries 0.80 g, 4 percent of the EU benchmark of 20 g per day from all sources (P, Regulation (EU) 2021/1175, recital 5). EU rules generally do not allow polyols in beverages and require a laxative warning above 10 percent added polyol in the food (Regulation 1169/2011, Annex III, 2.4); the cube as sold is about 21 percent binder by dry mass. These are EU rules, not Thai rules, and no Thai polyol warning rule was searched (limit).
- **Energy.** 0.80 g x 1.6 kcal per gram = 1.3 kcal per serve; total 7.6 kcal with the citric acid, declared as 10 kcal under clause 2.6 (DERIVED from P factors). If the energy is calculated with the sugar-alcohol factors, clause 2.3 also puts a sugar alcohols line (0.80 g) on the panel.

### 1.3 Fallback: isomalt, direct-compression grade, 0.80 g per cube

- Why isomalt: it is permitted on the same terms as mannitol (468, P); its JECFA monograph names bulking agent as a function and gives a lower ash limit (0.13 mg of sodium at most) and a reducing-sugar limit; the maker markets direct-compression grades for tablets and a peer-reviewed abstract (abstract only, S) reports tensile strength rising with compression force in direct-compression isomalt tablets, with lubricants not reducing it; the model actives there were 50 percent ascorbic acid or acetylsalicylic acid, and there was no effervescent (acid plus bicarbonate) system. Among the candidates other than mannitol it is the one with a Thai permission, a specification and tableting evidence together.
- What counts against it: JECFA calls it "slightly hygroscopic" and allows up to 7.0 percent water (our reading: largely the crystal water of the dihydrate component), which is a larger water load beside the bicarbonate than mannitol's 0.3 percent. No effervescent test of isomalt was found; the maker's non-hygroscopic and effervescent statements are COMPANY CLAIM. Its price is UNVERIFIED.
- Proposed switch rule (the team's setting; ASSUMPTION, based on the Gate 5 and Gate 6 tests already in the plan): if mannitol cubes fail the hardness and friability test at the bench trial, trial isomalt; if isomalt cubes lose more than 0.5 percent of the bicarbonate in the pack at 35 C over 8 weeks (the Gate 5 pass test), return to mannitol with a stronger moisture barrier.
- Maltodextrin was not chosen as the fallback: it is permitted and untested for tableting and moisture, and its free glucose and maltose would show as sugars.

### 1.4 What could not be closed for the binder

- **Supplier sheets.** No tableting-grade mannitol or isomalt sheet, and no Thai supplier price, could be obtained within the rules (rule 5 excludes supplier contact). The JECFA limits are the primary bound for sodium and sugars. Closed as a stated limit; the certificate of analysis of the chosen lot is the Gate 6 and Gate 9 item.
- **Hygroscopicity and compression of the chosen lot.** Not measured. Tests: Karl Fischer water of the lot; moisture uptake at 25 C at 60 and 75 percent RH; cube hardness and friability at the chosen compression; 35 C storage for 8 weeks (Gate 5).
- **Isomalt in an effervescent system.** No peer-reviewed test found. Closed as a stated limit.

### 1.5 Lubricant: none in the formula, and no sodium salt allowed

- **The formula has no lubricant.** The five specified components (potassium bicarbonate 0.81 g, citric acid 2.10 g, sucralose 0.033 g, mannitol 0.80 g, lemon flavour 0.05 g) add to 3.793 g, the 3.79 g central dry mass (script block formula_mass_and_lubricant_check, DERIVED). No lubricant, glidant or other excipient is carried in the mass or in the sodium budget.
- **Why this needs saying.** The one effervescent filler paper read (Rosch et al. 2021, Pharmaceuticals 14(12):1327; text read to character 100,000 of 133,725) used sodium stearyl fumarate, a sodium salt, at 0.5 percent in its mannitol, maltose, lactose and dextrates batches, and a monosodium citrate-coated citric acid in the moisture test. Tablet makers usually add a lubricant to prevent sticking to the die. A recipe that says "no added sodium salt" cannot use those.
- **Specification (the team's setting; ASSUMPTION until the supplier declarations are in).** No sodium salt is permitted anywhere in the recipe: not as a lubricant or glidant (sodium stearyl fumarate, sodium lauryl sulfate), not as a preservative (sodium benzoate), not as a buffer or coating (monosodium citrate-coated acid grades), not as a flavour carrier. The same rule reaches the pouch film seal layer (section 2).
- **If the bench trial shows sticking.** Whether a 14 mm cube can be pressed without a lubricant is untested (stated limit). The soluble non-sodium option whose Thai level was read is PEG (INS 1521): at most about 167 mg per cube at the Thai level of 500 mg/kg as ready to drink (P, 468 row; DERIVED arithmetic). JECFA limits its sulfated ash to 0.1 percent (P), so it would add at most 0.054 mg of sodium; JECFA ADI 0 to 10 mg/kg bw (P). That would be a formula change, entered as a new line in the sodium budget (script block peg_lubricant_contingent); it is not in the totals. Adipic acid (Rosch used 10 percent) is not a sodium salt, but its Thai 468 row and its effect on the cube were not read.

### 2.1 Reading the range

- Three high cases are bounds derived from monograph limits (P): citric acid, sucralose, mannitol; together 0.67 mg. Two are planning values (ASSUMPTION): potassium bicarbonate and lemon flavour; together 1.31 mg. The planning values are chosen to show scale, not specifications. The low end (0) assumes no sodium in any input, which the supplier declarations would have to show.
- Sensitivity (DERIVED, section 2.1a): the water is the term that decides the sodium-free verdict when it is counted. With the formula at zero, water alone reaches 5 mg at about 15 mg per litre on the consumed serving and about 12.5 mg per litre on the two-fold reference basis. With the formula at its high case (1.98 mg) the water ceilings for the sodium-free limit are about 9.1 mg per litre (consumed serving, 330 mL) and about 6.5 mg per litre (two-fold reference basis, 400 mL equivalent: formula 2.40 mg, water 0.4 L). If both bases apply the stricter, about 6.5 mg per litre, binds. The WHO typical value of 20 mg per litre is above both.
