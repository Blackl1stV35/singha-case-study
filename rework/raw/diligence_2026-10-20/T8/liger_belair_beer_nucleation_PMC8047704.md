# Liger-Belair and Cilindre 2021: How many CO2 bubbles in a glass of beer? (ACS Omega 6, 9672-9679)

- Date read: 20 October 2026. Route: WebFetch (model-summarised; equations 6 and 7 are images and were not read).
- Tier: S in this record (peer-reviewed, read via a summariser, equations not seen). Status: SOURCED.

## Extracted facts
- Lager 5 % ABV with dissolved CO2 near 5.5 g/L. Henry constant about 1.6 g/L per bar at 20 C and about 2.4 g/L per bar at 6 C; dissolution enthalpy about -20 kJ/mol.
- Surface tension used: 43 mN/m (lager), 47 mN/m (champagne).
- Critical radius for heterogeneous nucleation: about 0.7 micrometre for beer at 6 C with about 5.2 g/L, and about 0.3 micrometre for champagne at about 10 C with about 8 g/L. The critical radius depends on surface tension, Henry constant, ambient pressure and dissolved concentration.
- Nucleation sites are microcrevices in glass and hollow cellulose fibres; the modelling uses site radii between 1 and 10 micrometres. Bubbling becomes thermodynamically impossible once the dissolved CO2 falls to the critical concentration for the available site radius; colder liquid needs a higher critical concentration.
- The paper says almost all of the dissolved CO2 retained in beer must desorb by bubbling and diffusion through the free surface; it gives no numerical split.

## Limits
- No value for a soft drink at 1 g/L. The model uses the physics (critical radius = 2 x surface tension / supersaturation pressure) with the 1 to 10 micrometre site range, as an ASSUMPTION for a drinking glass.
