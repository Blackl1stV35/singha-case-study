# Re-grounding from zero: the case problem, our outcome, and the method (2026-10-17)

Status: evaluation and proposed method, for team decision. The export study is parked as an appendix pending the team's decision.

## 1. The case problem, restated

The competition asks for a food or beverage product that:
1. uses new ingredients or raw materials, and/or production innovation;
2. has never existed before, or has a new form of consumption;
3. creates a change in the food and beverage industry.

Three themes frame the entry: Ingredients and Nutrition; Alternative Formats (new shapes and serving formats that create new experiences); New Trend-Driven Target (responding to consumers and future consumption trends).

The corpus gives the case owner's direction (lines 11 to 12): the solution is a new way of drinking or consuming the product, not a new flavour of the same drink. The corpus's three problem pillars are Active Hydration, Daily Gut Health (a soluble-fibre stick pack) and Mix, Customize, Explore (a Thai botanical and citrus flavour system).

**One-sentence restatement:** propose a new way of drinking or consuming a sparkling or functional drink, with a new ingredient or production route, that a company with carbonation and canning capability could make, and that changes how the category is consumed.

## 2. Scoring rubric

Each concept scores 0 to 2 on each brief criterion (new ingredient or production; new consumption form; industry change), plus a gate: presentable by 21 October (one-page proposition, tested prototype, novelty evidence table, honest claim limits).

## 4. Where the effort went, and what it missed

**Over-invested:**
- Export market-entry gates and the 20 percent contribution test, applied to a product that was not new. The brief does not score export economics.
- Valuation frameworks (sum-of-parts, DCF, WACC) and strategy frameworks (Five Forces, OLI). These serve a corporate investment decision, not a product brief.
- A company-identity question (double-blind candidate or group insider). It was settled late and does not bear on the criteria.
- Parallel screening of many tracks to partial verdicts, instead of narrowing to one candidate early.
- Full logging of company claims that could never be verified.

**Missed:**
- The case owner's direction (new way of drinking). No consumption-format idea was screened to a verdict until today.
- The corpus's most direct new-consumption concepts: Fresh on Demand (separated components mixed at drinking) and Temperature Twist. Neither was taken further.
- The team's own planned tests: two small prototypes judged by blind taste, and the internal capability audit.

**Verdict:** the current outcome is not presentable. It is an export-entry study of a product that is not new, and it scores low on all three criteria. The records also disagree: the malt-fibre verdict conflicts across memory notes, and the market frame must be settled.

## 5. Method for this kind of problem (adapted to our position)

The reviewer's eight-step method assumed internal R&D and plant access. As a double-blind external team we do not have that access. The adapted method uses public evidence, our own bench tests, and labelled assumptions, and it flags the capability gaps for mentors and the plant tour.

| Step | Time box | Work | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Re-ground | 17 October, 2 hours | Restate the problem (section 1). Park the export study. Set the rubric. Name one decision owner | One-page rubric; parked export file |
| 2. Capability sheet | 17 to 18 October | Public evidence per process: carbonation (glass only at Surat Thani, 2015), can filling (unnamed filler), fibre dosing (none found), flavour (TFF/TCFF, unchecked), tea extraction (JV, no site). Mark each as evidenced, assumed or unknown. Ask mentors on the plant tour, if the team reaches it | Capability sheet with evidenced, assumed or unknown per process |
| 3. Diverge | 17 October afternoon | Grid of the three pillars, the stick pack and the format concepts against the three themes. 15 to 20 concept cards, including non-citrus | Concept cards |
| 4. Converge with kill tests | 18 October morning | Brief test (new ingredient or process AND new consumption act). Capability test (the sheet says evidenced or assumed). Novelty test (shelf and online check in Thailand and Singapore, record SKU, date, source). Keep 3 to 5 | Shortlist with a novelty evidence table |
| 5. Bench and blind test | 18 October afternoon to 19 October noon | Home or lab bench tests with cans, a freezer, a pH meter and a sparkling maker. Test each concept's central mechanism (section 6). Blind sensory where possible | Pass or fail sheet per concept |
| 6. Consumer concept test | 19 October afternoon | 30 to 50 respondents in the target segment (working adults 25 to 40). Purchase intent and a format question. In the same block, trace the key trend figures to original sources and strike any that cannot be traced | Concept scores; corrected source table |
| 7. Choose and write | 20 October | One primary concept and one fallback, scored on tested evidence. Two-page proposal: problem, product, new ingredient or process, new consumption act, target, novelty evidence, bench and taste results, consumer results, industry-change statement, claim limits | Proposal; short deck; rubric scores |
| 8. Dry run and submit | 21 October (22 October is the backstop) | Dry run against the rubric and hard questions (what is new, why now, what is proven, what is not). Check every claim against the source table. Submit on 21 October | Submitted registration and idea entry |

## 7. Source and claim hygiene

- Strike any figure that cannot be traced to its original report within 48 hours. The NIQ 77 percent figure carries a tracking tag and must be traced. The Krungsri figure is for ready-to-eat food, not drinks.
- Beer and temperature studies are analogies, not proof. Say so.
- Claim limits: no health or gut wording in Singapore; no functional wording in Korea without an approved raw material.

## 9. Limits

- No web checks were run on novelty. Every "new" is unverified until the shelf and online check is done.
- The capability read is from public sources. The team has no internal access, so capability answers are evidenced or assumed, and must be labelled as such.
- The corpus is China-specific in its operating detail. Singapore and Korea content comes from the team's later work, not the corpus.
