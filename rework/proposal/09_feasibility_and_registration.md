# Feasibility and Thai registration route

Status at 19 October 2026: desk study only, with no bench test, supplier quote or submission. The product is Lime Cube; one cube per pouch is inferred, not confirmed with the organiser.

## Tier key

P: a government or regulator text read in full, a company's own filing or release, official statistics, a peer-reviewed article read in full, or a primary database. S: any other source, including press, trade portals, consultancy pages, company web pages that are not filings, handbooks reproduced online, and search snippets. SOURCED: the page text was read, and the public URL is given. UNVERIFIED: not read, blocked, unreadable, or only a snippet was read. DERIVED: our arithmetic, with the inputs and their tiers shown. PROXY: a stand-in for what is not measured. ASSUMPTION: a value chosen for the model, not sourced.

## Technical feasibility (desk)

The design dose is 0.55 volumes of CO2 per 330 mL serve (ASSUMPTION) at a working pH of 3.5 (ASSUMPTION). Taste is untested. Potassium bicarbonate at 0.81 g releases about 8.1 mmol of CO2 (DERIVED). Citric acid is 0.52 g stoichiometrically but 2.10 g to reach pH 3.5, leaving about 1.58 g free (DERIVED). The acid and CO2 calculations use citric acid, carbonic acid and water pKa values (S, UNVERIFIED: not read).

CO2 solubility at 35 C is 0.592 volumes (S, SOURCED, Lange-attributed; 35 C page basis ambiguous). The 0.55 dose sits below it at every modelled serve from 5 to 35 C (DERIVED). The NIST table (P, SOURCED) has no 35 C row and is not used.

The desk model gives a fizz duration of up to about 96 minutes above a sparkling-sensation threshold, and a mass-transfer time constant of up to about 106 minutes (DERIVED). This is a surface-only upper bound with no nucleation. The 0.44 g/L threshold is S, SOURCED (abstract only) and a PROXY: a mineral-water value applied to a soft drink.

One serve holds about 317 mg of potassium, 9.1% of the Thai reference of 3,500 mg per day (DERIVED from S, SOURCED: a third-party copy of Notification 182 and a consultancy page citing the current Notification 445; primary texts not opened). The EFSA adequate intake for adults is also 3,500 mg per day (P, SOURCED).

Dry-cube shelf life is not tested. The 35 C storage temperature is an ASSUMPTION; Bangkok climate figures are UNVERIFIED (PDF unreadable), so none is used.

Limit: the bench gate that would confirm that 0.55 volumes is reached and kept at serve is not yet defined, so no gate value is stated here.

## Ingredients and supply

Potassium bicarbonate and citric acid are quantum satis in category 14.1.4. Sucralose is limited to 300 mg/kg there, under condition 127 (P, SOURCED). The design uses 33 mg per serve, about 100 mg/kg (DERIVED).

- Potassium bicarbonate (38.1 baht per kg) and citric acid (31.4 baht per kg) are PROXY values from UN Comtrade import unit values (P, SOURCED; 2024 and 2025 data), converted at 33.55 baht per US dollar (P, SOURCED: Bank of Thailand, 15 October 2026). The weights are estimates; the potassium line may mix carbonate and bicarbonate.
- Sucralose at 655 baht per kg is a Thai online asking price with no URL given (UNVERIFIED: not read). Reactants: 0.118 baht per cube (DERIVED).
- Pouch 1.30 (range 0.67 to 2.70), print 1.50 and processing 0.50 are ASSUMPTIONS, or rest on a non-Thai listing (UNVERIFIED: not read). No Thai quote was requested.
- The 12-cube cost includes a carton share of THB 0.42 per cube (THB 5 per 12-cube carton, from a retail box listing used as a PROXY). Six-cube and 24-cube cartons at 4 and 9 baht are ASSUMPTIONS.
- Cost per cube at 0.55 volumes and pH 3.5: THB 3.84 in a 12-cube carton (DERIVED). The ex-factory planning value of 9.00 baht per cube is an ASSUMPTION (60% of an assumed 15-baht shelf price).

About 128,580 cubes fit a 20-foot container in 12-cube cartons, limited by volume (DERIVED from 33.2 cubic metres; S, SOURCED: SeaCube and ONE pages). Carton size and packing are ASSUMPTIONS.

## Competitor overlap

The Singha sparkling water, launched March 2026, is a 330 mL lime can at 15 baht (company release, P, SOURCED; news reprint, S, SOURCED). Flavour overlaps with our product. We differ in form and pack: a dissolvable cube that makes the drink in the glass, one cube per pouch, against the competitor's 330 mL ready-to-drink can. The company describes its can as sugar-free with 0% calories (the company's claim). Our product's sugar-free wording stays barred. The Singha lemon soda is a separate product and not the lime product.

No domestic dissolvable-cube product was found. Overseas analogues are cited as evidence only: coffee cubes and freeze-dried cubes in pouches (both S, SOURCED).

## Thai regulatory route

The limits come from the flavoured-drink rows (category 14.1.4) of Annex 1 to Ministry of Public Health Notification No. 468, amendment No. 4 (P, SOURCED for those rows only). The full annex was not read (UNVERIFIED); the full notification was too large to fetch. A Thai FDA clarification, in force from 12 December 2025, says the 14.1.4 permission also covers sub-categories 14.1.4.1 to 14.1.4.3 (S, SOURCED; read in part, so it is not tiered P).

Condition 127 applies the sucralose limit to the drink as ready to drink, which matches the cube (P, SOURCED). Condition TH3 applies only to electrolyte (mineral) drinks, and the annex extract does not read a flavoured sparkling drink as one. This is a regulatory question, not settled.

A Thai food registration step applies as classified; its step, form, fees and timing are UNVERIFIED. No registration number or outcome exists.

## Labelling and claims

The composition may be stated as fact: potassium bicarbonate, citric acid and sucralose, with no sodium carbonate.

Barred claims: sugar-free, zero-sugar, sodium-free, health, electrolyte, hydration, potassium-benefit and validated-fizz. The 9.1% figure and fizz duration stay internal and off pack.

The Food Act labelling notification is UNVERIFIED (number, date, sweetener and nutrition rules). Label text stays provisional until it is read.

## Classification and excise

Excise item 02.02 covers sweetened or flavoured drinks, including intensively sweetened ones. The 14% ad valorem rate applies if the value base applies; the base was not read (P, SOURCED from the saved copy of the Royal Gazette notice). Per-litre rates depend on sugar per 100 mL: 0 baht up to 6 g, and 1, 3 and 5 baht above (from 1 October 2566; same status). The desk reading assumes no added sugar, which places the cube in the 0-baht band; classification is open.

Item 02.01 (no added sweetener, no flavouring) does not fit. Its rate is 0 baht per litre and 14% ad valorem if the value base applies (same status; the base was not read, so the 14% cannot be converted to baht). Later schedules were not searched, and the Thai numerals were read from page images.

Classification (beverage, edible ice or electrolyte drink) is open and is not settled here; it decides the route, the excise line and whether TH3 applies.
