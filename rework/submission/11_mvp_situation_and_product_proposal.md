# Appendix B: MVP evidence and product case (Singha Lemon Soda and the Cube)

Date: 20 October 2026. Scope: the Singha Lemon Soda product line, consumed from the 330 mL can, and the addition form (the Cube, one dissolvable cube dropped into 330 mL of water). The Cube is shown as "Lemon Soda" in the presentation. No other product is in scope.

Tiers are defined once, in Appendix A.

## 0. Reading of the brief

- The MVP product is Singha Lemon Soda, a ready-to-drink lemon soda in a 330 mL can. We read "addition way of consumption" as the Cube: one cube dropped into 330 mL of water to make the same lemon drink in the glass.
- Only lemon is in scope. Other Singha flavours appear only as options in the proposal, not as a plan.
- Proposing entity: Singha Corporation. Boon Rawd Brewery is the holding company in the company's own description; press and company sources describe the group differently. A trademark aggregator lists Boon Rawd Brewery as owner of 21 marks, 12 of them with SINGHA (S). The Thai owner of the SINGHA mark is not confirmed, so written confirmation or a licence from the mark holder is a condition before pack print (Gate 7).
- Product-level figures for Singha Lemon Soda are mostly launch-era or press figures. No audited product figure is public. Where a figure is missing, section 4 says so. Where a figure is estimated, it is labelled DERIVED, PROXY or ASSUMPTION and is not a Singha figure.

## 2. Situational analysis

### 2.1 Customer base

What is known:

- The company has described its target buyers in three ways over time: healthy refreshment (2020 launch, S), younger and health-conscious buyers (2021 CEO statement, S), and Gen Z (2024 release, S). These are company claims, tagged COMPANY CLAIM.
- The only category profile is a Bangkok study of Gen X and Gen Y carbonated-drink buyers, n = 400, S, 31 August 2025. It gives a modal profile only, a man aged 27 to 44. The sampling method is not stated and no percentages are given.
- Purchase pattern (Kantar, S, 9 April 2024): 90% of urban consumers drink carbonated drinks out of home, more than 22 purchases a year, about THB 19 per trip. Thai press of 11 January 2024 reports the same figures.
- Preference (Mintel, S, 27 December 2023): 37% of Gen Z prefer sweet-flavoured non-alcoholic drinks, against 30% of the total sample; 43% of consumers aged 45 and over prefer low, no or reduced sugar, against 33% of Gen Z.
- Sugar and sweetness (Mintel, S, 16 February 2026): 44% say they strictly try to reduce sugar; 27% are open to sugar or salt alternatives; sweetness preference is 40% full, 36% less sweet, 24% undecided.
- Population context (NSO sample, S): the 2021 Health Behaviour of Population Survey base is 57% female and 53% rural. This is the adult population, not Singha buyers.

What is not known: age, sex, income, generation and purchase channel for Singha Lemon Soda buyers; any generation split of lemon-soda buyers. No Singha buyer data exists, so every customer group in this proposal is a market proxy. These are gaps (section 4).

Persona (proposed, ASSUMPTION): urban adults aged 25 to 44 who buy sparkling soft drinks out of home and want less sugar without losing taste. The proposed lead band, men aged 25 to 34, is a test and not a finding (section 3.1). The only confirmed band is men aged 27 to 44.

### 2.4 What consumers like, and why

- Refreshment is the category reason most often cited (Thai press, 11 January 2024, S; StrategyHelix, 2026, S).
- Taste matters: about two in five consumers name taste as a decision factor for soft drinks (Innova, 31 October 2025, S).
- Cost can be decisive, and soft drinks are price sensitive (Innova, S; Euromonitor summary, November 2025, S).
- Carbonated drinks are bought on impulse and paired with meals (Euromonitor summary, S).
- Sugar: consumers expect taste to match zero sugar (Coca-Cola Thailand, June 2021, S; Mintel, February 2026, S).
- No lemon-specific survey on reasons to choose was found. These are category reasons, not lemon reasons.

## 3. Product proposal

### 3.1 Target customer

- Does the base still hold? Not measurable. No actual volume or buyer data after 2021 is confirmed, so the base trend cannot be shown. This is a limit.
- Segments to convert: people who want less sugar and still want the taste. Mintel (2026, S) puts the pool at about a quarter of its sample (27% open to sugar or salt alternatives; sample not stated). Use 27% as an ASSUMPTION base; the sample is not stated.
- Percentage of base lost: no sourced figure. Cannibalisation of the can is an ASSUMPTION in the value model. The value is zero when about 7.5% of cube buyers would otherwise buy a can, and negative above that. The review level is 7% (DERIVED on assumption inputs): if 7% or more of cube buyers say they would otherwise have bought a can, the value model is rerun before any scale.
- Lead segment: men aged 25 to 34 are the proposed lead, and the lead is a test, not a finding. In a model that compares the customer groups a Singha product would reach, varying every input across its range in 40,000 simulated outcomes (including Bangkok's monthly temperature, 27 to 31 degrees Celsius), men aged 25 to 34 who drink alcohol rank first per person among six drinker groups in 94% of outcomes (87% to 97% by month). Their margin over men aged 35 to 44 is about 3% on purchase intent (about 0.9 points at a 30% baseline) and about 6% after the potassium-caution exclusion. Both ranges include no difference. By total reach no group leads clearly, and the order depends on how the groups are defined. Men aged 45 to 59 are a later volume segment, pended. The confirmed category band is men aged 27 to 44 (Bangkok sample, n = 400, S). The heat effect is an ASSUMPTION, because no Thai temperature-to-demand value was found; it changes the leader only if men aged 35 to 44 respond to heat much more strongly than men aged 25 to 34, which no source shows. The comparison is a model result under stated assumptions, not a consumer finding. Its population counts and age gradient are carried from earlier work and are UNVERIFIED.

### 3.2 Competitive advantage

- Form: a dissolvable cube in a single-serve pouch replaces a 330 mL can. It is lighter and smaller to carry.
- Price per serve: the can reads THB 17.00 as a single can and THB 13.54 to 13.75 a can in 24-can packs (online, 20 October 2026, S), and THB 16 to 17 in the press of 2020 to 2023. The Cube has a planning price of THB 9.00 ex-factory and a THB 15 base shelf price (ASSUMPTION). At the assumed THB 15, the Cube sits above the sugar-free pack prices (THB 12.40 to 14.63 per 330 mL) and below the sugar-free single cans (THB 16.25 and THB 17.00). The proposal presents the Cube as competing on form and on the Singha name, not on price. Any per-serve comparison is assumption-driven.
- Sugar: a sugar-free claim requires less than 0.5 g of sugar per reference serving (Notification 445, P). The Cube's sugar-free status is not yet tested against that threshold, and the Cube carries no sugar-free claim.
- Not confirmed: taste, carbonation and consumer preference against cans. These need a blind test against the can (plan, Gate 4: at least 55% blind preference, an assumption).

### 3.3 Value-chain change

- Cost minimisation: the Cube's cost is dominated by pouch and print (THB 2.80 of THB 4.09 per cube, ASSUMPTION inputs). The plan's figure is not verified against a quote. The reactants line of THB 0.12 per cube covers potassium bicarbonate, citric acid and sucralose (DERIVED); the sucralose price is UNVERIFIED. The THB 4.09 is before binder and flavour. With mannitol at the import-value range of THB 0.08 to 1.27 a cube, the cost is THB 4.17 to 5.36 (DERIVED). The lemon flavour is not priced.
- Asset turnover: the Cube has no asset base yet. It avoids can filling and cold chain, but no co-packer line is verified. Peer asset turnover is in section 2.2 as context only.
- Scale: at 200,000 cubes a year (ASSUMPTION base), fixed cost is THB 1.25 per cube at THB 250,000 and THB 2.50 at THB 500,000 (DERIVED). At 50,000 cubes, THB 5.00 and THB 10.00.
- Carton: THB 0.67 per cube in a six-cube carton at THB 4 (ASSUMPTION), THB 0.42 in a twelve-cube carton at THB 5 (ASSUMPTION, scaled).
- Co-packer scale: a co-packer minimum run of 500,000 to 1,500,000 units per product and formula is the binding constraint; line time is not (section 2.2). One Thai dry-powder maker states a minimum of 500,000 units per SKU (S, company statement). That is 2.5 years of the 200,000-cube base, and 7.5 years at 1,500,000, so the base is below the minimum unless the minimum is negotiated.
- Can scale (desk model, DERIVED on PROXY and ASSUMPTION inputs): the variable cost is about THB 3.3 per can before aluminium (90% range THB 2.6 to 3.9). With THB 300 million of fixed cost a year, the cost per can is THB 3.87 at 500 million cans a year and THB 3.57 at 1,000 million; with THB 600 million of fixed cost it is THB 4.47 and THB 3.87. Aluminium is not included, because no LME settlement was obtained. The earlier variable cost of THB 4.94 a can used a metal input that could not be reproduced, and it is withdrawn.

### 3.4 Regulation and trademark

Regulation (Thai FDA, P):

- Notification 468 on food additives is in force from 12 December 2025, with a clarification signed 6 January 2026. Existing products have until 12 December 2027 to comply. A new launch such as the Cube is not in that grace period (our inference, S).
- Sweeteners are a defined functional class. Notification 450 (19 July 2024) requires the sweetener to be declared by functional class and name.
- Sucralose is listed for flavoured drinks (category 14.1.4) at up to 300 mg/kg (Notification 468, condition 127; P, flavoured-drink rows only). The Cube's level is about 167 mg per kg of drink (DERIVED). Only the flavoured-drink rows were read, so the sub-category rows, the annex edition and the wording of condition 127 are not confirmed. Mannitol (INS 421) is listed for flavoured drinks at quantum satis (Notification 468, P), with condition TH3, which applies only if the product is classed as an electrolyte drink. Citric acid is quantum satis in category 14.1.4.
- Sugar-free: less than 0.5 g sugar per reference serving; sodium-free: less than 5 mg sodium per reference serving (Notification 445, 2023).
- Sodium: the recipe has no sodium salt, so sodium from the recipe is 0 to about 2 mg a serve (DERIVED). The made-up water adds its own sodium: 0 to about 7 mg a serve at the WHO typical level of 20 mg per litre (P), or about 33 to 66 mg from water at the 100 to 200 mg per litre reported for Thai tap water (S, a news report; not measured), which is about 33 to 68 mg in total with the recipe. These are calculations, not measurements. No sodium-free or low-sodium claim is made.
- Mineral-salt beverages (Notification 195, P; read from page images): clause 4(1.3) sets potassium at no more than 195 mg per litre where it is present. The text is unchanged from the Gazette original through the Thai FDA compilation of about 29 March 2023. No later amending notification was found by title on the Thai FDA list to 20 October 2026 (P, negative finding), but an amendment after March 2023 inside a general notification is not excluded. The 2011 and 2020 amendments did not change clauses 3, 4(1) or 7. The clause is a composition standard for the class that clause 3 defines, drinks with mineral salts as the main ingredient. Whether the Cube is in that class is the Gate 1 question; the proposed class is flavoured drink. The Cube holds about 960 mg of potassium per litre, about 4.9 times the ceiling (DERIVED), so if the Gate 1 opinion places the Cube in the class, the formula would have to change.
- Potassium: about 317 mg a serve, 9% of the 3,500 mg Thai reference (Notification 445 Annex 3, P). A medical review is required at Gate 2 before sale. The desk model does not cover people on potassium-retaining medicines or with kidney disease, who are about 1% of men aged 25 to 34 and about 13% of adults aged 25 and over (model bounds 0.1% to 5.5% and 5% to 25%; DERIVED). No clinician has reviewed the serve or the potassium wording.
- Classification: flavoured drink (category 14.1.4) is proposed and is an ASSUMPTION until the written Thai regulatory opinion at Gate 1. It is a supplement only if marketed as one.
- Label: food serial number and net weight are required (Notification 450). No sugar-free, zero-sugar, health, hydration, sodium-free or fizz claim is printed. Potassium appears only where the Thai panel requires it; any warning waits for Gate 2.

Trademark:

- The Department of Intellectual Property register was not reachable on 19 October 2026 (HTTP 403). No Thai status is confirmed for SINGHA LEMON SODA or SINGHA LEMON SODA (CUBE).
- Owner: an aggregator lists Boon Rawd Brewery as owner of 21 marks, 12 of them with SINGHA (S), none labelled for Thailand. The brief says "Singha". The proposer is Singha Corporation. The owner of the Thai SINGHA mark is not confirmed. The SINGHA name and the lion are used in this proposal only; any further use needs written sign-off from the mark holder (Gate 7).
- A US application for SINGHA lemon soda goods (serial 98124128, filed 9 August 2023) was seen in a snippet only (UNVERIFIED).
- Thai trademark term is 10 years, first to file (original 1991 Act, P). The amended text was not read.
- Name: the name Lemon Soda is not cleared and needs a register search before use (Gate 7). The brief uses SINGHA LEMON SODA (CUBE). The company's flavour list includes "Lemon Soda" as a name (company product page), which is close to the product line's own name.

## 5. Recommendation

- Keep the MVP to the lemon can and the Cube, as the brief says.
- Treat men aged 25 to 34 as the proposed lead and test it first. The concept pre-test (M3 to M5) reads the lead under a rule fixed before fieldwork: the lead holds if the lower bound of the 95% interval on their purchase intent over men aged 35 to 44 is above zero, is reversed if the upper bound is below zero, and is undetermined otherwise. The confirmed category band is men aged 27 to 44. Men aged 45 to 59 are a later volume segment, pended.
- Run the pilot as a gated proof programme, not a launch. Route A is an in-store trial in 20 stores (three sampling days a store, 20 samples a day, THB 15 a sample; committed THB 18,000, range THB 12,000 to 30,000 at THB 10 to 25 a sample; the inputs are ASSUMPTIONS), read at weeks 8 and 13: scale if both reads are at or above 3 units per store per week, stop if both are below, otherwise hold. Route B, paid social, starts only if non-paid traffic reaches a 1.0% click-to-first-purchase rate (assumption), and it is capped at THB 27.38 per first purchase at a 30% repeat (THB 24.22 at 15%). Nine creators (four mid-tier and five mega-tier) are on commission-led offers with no flat fee committed; none has been contacted. Scale needs repeat of 15% or more in both cohorts, a contribution of at least THB 3.51 per cube before excise (THB 2.54 after excise), and a cannibalisation share under 7%. The measured decision at M10 to M11 is to scale one route, repeat once in the same stores, or stop.
- Budget (the team's setting; owner: finance lead, by M4): committed THB 24,686 (creator kits and commission cap THB 4,442, Route A THB 18,000, contingency 10%); conditional THB 60,000 for paid social in two tranches (learning THB 20,000, scale THB 40,000); ceiling THB 84,686. Flat creator fees at lower-bound benchmark rates would total about THB 3.49 million for one post each and are not recommended.
- The one ask is the signature of the executive sponsor on the Gate 0 record, which names one owner role for each of the ten gates and the first budget ceiling. Every other setting is the team's, with its basis, owner role and timing. The owner roles are the executive sponsor, the regulatory affairs head, the R&D lead, the operations director, the marketing lead, the finance lead, the sales and channel head, the brand and legal function, and the research lead. Gate owners are roles, not people, and no outside party has been contacted. Sale is blocked until Gates 1, 2, 7 and 9 pass.
- Before any use of the SINGHA name or the lion beyond this proposal, confirm the trademark owner and search the DIP register (Gate 7). The company page is tiered S, and its statements are company claims.
- Treat every unit-economics figure as assumption-driven until co-packer quotes and a store shelf-price check are in hand.

## 6. Entity focus and shareholder value

Entity. The case is framed for Singha Corporation as proposer. Boon Rawd Brewery is the holding company in the company's own description (S, Singha Corporation site); press and company sources describe the group differently. The desk model books the profit in Singha Corporation, and the booking entity is a stated limit. How value passes to the shareholders of Boon Rawd Brewery is shown only as an illustration. If Boon Rawd Brewery held all of Singha Corporation and Singha Corporation paid out all of the profit, Boon Rawd would receive about THB 151,000 a year in years 2 to 5 (about THB 136,000 if only half of each dividend is excluded from its tax); year 1 is a loss year and pays no dividend. Each 10% stake in Boon Rawd would receive about THB 15,000 a year before holder tax if it passes the dividend through. Neither the holding nor the payout was found in the sources read.

Scale. Singha Corporation's 2025 net profit was about THB 3,085 million (MGR Online, 23 May 2026, S; a press report on 2025 results, not checked against a filing). The base-case after-tax NPV of THB 0.17 million is about 0.005% of one year's profit (DERIVED). Boon Rawd Brewery's 2022 net profit was THB 5,793 million (Thaiger, 18 April 2025, S), so against a wider base the shares would be smaller. Incremental profit equal to 0.1% of Singha Corporation's 2025 net profit needs about 1.1 million cubes a year with no cannibalisation (DERIVED). The case is small in the base case and must be judged on volume and on capped fixed cost.

Value drivers (desk model, DERIVED from ASSUMPTION inputs): five years, a 10% discount rate (the Bank of Thailand policy rate of 1.00% from the decision of 26 August 2026, S, plus a 9-point premium; assumption), no terminal value, corporate income tax of 20% (Revenue Department, S), after excise and tax. The base is 200,000 cubes a year and THB 500,000 fixed cost. The setup cost is depreciated over five years; year 0 carries one year of fixed cost and year 1 sells half the base volume (assumptions).

- Base case: the NPV is THB 0.17 million after excise and tax, with a payback of about 3.3 years. Across the stated input ranges (200,000 draws of every input, fixed seed), the chance of a positive NPV is 0.26 after excise and tax (0.40 before excise and tax; 0.58 on a simpler structure). The test fixed in advance needs 0.95, and no basis reaches it. The expected NPV after excise and tax is minus THB 0.88 million, with a 90% range of minus THB 3.40 to plus THB 1.59 million.
- Contribution: THB 3.94 per cube after excise (THB 4.91 before excise, on the planning price of THB 9.00). The contribution excludes binder and flavour, so the base NPV of THB 0.17 million and the 184,325 break-even below also exclude them. At the top of the binder range (THB 1.27 a cube) the contribution falls to about THB 2.67 after excise (DERIVED), close to the after-excise floor of THB 2.54, and the NPV falls to about minus THB 0.51 million (DERIVED; proposal 05). THB 3.94 is an upper bound until binder and flavour are quoted at Gate 5.
- Volume: the NPV is zero at 184,325 cubes a year with no cannibalisation (148,076 before excise, after tax). The operating break-even, fixed cost divided by the contribution after excise, is 127,000 cubes (steady state and undiscounted; not the NPV break-even). The NPV is minus THB 1.41 million at 50,000 cubes, THB 0.17 million at 200,000, THB 3.32 million at 500,000 and THB 8.59 million at 1,000,000. The expected NPV is zero at about 335,000 cubes across the ranges. The earlier break-even of 146,230 cubes a year is on the before-excise, before-tax basis (a contribution of THB 4.91 a cube), where the NPV at 200,000 cubes is THB 0.88 million; that basis is shown for comparison only, and the valuation uses the after-excise, after-tax basis.
- Fixed cost and shelf price: these explain most of the spread. At 200,000 cubes the NPV stays positive up to THB 0.54 million a year of fixed cost after excise and tax (THB 0.68 million before excise and tax).
- Cannibalisation: the NPV is zero when about 7.5% of cube buyers would otherwise buy a can. The review level is 7%.
- Option: the pilot reads demand after 13 weeks and can stop. In the model the demand read is worth THB 0.03 to 0.25 million. Pilot spend is the team's budget in section 5 (committed THB 24,686; ceiling THB 84,686) and is not sized from sources.
- Takeaway: at the base volume the Cube is a learning option, not a value driver. Its value is small on every basis and can be zero or negative.
