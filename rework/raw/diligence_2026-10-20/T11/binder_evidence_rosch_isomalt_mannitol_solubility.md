# Binder and filler evidence: effervescent literature, isomalt sources, mannitol solubility (saved reading, second pass)

Date read: 2026-10-20 for every item. This page replaces, for the purposes of record 74, the mannitol solubility figure of 0.25 kg per kg of water (a conference abstract that was not opened; dropped) and the unopened tolerance statements of the earlier literature page. Our wording is a summary; numbers are as printed.

## 1. Rosch et al. 2021, effervescent filler comparison (re-opened in full text)

- Rosch M, Lucas K, Al-Gousous J, Poeschl U, Langguth P. Formulation and Characterization of an Effervescent Hydrogen-Generating Tablet. Pharmaceuticals 2021;14(12):1327. DOI 10.3390/ph14121327. URL read: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8707073/ (first 100,000 of 133,725 characters read through the fetch tool; the rest was not read). Tier P (peer-reviewed).
- Fillers compared: maltose, mannitol, mannitol with adipic acid, lactose, dextrates (Section 2.2, Table 1). Magnesium plus citric acid effervescent tablet, roller compaction.
- Complete disintegration: mannitol 72 +/- 3 s; mannitol with adipic acid 83 +/- 3 s; maltose 238 +/- 25 s; dextrates 257 +/- 15 s; lactose 300 +/- 32 s.
- Crushing strength and tensile strength (Section 2.3, Table 3; four batches at 40 kN): maltose 121.1 N and 0.963 N/mm2; mannitol 112.0 N and 0.865 N/mm2; lactose 129.3 N and 1.057 N/mm2; dextrates 147.4 N and 1.204 N/mm2. The mannitol with adipic acid batch was compacted at 25 kN to avoid capping (72.3 N and 0.513 N/mm2).
- Friability: only the dextrates batch passed (0.72 percent); one lactose, three mannitol and five maltose tablets broke in the test (Section 2.3.2).
- Moisture (Section 2.4.1 and 3.3), on a granule mixture that is mannitol based and also contains magnesium and a citric acid coated with a monosodium citrate (not our recipe): "no mass changes" above the criterion in the 0 to 70 percent RH stages; a large mass increase from 80 percent RH; the first-cycle mass increase was 56.5 percent. The paper says regular anhydrous citric acid "is known to start sorption of water at 62% P/P0 H2O at 25 C".
- Storage (Section 2.4.2, Table 4): mannitol with adipic acid tablets stored 8 weeks at 25 C and 60 percent RH gained 2.039 +/- 0.161 percent in weight, and hydrogen generation fell from 97.86 +/- 2.98 percent at t0 to 87.33 +/- 1.47 percent.
- Lubricants: sodium stearyl fumarate (a sodium salt) at 0.5 percent in the mannitol, maltose, lactose and dextrates batches; adipic acid at 10 percent in the mannitol with adipic acid batch. The final composition was mannitol 686 mg, adipic acid 150 mg and the reactants in a 1.65 g tablet.
- Reading for the cube (DERIVED): mannitol gave the fastest disintegration but the weakest tablet and no friability pass at the roller-compaction setting used; the 25 C and 60 percent RH result shows the reaction pair loses yield in storage even with mannitol, so moisture protection is a pack and test matter. Dextrates are not a maltodextrin and are not used as evidence for the fallback.

## 2. Mannitol solubility (secondary)

- PubChem compound record for D-mannitol (CID 6251), solubility section, read through the PUG View service: https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/rest/pug_view/data/compound/6251/JSON?heading=Solubility. Values listed: 216 mg/mL at 25 C (citing Yalkowsky and Dannenfelser 1992, through DrugBank and HMDB); "1 g in about 5.5 ml water" (HSDB, citing the Merck Index 1996). Tier S (database that cites older primary sources; the primary was not opened).
- The cube dose is 0.80 g in 330 mL, 2.42 g per litre, about 1.1 percent of 216 g per litre (DERIVED). JECFA says only "soluble in water" (see jecfa_mannitol_isomalt_sucralose_specifications.md).

## 3. Isomalt as the fallback

- JECFA monograph (P): "slightly hygroscopic", bulking agent among its functional uses, sulfated ash not more than 0.05 percent, water not more than 7.0 percent, reducing sugars not more than 0.3 percent, ADI not specified (see the JECFA page).
- Peer-reviewed tableting evidence, not effervescent: "Studies of the properties of tablets from directly compressible isomalt", Ceska a slovenska farmacie 2009;58(4):172-178, authors J. Mu... and V. Pavlasova (the first author's surname is garbled by the site's encoding, probably Muzikova; not confirmed). English abstract read at https://csfarmacie.cz/en/artkey/csf-200904-0006_studies-of-the-properties-of-tablets-from-directly-compressible-isomalt.php. Tier P (abstract read). Two direct-compression isomalt grades (galenIQ 720 and 721) were tested at 6, 8 and 10 kN with magnesium stearate or sodium stearyl fumarate and with 50 percent ascorbic acid or acetylsalicylic acid: tensile strength rose with compression force and lubricants did not reduce it; disintegration was longer with galenIQ 720 and rose with force. No citric acid, bicarbonate or humidity test.
- Maker statements (COMPANY CLAIM, marketing copy, not evidence of performance under the sourcing policy): BENEO press release "Overcoming challenges in effervescent dosage manufacturing", 13 October 2021 (https://beneo.com/?p=22815), and Manufacturing Chemist, 18 October 2021 (https://manufacturingchemist.com/beneo-introduces-filler-binder-for-effervescent-tablets-179965): galenIQ 721 is "non-hygroscopic, water-soluble and directly compressible"; neither gives data or names isomalt in the text read.
- Polyol melting points by DSC from the 2025 melt-granulation paper (Pharmaceutics 2025;17(5):676, PMC12115153, full text read by the earlier T11 literature record): isomalt 165.6 C, mannitol 174.3 C. Context only.
- Not found: any peer-reviewed isomalt test in a citric acid and bicarbonate effervescent system, or an isomalt moisture isotherm. The fallback therefore has permission and specification support (P) and maker claims (COMPANY CLAIM), but no effervescent test of its own.

## 4. Tolerance (what is opened)

- JECFA: ADI "not specified" for mannitol (30th JECFA, 1986) and for isomalt (29th JECFA, 1985), both read in the monographs (P).
- Commission Regulation (EU) 2021/1175 recital 5: polyols unlikely to cause laxative symptoms below 20 g per day from all sources in solid foods (P, read earlier; EU rule, context only). The cube dose of 0.80 g is 4 percent of that figure (DERIVED).
- The abstracts of Storey 2007 (erythritol), Koutsou 1996 (isomalt in chocolate) and the mannitol bowel-preparation trials were read by the earlier T11 record only as PubMed or Consensus abstracts and were not re-opened in this pass. They are not used for any figure in record 74.
