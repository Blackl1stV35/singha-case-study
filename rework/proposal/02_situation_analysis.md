## What the situation means for the cube

1. **The problem is form, not flavour.** The company already sells a lemon soda can on the same flavour (S7; online listings of 20 October 2026, S32), so the cube must win on form and pack: a dissolvable Lemon Soda cube that makes a carbonated drink from water. Fizz and taste are untested.
2. **Price is an assumption.** The planning shelf price is THB 15 a serve of 330 ml (ASSUMPTION), and the planning price to the producer is THB 9.00, which is 60% of THB 15 (ASSUMPTION). Against the online listings read on 20 October 2026 (S), THB 15 sits above the sugar-free 24-can pack prices of THB 12.40 to 14.63 per 330 ml and below the sugar-free single cans at THB 16.25 and THB 17.00. THB 15 also sits above the Singha Lemon Soda can's pack price of THB 13.54 to 13.75 a can and below its single-can price of THB 17.00. The proposal presents Lemon Soda as competing on form and on the Singha name, not on price. No store shelf price was read.
3. **Cost per cube is 4.09 THB** in the recommended 6-cube carton at pH 3.5, before the binder and the lemon flavour (DERIVED); 3.84 THB in the 12-cube carton, the larger option, on the same basis. Reactants (0.118, shown as 0.12 on the slide) are DERIVED from UN Comtrade unit values (S26; PROXY), with an UNVERIFIED sucralose price. Pouch 1.30 and print 1.50 (pouch and print 2.80 together) and processing 0.50 are ASSUMPTIONS; no Thai quote was requested. The binder is 0.80 g of mannitol, tableting grade, priced only as an import-value proxy of THB 0.08 to 1.27 per cube (S, PROXY), which takes the 6-cube cost to THB 4.17 to 5.36 (DERIVED). The 0.05 g lemon flavour is not priced. The 6-cube cost includes a carton share of THB 0.67 per cube (THB 4 per 6-cube carton, an ASSUMPTION scaled from the 12-cube price). The 12-cube cost includes a carton share of THB 0.42 per cube (THB 5 per 12-cube carton, from a retail box listing used as a PROXY). A 20 ft container holds about 21,386 cartons and 128,316 cubes in 6-cube cartons (a volume estimate at 92% of ONE's internal volume; DERIVED; S24; packing is an ASSUMPTION), with a payload of 28.33 t (S24).
4. **The pouch base is an ASSUMPTION.** No Thai pouch price was found. The only evidence is an overseas listing of USD 0.03 to 0.05 per piece (S14; UNVERIFIED), which at 33.55 THB per USD (S13; P, SOURCED; DERIVED mid-rate of 15 October 2026) is 1.01 to 1.68 THB. Across a 0.67 to 2.70 THB band (the top is an ASSUMPTION), the cube costs 3.46 to 5.49 THB in the 6-cube carton, or 3.21 to 5.24 THB in the 12-cube carton (DERIVED), before the binder proxy (THB 0.08 to 1.27 per cube) and the lemon flavour, which is not priced. One cube per single-serve pouch and a six-cube carton (190 x 150 x 50 mm, ASSUMPTION) are the team's setting for the sold unit; the owner is the marketing lead, with finance, by M2.
5. **Excise classification is open.** Item 02.02 covers sweetened or flavoured drinks, with or without added sugar (S12). The desk reading puts the cube in the 0 THB per litre band, but the ad valorem base was not read. The team's setting for the product class is flavoured drink (category 14.1.4). The Gate 1 written Thai regulatory opinion confirms the class and the sweetener, and tests the mineral-salt rule in point 6 (owner: regulatory affairs head, by M1). The excise charge in the value case is therefore an assumption.
6. **Acid, potassium and class at 0.55 volume.** The desk model gives a pH of about 3.4 to 3.5, with 3.5 as the working anchor (ASSUMPTION); taste is untested. Per 330 ml: potassium bicarbonate 0.81 g; citric acid 0.52 g at stoichiometric balance or 2.10 g to reach pH 3.5 (DERIVED; acid constants S, UNVERIFIED, S22). Potassium is about 317 mg a serve, about 9% of the Thai reference of 3,500 mg per day (DERIVED), and about 960 mg per litre (DERIVED). The reference is Notification 445, Annex 3, in the English translation published by the Thai FDA (P; S33; the Thai Gazette text was not opened); the earlier third-party copies are S20 (S). A Thai notification for mineral-salt beverages (No. 195, clause 4(1.3)) sets potassium at no more than 195 mg per litre where it is present. The text of the clause is unchanged from the Gazette original through the Thai FDA compilation of about 29 March 2023 (P; read from page images; S34). No later amending notification was found by title on the Thai FDA list to 20 October 2026 (P, negative finding), but an amendment after March 2023 inside a general notification is not excluded. The 2011 and 2020 amendments did not change clauses 3, 4(1) or 7. Whether the cube is a mineral-salt beverage under clause 3 is the Gate 1 question. The proposed class is flavoured drink. If the Gate 1 opinion classes the cube as a mineral-salt beverage, the potassium would be about 4.9 times the ceiling (DERIVED) and the formula would have to change. A medical review of the potassium is required at Gate 2 before sale. Composition (basis B): potassium bicarbonate, citric acid, sucralose, mannitol as binder and filler (tableting grade, INS 421; 0.80 g, ASSUMPTION; fallback isomalt) and natural lemon flavour (0.05 g, ASSUMPTION; carrier not yet specified). The recipe has no sodium salt and no sodium carbonate. Sodium from the recipe is 0 to about 2 mg a serve (DERIVED; an ASSUMPTION until supplier declarations are in hand). Water adds 0 to about 7 mg a serve at the WHO typical level of 20 mg per litre (P; S40), or about 33 to 68 mg at the 100 to 200 mg per litre reported for Thai tap water (S, a news report; not measured; S40). No sodium-free or low-sodium claim is made. The binder and flavour amounts are assumptions until a cube is weighed.
7. **Dose, solubility, dissolution and fizz.** The dose is 0.55 volume of CO2 per 330 ml, the carbon dioxide the reaction releases at 0 °C; how much stays dissolved at serve is not measured. Solubility at 1 atm is 0.655 volume at 30 °C and 0.592 at 35 °C (S15, S16; S, SOURCED), so the dose sits within solubility at every modelled serve from 5 to 35 °C (DERIVED). That range is a modelling range; Bangkok climate figures are UNVERIFIED. NIST rows for 0 to 25 °C differ from Lange by -2% to +11% (S17). A desk model, with no bench test and no tasting, gives the following (DERIVED; ASSUMPTION inputs). At 25 °C the cube dissolves in about 6 minutes stirred and about 11 minutes not stirred for a central set of assumed inputs, and in 3 to 22 minutes stirred and 5 to 74 minutes not stirred across the assumed ranges. At 20 °C, surface loss alone keeps the dissolved carbon dioxide above the 0.44 g/L sparkling threshold (S27; abstract only; PROXY for a soft drink) for about 1.7 to 3.4 hours if the full dose dissolves at serve. Bubble formation could cut this to about 15 minutes (champagne proxy). If less than 41% of the dose is dissolved at serve, the drink starts below the threshold. At Gate 3 (section 10) the R&D lead restates the pass rule for the 0.55 volume dose before the bench run, and bench cubes are made by M2.
8. **Sweetener and permissions.** Sucralose is 55 mg a serve of 330 ml, about 167 mg per kg of drink (DERIVED), set to give a sweetness equal to about 10 g of sugar per 100 ml (ASSUMPTION for the sweetness target). The Thai level for sucralose in category 14.1.4 is 300 mg/kg under condition 127, which the annex applies to the drink ready to drink (S18; P, SOURCED; flavoured-drink rows read, not the full annex). Bicarbonate and citric acid are quantum satis (S18). Mannitol (INS 421) is listed for flavoured drinks at quantum satis in Notification 468 (S18; P, flavoured-drink rows only), with condition TH3, which applies only if the product is classed as an electrolyte drink; the product makes no electrolyte claim. Isomalt is the fallback binder. The natural lemon flavour has not been checked against these rules. The FDA clarification extends major-category permissions to sub-categories; its sucralose row was not read (S19). The Gate 1 written Thai regulatory opinion covers the class, condition 127, the annex edition and the sucralose level before sale (owner: regulatory affairs head, by M1).
9. **Demand is untested.** A purchase-intent model, not a consumer finding, ranks men aged 25 to 34 who drink alcohol first among six drinker groups in about 94% of 40,000 simulated outcomes (87% to 97% by month), supporting direction only (DERIVED; ASSUMPTION inputs). Their margin over men aged 35 to 44 is about 3% on purchase intent (about 0.9 points at a 30% baseline) and about 6% after the potassium-caution exclusion; both ranges include no difference, so the size of the gap is not supported. No Singha buyer data exists, so every group is a market proxy.
10. **Brand and sign-off.** The product is the Singha Lemon Soda Cube, shown as Lemon Soda. The SINGHA name and the lion emblem are authorised for this proposal only. Any use beyond it needs written sign-off from the mark holder at Gate 7, where the brand and legal function is the owner. That sign-off is not held, the alcohol-cue ruling is outstanding, and the cube name needs a register search before use. The Thai owner of the SINGHA mark is not confirmed: a trademark aggregator lists Boon Rawd Brewery as owner of 21 marks, 12 with SINGHA, none labelled Thailand, and the Thai register was not reached (S; S39).
