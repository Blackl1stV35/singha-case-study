# Polyol tolerance, laxative thresholds and the PEG acceptable intake (read 20 October 2026)

Tier P (peer-reviewed or regulator opinion) unless marked S. Read as abstracts through PubMed (get_article_metadata) and Consensus; none opened in full unless stated.

## Mannitol

- Spada C et al. Oral mannitol for bowel preparation: a dose-finding phase II study. Eur J Clin Pharmacol 2022;78(12):1991-2002. DOI 10.1007/s00228-022-03405-z. PMID 36287232, PMC9649514. Tested 50, 100 and 150 g mannitol as a bowel-cleansing laxative; 100 g was the best dose. (Lowest dose tested: 50 g.)
- Fiori G et al. Pharmacokinetics of oral mannitol for bowel preparation for colonoscopy. Clin Transl Sci 2022;15(10):2448-2457. DOI 10.1111/cts.13373. PMID 37074807. Oral bioavailability just over 20 percent at 50, 100 and 150 g.
- Vecchi M et al. Mannitol for bowel preparation (SATISFACTION trial). Dig Liver Dis 2024. Single-dose 100 g in 750 mL compared with a standard preparation (abstract read via Consensus).
- Ajaj W et al. Oral contrast agents for small bowel MRI. Eur Radiol 2004;14(3):458-64 (online 22 November 2003). DOI 10.1007/s00330-003-2177-0. PMID 14634782. Compared 2.5 percent mannitol (2.5 g per 100 mL) and 1.5 to 2.5 percent sorbitol solutions in 12 volunteers; nausea, vomiting, flatulence and diarrhoea were recorded for each solution and the lowest side effect rate was with sorbitol at 2.0 and 1.5 percent (abstract read; the mannitol rates were not read). Used only for scale: the cube drink holds 0.24 g per 100 mL.
- Encyclopaedia (S): mannitol is an osmotic laxative in oral doses larger than 20 g.

## Erythritol

- Storey D et al. Gastrointestinal tolerance of erythritol and xylitol ingested in a liquid. Eur J Clin Nutr 2007;61(3):349-54. DOI 10.1038/sj.ejcn.1602532. PMID 16988647. 20 and 35 g erythritol in water were tolerated without symptoms; 50 g gave nausea and borborygmi.
- Jacqz-Aigrain E et al. Gastrointestinal tolerance of erythritol-containing beverage in young children. Eur J Clin Nutr 2015;69(6):746-51. DOI 10.1038/ejcn.2015.4. PMID 25744158. No observed effect level for diarrhoea or severe symptoms: 15 g (0.73 g/kg bw) in 250 mL.
- EFSA. Re-evaluation of erythritol (E 968) as a food additive. EFSA J 2023;21(12):e8430. DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2023.8430. PMID 38125972, PMC10731997. Numerical ADI 0.5 g/kg bw per day (the lower bound of the NOAEL range for diarrhoea); exposure estimates for high consumers above the ADI.

## Isomalt

- Koutsou GA et al. Dose-related gastrointestinal response to the ingestion of either isomalt, lactitol or maltitol in milk chocolate. Eur J Clin Nutr 1996;50(1):17-21. PMID 8617186. 40 g isomalt in 100 g chocolate gave mild laxation (P<0.01); 30 g gave only mild borborygmi, flatulence, colic and laxation (P<0.05).
- Encyclopaedia (S): isomalt risks intestinal distress above about 20 to 30 g per day.

## Polyethylene glycol (PEG 6000 as a dry binder or lubricant)

- EFSA. Refined exposure assessment of polyethylene glycol (E 1521) from its use as a food additive. EFSA J 2018. Abstract read via Consensus. Group ADI 10 mg/kg bw per day for PEG 200 to 10,000 (JECFA, 1980) and 5 mg/kg bw per day for PEG 300 to 4,000 (SCF, 1997).
- Thai limit for PEG (INS 1521) in category 14.1.4: 500 mg/kg as ready to drink, condition 127 (Notification 418 Annex 1, printed page 389; see thai_food_additive_rows_binder_candidates.md).

## Sodium-containing excipients (to avoid)

- Sodium stearyl fumarate, a "water-soluble lubricant" used in the effervescent papers (see effervescent_formulation_literature.md), and monosodium citrate-coated citric acid, are sodium salts and are excluded by the no sodium salt rule.
