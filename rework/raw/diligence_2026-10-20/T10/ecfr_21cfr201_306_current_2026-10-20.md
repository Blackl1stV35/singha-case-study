## What the fetch returned
- Heading: "§ 201.306 Potassium salt preparations intended for oral ingestion by man."
- Paragraph (a), in the tool's summary: no regulatory action against continued marketing of coated potassium-salt tablets that supply 100 mg or more of potassium per tablet, if stated conditions are met (prescription-caution labelling, full-disclosure labelling with the small-bowel warning, and an application).
- Paragraph (b), in the tool's summary: regulatory proceedings may begin against uncoated potassium-salt tablets of 100 mg or more of potassium per tablet and liquid preparations of 20 mg or more of potassium per mL, unless the labelling carries the prescription statement and advises dissolving tablets and diluting liquids adequately.
- Source note, quoted by the tool: "[40 FR 13998, Mar. 27, 1975, as amended at 55 FR 11576, Mar. 29, 1990; 67 FR 4906, Feb. 1, 2002]". The page showed no effective date of its own.
- Version records, as listed by the tool: two records, both with amendment_date 2016-11-29, issue dates 2016-12-31 (substantive: true) and 2023-03-30 (substantive: false); neither removed. These are the eCFR's own version dates for the section and are not explained in the section's source note.

## What this settles
- The 100 mg per tablet or unit line and the 20 mg per mL line stand in the current text as they do in the 2014 print. The section's own amendment note ends in 2002. The earlier limit "later amendments not checked; eCFR blocked" is closed.
- The text has no 99 mg figure and does not mention foods, drinks or dietary supplements.
- Not settled: whether a dissolvable food cube is within "tablets" or "capsule or coated tablet ... intended for oral ingestion without prior dilution". That is a question for a US regulatory reader.
