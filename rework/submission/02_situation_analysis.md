## Competitive situation

- **The overlap is flavour.** The Singha Lemon Soda can is listed on the company product page (S7; S, SOURCED), which describes it as 0% sugar and no calories and names sucralose and acesulfame potassium as sweeteners (COMPANY CLAIM). It is on sale online: a single can at THB 17.00 at Lotus's Shop Online, and 24-can packs at THB 13.54 to 13.75 a can (pack prices of THB 325 at Makro PRO and THB 330 at 7-Eleven ALL Online and Lotus's, SOURCED; per-can figures DERIVED), all read online on 20 October 2026 (S; S38). Across six retail rows the band is THB 13.54 to 17.00 with a median of THB 13.75 (DERIVED). No store shelf price was read.
- **Other sugar-free lemon and lime cans, read online on 20 October 2026 at named sellers (S; S39).** 7UP No Sugar lemon soda THB 12.40 to 16.25 per 330 mL; Sprite No Sugar THB 14.13 to 19.29 per 330 mL across four listings; Schweppes lime soda with no sugar THB 13.92 to 16.33 per 330 mL across four listings. In 24-can packs the sugar-free lemon and lime cans read THB 12.40 to 14.63 per 330 mL. Per 330 mL figures are DERIVED from pack prices. Regular Sprite (not sugar-free) is THB 16.00 a can at one retailer.
- **Form and pack differ.** Our product is a dissolvable Lemon Soda cube that makes the drink in the glass, one cube per pouch. The company's lemon soda is a ready-to-drink can. Lemon Soda is a desk model: no sale, pilot or blind test has run.
- **No dissolvable drink cube was found on sale in Thailand.** Drink cubes sell overseas: waterdrop in the UK and the US, and Ricola Drink Cubes, sold through waterdrop's EU store (P for the company release; S for the store pages; S40). None was found in searches of Thai retail listings read on 20 October 2026. The claim is "not found in desk searches", not "none exists".
- **The can is the nearest substitute.** The company's lemon soda can is the taste reference for the blind test (Gate 4). The modelled value of the cube is zero when about 7.5% of cube buyers would otherwise buy a can, and negative above that (DERIVED on ASSUMPTION inputs). The review level is 7%: if 7% or more of cube buyers say so, the value model is rerun before any scale.

## What the situation means for the cube

1. **The problem is form, not flavour.** The company already sells a lemon soda can on the same flavour (S7; online listings of 20 October 2026, S38), so the cube must win on form and pack: a dissolvable Lemon Soda cube that makes a carbonated drink from water. Fizz and taste are untested.
2. **Price is an assumption.** The planning shelf price is THB 15 for one 330 ml serve, about THB 45 per litre (ASSUMPTION; per litre DERIVED). At THB 15, Lemon Soda sits above the sugar-free pack prices (THB 12.40 to 14.63 per 330 mL) and below the sugar-free single cans (THB 16.25 and THB 17.00). The proposal presents Lemon Soda as competing on form and on the Singha name, not on price. The price chain at THB 15 is an invoice of THB 9.34 after VAT and margins, excise of THB 1.31 and a net of THB 8.03, which leaves a contribution of THB 3.94 a cube after the cost of THB 4.09 (DERIVED on PROXY and ASSUMPTION inputs). Before excise, at a planning price of THB 9.00 (60% of THB 15, ASSUMPTION), the contribution is THB 4.91. The two bases are shown separately, and the valuation uses THB 3.94. The 21.6% retailer margin is a group all-category proxy (PROXY). CP ALL's 2025 group gross margin is 22.7% and its net margin is 2.8% (P, DERIVED for the net margin; group figures that cover all categories; S46). No sourced Thai distributor margin was found. The closest sourced value is the National Statistical Office's 2023 wholesale value-added share for non-alcoholic beverages, 16.2% (P, PROXY; a value-added measure, not a margin; S45). The 10% to 20% distributor band used in the model is an ASSUMPTION.
3. **Cost per cube is 4.09 THB** in the recommended 6-cube carton at pH 3.5, before the binder and the lemon flavour (DERIVED); 3.84 THB in the 12-cube carton, the larger option, on the same basis. Reactants (0.118) are DERIVED from UN Comtrade unit values (S26; PROXY), with an UNVERIFIED sucralose price. Pouch 1.30, print 1.50 and processing 0.50 are ASSUMPTIONS; no Thai quote was requested. The binder is 0.80 g of mannitol, tableting grade (INS 421), with isomalt as the fallback. Its price is an import-value proxy of THB 0.08 to 1.27 per cube (S, PROXY; S53), which takes the 6-cube cost to THB 4.17 to 5.36 (DERIVED). The lemon flavour (0.05 g) is not priced. The 6-cube cost includes a carton share of THB 0.67 per cube (THB 4 per 6-cube carton, an ASSUMPTION scaled from the 12-cube price). The 12-cube cost includes a carton share of THB 0.42 per cube (THB 5 per 12-cube carton, from a retail box listing used as a PROXY; S21). A 20 ft container holds about 21,386 cartons of six, which is 128,316 cubes (a volume estimate at 92% of ONE's internal volume; DERIVED; S24, with S25 giving a count within 0.3%; the carton size and packing are ASSUMPTIONS), and its payload is 28.33 t (S24). The 12-cube carton (190 x 150 x 100 mm, an ASSUMPTION) gives 10,693 cartons and the same 128,316 cubes (DERIVED).
4. **The pouch base is an ASSUMPTION.** No Thai pouch price was found. The only evidence is an overseas listing of USD 0.03 to 0.05 per piece (S14; UNVERIFIED), which at 33.55 THB per USD is 1.01 to 1.68 THB (DERIVED; the rate is the midpoint of the Bank of Thailand buying sight and selling rates of 15 October 2026, S13; the published Bank of Thailand reference rate of 33.5410, P, gives the same THB 1.01 to 1.68). Across a 0.67 to 2.70 THB band (the top is an ASSUMPTION), the cube costs 3.46 to 5.49 THB in the 6-cube carton, or 3.21 to 5.24 THB in the 12-cube carton (DERIVED), before the binder and the lemon flavour. One cube per single-serve pouch is the team's setting for the sold unit (owner: marketing lead, with finance, by M2).
5. **Excise classification is open.** Item 02.02 covers sweetened or flavoured drinks, with or without added sugar (S12). The specific rate is 0 THB per litre up to 6 g of sugar per 100 ml, and the cube contains no sugar. The 14% ad valorem rate applies if the value base applies, and that base was not read. The valuation therefore charges excise on the ad valorem basis as an ASSUMPTION, THB 1.31 a cube on an invoice of THB 9.34 (DERIVED), and shows the before-excise case separately. The written Thai regulatory opinion at Gate 1 confirms the class and the excise tier (owner: regulatory affairs head, by M1).
6. **Acid, potassium and sodium at 0.55 volume.** The desk model gives a pH of about 3.4 to 3.5; the working pH of 3.5 is an ASSUMPTION, and taste is untested. Per 330 ml: potassium bicarbonate 0.81 g; citric acid 0.52 g neutralises the bicarbonate and 2.10 g sets the working pH of about 3.5 (DERIVED; acid constants S, UNVERIFIED, S22). Potassium is about 317 mg a serve, which is 9% of the Thai reference of 3,500 mg a day (P; DERIVED share; S47; secondary copies S20), and about 960 mg per litre (DERIVED). The desk model does not cover people on potassium-retaining medicines or with kidney disease, so a medical review is required at Gate 2 before sale. A Thai notification for mineral-salt beverages (No. 195, clause 4(1.3)) sets potassium at no more than 195 mg per litre where it is present. The text is unchanged from the Gazette original through the Thai FDA compilation of about 29 March 2023 (P; read from page images; S48). No later amending notification was found by title on the Thai FDA list to 20 October 2026 (P, negative finding), but a post-March-2023 amendment inside a general notification is not excluded. The 2011 and 2020 amendments did not change clauses 3, 4(1) or 7. Whether the cube is a mineral-salt beverage under clause 3 is the Gate 1 question; the proposed class is flavoured drink. If the Gate 1 opinion classes the cube as a mineral-salt beverage, its potassium would be about 4.9 times the ceiling and the formula would have to change. Composition: potassium bicarbonate, citric acid, sucralose, mannitol as binder and filler (0.80 g, ASSUMPTION) and natural lemon flavour (0.05 g, ASSUMPTION); no sodium salt and no sodium carbonate. The binder and flavour amounts are assumptions until a cube is weighed. Sodium from the recipe is 0 to about 2 mg a serve (DERIVED). Water adds its own sodium: 0 to about 7 mg a serve at the WHO typical level of 20 mg per litre (P; S50), or about 33 to 68 mg in total at the 100 to 200 mg per litre reported for Thai tap water (S, a news report; not measured; S51). No sodium-free or low-sodium claim is made.
7. **Dose and solubility.** The dose is 0.55 volume of CO2 per 330 ml, the carbon dioxide the reaction releases at 0 °C; how much stays dissolved at serve is not measured. Solubility at 1 atm is 0.655 volume at 30 °C and 0.592 at 35 °C (S15, S16; S, SOURCED), so the dose sits within solubility at every modelled serve from 5 to 35 °C (DERIVED). That range is a modelling range; Bangkok monthly mean temperatures run from 27.3 to 30.7 °C (Thai Meteorological Department climate normals 1991 to 2020, Bangkok Metropolis station; P; S49). NIST rows for 0 to 25 °C differ from Lange by -2% to +11% (S17). The rest is a desk model with no bench test. Dissolution at 25 °C takes about 6 minutes stirred and about 11 minutes not stirred for a central set of assumed inputs, and 3 to 22 and 5 to 74 minutes across the assumed ranges; this holds only if the cube breaks into fine pieces. Surface loss alone keeps the dissolved carbon dioxide above the 0.44 g/L sparkling threshold (S27; abstract only; PROXY for a soft drink) for about 1.7 to 3.4 hours at 20 °C if the full dose dissolves at serve (DERIVED). Bubble formation could cut this to about 15 minutes (champagne proxy; S52). If less than 41% of the dose is dissolved at serve, the drink starts below the threshold. The R&D lead restates the pass rule for the 0.55 volume dose before the bench run (Gate 3, by M1).
8. **Sweetener and permissions.** Sucralose is 55 mg a serve, about 167 mg per kg of drink (DERIVED), equal in sweetness to about 10 g of sugar per 100 mL (ASSUMPTION for the sweetness target). The Thai level for sucralose in category 14.1.4 is 300 mg/kg for flavoured drinks under condition 127, which the flavoured-drink rows read as applying to the drink ready to drink, and the condition 127 wording is a Gate 1 item (S18; P, SOURCED; flavoured-drink rows read, not the full annex or other sub-category rows). Bicarbonate and citric acid are quantum satis (S18). Mannitol (INS 421) is listed for flavoured drinks at quantum satis (S18; P), with isomalt as the fallback on the same permission. Condition TH3 applies only if the product is classed as an electrolyte drink, and the product makes no electrolyte claim. The carrier of the lemon flavour is not yet specified, and the flavour is not yet checked against these rules. The FDA clarification extends major-category permissions to sub-categories; its sucralose row was not read (S19). The Gate 1 written Thai opinion confirms the class (flavoured drink, category 14.1.4, is the team's setting), condition 127, the annex edition and the sucralose level (owner: regulatory affairs head, by M1).
9. **Demand is untested.** A purchase-intent model, not a consumer finding, compares six groups of drinkers per person within each age and sex group and varies every input across its range in 40,000 simulated outcomes, including Bangkok's monthly temperature (27 to 31 °C; the heat effect is an ASSUMPTION). Men aged 25 to 34 who drink alcohol rank first in 94% of simulated outcomes (87% to 97% by month). Their margin over men aged 35 to 44 is about 3% on purchase intent (about 0.9 points at a 30% baseline) and about 6% after the potassium-caution exclusion, and both ranges include no difference. By total reach no group leads clearly, and men aged 45 to 59 are a later volume segment, pended. No Singha buyer data exists, so every group is a market proxy. The result supports direction only (DERIVED; ASSUMPTION inputs).
10. **Brand and sign-off.** The product is shown as Lemon Soda, and the name needs a register search before use (Gate 7). The lion emblem and the SINGHA name are used for this proposal only. Any use beyond it needs written sign-off from the mark holder and an alcohol-cue ruling at Gate 7 (owner: brand and legal function, by M2); that sign-off is not held. Which entity owns the SINGHA mark in Thailand is unconfirmed, because the trademark register was not reached. Until a written ruling, no glassware, lemon imagery, bubbles or alcohol cues are used.
