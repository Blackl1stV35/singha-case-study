## Clause 3 (definition)

Thai: "เครื่องดื่มเกลือแร่ หมายความว่า เครื่องดื่มที่มีเกลือแร่เป็นส่วนประกอบหลัก และหมายความรวมถึงเครื่องดื่มเกลือแร่ชนิดแห้งด้วย"

Our translation: an electrolyte (mineral salt) drink means a drink that has mineral salts as its main ingredient, and includes the dry form of an electrolyte drink.

## Clause 4 (1) (quality or standard, per litre of electrolyte drink)

- (1.1) sodium not less than 460 mg and not more than 920 mg.
- (1.2) sugar: glucose or fructose not less than 2 percent by weight, or sucrose not less than 4 percent by weight.
- (1.3) potassium not more than 195 mg (if present).
- (1.4) bicarbonate not more than 793 mg (if present).
- (1.5) citrate not more than 819 mg (if present).
- The paragraph after (1.5) says a drink must have the ingredients of (1.1) and (1.2); other mineral salts than (1.3) and (1.4), or other sugars, may be used in amounts the Food and Drug Administration (food committee) has approved.
- Later clauses: flavour and taste specific to the drink; no sediment; water for production must meet the sealed-container drinking water standard; microbiological limits; no alcohol (with a carrier exception); no caffeine; dry-form drinks have moisture not over 5 percent by weight.

## Clause 7 (label statements for an electrolyte drink)

Statements in letters not less than 2 mm high in a red-bordered rectangle on a white ground: children and infants should not drink it (2.1); only for those who have lost sweat through exercise (2.2); not to be drunk more than a stated number of units a day, the total not over 1 litre a day (2.3).

## Footnotes on page 2 (amendments)

Clause 4 (5) (ก), (6) and (7) and the final paragraph were replaced by later notifications (Nos. 413, 415 and 417 B.E. 2563, and No. 2 B.E. 2554). The footnotes mention no change to clause 4 (1). Whether any later notification changed clause 3 or clause 4 (1) after the March 2023 compilation was not searched (limit).

## What this settles for record 74

- Condition TH3 of Notification 468 refers to "the notification on electrolyte drinks", which is this notification. TH3 requires the additive to be used together with sugar of the kind and amount in clause 4 (1.2): glucose or fructose at least 2 percent or sucrose at least 4 percent.
- The Lemon Soda Cube made up in 330 mL has sodium of about 0 to 6 mg per litre from the formula (script), against 460 to 920 mg per litre required; no glucose, fructose or sucrose; potassium about 959 mg per litre against not more than 195 (the figures are in rework/screen/t11_sodium_results.json, block electrolyte_drink_standard_check_notification_195). It cannot meet the standard of an electrolyte drink and must not be named or presented as one.
- Risk: the clause 3 definition turns on "mineral salt as the main ingredient". Potassium bicarbonate is the carbonating agent of the cube, not a mineral supply. If a regulator read it as the main mineral salt, TH3 would apply and mannitol and isomalt would need sugar; the only binder row read without a condition would then be maltodextrin (no tableting or saccharide evidence read). The classification is part of the Gate 1 opinion.
