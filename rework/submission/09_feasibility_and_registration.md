# Feasibility and Thai registration route

Status at 19 October 2026: desk study only, with no bench test, supplier quote or submission. The product is Lemon Soda; one cube per pouch is inferred, not yet confirmed with Singha Corporation.

## Technical feasibility (desk)

The design dose is 0.55 volumes of CO2 per 330 mL serve (ASSUMPTION) at a working pH of 3.5 (ASSUMPTION). Taste is untested. Potassium bicarbonate at 0.81 g releases about 8.1 mmol of CO2 (DERIVED). Citric acid is 0.52 g stoichiometrically but 2.10 g to reach pH 3.5, leaving about 1.58 g free (DERIVED). Basis B adds a binder and filler of 0.80 g (ASSUMPTION; range 0.5 to 1.2 g; not yet named; sodium unconfirmed, gate G3) and natural lemon flavour of 0.05 g (ASSUMPTION; range 0.02 to 0.10 g). Reactants are 2.94 g, and the central cube mass is 3.79 g (DERIVED on ASSUMPTION inputs; range 3.46 to 4.24 g), in a 14 mm cube at 1.38 g/cm3 (band 1.25 to 1.60 an ASSUMPTION). Nothing is weighed. The acid and CO2 calculations use citric acid, carbonic acid and water pKa values (S, UNVERIFIED: not read).

CO2 solubility at 35 C is 0.592 volumes (S, SOURCED, Lange-attributed; 35 C page basis ambiguous). The 0.55 dose sits below it at every modelled serve from 5 to 35 C (DERIVED). The NIST table (P, SOURCED) has no 35 C row and is not used.

The desk model gives a fizz duration of up to about 96 minutes above a sparkling-sensation threshold, and a mass-transfer time constant of up to about 106 minutes (DERIVED). This is a surface-only upper bound with no nucleation. The 0.44 g/L threshold is S, SOURCED (abstract only) and a PROXY: a mineral-water value applied to a soft drink.

One serve holds about 317 mg of potassium, 9.1% of the Thai reference of 3,500 mg per day (DERIVED from S, SOURCED: a third-party copy of Notification 182 and a consultancy page citing the current Notification 445; primary texts not opened). The EFSA adequate intake for adults is also 3,500 mg per day (P, SOURCED).

Dry-cube shelf life is not tested. The 35 C storage temperature is an ASSUMPTION; Bangkok climate figures are UNVERIFIED (PDF unreadable), so none is used.

Limit: the bench gate that would confirm that 0.55 volumes is reached and kept at serve is not yet defined, so no gate value is stated here.

## Ingredients and supply

Potassium bicarbonate and citric acid are quantum satis in category 14.1.4; the binder, filler and flavour are not yet checked against that rule. Sucralose is limited to 300 mg/kg there, under condition 127 (P, SOURCED). The design uses 33 mg per serve, about 100 mg/kg (DERIVED).

- Potassium bicarbonate (38.1 baht per kg) and citric acid (31.4 baht per kg) are PROXY values from UN Comtrade import unit values (P, SOURCED; 2024 and 2025 data), converted at 33.55 baht per US dollar (P, SOURCED: Bank of Thailand, 15 October 2026). The weights are estimates; the potassium line may mix carbonate and bicarbonate.
- Sucralose at 655 baht per kg is a Thai online asking price with no URL given (UNVERIFIED: not read). Reactants: 0.118 baht per cube (DERIVED). The binder and filler and the lemon flavour are not priced and are not in this figure.
- Pouch 1.30 (range 0.67 to 2.70), print 1.50 and processing 0.50 are ASSUMPTIONS, or rest on a non-Thai listing (UNVERIFIED: not read). No Thai quote was requested.
- The recommended 6-cube cost includes a carton share of THB 0.67 per cube (THB 4 per 6-cube carton, an ASSUMPTION scaled from the 12-cube price; no listing). The 12-cube cost includes a carton share of THB 0.42 per cube (THB 5 per 12-cube carton, from a retail box listing used as a PROXY). The 24-cube carton at 9 baht is an ASSUMPTION.
- Cost per cube at 0.55 volumes and pH 3.5: THB 4.09 in the recommended 6-cube carton and THB 3.84 in the 12-cube carton, the larger option (DERIVED; both exclude binder and flavour, which are not priced). The ex-factory planning value of 9.00 baht per cube is an ASSUMPTION (60% of an assumed 15-baht shelf price).

The recommended pack is a 6-cube carton, 190 x 150 x 50 mm outside (ASSUMPTION). Six flat pouches of 90 x 70 x 20 mm fit in two layers of up to four (two by two per layer), using 40 mm of the 50 mm height (ASSUMPTION; checked by arithmetic, not by a packing trial). About 128,586 cubes fit a 20-foot container in 21,431 six-cube cartons, limited by volume, with a container mass of 2,823.8 kg against a 28.2-tonne payload. A 40-foot container holds about 262,326 cubes in 43,721 cartons (5,760.9 kg; 26.84-tonne payload). These counts are DERIVED from 33.2 cubic metres at a 92% packing share (S, SOURCED: SeaCube and ONE pages); the carton sizes and the packing share are ASSUMPTIONS. The larger option is a 12-cube carton, 190 x 150 x 100 mm (ASSUMPTION): about 128,580 cubes in 10,715 cartons in a 20-foot container (1,752.2 kg), and 262,320 cubes in 21,860 cartons in a 40-foot container (3,574.7 kg).

## Competitor overlap

The comparison product is the Singha Lemon Soda can, a 330 mL can listed on the company product page (tier S); retail sale not dated. Its price is not verified. Flavour overlaps with our product. We differ in form and pack: a dissolvable cube that makes the drink in the glass, one cube per pouch, against the competitor's 330 mL ready-to-drink can. Our product's sugar-free wording stays barred. The Singha sparkling lime water, a different product, launched March 2026 at 15 baht (company release, P, SOURCED; news reprint, S, SOURCED), is not the comparison product.

No domestic dissolvable-cube product was found. Overseas analogues are cited as evidence only: coffee cubes and freeze-dried cubes in pouches (both S, SOURCED).

## Thai regulatory route

The limits come from the flavoured-drink rows (category 14.1.4) of Annex 1 to Ministry of Public Health Notification No. 468, amendment No. 4 (P, SOURCED for those rows only). The full annex was not read (UNVERIFIED); the full notification was too large to fetch. A Thai FDA clarification, in force from 12 December 2025, says the 14.1.4 permission also covers sub-categories 14.1.4.1 to 14.1.4.3 (S, SOURCED; read in part, so it is not tiered P).

Condition 127 applies the sucralose limit to the drink as ready to drink, which matches the cube (P, SOURCED). Condition TH3 applies only to electrolyte (mineral) drinks, and the annex text does not read a flavoured sparkling drink as one. This is a regulatory question, not settled.

A Thai food registration step applies as classified; its step, form, fees and timing are UNVERIFIED. No registration number or outcome exists.

## Labelling and claims

The composition may be stated as fact: potassium bicarbonate, citric acid, sucralose, a binder and filler (not yet named; not yet checked for sodium; sodium not claimed) and natural lemon flavour, with no sodium carbonate. The binder and flavour amounts are assumptions until a cube is weighed. Net weight on the label is the 3.8 g specification, verified by weighing before print (Gate 9).

Barred claims: sugar-free, zero-sugar, sodium-free, health, electrolyte, hydration, potassium-benefit and validated-fizz. The 9.1% figure and fizz duration stay internal and off pack.

The Food Act labelling notification is UNVERIFIED (number, date, sweetener and nutrition rules). Label text stays provisional until it is read.

## Classification and excise

Excise item 02.02 covers sweetened or flavoured drinks, including intensively sweetened ones. The 14% ad valorem rate applies if the value base applies; the base was not read (P, SOURCED from the saved copy of the Royal Gazette notice). Per-litre rates depend on sugar per 100 mL: 0 baht up to 6 g, and 1, 3 and 5 baht above (from 1 October 2566; same status). The desk reading assumes no added sugar, which places the cube in the 0-baht band; classification is open.

Item 02.01 (no added sweetener, no flavouring) does not fit. Its rate is 0 baht per litre and 14% ad valorem if the value base applies (same status; the base was not read, so the 14% cannot be converted to baht). Later schedules were not searched, and the Thai numerals were read from page images.

Classification (beverage, edible ice or electrolyte drink) is open and is not settled here; it decides the route, the excise line and whether TH3 applies.

## Trademark and registration

The proposal names Singha Corporation as proposer. Which entity owns the SINGHA mark in Thailand is not confirmed (UNVERIFIED).

An aggregator page read on 19 October 2026 lists 12 SINGHA marks owned by Boon Rawd Brewery Co., Ltd., the holding company (S, SOURCED; https://www.ipqwery.com/ipowner/en/owner/ip/177624-boon-rawd-brewery-co-ltd.html). None of the 12 is labelled for Thailand on that page, and most are in Nice class 32. This is a secondary listing, not a register record.

The Department of Intellectual Property register at https://tmsearch.ipthailand.go.th/ returned HTTP 403 on 19 October 2026 (UNVERIFIED). No record for SINGHA LEMON SODA or SINGHA LEMON SODA (CUBE) was retrieved. A US application for SINGHA lemon soda goods, serial 98124128, filed 9 August 2023, was seen in a search snippet only (UNVERIFIED; no URL or snippet date recorded). A US filing does not establish rights in Thailand.

Thai trademark law is first to file, with a 10-year term and renewal within 90 days (P, SOURCED; original 1991 Act on WIPO Lex, https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/3803). The amended text was not read, so these points may have changed.

Nice class 32 covers non-alcoholic beverages (P, SOURCED; 2014 edition, https://nclpub.wipo.int/enfr/20140101/information_files/class/32/?lang=en). Class 30 was not checked.

Before pack print, four actions are needed. The cube name must be searched and filed in class 32 and in any food class. Singha Corporation must confirm which entity owns the SINGHA mark in Thailand. Written confirmation or a licence from the mark holder for the proposer's use must be obtained. Any food notification must be confirmed with the Thai FDA.
