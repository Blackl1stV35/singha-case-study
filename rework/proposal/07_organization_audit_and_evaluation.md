# Organization audit and evaluation of the group

Public sources only; no outside contact or plant access. Evidence reviewed to 20 October 2026.

## Tiers

Tiers are defined once, in Appendix A.

## Scope: the proposer and its holding company

- The proposer is Singha Corporation, and its holding company is Boon Rawd Brewery. The About page puts the companies Singha Corporation manages at more than 150 (S, SOURCED, COMPANY CLAIM; undated).
- Singha Corporation is the proposer, not a client. The group's data and factories are assumed unavailable, so group capability is a benchmark only.
- Lemon Soda is a lemon drink cube for domestic Thailand only, read as one component in the brief's sense (an unchecked reading, not an ingredient count); its ingredients are potassium bicarbonate, citric acid, sucralose, mannitol as binder and filler (isomalt is the fallback) and lemon flavour (basis B). Its carbonation is an untested desk model.
- Competitor overlap: the comparison product is the Singha Lemon Soda can, a 330 ml ready-to-drink can (S, SOURCED). Its product page describes it as 0% sugar with no calories (COMPANY CLAIM). On 20 October 2026 Lotus's Shop Online listed a single can at THB 17.00, and 24-can packs read THB 13.54 to 13.75 a can at Makro PRO, Lotus's and 7-Eleven ALL Online (S, SOURCED for the pack prices, DERIVED per can). The band is THB 13.54 to 17.00 across six retail rows, with a median of THB 13.75. These are online prices; no store shelf price was read. The can overlaps on flavour. Lemon Soda differs in form (a dissolvable cube made in the glass, one per pouch) and in pack from that can. Lemon Soda makes no sugar claim. The group's lime sparkling water (P, SOURCED) is a different product and is not the comparison product.

**Sodium.** The recipe specifies no sodium salt, no sodium carbonate and no lubricant. Sodium from the recipe is 0 to about 2 mg a serve (DERIVED). Water adds its own sodium: 0 to about 7 mg a serve at the WHO typical level of 20 mg per litre (P), or about 33 to 68 mg at the 100 to 200 mg per litre reported for Thai tap water (S, a news report; not measured). These are calculations, not measurements. No sodium-free, low-sodium or very-low-sodium claim is made.

**Potassium and class.** The serve holds about 317 mg of potassium, 9% of the 3,500 mg Thai reference (P), or about 960 mg per litre. A medical review is held at Gate 2 before any sale. Notification 195 (mineral-salt beverages), clause 4(1.3), sets potassium at no more than 195 mg per litre where it is present, so the cube would hold about 4.9 times that ceiling if it were classed as a mineral-salt beverage, and the formula would then have to change. The text of the clause is unchanged from the Gazette original through the Thai FDA compilation of about 29 March 2023 (P; read from page images), and the 2011 and 2020 amendments did not change clauses 3, 4(1) or 7. No later amending notification was found by title on the Thai FDA list to 20 October 2026 (P, negative finding), but a post-March-2023 amendment inside a general notification is not excluded. Whether the cube is a mineral-salt beverage under clause 3 is the Gate 1 question. The proposed class is flavoured drink.

**Cost per cube.** DERIVED: THB 4.09 before binder and flavour at pH 3.5 in the recommended six-cube carton, and THB 3.84 in the twelve-cube carton, the larger option. Reactants of about THB 0.12 rest on China ex-works asking prices read on 20 October 2026 (S), not Thai landed prices: food-grade-labelled rows for potassium bicarbonate and citric acid, and listing rows not labelled food grade for sucralose. Freight, duty and VAT on inputs are not included. Pouch 1.30, print 1.50 and processing 0.50 are ASSUMPTIONS; pouch and print make up THB 2.80. The cost excludes the binder and the flavour. Mannitol is priced only as an import-value proxy (Thailand imports, HS 2905.43, 2025; THB 0.08 to 1.27 per cube by origin; S, PROXY; not a Thai quote), which takes the cost to THB 4.17 to 5.36 (DERIVED). The lemon flavour (0.05 g) is not priced. The 6-cube cost includes a carton share of THB 0.67 per cube (THB 4 per 6-cube carton, an ASSUMPTION scaled from the 12-cube price). The 12-cube cost includes a carton share of THB 0.42 per cube (THB 5 per 12-cube carton, from a retail box listing used as a PROXY). A 20 ft container holds about 21,386 six-cube cartons, or 128,316 cubes, limited by volume (DERIVED: a volume estimate at 92% of the internal volume on the carrier's page, with an assumed carton size). The payload is 28.33 t (P). Twelve-cube cartons give the same 128,316 cubes in about 10,693 cartons (DERIVED).

## Plant allocation: can an existing line take this cube?

Not shown; the evidence cannot answer yes or no. The cube needs a dry-solids route; no line type, capacity or utilisation is traceable, so no group volume is used. A yes needs line data, dry-powder and sealing capability, site certificates and the legal entity per site.

The team's setting for the route is a pilot batch at a dry-solids co-packer named at Gate 6 (owner role: operations director, by M2). Line time is small, about 79 hours for 200,000 cubes at the planning point (about 2% of a 4,000-hour year; DERIVED on assumed inputs). A co-packer's minimum run of 500,000 to 1,500,000 units per product and formula (COMPANY CLAIM, one co-packer's own page) is the binding constraint, not line time, and the plan states it as a constraint.

## Limits

1. No Singha Corporation or Boon Rawd Brewery cost, supply, capacity, line or innovation-budget data was found; Singha Corporation has not been asked.
2. The product class is an open regulatory question. Flavoured drink is the proposed class, and the Gate 1 opinion also tests whether the cube is a mineral-salt beverage under Notification 195, whose potassium ceiling is 195 mg per litre against about 960 mg per litre in the cube. Notification 195 was read from page images, and a post-March-2023 amendment inside a general notification is not excluded. Sale is blocked until Gates 1, 2, 7 and 9 pass; a pilot batch also needs Gates 3 to 6 and 8.
3. Sucralose is checked at flavoured-drink level only (300 mg/kg); sub-category rows were not read, so the design holds only if none sets a level below about 167 mg/kg (55 mg a serve).
4. The recommended dose, 0.55 volume of CO2 per 330 mL, is below solubility at every serve from 5 to 35 °C (0.592 volume at 35 °C). The R&D lead restates the pass rule before the run (section 10, Gate 3; by M1); this audit sets none.
5. Taste is untested; the pH 3.5 target is a working anchor (desk model pH about 3.4 to 3.5); carbonation is unmeasured. Dissolution is a model estimate: about 6 minutes stirred and 11 not stirred for central assumed inputs (3 to 22 and 5 to 74 minutes across the assumed ranges). The Bangkok April temperature for the 35 °C serve limit is UNVERIFIED.
6. Singha Corporation's registration form refers to three components; this is unchecked. The design uses one component in the brief's sense (one cube, one of the brief's axes), not one ingredient: its recipe lists potassium bicarbonate, citric acid, sucralose, mannitol as binder and filler and lemon flavour (basis B).
7. One cube per pouch is an ASSUMPTION. The alcohol-cue risk is not assessed here; the ruling is a Gate 7 dependency on the mark holder's brand and legal function. The Thai owner of the SINGHA mark is unconfirmed.
8. Pouch, print and processing costs are assumptions. Ingredient prices are China ex-works asking prices (S), not Thai landed prices. The mannitol price is an import-value proxy, and the lemon flavour is not priced.
9. The net profit figures are single-company press figures for different years and were not checked against a filing. The entity that books the cube's profit is not known.
10. The can's retail reach and the number of outlets that carry it are not verified. Online prices are not shelf prices.
