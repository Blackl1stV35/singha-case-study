# Lime Cube: R&D handover of figures

19 October 2026. Desk figures only. No bench, sensory or consumer test has been run.

## What is handed over

The Lime Cube is a dissolvable drink cube. One cube dissolves in one 330 mL serve to make a carbonated lime drink. One cube per pouch is inferred, not confirmed with the organiser. The composition is potassium bicarbonate, citric acid and sucralose; no sodium carbonate is used.

The recommended dose is 0.55 volume of carbon dioxide per 330 mL. The working pH anchor is 3.5; taste at that pH is untested. Packs are cartons of 6, 12 or 24 cubes.

Only the composition is stated as fact: no health, hydration, electrolyte, potassium-benefit, sugar, sodium or fizz-performance claim is made. International expansion is out of scope. No domestic cube listing was found, so overseas cube products are cited as analogues (PROXY): Japanese freeze-dried tea and coffee cubes in pouches (2021), and cube brands abroad (2018, 2024).

## The figures per 330 mL at the recommended dose

- Carbon dioxide: 0.55 volume, about 8.1 mmol (DERIVED: 0.55 times 0.330 L, standard molar volume).
- Potassium bicarbonate: 0.81 g (DERIVED: one mole of bicarbonate per mole of carbon dioxide).
- Citric acid: 0.52 g to neutralise the bicarbonate (DERIVED: 8.1 mmol divided by three acid protons). The total for pH 3.5 is 2.10 g, about 6.4 g per litre (DERIVED from citric and carbonic acid pKa values, S, SOURCED).
- Potassium: about 317 mg (DERIVED: 8.1 mmol at 39.1 mg per mmol). This is 9.0% of the Thai reference of 3,500 mg per day (S, SOURCED). Two secondary pages give that reference with different notification numbers (182, B.E. 2541, and 445); the primary is unopened. The EFSA adult adequate intake is also 3,500 mg (P, SOURCED).
- Sucralose: 33 mg, about 100 mg per kg (DERIVED: a sweetness target of 6 g sugar per 100 mL, an ASSUMPTION, divided by 600, the low end of the 600 to 650 times sucrose range, S, SOURCED).
- Sucralose ceiling: 300 mg per kg in category 14.1.4, under condition 127, applying to the drink as consumed (P, UNVERIFIED: not yet checked against a saved copy). Condition TH3 applies only to electrolyte drinks. Potassium bicarbonate and citric acid are quantum satis in the same category (P, UNVERIFIED for the same reason). A Singha lemon soda company page names sucralose (S, SOURCED).

## Serve temperature and carbon dioxide retention

Solubility is 0.592 volume at 35 C, the lowest across the 5 to 35 C serve range (S, SOURCED; at one atmosphere). So 0.55 volume is within solubility at every modelled serve.

The desk model decays each serve from 0.55 volume exponentially (form DERIVED), with an upper-bound time constant of 106 minutes. At 25 C it keeps about 0.50 volume after 10 minutes (DERIVED). The fizz threshold of 0.44 g per litre, about 0.22 volume (DERIVED), comes from a mineral-water study (abstract only; PROXY, S, SOURCED). Time above it is about 96 minutes at every serve from 5 to 35 C (DERIVED). This is a surface-only upper bound: the model assumes no bubble formation.

Ice lowers the starting level: 30 g of ice gives about 21 C at 0.50 volume, and 60 g about 13 C at 0.47 volume (DERIVED).

## Cost per cube and per container

Cost per pouch before carton is THB 3.42 (DERIVED from four inputs): reactants THB 0.12, pouch THB 1.30, print THB 1.50 and processing THB 0.50, all ASSUMPTIONS. The pouch figure ranges from 0.67 to 2.70; a non-Thai listing stands in for a Thai price (PROXY).

At 0.55 volume and pH 3.5, cost per cube is THB 4.09 in a 6-cube carton, THB 3.84 in a 12-cube carton and THB 3.79 in a 24-cube carton (DERIVED). The gap to THB 3.42 (THB 0.37 to 0.67) is unsplit; no carton price is sourced. The model's THB 9.00 ex-factory figure is a placeholder, not a proposed price (ASSUMPTION).

A 20-foot container holds about 128,580 cubes in 12-cube cartons (10,715 cartons). Volume, not weight, is the limit (DERIVED, S, SOURCED). This implies about 0.26 litres of container volume per cube, an unstated packing assumption. The modelled load is about 1.6 tonnes against a payload of 28.2 tonnes.

## What R&D must define

1. What retention at 25 C, after how many minutes, should the gate for 0.55 volume be? So far the model keeps about 0.50 volume after 10 minutes, a value to test.
2. What pH and acid level should the drink reach? So far 3.5 is a working anchor; no lemon-lime acid level has been sourced.
3. What sweetness do panellists accept at 33 mg of sucralose per serve? So far the figure rests on an assumed sugar equivalence.
4. Should the pack say chilled, iced or ambient, and with what serve rule? So far the model covers 5 to 35 C and two ice cases. No serve rule or wording exists.
5. What fizz duration is acceptable, and how will it be measured? So far the threshold is a mineral-water proxy; the 96-minute figure is an upper bound.
6. What potassium declaration and medical wording are permissible, and who reviews the risk? So far no medical review has been done; kidney function and potassium-sparing drugs come first.
7. Is the product a beverage, an edible ice or an electrolyte drink, and which limits follow? So far classification is open. A Thai FDA clarification extends 14.1.4 permissions to sub-categories, not the reverse (P, SOURCED); the sub-category rows were not read. Excise item 02.02 (14% ad valorem plus per-litre rates) is from a desk note; its gazette PDF was unopened (UNVERIFIED).
8. What pouch barrier and shelf life can be supported? So far no water-vapour transmission rate has been sourced, and no shelf-life test exists. Bangkok humidity (73% annual mean, 79% September) is UNVERIFIED: the climate table could not be read.
9. What are the Thai prices for pouches, print and cartons? So far every price is an assumption, no quote was requested, and R&D should obtain them itself.

## What is not yet known

- The time constant does not vary with serve temperature, so the flat result is a model limit, not a finding.
- Pack pressure, headspace and storage temperature are not modelled.
- The 25 C solubility values differ by convention (0.759 and 0.839 volume; S and P, SOURCED), so the gate must state its basis.

## Tier key

P: a government or regulator text read in full, a company's own filing, official statistics, a peer-reviewed article read in full, or a primary database. S: any other source, including press, trade portals, consultancy pages, company web pages that are not filings, handbooks reproduced online, and search snippets. SOURCED: the page text was captured and read; name the capture file. UNVERIFIED: not captured, blocked, unreadable, or only a snippet read. DERIVED: our arithmetic, inputs and tiers shown. PROXY: a stand-in for what is not measured. ASSUMPTION: a value chosen for the model, not sourced.
