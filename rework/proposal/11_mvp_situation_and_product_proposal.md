## 2. Situational analysis

### 2.1 Customer base

What is known:

- The company has described its target buyers in three ways over time: healthy refreshment (2020 launch, S), younger and health-conscious buyers (2021 CEO statement, S), and Gen Z (2024 release, S). These are company claims, tagged COMPANY CLAIM.
- The only category profile is a Bangkok study of Gen X and Gen Y carbonated-drink buyers, n = 400, S, 31 August 2025. It gives a modal profile only, a man aged 27 to 44. The sampling method is not stated and no percentages are given.
- Purchase pattern (Kantar, S, 9 April 2024): 90% of urban consumers drink carbonated drinks out of home, more than 22 purchases a year, about THB 19 per trip. Thai press of 11 January 2024 reports the same figures.
- Preference (Mintel, S, 27 December 2023): 37% of Gen Z prefer sweet-flavoured non-alcoholic drinks, against 30% of the total sample; 43% of consumers aged 45 and over prefer low, no or reduced sugar, against 33% of Gen Z.
- Sugar and sweetness (Mintel, S, 16 February 2026): 44% say they strictly try to reduce sugar; 27% are open to sugar or salt alternatives; sweetness preference is 40% full, 36% less sweet, 24% undecided.
- Population context (NSO sample, S): the 2021 Health Behaviour of Population Survey base is 57% female and 53% rural. This is the adult population, not Singha buyers.

What is not known: age, sex, income, generation and purchase channel for Singha Lemon Soda buyers; any generation split of lemon-soda buyers. These are gaps (section 4).

Persona (proposed, ASSUMPTION): urban adults aged 25 to 44 who buy sparkling soft drinks out of home and want less sugar without losing taste. The proposed lead is men aged 25 to 34, a test and not a finding (section 3.1). The only confirmed category band is men aged 27 to 44.

### 2.4 What consumers like, and why

- Refreshment is the category reason most often cited (Thai press, 11 January 2024, S; StrategyHelix, 2026, S).
- Taste matters: about two in five consumers name taste as a decision factor for soft drinks (Innova, 31 October 2025, S).
- Cost can be decisive, and soft drinks are price sensitive (Innova, S; Euromonitor summary, November 2025, S).
- Carbonated drinks are bought on impulse and paired with meals (Euromonitor summary, S).
- Sugar: consumers expect taste to match zero sugar (Coca-Cola Thailand, June 2021, S; Mintel, February 2026, S).
- No lemon-specific survey on reasons to choose was found. These are category reasons, not lemon reasons.

## 3. Product proposal

### 3.1 Target customer

- Does the base still hold? Not measurable. No actual volume or buyer data after 2021 is confirmed, so the base trend cannot be shown. This is a limit.
- Segments to convert: people who want less sugar and still want the taste. Mintel (2026, S) puts the pool at about a quarter of its sample (27% open to sugar or salt alternatives; sample not stated). Use 27% as an ASSUMPTION base; the sample is not stated.
- Percentage of base lost: no sourced figure. Cannibalisation of the can is an ASSUMPTION in the value model. The valuation reaches zero when about 7.5% of cube buyers would otherwise buy a can. The review level is 7%: if 7% or more of cube buyers say they would otherwise have bought a can, the value model is rerun before any scale (DERIVED on assumption inputs).
- Lead segment: the proposed lead is men aged 25 to 34 (ASSUMPTION; a test, not a finding). The team compared the customer groups a Singha product would reach in 40,000 simulated outcomes, varying every input across its range, including Bangkok's monthly temperature (27 to 31 degrees Celsius). No Singha buyer data exists, so every group is a market proxy. Compared per person within each age and sex group, men aged 25 to 34 who drink alcohol rank first among six drinker groups in 94% of simulated outcomes (87% to 97% by month). Their margin over men aged 35 to 44 is about 3% on purchase intent (about 0.9 points at a 30% baseline) and about 6% after the potassium-caution exclusion. Both ranges include no difference. The confirmed category band is men aged 27 to 44 (Bangkok sample, n = 400, S). By total reach no group leads clearly. Men aged 45 to 59 are a later volume segment, pended. The customer comparison's population counts and age gradient are carried from earlier team work and are UNVERIFIED.
- Segment reading rule, set before the concept test: men aged 25 to 34 keep the lead if the lower bound of the 95% interval on their intent over men aged 35 to 44 is above zero; the lead is reversed if the upper bound is below zero; otherwise it is undetermined. Owner: research lead, rule fixed before fieldwork (about M3).
- Survey limit: the concept pre-test (M3 to M5; recruited online panel of men aged 25 to 59 who drank alcohol in the past year; top-2-box purchase intent at one test price of THB 15, ASSUMPTION) is a screen. At 1,377 completes per cell it reads a 5-point lead at a 30% baseline. The modelled lead is about 1.2 points, which would need about 22,000 per cell. At 1,377 per cell the test confirms the direction of the lead in 12.7% of simulated runs and rules out a 5-point lead in 56.4% (simulated runs, not data). Fieldwork waits for Gate 1; the panel provider and cost are not sized from sources, and no market is sized from the survey.

### 3.2 Competitive advantage

- Form: a dissolvable cube in a single-serve pouch replaces a 330 mL can. It is lighter and smaller to carry. Per serve, the can reads THB 13.54 to 17.00 in online listings of 20 October 2026 (S; THB 16 to 17 in the press of 2020 to 2023) against a Cube planning price of THB 9.00 ex-factory and a THB 15 base shelf price (ASSUMPTION). Any per-serve comparison is assumption-driven.
- Sugar: a sugar-free claim requires less than 0.5 g of sugar per reference serving (Notification 445, P). The Cube is not presented as sugar-free: no sugar-free, zero-sugar or no-added-sugar wording is used, and the label rules bar it.
- Not confirmed: taste, carbonation and consumer preference against cans. These need a blind test against the can (plan, Gate 4; at least 55% blind preference, ASSUMPTION).

### 3.3 Value-chain change

- Cost minimisation: the Cube's cost is dominated by pouch and print (THB 2.80 of THB 4.09 per cube, ASSUMPTION inputs). The THB 4.09 is before binder and flavour. With mannitol at the import-value range of THB 0.08 to 1.27 a cube, the cost is THB 4.17 to 5.36 (DERIVED). The lemon flavour is not priced. The plan's figure is not verified against a quote, and it rests on China ex-works listings, not Thai landed prices.
- Asset turnover: the Cube has no asset base yet. It avoids can filling and cold chain, but no co-packer line is verified. Peer asset turnover is in section 2.2 as context only.
- Scale: at 200,000 cubes a year (ASSUMPTION base), fixed cost is THB 1.25 per cube at THB 250,000 and THB 2.50 at THB 500,000 (DERIVED). At 50,000 cubes, THB 5.00 and THB 10.00.
- Carton: THB 0.67 per cube in a six-cube carton at THB 4 (ASSUMPTION), THB 0.42 in a twelve-cube carton at THB 5 (ASSUMPTION, scaled).
- Co-packer scale: one Thai dry-powder maker (Arnifo) states a minimum run of 500,000 to 1,500,000 units per product and formula, phased within 12 months (COMPANY CLAIM, S). That is 2.5 to 7.5 years of the 200,000-cube base, so the base is below the minimum unless the minimum is negotiated. The same page lists a pilot-batch step and also says it does no small trial or pilot production; the two statements conflict. The minimum run, not line time, is the binding constraint. The team's setting for the dry-solids route is a pilot batch at a co-packer named at Gate 6, with the minimum run held as a stated constraint (owner: operations director, by M2).
- Line capacity: 200,000 cubes a year need about 79 hours of pouch-line time at 42 pouches a minute (60 a minute at an assumed 70% efficiency; both assumptions), with a 90% range of 45 to 152 hours, about 2% of a 4,000-hour year. 60 a minute is a mid-range maker figure: pre-made pouch makers list about 15 to 80 pouches a minute as dry-cycle maxima (P, maker pages, each a COMPANY CLAIM). No with-product powder rate was found, and no source supports the 70% efficiency (ASSUMPTION).
- Can line speed: a maker table lists about 100 to 300 cans a minute for 330 mL lines (dated, P, COMPANY CLAIM). Installed lines read 200 (S) and about 483 to 550 (S, COMPANY CLAIM; the two figures conflict inside one article).
- Can scale (desk model, DERIVED): the variable cost of a can before aluminium is about THB 3.27 with no fixed cost. With THB 300 million of fixed cost a year, it is THB 3.87 at 500 million cans a year and THB 3.57 at 1,000 million. With THB 600 million of fixed cost, it is THB 4.47 at 500 million cans and THB 3.87 at 1,000 million. The earlier figures built on THB 4.94 and on a 1,676 million can volume are withdrawn.

### 3.4 Regulation and trademark

Regulation (Thai FDA, P):

- Notification 468 on food additives is in force from 12 December 2025, with a clarification signed 6 January 2026. Existing products have until 12 December 2027 to comply. A new launch such as the Cube is not in that grace period (research inference, S).
- Sweeteners are a defined functional class. Notification 450 (19 July 2024) requires the sweetener to be declared by functional class and name.
- Sucralose: the limit for flavoured drinks in category 14.1.4 is 300 mg/kg (Notification 468, condition 127; P, flavoured-drink rows only). The planned level, about 167 mg per kg, is inside it. The annex edition, the condition 127 wording and the sub-category rows were not checked and are confirmed at Gate 1.
- Other ingredients: citric acid is at quantum satis in category 14.1.4 (flavoured-drink rows of Notification 468). Mannitol (INS 421) is listed for flavoured drinks at quantum satis (Notification 468, P), with condition TH3, which applies only if the product is classed as an electrolyte drink. Mannitol is a polyol, which the Thai nutrition definition does not count as sugar, and its total sugars are at most 1.0% (FAO and WHO specification), so it does not change the excise tier.
- Sugar-free: less than 0.5 g sugar per reference serving; sodium-free: less than 5 mg sodium per reference serving (Notification 445, 2023).
- Sodium: the recipe specifies no sodium salt, so sodium from the recipe is 0 to about 2 mg a serve (DERIVED; the specification is an ASSUMPTION until supplier declarations are held). Water adds 0 to about 7 mg a serve at the WHO typical level of 20 mg per litre (P), or about 33 to 68 mg at the 100 to 200 mg per litre reported for Thai tap water (S, a news report; not measured). The one-serve total is therefore about 0 to 9 mg at the WHO typical level and about 33 to 68 mg with Thai tap water. These are calculations, not measurements. Against the Notification 445 limits per serving (sodium-free below 5 mg, very low below 35 mg, low below 140 mg), sodium-free fails once water is counted at 20 mg per litre, and only a low-sodium claim could pass at Thai tap-water levels. No sodium-free, low-sodium or very-low-sodium claim is made. A claim would need sodium measured in the made-up drink from three production batches by an accredited laboratory, and the Gate 1 opinion.
- Potassium: the cube holds about 317 mg of potassium a serve (0.81 g potassium bicarbonate), which is 9% of the 3,500 mg Thai reference (Notification 445 Annex 3, P), and about 960 mg per litre. A medical review is required at Gate 2 before sale. About 1% of men aged 25 to 34 and about 13% of adults aged 25 and over are in the potassium caution group (model bounds 0.1% to 5.5% and 5% to 25%; DERIVED). The desk model does not cover people on potassium-retaining medicines or with kidney disease. A Thai nutrition guideline recommends 1,500 to 2,000 mg a day for kidney patients on a low-potassium diet; for them one serve is 16% to 21% of the day (S). The reviewer may reject the serve, require a precaution, or ask for a smaller serve.
- Notification 195 (Thai mineral-salt beverages), clause 4(1.3): potassium is set at no more than 195 mg per litre where it is present. The text is unchanged from the Gazette original through the Thai FDA compilation of about 29 March 2023 (P; read from page images). No later amending notification was found by title on the Thai FDA list to 20 October 2026 (P, negative finding), but a post-March-2023 amendment inside a general notification is not excluded. The 2011 and 2020 amendments did not change clauses 3, 4(1) or 7. Whether the cube is a mineral-salt beverage under clause 3 is the Gate 1 question. If the Gate 1 opinion classes the cube that way, its potassium would be about 4.9 times the ceiling and the formula would have to change.
- Classification: a food, flavoured drink (category 14.1.4). This is the team's setting (regulatory affairs head, by M1) and an ASSUMPTION until the Gate 1 opinion confirms it and tests the mineral-salt rule. It is a supplement only if marketed as one.
- Label: food serial number and net weight are required (Notification 450). Required declarations: the Thai nutrition panel and GDA panel, with sodium and potassium shown, from laboratory analysis before pack print (Gate 9); where the pouch front is under 65 sq cm, Thai Notification 394 allows the panels on the wholesale package. These are declarations, not claims. Barred wording with no clearance route: sugar-free, zero-sugar and no-added-sugar; sodium-free, low-sodium and very-low-sodium; health, function, electrolyte, hydration and potassium-benefit wording; calorie wording; certification and award wording, none of which is verified. The drink does not meet the Thai standard for an electrolyte drink (Notification 195) and is not presented as one. Held until Gate 7 or a Thai legal review: "no salt added" or "unsalted" wording; novelty words; fizz, sparkling, bubbles and CO2 release until a measured test exists; lemon fruit imagery, glassware and bottle shape; alcohol cues; and the lion or SINGHA beyond this proposal without written sign-off.
- Pack precaution (draft for the Gate 2 reviewer, not yet checked by a Thai medical reviewer; any warning waits for Gate 2): "Contains about 320 mg of potassium per cube. Value (desk model, DERIVED from ASSUMPTION inputs; 200,000 cubes a year, THB 500,000 fixed cost, five years, 10% discount rate, no terminal value, after excise and tax):

- Base case: NPV THB 0.17 million. The chance of a positive NPV across the stated input ranges is 0.26 (0.40 before excise and tax; 0.58 on a simpler structure), against a test of 0.95 fixed in advance that no basis reaches. The expected NPV is minus THB 0.88 million, and its 90% range is minus THB 3.40 to plus THB 1.59 million. Every 90% range includes zero.
- Contribution: THB 3.94 per cube after excise; THB 4.91 per cube before excise, on the planning price of THB 9.00.
- Volume: NPV is minus THB 1.41 million at 50,000 cubes, THB 3.32 million at 500,000 cubes and THB 8.59 million at 1,000,000 cubes. Break-even with no cannibalisation is 184,325 cubes a year after excise and tax (148,076 before excise). Operating break-even, fixed cost divided by contribution after excise, is about 127,000 cubes. On the older before-excise, pre-tax basis the base NPV is THB 0.88 million and the break-even is 146,230 cubes; that basis is not the valuation basis. The expected NPV is zero at about 335,000 cubes across the ranges.
- Fixed cost: at 200,000 cubes the NPV stays positive up to about THB 0.54 million a year of fixed cost after excise and tax (THB 0.68 million before excise and tax). Fixed cost and shelf price explain most of the spread.
- Cannibalisation: the NPV is zero when about 7.5% of cube buyers would otherwise buy a can, and negative above that. The review level is 7%, just under the break-even.
- Option: the pilot reads after 13 weeks and can stop. In the model the demand read is worth THB 0.03 to 0.25 million. The sales and channel head owns the pilot routes, by M4. Route A is 20 stores, three sampling days a store, 20 samples a day, at THB 15 a sample (committed THB 18,000; range THB 12,000 to 30,000).
