# Thai Notification of the Ministry of Public Health No. 445 B.E. 2566 (2023), Nutrition Labelling: potassium extract (T10)

Date read: 20 October 2026. Tier P (regulator text, read in the English translation published by the Thai FDA; 65 pages; text layer extracted with PyMuPDF). The Thai text as gazetted in the Royal Gazette was not opened.
- URL: https://food.fda.moph.go.th/media.php?id=689363893683888128&name=P445_EN.pdf
- Document: "Notification of the Ministry of Public Health (No. 445) B.E. 2566 (2023) Issued By the Virtue of Food Act B.E. 2522 (1979) Re: Nutrition Labelling". Notified 17 November B.E. 2566 (2023); in force 180 days after publication in the Government Gazette (Clause 8). It repeals Notification No. 182 B.E. 2541, No. 219 B.E. 2544 and No. 392 B.E. 2561 (Clause 1).

## Thai RDI for potassium (Annex 3 table, PDF page 37)
- Row 32: Potassium 3500 milligram (mg). Row 31: Sodium 2000 mg. Row 33: Chloride 2300 mg. (The old No. 182 table had potassium 3,500 and sodium 2,400.)
- So the 3,500 mg reference used on slide 7 is a primary figure (P), not only a secondary copy.

## Which foods must carry a nutrition label (Clause 3)
- Foods with a nutrition claim, foods with a health claim, foods using nutritional value in sales promotion, and other foods the Minister notifies. Exemptions in Clause 7 include foods not sold directly to consumers.

## Potassium in the label format (Annex 1)
- Standard format lists "Potassium" (mg and % of Thai RDI) under Section 3 Part 1, and the remark states that the starred nutrients, including Potassium, are mandatory in the standard format.
- Rounding rule for sodium and potassium (milligram): below 5 declared as 0; 5 to 140 rounded in steps of 5; above 140 rounded in steps of 10. Our arithmetic: 316.6 mg would be declared as 320 mg.

## Nutrition claim thresholds (Annex 4, Table 1, PDF pages 58 to 59)
- Vitamins and minerals excluding sodium: "high, rich in, excellent source of" needs 30 percent or more of the Thai RDI per serving criteria; "good source, contains, provides" needs more than 15 percent of the Thai RDI. Potassium is not excluded from this row.
- Our arithmetic: 15 percent of 3,500 mg is 525 mg and 30 percent is 1,050 mg; 316.6 mg is 9.0 percent, so on the per serving basis (Table 1) neither a "source of" nor a "high in" potassium claim is open to the cube. The per 100 kcal basis of Table 2 is dealt with below.

## Reference serving, the per 100 mL table and the per 100 kcal basis (Annex 2 and Annex 4 Table 2)
- Annex 2, Category 2 Beverages: carbonated and non-carbonated beverages reference serving size 200 milliliters; beverages made from powder or concentrate "prepared before consumption" are also 200 milliliters (defined amount for preparing).
- Annex 4 section 2.1 (PDF pages 39 to 40): Case 1 is food with a prescribed reference serving size in Annex 2 (and similar foods), which follows Table 1 (per serving). Case 2 is food that does not follow Case 1, which follows Table 2 (per 100 grams or 100 milliliters). Which case applies to a dissolvable cube is not settled by the text: a drink made from it has a prescribed 200 mL reference serving (Case 1), while the cube itself, as a solid, may have none (Case 2).
- Annex 4 Table 2, row "vitamins and minerals, excluding sodium" (PDF page 65), liquid food: "good source, contains, provides" needs 7.5 percent or more of the Thai RDI per 100 milliliters OR 5 percent or more of the Thai RDI per 100 kilocalories; "high, rich, rich in, excellent source of" needs 15 percent or more per 100 milliliters OR 10 percent or more per 100 kilocalories. The solid-food column carries the same kilocalorie lines (5 percent and 10 percent per 100 kilocalories); the word "or" is missing from the text layer in the good source solid cell and is read as "or" by comparison with the neighbouring cells. Both also have to follow conditions 1.1 and 2.2 of the Annex.
- Our arithmetic, per serving and per 100 mL (Table 1 and Table 2 volume bases): 15 percent of 3,500 mg is 525 mg; 7.5 percent is 262.5 mg per 100 mL; the cube drink has 316.6 mg per serve (9.0 percent per 330 mL, 5.5 percent per 200 mL reference serving) and about 96 mg per 100 mL (2.7 percent). Neither basis is met.
- Per 100 kilocalories was NOT tested against a measured value: energy per serve is not known (the binder and filler is not named; no energy figure exists in the repo). The threshold is 5 percent of 3,500 mg = 175 mg per 100 kcal (good source) and 10 percent = 350 mg per 100 kcal (high). The basis is met whenever the serve has less than 180.9 kcal (good source) or less than 90.5 kcal (high).
- Bound on energy (our arithmetic with the Notification's own factors, Annex 1 section 2.4, PDF page 12: protein 4, carbohydrate 4, fat 9, fibre 2, ethanol 7, organic acid 3, sugar alcohols 0 to 3.0 kcal per gram; no factor for mineral salts): the whole cube of 3.794 g at the highest factor (9 kcal/g) is at most 34.1 kcal; counting citric acid (2.10 g) at the organic acid factor of 3 kcal/g and the binder, flavour and sucralose (0.883 g) at 9 kcal/g, at most 14.25 kcal; potassium bicarbonate is taken as 0. At those bounds the cube carries at least 927 mg to 2,222 mg of potassium per 100 kcal (26.5 percent to 63.5 percent of the Thai RDI per 100 kcal).
- Reading: if Table 2 applies and energy is counted on the per 100 kcal basis, the per 100 kcal conditions would be met for both "good source" and "high". Notification 445 therefore does not by itself close a potassium content claim. The no-claim setting is the team's choice (it is on the barred-claims list), not a consequence of the thresholds.

## What it does not contain
- No potassium warning or caution statement, no limit on potassium in a beverage and no rule for potassium salts in food. Those would sit in other notifications not read.
