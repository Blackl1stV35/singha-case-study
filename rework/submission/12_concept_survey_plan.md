# Appendix C: concept survey plan

Status on 20 October 2026: Not run. No outside contact, quote or fieldwork has happened.

Tiers follow Appendix A, which holds the full definitions. Each figure carries its tier where it first appears. In brief, P means a primary source read in full: a regulator's text, a company's own filing, release or product page, official statistics or a primary database. S means any other source that was read and opened, such as press, a trade portal or a consultancy page. UNVERIFIED means the figure was not read, was blocked or not found, or only a search snippet was read. In this plan UNVERIFIED is also used for a figure carried from earlier team work that has not been re-read for this plan. DERIVED means arithmetic from the inputs shown. PROXY means a stand-in for a value that has not been measured. ASSUMPTION means a value chosen for the model. COMPANY CLAIM means a statement a company makes about itself, not checked independently. A bracketed qualifier narrows what a tier covers, for example "negative finding", "flavoured-drink rows only" or "read from page images".

Appendix A would class a company's own product page read in full as P. The Singha Lemon Soda product pages are tiered S, company in this plan, which is one step lower. That is the proposal's setting and it is kept, not raised: the pages are undated, and what they say about the can is a COMPANY CLAIM.

## 1. Purpose

This plan sets out a concept survey that would test whether men aged 25 to 34 state a higher purchase intent than men aged 35 to 44 for a dissolvable lemon-flavoured drink cube. It fixes the design, sample size, decision rule and preconditions before any fieldwork. It reports no result.

The survey measures stated purchase intent, which is a proxy for conversion. None of the sources reviewed holds a conversion outcome. The conversion comparison in this proposal therefore rests on a model built from published inputs, with no respondents. This plan is the recruited design. It is the concept pre-test planned for months 3 to 5 (M3 to M5) of the proposal, and it is a screening test. At the sample size set in section 8, it reads the direction of the difference and a 5-point size rule. It cannot reliably confirm the modelled lead of about 1.2 points (DERIVED on ASSUMPTION inputs, section 8). The simulation in section 9 shows it confirming the direction in about 13 percent of runs at a 30% baseline. No market is sized from it. The proposal gives the modelled lead as about 0.9 points in one place and about 1.2 points in another (both DERIVED). Both come from two runs of the same age gradient, and section 8 shows both with their inputs and tiers. The survey is deferred. Fieldwork waits for Gate 1. It also waits for the executive sponsor's signature on the Gate 0 record, which names the concept panel provider and the first budget ceiling (section 11). The panel provider and the cost are not sized from sources.

## 6. Stimulus and questionnaire

### Stimulus rules (track 1)

- Describe what the consumer does. One cube is dropped into 330 ml of water and stirred, and it dissolves. No fizz, sparkling, bubble or carbon dioxide wording is used until a measured test exists. The dissolution time and the dissolved carbon dioxide are desk-model estimates, not measurements. For a central set of assumed inputs the model gives about 6 minutes stirred and about 11 minutes not stirred (ASSUMPTION inputs), so no time or fizz level goes into the stimulus.
- No health, nutrition, sugar, sodium or brand claim. The following words are not used: "sugar-free", "no sugar", "zero", "sodium", "sodium-free", "low", "healthy", "natural", "for hydration", and any botanical health wording. No sodium-free or low-sodium claim is made. The recipe specifies no sodium salt, but the water adds its own sodium, and a claim would need sodium measured in the made-up drink by an accredited laboratory.
- No SINGHA name, lion or public product name.
- The ingredient list reads: potassium bicarbonate, citric acid, an intensive sweetener, mannitol (binder and filler) and lemon flavour. The sweetener name stays in brackets until the Gate 1 opinion confirms it. The working choice is sucralose at 55 mg a serve, which is about 167 mg per kg of drink (DERIVED) and equal in sweetness to about 10 g of sugar per 100 mL (ASSUMPTION for the sweetness target). The limit is 300 mg/kg for flavoured drinks (P, flavoured-drink rows only). The stimulus does not print the amount. Mannitol, tableting grade, is listed for flavoured drinks at quantum satis (P). Its amount of 0.80 g is an ASSUMPTION, and isomalt is the fallback.
- The test price is THB 15 per cube (ASSUMPTION). The sold unit is one cube per single-serve pouch.
- Concept image: one cube dropped into water. A fuller version could add a glass and a lemon wedge. Both wait for the Gate 7 rulings on glassware and lemon imagery. A claim check of the image and text runs before use. The image is not yet produced.

Draft stimulus text. Bracketed values are not yet set.

## 7. Primary outcome and secondary items

- **Primary outcome.** The share of respondents giving codes 4 or 5 at Q2, per person exposed. This is a PROXY for conversion. It measures stated intent, not a purchase.
- **Primary contrast.** Top-2-box share in cell A minus cell B. Two-sided alpha 0.05. The 95% confidence interval uses the Newcombe-Wilson method.
- **Secondary items.** Q3 Yes rate at the stated price by cell, with the same test. Q4 occasion incidence by cell, to check whether the occasion mix explains any age gap. Q6 disclosure reaction. Q5 comprehension. Cell C compared descriptively with A and B.

## 8. Sample and power

**Method (DERIVED).** Two independent cells, two-sided alpha 0.05, power 0.80. The formula is:

n per cell = [z(alpha/2) × sqrt(2 × pbar × qbar) + z(beta) × sqrt(p1 × q1 + p2 × q2)]^2 / (p1 - p2)^2

where cell A has proportion p1, cell B has proportion p2, pbar = (p1 + p2) / 2 and qbar = 1 - pbar. No continuity correction and no design effect are applied. Both would increase n. A one-sided alpha of 0.05 would reduce it. At a 30% baseline and a 5-point lead, one-sided alpha gives 1,084 per cell.

**Baseline (ASSUMPTION).** No source gives a top-2-box baseline for men aged 35 to 44. The grid covers 20%, 30%, 40% and 50%.

**Grid: n per cell for 80% power (DERIVED).**

| Baseline (cell B) | 5 points | 10 points | 15 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20% | 1,094 | 294 | 138 |
| 30% | **1,377** | 356 | 163 |
| 40% | 1,534 | 388 | 173 |
| 50% | 1,565 | 388 | 170 |

The 5-point test at a 30% baseline uses 1,377 per cell. A power check at that size gives about 0.80 for a fixed 5-point lead (DERIVED).

**Modelled lead.**

- Ratio A, men 25 to 34 over men 35 to 44: median 1.04, 90% interval 1.00 to 1.09, probability above 1 of 0.94 (ASSUMPTION). It comes from the age gradient alone. The per-year odds ratio for the age gradient has an interval of 0.991 to 1.001 that includes 1.0 (UNVERIFIED). Ratio A is a model assumption, not a finding.
- At a 30% baseline, ratio A is a lead of 1.23 percentage points (DERIVED). Eighty percent power on a lead that size needs about 22,166 per cell (DERIVED). The 1,377 per cell test is not sized for a lead of that size. Averaged over the uncertainty in ratio A, power at 22,166 per cell is about 66% (DERIVED).
- Ratio B, men 25 to 34 over men 45 to 59: median 1.15, 90% interval 1.03 to 1.28 (ASSUMPTION). Reference only. At a 30% baseline it is a 4.4-point lead, which needs about 1,751 per cell (DERIVED).
- The proposal's customer model, run again on the same age gradient with an assumed intent elasticity, gives a margin of men aged 25 to 34 over men aged 35 to 44 of about 3%, or about 0.9 points at a 30% baseline. After the potassium-caution exclusion the margin is about 6% (DERIVED; the odds ratio is UNVERIFIED and the exclusion is a team model on PROXY inputs). Both ranges include no difference. Ratio A, which sizes this survey, is about 4%. The 0.9 point figure in the proposal is the customer model's margin above, and the 1.2 point figure is ratio A's lead. Both come from the same age gradient. All of these values are far below a 5-point lead, so the survey cannot reliably confirm any of them. For ratio A, section 9 shows that it confirms the direction in about 13 percent of simulated runs at a 30% baseline.

**Interval width (DERIVED, normal approximation).** At 1,377 per cell, the 95% interval on the difference is about plus or minus 3.4 points at a 30% baseline and about plus or minus 3.7 points at 50%.

## 9. Simulation of the test outcome

**Method (DERIVED).** In each simulated run, a person in cell B is top-2-box with probability p, and a person in cell A with probability p times R. R is drawn from a log-normal distribution fitted to the median and 90% interval of the ratio. A pooled two-proportion z-test runs in each of 20,000 draws per case, with a fixed seed. The outcomes are the shares of draws that confirm the lead, reverse it, or leave it inconclusive. The share of draws whose 95% upper bound is below 5 points is also reported. The inconclusive share is the remainder after the two rounded shares, so the three add to 100.

**Result at a 30% baseline and 1,377 per cell, ratio A (DERIVED).**

| Outcome | Share of runs |
|---|---|
| Confirms the lead (men 25 to 34 significantly higher) | 12.7% |
| Reverses the lead (men 35 to 44 significantly higher) | 0.8% |
| Inconclusive | 86.5% |
| Rules out a 5-point lead (95% upper bound below 5 points) | 56.4% |

The shares are rounded to one decimal. The inconclusive share is shown as the remainder, 86.5 percent, so that the three add to 100.0. Its unrounded value is 86.6 percent.

**Other checks (DERIVED, at a 30% baseline unless stated).**

- No lead (ratio 1): confirms about 2.5% of runs and reverses about 2.7%. It rules out a 5-point lead in 82%. The test behaves as a two-sided 5% test.
- Ratio A at a 10-point size (356 per cell): confirms 6.0%. At a 15-point size (163 per cell): confirms 4.5%.
- Ratio A at its own 80% power size (22,166 per cell): confirms about 66%, averaged over the uncertainty.
- Ratio B at 1,377 per cell: confirms 63% and reverses 0.3%.
- Ratio A confirms in 7.7% of runs at a 20% baseline (1,094 per cell), 12.7% at 30% (1,377 per cell), 18.6% at 40% (1,534 per cell) and 25.9% at 50% (1,565 per cell).

**Reading.**

- If the lead is as modelled (ratio A), the test confirms it in about 8% to 13% of runs at a 20% to 30% baseline, and about 26% of runs at a 50% baseline. It almost never reverses it.
- At a 30% baseline the test rejects a 5-point or larger lead in more than half of runs. That is the rejection the design can deliver.
- A result that shows no difference is not evidence against the lead. Under ratio A it is the most likely outcome.

## 10. Decision rules

The team's setting is the interval rule. Its owner is the research lead, who records the rule before fieldwork (about M3), so the rule is fixed before any respondent is reached. The basis is that the interval rule reads direction and a 5-point size, which are the two readings the design can deliver (sections 8 and 9).

**Set: the interval rule.** The primary contrast is the top-2-box share in cell A minus cell B, with its 95% Newcombe-Wilson interval. Men aged 25 to 34 keep the lead if the lower bound of the interval is above zero. The lead is reversed if the upper bound is below zero. Otherwise it is undetermined.

- **Direction.** "Lead supported" if the lower bound is above 0. "Lead reversed" if the upper bound is below 0. Otherwise "direction undetermined."
- **Size.** "5-point lead supported" if the lower bound is at or above 5 percentage points. "5-point lead not supported" if the upper bound is below 5 percentage points. Otherwise "size undetermined."
- Both statements are reported together. A result can support the direction and still rule out a 5-point lead.

**Alternative, not adopted: the probability rule.** It reports the posterior probability that cell A's share is higher than cell B's, under a prior stated before fieldwork, and it sets a decision threshold before fieldwork. The prior and the threshold are not set in this plan. The result depends on the prior, which here rests on ratio A (ASSUMPTION).

## 13. Outputs and limits

**Limits.**

- **Proxy outcome.** The survey measures stated purchase intent, a PROXY for conversion. It does not measure actual conversion, repeat purchase or sales. Intent is not behaviour. No conversion data exists in the sources reviewed.
- **Assumed baseline.** The 30% baseline is assumed (ASSUMPTION). No source gives it.
