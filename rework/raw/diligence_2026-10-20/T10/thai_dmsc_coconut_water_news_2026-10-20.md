## What the article ties each statement to (the reasons, as read in this pass)
1. **The one-coconut-a-day limit is tied to sugar, not potassium.** The sentence before the limit is "กรมอนามัยได้แนะนำว่าควรบริโภคน้ำตาลไม่เกิน 32 กรัมต่อวัน" (the Department of Health recommends sugar of not more than 32 g a day). The article gives one coconut or bottle (about 200 to 300 mL) as 7 to 25 g of sugar (tool summary). The limit follows: "ดังนั้นการบริโภคในปริมาณที่เหมาะสมไม่ควรเกิน 1 ผลต่อวัน" (so a suitable amount is not more than 1 coconut a day), and the article says this keeps blood sugar well regulated (tool summary).
2. **The kidney warning is tied to diuresis and dehydration.** "ไม่เหมาะสำหรับคนที่เป็นโรคไตเสื่อม เพราะน้ำมะพร้าว จะไปกระตุ้นการขับปัสสาวะ ถ้าร่างกายขาดน้ำ" then "คนที่เป็นโรคไตอาจจะหัวใจวายได้" (not suitable for people with degenerative kidney disease, because coconut water stimulates urination; if the body is dehydrated, people with kidney disease may suffer heart failure). Potassium is not named in this reason.
3. **Potassium is named only for the irregular heartbeat warning.** "และคนที่มีอาการหัวใจเต้นผิดจังหวะ ถ้าร่างกายได้รับโพแทสเซียมมากเกินไป" "อาจสร้างปัญหาให้หัวใจ และเป็นอันตรายถึงชีวิตได้ ดังนั้นผู้บริโภคจึงควรบริโภคในปริมาณ ที่เหมาะสม" (and for people with an irregular heartbeat, if the body receives too much potassium it may cause problems for the heart and can be fatal; so consumers should consume a suitable amount).
4. **Mineral figures:** "โดยเฉพาะโพแทสเซียมและคลอไรด์ในปริมาณเฉลี่ย 133.81 - 215.20 มิลลิกรัมต่อ 100 กรัม" (especially potassium and chloride, average amount 133.81 to 215.20 mg per 100 g). It gives one range for the two minerals. The sentence opens "ผลการตรวจวิเคราะห์ พบแคลเซียม ..." (the analysis found calcium ...) in the first pass. It does not say whether the range is a sum of potassium and chloride or the range for each mineral, and it gives no potassium-only value. The article says fresh coconut water carries more minerals (calcium, magnesium, sodium, potassium, chloride) than packaged coconut water (tool summary).
5. An earlier read noted that the mineral figures are per 100 g while the sugar and vitamin figures are per 100 mL; this note came from a model summary and was not seen again.

## Limits for use
- The article supports one thing about potassium: a warning, for people with an irregular heartbeat, that too much potassium may harm the heart. It is a weak precedent for a potassium precaution on a drink. It does not tie the kidney warning to potassium, and it does not tie the one-coconut limit to potassium. It is not support for any daily limit on the cube.
- The range cannot be used as a potassium value, because the page does not separate potassium from chloride.
- If the range were for each mineral, potassium would be 134 to 215 mg per 100 g, below the USDA 250 mg per 100 g (USDA FDC 170174, P; saved in `usda_fdc_api_potassium_2026-10-20.md`). If it were a sum, potassium alone would be lower still and unknown. The two sources therefore differ or measure different things. The record uses the USDA value.
- It is a news report of a statement; the Department's own release was not found.
