Date read: 2026-10-20. Tier: P for 445 and 446 (regulator text, FDA English translation read from the text layer with PyMuPDF; the Thai original was read from rendered pages for Table 1 and Table 2 in the first pass, see thai_notification_445_sodium_claims.md). Our translation is marked where Thai is quoted.

## 1. Notification 445, Annex 1: what the panel must show (English PDF p. 8, 11)

- URL: https://food.fda.moph.go.th/media.php?id=689363893683888128&name=P445_EN.pdf (65 pages).
- Clause 1: formats 1.1 to 1.8 are all "standard format" panels. The text layer shows a potassium line in formats 1.1, 1.3, 1.4, 1.6, 1.7 (dual display) and 1.8 (bilingual); the horizontal layouts 1.2 and 1.5 were not checked line by line. No simplified format is defined in this notification (a search of the English text for "simplif" returns nothing).
- Clause 1.7, dual display: "In the case that foods could be consumed with other food contained in the same container or such food need to be mixed with other ingredients and/or have to be processed/cooked as instructed on the label, the declaration of the nutrition labelling of that product shall be declared as condition as sold and prepared/cooked condition". The format shows two columns, "product" and "prepared product".
- Clause 2.2: every nutrient and energy value defined in a format must be declared even if insignificant under clause 2.6.
- Clause 2.3 (sequence list): "Total fat*, Saturated fat*, ... Cholesterol*, Protein*, Total Carbohydrate*, Total sugars*, Sugar Alcohols**, ... Sodium*, Potassium*". Remark: "*mandatory nutrients to be declared in the standard format of the nutrition labelling; **Their contents shall be displayed in the nutrition labelling if the energy value was calculated from total carbohydrate using the approach stated in No. 2.4 2) 2.2)", the approach that uses the conversion factor of each sugar alcohol.
- Clause 2.4: conversion factors include organic acid 3 kcal/g; sugar alcohols isomalt and lactitol 2; xylitol 2.4; maltitol 2.1; sorbitol 2.6; mannitol 1.6; polyglucitol 3.0; erythritol 0.
- Annex 3 (Thai RDI): sodium 2,000 mg; potassium 3,500 mg (English PDF p. 37).
- Clause 2.6 rounding (read from a rendered image in the first pass): sodium and potassium below 5 mg declared as 0; 5 to 140 mg in steps of 5; above 140 mg in steps of 10.

## 2. Notification 446 (B.E. 2566, FDA unofficial English translation)

- URL: https://food.fda.moph.go.th/media.php?id=585712415111520256&name=P446_EN.pdf (2 pages; given 17 December B.E. 2566; in force 180 days after Gazette publication).
- Clause 1: clause 5(1) of Notification 394 (B.E. 2561) is repealed and replaced with: "(1) Nutrition Labelling shall be complied with format and provisions of displaying of nutrition information of attachment No.1 of the Notification of Ministry of Public Health, Re: Nutrition Labelling".
- Reading (DERIVED): the simplified format of the original 394 clause 5 (1.2) (energy, fat, protein, carbohydrate, sugar and sodium only) no longer exists in the amended clause; the panel follows Annex 1 of 445, whose formats carry potassium. The Thai original of 446 was not read.

## 3. Notification 445, Annex 2 and Annex 4: reference serving and claim bases

- Annex 2, category 2 (beverages), item 3, English text layer: "Beverages, powder or concentrated, prepared before consumption | Defined amount for preparing the beverage of 200 milliliters". Item 2 (carbonated and non-carbonated beverages) is 200 milliliters. The reference serving of a beverage powder is therefore a product amount, the amount that makes 200 mL.
- Annex 2, remark: for a dry food that must be reconstituted "either by adding water or other liquid with insignificant nutrient content, the reference serving size shall be defined based on the food category of such food product".
- Annex 2, clause 4.1 (2): "the amount consumed per serving of food product shall not be higher than 200% of its reference serving size" (330 mL is 165 percent of 200 mL).
- Annex 4, clause 2.1, Case 1: for food with a prescribed reference serving size, claims follow Table 1. "1) ... more than 30 grams or more than 2 tablespoons ... calculated from the amount of food consumed per serving as declared on the label and the amount of food per reference serving sizes. 2) ... not more than 30 grams or not more than 2 tablespoons ... calculated from the amount of food consumed per serving as declared on the label and two-fold of the amount of reference serving size."
- Annex 4, clause 2.1, Case 2: other foods; claims are calculated per 100 g or 100 mL under Table 2.
- Table 1 and Table 2 sodium rows are re-confirmed in the English text layer: free "less than 5 milligrams"; very low "less than 35"; low "less than 140" (Table 1); free not more than 5, very low not more than 20, low not more than 60 per 100 mL (Table 2). "salt free, without salt" follows the "free of sodium" conditions. "unsalted, no salt added" needs "No salt added during the production process" and a reference food that normally uses salt, with the statement "not a food that is free from sodium" if the product is not sodium-free.
- Our reading (DERIVED): if the reference amount of the cube counts as not more than 30 g, a second basis applies, two-fold the amount that makes 200 mL, which is about 1.21 cubes made up to 400 mL (about 4.6 g of product, the 3.79 g cube scaled by 400/330). Whether the regulator applies this to a beverage powder is UNVERIFIED.
