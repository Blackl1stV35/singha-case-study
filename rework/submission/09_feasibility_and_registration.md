# Feasibility and Thai registration route

Status at 20 October 2026: desk study only, with no bench test, supplier quote or submission. The product is the Singha Lemon Soda Cube, shown as Lemon Soda. One cube per single-serve pouch is the team's setting (owner: marketing lead, with finance, by M2).

## Technical feasibility (desk)

The design dose is 0.55 volumes of CO2 per 330 mL serve (ASSUMPTION), counted as the gas the reaction releases at 0 C and 1 atmosphere (DERIVED). How much stays dissolved at serve is not measured. The working pH is 3.5 (ASSUMPTION); the desk model gives a pH of about 3.4 to 3.5. Taste is untested. The acid load is about 1.3 to 4 times that of Sprite, depending on the basis, and the balance of sweetness and acid has not been tasted (DERIVED; Sprite benchmark from a Serbian titration and a Peshawar assay, S).

Potassium bicarbonate at 0.81 g releases about 8.1 mmol of CO2 (DERIVED). Citric acid is 0.52 g stoichiometrically but 2.10 g to reach pH 3.5, leaving about 1.58 g free (DERIVED). The recipe adds a binder and filler of 0.80 g (mannitol, tableting grade, INS 421, with isomalt as the fallback; the amount is an ASSUMPTION, range 0.5 to 1.2 g), natural lemon flavour of 0.05 g (ASSUMPTION; range 0.02 to 0.10 g) and sucralose of 55 mg (ASSUMPTION). The central dry cube mass is 3.79 g (DERIVED on ASSUMPTION inputs; range 3.46 to 4.24 g, set by the binder and flavour allowances), in a 14 mm cube at 1.38 g/cm3 (band 1.25 to 1.60 an ASSUMPTION). Nothing is weighed. Limit: the 3.79 g was computed at the earlier sucralose dose of 33 mg. At 55 mg the listed components sum to about 3.82 g, and the central mass is restated at Gate 5. The 3.8 g net weight specification is unchanged within rounding. The acid and CO2 calculations use citric acid and carbonic acid pKa values at zero ionic strength (S, SOURCED: Wikipedia pages read 20 October 2026; the first carbonic acid constant, 6.35, is an ASSUMPTION from a textbook).

CO2 solubility at 35 C is 0.592 volumes (S, SOURCED, Lange-attributed; 35 C page basis ambiguous). The 0.55 dose sits below it at every modelled serve from 5 to 35 C (DERIVED). The NIST table (P, SOURCED) has no 35 C row and is not used.

The desk model gives the times below. None is measured, and all are DERIVED from literature solubility and diffusion values (S) on ASSUMPTION inputs. Dissolution at 25 C takes about 6 minutes stirred and about 11 minutes not stirred for a central set of assumed inputs, and 3 to 22 minutes stirred and 5 to 74 minutes not stirred across the assumed ranges. This holds only if the cube breaks into fine pieces. At 20 C, surface loss alone keeps the dissolved carbon dioxide above the 0.44 g/L sparkling threshold for about 1.7 to 3.4 hours if the full dose dissolves at serve. Bubble formation could cut this to about 15 minutes (champagne proxy, PROXY). If less than 41% of the dose is dissolved at serve, the drink starts below the threshold. The 0.44 g/L threshold is S, SOURCED (abstract only) and a PROXY: a mineral-water value applied to a soft drink.

One serve holds about 317 mg of potassium, 9% of the Thai reference of 3,500 mg per day (DERIVED from P, SOURCED: Notification 445, Annex 3, in the English translation published by the Thai FDA). The EFSA adequate intake for adults is also 3,500 mg per day (P, SOURCED). The desk model does not cover people on potassium-retaining medicines or with kidney disease, so a medical review at Gate 2 is required before any sale.

Dry-cube shelf life is not tested. The storage test (35 C for 8 weeks, an ASSUMPTION) starts by M2 under Gate 5, which passes only if the reaction in the pack is at most 0.5% of the bicarbonate at 35 C (ASSUMPTION). Bangkok monthly mean air temperature runs from 27.3 C in December to 30.7 C in April, with an annual mean of 28.9 C (P, SOURCED: Thai Meteorological Department climate normals 1991 to 2020, Bangkok Metropolis station). The 35 C test temperature is an ASSUMPTION.

Limit: the pass value for dissolved CO2 at serve is not yet set. Gate 3 restates the pass rule before the run (bench cubes at 0.55 volume made at a laboratory by M2; dissolved CO2 measured at 5, 20 and 35 C; owner: R&D lead), so no gate value is stated here.

## Thai regulatory route

The limits come from the flavoured-drink rows (category 14.1.4) of Annex 1 to Ministry of Public Health Notification No. 468, amendment No. 4 (P, SOURCED for those rows only). The full annex was not read (UNVERIFIED); the full notification was too large to fetch. A Thai FDA clarification, in force from 12 December 2025, says the 14.1.4 permission also covers sub-categories 14.1.4.1 to 14.1.4.3 (S, SOURCED; read in part, so it is not tiered P).

Condition 127 applies the sucralose limit to the drink as ready to drink, which matches the cube (P, SOURCED). Condition TH3 applies only to mineral-salt (electrolyte) beverage products, where it pairs the sweetener with sugar, and the annex text does not read a flavoured sparkling drink as one. This is a regulatory question, not settled.

The proposed class is flavoured drink (category 14.1.4). It is the team's setting, owned by the regulatory affairs head, by M1, and the Gate 1 opinion confirms it and tests the mineral-salt rule. The basis is that the pack makes no electrolyte, mineral or hydration claim, and that citric acid, not a mineral salt, is the largest solid by mass (about 55% of the dry mass; DERIVED; context only, because the notification gives no method for judging a main ingredient). Gate 1 asks for a written Thai regulatory opinion on the class, condition 127, the annex edition, the Notification 195 potassium check and the sucralose level. If no sweetener is permitted for the class, there is no sale.

Notification 195 covers Thai mineral-salt beverages. Its clause 4(1.3) sets potassium at no more than 195 mg per litre where potassium is present. The text is unchanged from the Gazette original through the Thai FDA compilation of about 29 March 2023 (P, SOURCED; read from page images). No later amending notification was found by title on the Thai FDA list to 20 October 2026 (P, negative finding), but a post-March-2023 amendment inside a general notification is not excluded. The four amending texts, Notification (No. 2) of 2011 and Notifications 413, 415 and 417 of 2020, did not change clauses 3, 4(1) or 7 (P, SOURCED; read from page images; listed in Sources). Whether the cube is a mineral-salt beverage under clause 3 is the Gate 1 question. Clause 3, as printed in the Gazette original, defines the class as a drink with mineral salts as its main ingredient, and no document read gives the Thai FDA's method for judging that test. The class is decided by that test, not by whether a drink meets the composition limits, so failing the limits would not take a drink out of the class. The cube holds about 960 mg of potassium per litre, about 4.9 times the ceiling. If the Gate 1 opinion classes the cube as a mineral-salt beverage, the drink as designed would not meet the composition limits of that notification (no sodium by design, no sugar, potassium above the ceiling), and the formula would have to change. The stop rule is no sale until the class or the formula changes.

A Thai food registration step applies as classified; its step, form, fees and timing are UNVERIFIED. The food serial number and label approval must be obtained before pack print. No registration number or outcome exists.

## Labelling and claims

The composition may be stated as fact: potassium bicarbonate, citric acid, sucralose, mannitol (binder and filler) and natural lemon flavour. The recipe specifies no sodium salt, uses no sodium carbonate and carries no lubricant. This is a specification, backed by a supplier declaration for each ingredient before pack print (Gate 9). The binder and flavour amounts are assumptions until a cube is weighed. Net weight on the label is the 3.8 g specification, verified by weighing before print (Gate 9).

Sodium from the recipe is 0 to about 2 mg a serve (DERIVED). Water adds its own sodium: 0 to about 7 mg a serve at the WHO typical level of 20 mg per litre (P; the WHO notes higher levels in some places), or about 33 to 68 mg in total at the 100 to 200 mg per litre reported for Thai tap water (S, a news report; not measured). The one-serve total is therefore about 0 to 9 mg at the WHO typical level. These are calculations, not measurements. No sodium-free or low-sodium claim is made.

Barred claims: sugar-free, zero-sugar and no-added-sugar wording; sodium-free, low-sodium and very-low-sodium wording; health, function, electrolyte, hydration and potassium-benefit wording; calorie wording; certification and award wording, none of which is verified; and validated-fizz wording. The drink does not meet the Thai standard for an electrolyte drink (Notification 195) and is not presented as one. Held until Gate 7 or a Thai legal review: "no salt added" or "unsalted" wording (Thai rules allow it only against a comparable food that normally contains added salt); novelty words; fizz, sparkling, bubble and CO2 release wording until a measured test exists; lemon fruit imagery, glassware and bottle shape; alcohol cues; and the lion or SINGHA beyond this proposal.

The potassium share and the modelled fizz duration are not printed on the pack. Potassium appears only where the Thai nutrition panel requires it, and any warning waits for Gate 2. A draft precaution for people with kidney disease or on medicines that raise blood potassium is prepared for the Gate 2 medical review. The one-cube daily limit in it is a team assumption, and no guideline sets it.

The Thai nutrition panel and the GDA panel, with sodium and potassium shown, are required declarations, not claims. Their values come from laboratory analysis before pack print (Gate 9). Where the pouch front is under 65 sq cm, Notification 394 allows the panels on the wholesale package (S, SOURCED; unofficial English text). The pouch front area is not set, so whether that rule applies is open.

The general food-labelling notification is not read: the Thai FDA list shows No. 450 of B.E. 2567, in force from 19 July 2024 (P for the list entry; that it is the general labelling notification is DERIVED), and its sweetener and nutrition rules are UNVERIFIED. Label text stays provisional until it is read.

## Classification and excise

Excise item 02.02 covers sweetened or flavoured drinks, including intensively sweetened ones. The 14% ad valorem rate applies if the value base applies; the base was not read (P, SOURCED from the saved copy of the Royal Gazette notice). Per-litre rates depend on sugar per 100 mL: 0 baht up to 6 g, and 1, 3 and 5 baht above (from 1 October 2566; same status). The desk reading assumes no added sugar, which places the cube in the 0-baht band; classification is open. Mannitol is a polyol, which the Thai nutrition definition does not count as sugar, and its total sugars are at most 1.0% (FAO and WHO specification). On that reading it does not change the excise tier.

The value case applies the 14% to the invoice value of THB 9.34 a cube, which is THB 1.31 of excise a cube (DERIVED on an ASSUMPTION about the value base). The cube's excise classification is not verified, so the value case uses the excise as an assumption.

Item 02.01 (no added sweetener, no flavouring) does not fit. Its rate is 0 baht per litre and 14% ad valorem if the value base applies (same status; the base was not read, so any baht figure for the 14% rests on an assumed base). Later schedules were checked only in a press report, which keeps the 0-baht band for up to 6 g (S, SOURCED); the primary decrees were not opened, and the Thai numerals were read from page images.

Classification (beverage, edible ice or electrolyte drink) is open and is not settled here; it decides the route, the excise line and whether TH3 applies. The proposed class is flavoured drink, as set out in the regulatory route above.

Before pack print, five actions are needed. The team's setting is that the brand and legal function owns the first three under Gate 7, by M2. The brand and legal function must confirm which entity owns the SINGHA mark in Thailand, because the Thai register was not reached. Written confirmation or a licence from the mark holder for the proposer's use must be obtained; that sign-off is a dependency on the mark holder's brand and legal function. The last two actions are regulatory and are not Gate 7 items: any food notification must be confirmed with the Thai FDA, and the food serial number and label approval obtained. They sit with Gate 1, the written Thai regulatory opinion on the class (owner: regulatory affairs head, by M1), and with Gate 9, the pack release, which must pass before print. The proposal names no separate owner for these two beyond those gates.
