# Effervescent tablet literature: opened sources and leads (saved excerpts)

## Opened (abstract or metadata read), used as support

1. Ansar (2011). "PENGARUH SUHU DAN KELEMBABAN UDARA TERHADAP PERUBAHAN MUTU TABLET EFFERVESCEN SARI BUAH SELAMA PENYIMPANAN" (Influence of temperature and relative humidity on the quality of fruit juice effervescent tablets during storage). Jurnal Teknologi dan Industri Pangan, 22(1), 2011, p. 73.
   - URL read: https://journal.ipb.ac.id/index.php/jtip/article/view/3399. Date read: 2026-10-20. Tier: P (peer-reviewed article; abstract read, full text not read). Status: SOURCED for the abstract statements below.
   - Abstract (paraphrase from the fetch, marked): storage at high temperature (35 C) made the bicarbonate react with citric acid and slowed dissolution; at high humidity (85.5 percent RH) the reaction started before dissolution because of moisture uptake. The tablet listed citric acid, sodium bicarbonate, polyethylene glycol, aspartame and fruit granules. No sugar alcohol or binder was discussed.
   - Use in the record: moisture control is the main storage risk for the acid-bicarbonate couple. This supports a low-moisture-uptake binder and a moisture test at Gate 5.

2. Ipci K., Oktemer T., Birdane L., Altintoprak N., et al. (2016). "Effervescent tablets: a safe and practical delivery system for drug administration." ENT Updates, 6(1), 46-50. DOI 10.2399/jmu.2016001009. Review.
   - URL read: https://avesis.ogu.edu.tr/yayin/74eaa77e-bedd-400c-8b66-971ad6689809/effervescent-tablets-a-safe-and-practical-delivery-system-for-drug-administration. Date read: 2026-10-20. Tier: P (review; abstract read). Status: SOURCED for the abstract statements.
   - Abstract statements read: "Common acids utilized for effervescent reactions are citric, malic, tartaric, adipic and fumaric acids." "Citric acid is the most common acid used and adds a citrus taste." "Tartaric, adipic and fumaric acids are usually used in small amounts, due to their low water solubility." "a medication can be absorbed easily and effectively if it dissolves easily in water and is present at a sufficient dose." Excipient, moisture and sodium content: not in the abstract (limit).

3. USP monograph "Potassium and Sodium Bicarbonates and Citric Acid Effervescent Tablets for Oral Solution" (drugfuture mirror of USP 35). Search result read only as a title and summary line. Status: lead, not used as a figure.
