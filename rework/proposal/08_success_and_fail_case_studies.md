# Success and fail case studies for the Lime Cube

Date: 19 October 2026. Desk research only; no consumer, bench or Thai shelf test has been run. No domestic case of a dissolvable drink was found, so overseas analogues appear only as labelled evidence.

## Tier key

P: a government or regulator text read in full, a company's own filing or release, official statistics, a peer-reviewed article read in full, or a primary database. S: any other source, including press, trade portals, consultancy pages, company web pages that are not filings, handbooks reproduced online, and search snippets. SOURCED: the page text was read, and the public URL is given. UNVERIFIED: not read, blocked, unreadable, or only a snippet was read. DERIVED: our arithmetic, with the inputs and their tiers shown. PROXY: a stand-in for what is not measured. ASSUMPTION: a value chosen for the model, not sourced.

## Analogues that worked (evidence)

Overseas unless marked Thailand; labelled evidence only.

- **Waterdrop drink cubes** (Austria, 2016; US, 2021). A trade article reports 20 markets (only Austria and the US named), over 25,000 retail doors, $700,000 first-year revenue (year not stated) and over $100m in 2023 [1] (S, SOURCED), figures not checked here. A 2023 trade note reports sales at Walmart and Target in California and New York [2] (S, SOURCED).

## Analogues that failed (evidence)

- **Fizzies drink tablets** (US; dry drink tablets), the closest format match. Regional launch in 1956; eight nationwide flavours from 1962 [3] (S, SOURCED). Discontinued in the late 1960s over cyclamate concerns; a federal cyclamate ban, announced in 1969, took effect on 1 February 1970 [3] (S, SOURCED). Revived in 1996 and 2005; gone by 2016, reason not given [3] (S, SOURCED). An encyclopaedia's early-1970s end date is uncited [4] (S, UNVERIFIED).
- **Coca-Cola Life** (UK; sparkling-cola match only). Withdrawn in the UK in June (year not stated) to focus on three main cola variants. Sales fell 58%, base not given, yet it stays on sale in more than 30 other markets, including the US [5] (S, SOURCED).
- **Aha sparkling water** (Coca-Cola; sparkling match only). Coca-Cola cited portfolio realignment. First-quarter 2023 sales fell 42%, with a 1.9% category share, both second-hand [6] (S, SOURCED). From 2024, sales were to be limited to focused channels [6] (S, SOURCED).

Launched, outcome not evidenced:

- **Santo Cubes** (coffee cubes; crowdfunding). Kickstarter launch on 16 October 2018; over 5,000 samples, 78% positive; retail only a target [7] (S, SOURCED). The campaign pledged $83,592 from 1,749 backers against a $30,000 goal [8] (S, SOURCED), about 2.8 times the goal (DERIVED). Pledges are not sales.
- **Ricola Drink Cubes** (company release, 5 June 2025). Each cube is stated to flavour 400 to 600 ml of water [9] (P, SOURCED).
- **1,2,CUBE** (Coca-Cola Japan; freeze-dried tea and coffee cubes). About 15 cubes per JPY 600 pouch, sold on Amazon Japan from May or June [10] (S, SOURCED; year implied). No sales figure.
- **Simply Pop** (Coca-Cola prebiotic soda). Launch from late February 2025 in select regions, at $2.49 per 12-ounce unit [11] (S, SOURCED). No withdrawal found.
- **Incrediballs** (New Zealand; effervescent drink tablets). Online orders from 16 February; company claims and targets only [12] (S, SOURCED).
- **Singha Sparkling Water** (Thailand; lime flavour; 330 ml can). The company release gives the lime flavour, the can and 15 baht [13] (P, SOURCED); a news reprint matches it [14] (S, SOURCED). The company calls the can sugar-free with 0% calories; that is its claim, not checked here.

## What the analogues imply for the cube

1. **Overlap on flavour.** The company already sells a lime sparkling water in Thailand. The products overlap on flavour and differ in form and pack: a 330 ml ready-to-drink can at 15 baht, against a dissolvable Lime Cube that makes the drink in the glass, one cube per pouch. The company's lemon soda is a separate product.
2. **Novelty.** Dissolvable drink cubes and tablets exist overseas, but none was found in Thailand. A Thai patent record for soluble coffee tablets does not show its legal status [15] (S, UNVERIFIED). The case rests on the consumption form and the fizz mechanism, neither tested on Thai shelves.
3. **Sweetener and composition.** The cube is made with potassium bicarbonate, citric acid, sucralose, a binder and filler (not yet named; not yet checked for sodium; sodium not claimed) and natural lime flavour, and no sodium carbonate. The binder and flavour amounts are assumptions until a cube is weighed. The model recipe uses 33 mg of sucralose per 330 mL, about 100 mg/kg (DERIVED; recipe ASSUMPTION). The Thai annex sets 300 mg/kg for sucralose in flavoured drinks under condition 127, which applies the level to the drink as ready to drink; only its flavoured-drink rows were read [16] (P, SOURCED). Potassium bicarbonate and citric acid are quantum satis; the binder, filler and flavour are not yet checked against that rule. Condition TH3 covers electrolyte drinks only, so the product's classification is open. A Thai FDA clarification carries a major-category permission into sub-categories, not upward [17] (P, SOURCED; pages 1 to 3 read; our translation).
4. **Carbonation.** The working dose is 0.55 volume of CO2 per 330 mL. Solubility falls as serves warm: about 0.655 volume at 30 °C and 0.592 at 35 °C [18] (S, SOURCED). The desk model keeps 0.55 volume within solubility from 5 to 35 °C (DERIVED), with about 0.04 volume to spare at 35 °C. A 2021 abstract gives an average detection threshold of 0.44 g CO2 per litre for sparkling mineral waters [19] (S, abstract only; PROXY for a sweetened soft drink). No gate is set, and no fizz claim is made until a measured test exists.
5. **Taste.** Untested; the target pH of 3.5 is a working anchor (ASSUMPTION). A 2025 abstract on four effervescent supplements reports bitterness, astringency and a metallic quality, with no numbers [20] (S, SOURCED; abstract only).
6. **Demand.** Santo's backers and the 1,2,CUBE launch are not retail evidence. Nearest domestic data: an August 2025 nationwide survey of 5,467 respondents ranked quality (24.55%), low price (24.12%) and convenience (22.06%), base not stated [21] (P, SOURCED). None covers dissolvable drinks.
7. **Risk.** Trade-press market sizes are undated or rest on an unread analyst note, so none is used [11] (S, SOURCED). A news headline dated 28 July 2026 says a new study questioned prebiotic soda claims; only the headline was readable [22] (S, UNVERIFIED). The proposal makes no claim of this kind.
8. **Patent.** EP 4,516,115 A2, published 5 March 2025, has a machine-translated claim 1 headline for a carbonate effervescent powder or tablet made with no sodium carbonate [23] (P, SOURCED for publication data and the headline). Its legal status is not checked; its pending status is a mirror's assumption, and a freedom-to-operate review is needed before any launch.

## Limits

- The desk model is not a measurement.
- Outcomes are not evidenced for the six launched analogues; absence of withdrawal is not success.
- Fizzies is the only dissolvable drink tablet in this set with a recorded withdrawal. This is a limit, not a filled gap.
- The 35 °C value rests on one reproduced handbook table; a standards database gives 0.839 at 25 °C (Ostwald basis) against 0.759 in that table [24] (P, SOURCED). R&D should confirm before any carbonation wording.
- Check the 300 mg/kg level against the official annex before any labelling or filing.
- Patent statuses and the product's classification are unresolved.
