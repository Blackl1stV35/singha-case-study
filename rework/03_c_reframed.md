# Finalist C, reframed: non-alcoholic Thai-herb RTD for price-led switchers and alcohol-free occasions

Status: reframed on 18 October 2026, on the user's decision. The sober-curious framing is dropped because no Thai source shows young adults drinking less, and the national NSO data show drinking rising from 2021 to 2024 (see [02_desk_kill_tests.md](02_desk_kill_tests.md)). Nothing here is tested yet.

## Concept (unchanged product, new target)

- A 250 ml chilled can of non-alcoholic herbal sparkling drink with Thai botanicals (lemongrass, butterfly pea, kaffir lime, pandan and ginger are the three bases in the test).
- Positioned for two occasions, not for a lifestyle: (1) drinkers who want to spend less on drinks and still have something in hand, and (2) people who want an alcohol-free drink on a particular occasion (driving, work events, a period of alcohol-free observance such as Buddhist Lent, nights out with friends who do not drink).
- No health claims. No alcohol brand cues, names or colours that could read as an alcoholic drink.

## Target

Two segments, tested separately:

1. **Price-led switchers:** adults who drink alcohol at least weekly and have cut spending on drinks in the last three months. Screen question: "In the last three months, have you cut back on what you spend on alcoholic drinks because of cost?"
2. **Alcohol-free occasion buyers:** adults who chose a non-alcoholic drink for a particular occasion at least once in the last month. Screen question: "In the last month, have you chosen a drink without alcohol for a specific occasion?"

Age is not fixed in the evidence. Propose 25 to 45 for segment 1, and 22 to 45 for segment 2, to be confirmed by the team.

## Evidence (Thai, and how strong it is)

| Evidence | What it shows | Strength |
|---|---|---|

Strongest Thai demand signal we have. The 81% figure includes low-alcohol beer, so it is not a zero-alcohol RTD number |
| Buddhist Lent campaign evaluation (Alcohol Research Center, via [Dailynews](https://www.dailynews.co.th/news/6044943/)) | 8.6 million (32.3%) reduced or quit drinking during Lent 2568 | Event-specific, no method given |
| Bangkok youth survey, July 2025 (Naewna, [source](https://www.naewna.com/likesara/907169)) | 72.3% want alcohol-free activities offered during Lent; 34.3% had never drunk | Advocacy network, n = 2,200, sampling not stated. Supports alcohol-free occasions, not price |

Not Thai, and not used as evidence: the Australian Gen Z survey, Mintel UK's 40%, Mintel Thailand 2023's preference for sweet non-alcoholic drinks (a preference, not reduction).

## What the reframe changes

- **Kill test (new):** a segment shows less than the team's threshold of "would buy this instead of an alcoholic drink on the occasion you described". Proposed threshold: 30% yes in either segment, to be set by the team before the test.
- **Price:** the old 50 to 70 THB test point was set for a sober-curious premium. Price-led switchers are the segment most sensitive to price, so the price ladder must start from what Thai shoppers already pay for non-alcoholic drinks. GlobalData's own analyst says high prices on low- and no-alcohol products push consumers to cheaper alternatives. **Open:** no shelf-price data has been collected. Needs a price audit (see next steps).
- **Regulation:** the reframe removes the alcohol-association question for the target, but the product still needs no alcohol cues under Alcohol Control Act s.32/3. Whether "alcohol-free" wording is allowed on pack is unverified.
- **Score:** consumer case is re-scored to **1 (provisional)** for the reframed target. It has a named target, a reason, and Thai evidence, but the evidence is vendor-level and not yet tested on the segments. Total stays at 6, so C ties A and B on the current scores, pending the test.

## Test (3 days, after the price audit)

1. **Price audit (desk, before the test):** collect shelf prices for non-alcoholic sparkling and herbal drinks, per 250 to 330 ml, at 7-Eleven, Makro, Tops and Lazada, with dated captures. Add beer prices per 330 ml can for comparison. This sets the price ladder.
2. **Blind taste:** 40 respondents per segment (80 total). Three bases, blind, plus a plain sparkling water control. Record preferred base and whether the drink reads as alcohol-free.
3. **Occasion question:** for each respondent, "Would you buy this instead of an alcoholic drink on the occasion you described?" Yes, maybe or no. The yes share per segment is the primary result.
4. **Price ladder:** show three price points from the audit, in random order. Record purchase intent at each.
5. **Stop rules:** if the yes share is below the threshold in both segments, C is killed. If it passes in one segment only, C is narrowed to that segment and the other is dropped from the entry.

## Not settled by this plan

- Whether the price-led segment will pay any non-alcoholic premium at all.
- Shelf presence: the store audit for C is still incomplete (see [02_desk_kill_tests.md](02_desk_kill_tests.md)).
- Unit cost for this can at the audited price. No cost data exists for this concept yet.
- Whether the Lent and occasion wording is safe on pack.
