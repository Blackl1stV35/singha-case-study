## Implementation plan

The plan starts at M0, the date of the Gate 0 record, which the executive sponsor signs. The evidence does not date M0, so every duration is an ASSUMPTION. No step, quote, test, sign-off or outside contact has happened. Outside contact starts only once the Gate 0 record names the provider (Setting 3). The plan follows the strategy in section 04 (the lead segment is men aged 25 to 34, with men aged 45 to 59 a later volume segment, and the proposition is form, not flavour) and the gates, KPIs and kill criteria in section 06.

## Marketing campaign

**Segment.** Men aged 25 to 34 are the proposed lead segment. That is a proposal, not a consumer finding. The desk model puts the gap between men aged 25 to 34 and men aged 35 to 44 at a median of about 0.6 to 1.2 points at a 30 percent baseline, depending on the model scale (0.6 on the odds scale and 0.9 on the share scale in the age-band model; 1.2 in the survey power simulation). On the odds scale its 90 percent interval at a 30 percent baseline runs from minus 0.03 to 1.39 points, so it includes zero (DERIVED from a desk model with unsourced inputs). Among six drinker groups compared per person, men aged 25 to 34 who drink alcohol rank first in 94 percent of simulated outcomes. By total reach no group leads clearly, and the order depends on how the groups are defined. The confirmed category band is men aged 27 to 44 (Bangkok sample, n = 400, S). The model rests on drinking of sugar-sweetened drinks, and the cube contains no sugar, so transfer to a sweetener-only product is untested. The drinking shares for men aged 25 to 44 and 45 to 59 are 68.6 and 65.4 percent [R, P, UNVERIFIED]. Use of sugar-substitute drinks is 0.6 percent at ages 25 to 44 and 1.0 percent at ages 45 to 59 [S, P, UNVERIFIED; the survey's column definition is to be checked before any figure is printed]. The survey does not split ages 25 to 34 from 35 to 44; that split is modelled. Men aged 45 to 59 are a later volume segment and are pended.

**Concept pre-test (Phase 2; no media, no sale).** A recruited online concept test, run only after the Gate 0 record names the panel provider and its cost (Setting 3). The provider and cost are not sized from sources. Screen: men resident in Thailand aged 25 to 59 who drank alcohol in the past 12 months and had a drinking occasion in the past three months. Exclude people working in beverage, alcohol, food retail, advertising and research, and anyone in a beverage concept test in the past six months. Cells: ages 25 to 34 and 35 to 44, with 45 to 59 as reference. Stimulus: one brand-blind concept, text and image, showing the mechanism only, and signed off under Gate 7 by M3. The earlier concept image is withdrawn; no glassware or wedge appears in the track 1 concept until the Gate 7 rulings. Primary measure: top-two-box purchase intent by cell. Secondary: one price of 15 baht (an ASSUMPTION), against the Singha Lemon Soda can (THB 13.54 to 17.00 online on 20 October 2026, S); occasion incidence; open-text comprehension, with health, diet and sugar mentions flagged; reaction to the sweetener disclosure; attention check.

Decision rule, fixed before the run (Setting 12). The lead is supported if the lower bound of the 95 percent interval on men aged 25 to 34 minus men aged 35 to 44 is above zero. It is reversed if the upper bound is below zero. Otherwise it is undetermined. The pre-test reads direction and size under the interval rule set out in Appendix C, and the five-point rule is applied as stated there. About 1,377 respondents per cell give 80 percent power to detect a five-point gap against zero (DERIVED). Under the five-point rule, the lower bound must reach five points, which at that sample needs an observed gap of about 8 points (DERIVED from the design's interval).

**Message rules (all phases).** Allowed: "Drop one cube into 330 ml of water and stir"; "Serving: one cube made up to 330 ml"; "Single-serve pouch"; "Contains an intensive sweetener: sucralose (working choice; the Gate 1 regulatory opinion is to confirm it)". The working name is used only after Gate 7 clears it; until then a neutral placeholder is used. Potassium may appear as a composition fact; any amount or warning waits for Gate 2.

Barred with no clearance route: sugar-free, zero-sugar and no-added-sugar wording; sodium-free, low-sodium and very-low-sodium wording; health, function, electrolyte, hydration and potassium-benefit wording; calorie wording; certification and award wording, since none is verified. The drink does not meet the Thai standard for an electrolyte drink (Notification 195) and is not presented as one.

Held until the Gate 7 rulings or a Thai legal review: "no salt added" or "unsalted" wording; novelty words; fizz, sparkling, bubbles and CO2-release wording, until a measured test exists; lemon fruit imagery and "made with real lemon"; glassware and bottle shape; alcohol cues; the company's taglines and campaign artwork; the lion or SINGHA without written sign-off; recyclable or plastic-free claims. No novelty claim is made until a Thailand-only shelf and novelty audit is complete. All copy is checked against these lists before any media, sampling or listing text goes out.
