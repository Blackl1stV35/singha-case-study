# EFSA sucralose re-evaluation: acceptable daily intake 15 mg/kg body weight a day (read 20 October 2026)

URL: https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/news/efsa-finds-sucralose-safe-when-used-currently-authorised-cannot-confirm-safety-extending-its  (EFSA news story, 17 February 2026)
Linked opinion (not opened): "Re-evaluation of sucralose (E 955) as a food additive and evaluation of a new application on extension of use of sucralose (E 955) in fine bakery wares", https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2026.9854 (the Wiley host returns HTTP 403 to the fetch tool, as for other EFSA opinions).
Tier P (regulator's own news story). Read through WebFetch (extraction). The topic page https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/sweeteners (last reviewed 17 February 2026) lists E 955 with "Re-evaluation completed in 2026" and gives no ADI itself.

From the news story (paraphrase, page of 17 February 2026):
- EFSA confirmed the acceptable daily intake of 15 mg/kg body weight per day, so the value was kept.
- It says current consumer exposure remains below that level (no numbers given on the page).
- It concludes sucralose continues to be safe for its currently authorised uses and could not confirm the safety of the proposed extension to fine bakery wares (prolonged high temperature, possible chlorinated compounds).

Use in the record: the design serve of 33 mg is 0.55 mg/kg body weight for a 60 kg adult, 3.7% of 15 mg/kg, and 1.65 mg/kg for a 20 kg child, 11% of 15 mg/kg. The FDA figure on the page `fda_sucralose_sweetness.md` is 5 mg/kg and is the stricter reading (11% and 33%).

Not opened: the JECFA evaluation of sucralose (the WHO JECFA database page was not reached; the Wikipedia sucralose page names JECFA only in a list of safety evaluators and gives no JECFA figure, and it states an FDA ADI of 15 mg/kg that contradicts the FDA page, so it is not used). The JECFA ADI stays UNVERIFIED.
