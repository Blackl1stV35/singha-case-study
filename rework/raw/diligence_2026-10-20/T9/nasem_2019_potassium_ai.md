# National Academies (NASEM) 2019 potassium Adequate Intakes (read 20 October 2026; re-read from the text layer after verification)

Source: "Consensus Study Report HIGHLIGHTS: Dietary Reference Intakes for Sodium and Potassium", March 2019, The National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine. Page 2, text and Table 1.
URL: https://nap.nationalacademies.org/resource/25353/030519DRISodiumPotassium.pdf  (report DOI 10.17226/25353; 4 pages, 266 KB; the fetch tool kept a local copy outside the repository)
Tier P (the standards body's own document). First read 20 October 2026 from the page image; re-read the same day from the PDF text layer (PyMuPDF), which matches.

TABLE 1 as printed (potassium; AI in mg/d; UL and CDRR columns "ND", not determined):
- Males 19-30, 31-50, 51-70, >70 years: 3,400 mg/d (footnote a, updated value)
- Females 19-30, 31-50, 51-70, >70 years: 2,600 mg/d
- Pregnancy and lactation rows: 2,900 and 2,800 mg/d for ages 19-50 (read from the image)
- Footnote b on the UL column (paraphrase): not determined because there is no toxicological indicator specific to excessive potassium intake.
- Footnote c on the CDRR column (paraphrase): not determined because the evidence for causality and intake-response is not strong enough.

Text, paraphrased from page 2 (the wording can be checked on that page):
- The potassium AIs are the best estimates of intake for apparently healthy people, not estimates of requirements.
- The report says the absence of an upper level "does not mean that there is no risk" from excessive intake, overall or for parts of the population.
- It says caution against high intake through supplemental potassium is warranted for some groups, particularly people with or at high risk of compromised kidney function.

The NASEM news release (https://www.nationalacademies.org/news/2019/03/sodium-and-potassium-dietary-reference-intake-values-updated-in-new-report) gives only the range "2,300 to 3,400 mg per day" for the other age groups.

What this page does not say: it does not distinguish potassium from food and from supplements in the UL statement; it says no UL was determined and that this is not reassurance. The slide caveat must carry the "no risk" disclaimer as the source words it.
