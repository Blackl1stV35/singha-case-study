# Track D: sugar-free sparkling botanical tea, end to end (draft for team consensus)

Status: not presentable as it stands. Partner required. Not final. For the team meeting.

## 1. Concept and target

- A sugar-free sparkling tea-based drink in a can. Singapore first.
- Botanical options: jasmine, roselle, butterfly pea (where allowed), Thai tea.
- Target (team proposal, to confirm): urban adults and Gen Z looking for a non-sugar alternative with a botanical story.

## 2. Product specification (working)

- **Base:** carbonated water with a tea or botanical extract.
- **Colour:** butterfly pea is the blue option. It is magenta at pH 0.5 to 3, purple at pH 3 to 4, and blue only at pH 5 to 8 (Fu et al. 2021). A sparkling base at about pH 3 will read purple or magenta, so the blue concept is unlikely to hold.
- **Sweetener:** a non-nutritive blend suited to a tea matrix (Reb D or M suggested by proxies). Tea astringency is untested.
- **Caffeine:** from green tea and any Thai tea. Label implications to check.

## 3. Regulation

**Singapore (first)**
- SFA record of views (rev. 9 September 2025), by plant part: butterfly pea flower (whole extract only; a purified colour extract needs separate SFA evaluation); green tea leaves (Camellia sinensis leaves, S/N 21, black or fermented Thai tea not named); jasmine flower (S/N 52 and 53); roselle flower, leaves and calyx (S/N 49); star anise (S/N 50); tamarind (S/N 117); ginger rhizome (S/N 133). Annatto is absent.
- Whole-part use avoids the novel-food route. Purified or fortified extracts need SFA evaluation.
- BCRS: the 330 ml can is in scope, with the Deposit Mark required from 1 October 2026.
- Sugar-free: 0.5 g or less per 100 ml. Health claims are barred.

**Korea (blocked as specified)**
- Butterfly pea is not approved as a food (MFDS, 2022). The Korean version would use roselle, jasmine or green tea instead.
- Functional wording is blocked without an approved raw material.
- 무당 rule and caffeine labelling to be read from the primary text.

## 4. Production route

- **Route:** partner required.

- No tea-extraction line, named partner or extraction capability was found in-house.
- Hot-fill or aseptic can route for tea: not identified. Sterilisation alters the catechin profile.
- Clarity and cold creaming (catechin and caffeine complexes): no group evidence.

## 5. Supply and cost

- Butterfly pea colour: USD 10 to 20 per kg FOB Vietnam (1,000 kg MOQ, self-reported); INR 18 per kg India (50 kg MOQ); a Thai seller (300 kg MOQ) with no price. At 100 mg per can, about USD 0.001 to 0.002.
- Roselle, jasmine and Thai tea extracts: no supplier, price or certificate of analysis found. Thai tea extract was not searched.
- Monk fruit (50 percent mogroside V): about USD 0.0034 per can at 10 mg.

## 6. Economics (`strategy/data/track_economics.csv`)

Exporter cost per can, standalone route: Singapore S$0.45 in-house, S$0.50 partner. Korea KRW 495 in-house, KRW 549 partner.

Required CIF for the unit-margin test:
- Singapore: **S$0.64 in-house**, S$0.72 partner.

Customs-average CIF (S$0.48, KRW 528) fails both routes. Importer-ceiling CIF passes:
- Singapore at S$3.00 shelf: CIF S$1.33, net 56 percent. Volume for a 20 percent operating margin (in-house, S$300k fixed): about 622,000 cans a year, about 0.15 percent of the category.
- Korea at KRW 3,000 shelf: CIF KRW 1,384, net 54 percent. Volume: about 0.84 million cans a year.

Track D needs a higher price than Track A (S$2.50 to 3.50 against S$2.00 to 2.50), so the price basis and the premium evidence matter more.

## 7. KPIs and kill criteria

- K1 and K2 as in Track A.
- K6 (binary): colour concept, botanical whole-part status, Korea substitution, caffeine label.
- Kill if the blue-pea colour cannot be held at acidic pH. This is a redesign, not a launch.
- Kill if no tea-extraction partner quotes a clarity-stable extract.
- Kill if the sensory test in a tea matrix shows the astringency or sweetness cannot be balanced.
