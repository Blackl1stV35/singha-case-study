# Segment decision: optimise conversion, pend volume (18 October 2026)

**Decision (taken by the user):** lead with conversion. Volume is pended for later.

**Lead segment for conversion:** men aged 25–34.

## 1. Why this cell, and what it rests on

Conversion is measured per person reached, so the ranking uses per-person potency: current drinking share × daily sugar-sweetened share (age-adjusted). Population size is excluded on purpose.

| Rank | Cell | Current drinking, 2024 | Daily SSB share (age-adjusted) | Per-person potency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Men 25–34 | 68.6% | 16.5% | 0.113 |
| 2 | Men 35–44 | 68.6% | 15.8% | 0.109 |
| 3 | Men 45–59 | 65.4% | 15.1% | 0.099 |
| 4 | Men 15–24 | 36.2% | 17.1% | 0.062 |
| 5 | Men 60 and over | 35.4% | 14.1% | 0.050 |
| — | Women 25–34 | 25.2% | 16.5% | 0.042 |

Sources: the 2024 NSO current-drinking shares by sex and band; the 2021 daily sweet-drink share (15.8%, pooled 25–59) with its age odds ratio. Fit and reach are left out because they are common or near-common across cells.

## 2. What the decision rests on, and what it does not

- **The men 25–34 lead over men 35–44 is weak.** Both bands share one published drinking cell (25–44, 68.6%). The gap comes only from the extrapolated sugar age gradient. Treat the 25–34 and 35–44 order as an estimate, not a finding.
- **The men 25–34 lead over men 45–59 is stronger.** It comes from the published drinking shares (68.6% against 65.4%) and the same age gradient.
- **Women are a much smaller pool per person.** Their drinking shares are 25% (25–34) and 20% (45–59), so the female cells are not competitive on conversion.
- **Nothing here is purchase data.** Per-person potency is a proxy. The consumer corpus has no age tags, so the age skew of current public consumer voice is unknown.

## 3. Pended: volume

Pended for later, not dropped. The volume lead is men aged 45–59:
- about 4.35 million current drinkers, 23% of the total (population 6.65 million at 65.4% drinking);
- the largest pool, but about 12% below men 25–34 per person.

**Reopen volume when:** the team has a channel that reaches the 45–59 band at low cost, or a purchase test shows the per-person gap does not hold.

## 4. What the conversion lead means for the candidate

- The conversion test cell is **men 25–34** against **men 35–44** (the closest per-person cell). This tests the ranking directly.
- The cube's fit is still unevidenced for this cell. The consumer map found mixing and chilled-drinking use among young consumers, but no statement with an age tag.
- The conversion metric is purchase intent per person exposed. Volume is not measured in this phase.
