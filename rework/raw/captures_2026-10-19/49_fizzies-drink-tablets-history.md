- History, as stated on the page (dates as given):
  - c. 1954: credited to Lem Billings, advertising for Emerson Drug Company (maker of Bromo-Seltzer). The page says the aim was to compete with Alka-Seltzer; customers disliked the salty taste, so fruit flavouring was tried.
  - 1956: regional launch in Pennsylvania in sugar-free cherry, lemon-lime, grape and orange.
  - 1962: Emerson acquired by Warner-Lambert; the brand expanded to eight nationwide flavours. The page calls it a "10-million-dollar industry" with no date. This is a market-size claim, not a sales figure with a period.
  - Late 1960s: discontinued over cyclamate concerns. The page says the sweetener was invented in 1937 and that two studies linked it to tumours in lab rats. In 1969 the US Secretary of Health, Education, and Welfare banned it from consumer products, effective 1 February 1970.
  - 1996: revived by Premiere Innovations with NutraSweet (berry, cherry, grape, orange, root beer).
  - Early 2000s: Premiere Innovations went out of business.
  - 2005: revived by Amerilab Technologies, sweetened with Splenda.
  - By 2016: no longer sold.
