# Thai hypertension, diabetes, ACE inhibitor and ARB use, saved extracts (T10)

Date read: 20 October 2026. Values copied from the pages named. Tier of source: P (peer-reviewed paper on national survey or national insurer database).

## 1. NHES VI hypertension, 2019 to 2020
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11562084 (Aekplakorn W et al., BMC Public Health 2024;24:3149, DOI 10.1186/s12889-024-20643-1). Adults aged 20 and over; 2019 to 2020 round enrolled 21,305, analysed 19,062.
- Age-standardised hypertension prevalence 2019 to 2020: overall 25.7%, men 26.8%, women 24.6%.
- Crude prevalence by age 2019 to 2020 (both sexes): 20-39 7.7%; 40-59 25.6%; 60 and over 60.6%. (2014: 9.8, 30.3, 53.2.)
- Care cascade 2019 to 2020 among people with hypertension: aware 51.5%; treated 47.9%; controlled 22.7%; control among treated 47.4%. "Treated" means aware and on prescribed blood pressure medicine in the past two weeks.
- Age-by-sex prevalence and treatment tables are not in the article (only adjusted odds ratios). Men 25 to 34 specifically: not published there.

## 2. Anti-hypertensive drug classes, Thai NHSO database
- URL: https://pharmacy.mahidol.ac.th/journal/_files/2022-49-2_2.pdf (Luangmonkong T et al., Pharmaceutical Sciences Asia 2022;49(2):121-128, DOI 10.29090/psa.2022.02.21.086). PDF text layer extracted with pdfplumber on 20 October 2026.
- Data: DMHT-DAMUS database of the National Health Security Office, random hypertensive patients treated at least 12 months at MOPH hospitals (887 sites in 2014, 996 in 2015); 32,749 patients in 2014 and 31,808 in 2015; mean age about 65; about 62% female; age 25-34 was 0.5% of patients, 35-44 4.4%.
- Table 2, prescribed class as share of all patients (single or combined): 2014: CCB 56.11%, ACEI 42.70%, diuretics 33.58%, anti-adrenergics 30.68%, ARB 14.81%. 2015: CCB 57.69%, ACEI 41.01%, diuretics 28.17%, anti-adrenergics 28.55%, ARB 17.35%.
- ACEI plus ARB together: 0.23% (2014) and 0.18% (2015), but that is a share of the 7,104 and 7,280 patients without co-morbid disease (Table 4, dual therapy), not of all patients; the overlap for all patients is not reported. It is used only as a small overlap proxy (0.18 points).
- Patients under 55 with no co-morbidity who were on monotherapy (851 in 2014, 979 in 2015; Table 5): ACEI 28.91% (2014) and 28.40% (2015); ARB 6.70% and 6.33%; CCB 33.25% and 40.35% of those monotherapy patients.
- Co-morbidity in this hypertensive group: diabetes 12.91% / 13.11%; chronic kidney disease 17.28% / 15.00%; ischaemic heart disease 4.42% / 3.88%.
- Caveats: 2014 and 2015 data; older, MOPH-hospital sample, not young men; shares are of patients, not of the population. It is a PROXY for treated hypertension in young men.

## 3. Rural primary care hypertension, 2024
- URL: https://globalheartjournal.com/articles/10.5334/gh.1515 (Global Heart 2025;20(1):116, DOI 10.5334/gh.1515). 1,000 people with hypertension at 36 primary care units in 14 provinces, June to October 2024.
- No antihypertensive 8.3%; single 49.9%; dual 31.3%; poly 10.5%. No MRA used by anyone.
- ACEi or ARB single therapy: 35.6% (ages 20 to 44) and 61.7% (ages 45 to 64). Comorbidities: diabetes 33.2%, CKD 13.6% (men 4.7%).
- Overall class percentages are in a supplement not read.

## 4. Diabetes, NHES VI 2019 to 2020
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12391394/ (Scientific Reports 2025;15:31620, DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-17619-5). Adults 20 and over; 20,181 in the 2020 round.
- Age-standardised diabetes 2020: overall 10.1% (9.0 to 11.1); men 9.3% (7.9 to 10.7); women 10.7% (9.6 to 11.8).
- By age (age-standardised, both sexes): 20-39 3.2% (2.1 to 4.4); 40-59 10.4% (9.1 to 11.6); 60 and over 20.3% (19.0 to 21.8). Treated overall 42.9%.
- Diabetes defined as diagnosis, glucose-lowering medicine or fasting glucose 126 mg/dL or more. Age by sex not reported.

## 5. Heart failure
- A Thai population prevalence of heart failure was searched for and not found (a review states it is unknown; a five-hospital 2018 to 2019 study gives 0.1% to 0.2% of hospital patients, not the population). Stated limit; no figure is used.
