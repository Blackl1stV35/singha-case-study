# The 99 mg potassium figure: where it appears and what was and was not read (T10, 20 October 2026)

## What the primary text says (read)
- 21 CFR 201.306, in the official GPO print (CFR 2014 edition, page 81) and in the eCFR current text, sets its lines at 100 mg of potassium per tablet (paragraphs (a) and (b)) and 20 mg per mL for liquids (paragraph (b)). The word "99" does not appear in the section. See `us_21cfr201_306_govinfo_cfr2014.md` and `ecfr_21cfr201_306_current_2026-10-20.md`. Tier P.

## The page that answers the 99 mg question (S)
- Council for Responsible Nutrition (trade body), "CRN Experts" answer, undated, read 20 October 2026: "The upper limit of 99 mg per dose for potassium is specific to OTC drugs"; OTC products need a warning statement above 100 mg of potassium per serving; FDA has not ruled on potassium in dietary supplements; manufacturers have followed the limit in practice. Saved in `crn_99mg_otc_drugs_2026-10-20.md`. Tier S.
- So 99 mg is an over-the-counter drug labelling convention just under the 100 mg line, followed by makers; it is not a rule for dietary supplements and not a figure printed in 21 CFR 201.306.

## Other secondary reads
- Wikipedia "Potassium in biology", labelling under supplements, read 20 October 2026 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_in_biology): says the FDA requires some potassium salts containing more than 99 mg to carry a small-bowel warning and cites the NIH ODS fact sheet of 2 June 2022. The same sentence gives "about 1.3 mEq", which does not match 99 mg (2.53 mEq). Saved in `us_21cfr201_306_potassium_salts.md`. Tier S, with an internal inconsistency. Superseded by the CRN page above.
- The NIH ODS potassium pages (health professional and consumer) returned HTTP 403 on 20 October 2026 (four attempts across the two pages). A search summary said the NIH consumer sheet gives 99 mg per serving as the usual supplement amount; that is a lead only and was not opened.

## Leads from a search, snippets only (not opened; not cited for any figure)
- A supplement-data site (verifiedsupplementdata.com/potassium/dosage-guide/) and a supplement-information site (scienceinsights.org) state that single doses are kept at 99 mg to avoid the warning. Snippet only.
- A search summary stated that the warning covers solid oral potassium above about 100 mg and that supplement makers cap each pill at 99 mg. Snippet only.

## What this means for the T10 record
- 99 mg is an OTC-drug labelling convention followed by supplement makers (S, CRN). The comparator the record keeps as primary is the 100 mg line of 21 CFR 201.306 (P).
- The cube serve is 3.17 times 100 mg and 3.2 times 99 mg.
