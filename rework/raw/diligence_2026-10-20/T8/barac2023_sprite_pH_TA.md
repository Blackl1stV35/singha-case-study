## Method (paper, Material and methods)
- 50 mL of beverage in a beaker, stirred on a non-heating magnetic stirrer; pH measured with a calibrated Consort C830 meter immediately after opening the package at room temperature (the teas were cooled to 22 C).
- Titratable acidity (TA): volume of 0.9613 M NaOH needed to raise the pH to 5.5 and to 7.0, added in 0.3 mL aliquots while stirring, until a stable pH reading. Each value is the mean of three determinations.
- The paper does not say that the samples were degassed, and it does not report CO2 loss. It says only that the beverage was stirred. Carbon dioxide loss during the stirred, open titration is therefore likely but not stated (INFERENCE).

## Table 2 (Sprite row and two comparators)

| Beverage | Initial pH (mean, sd) | TA to pH 5.5 (mL 0.9613 M NaOH per 50 mL) | TA to pH 7.0 (mL) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coca-Cola (HBC Srbija) | 2.56 +/- 0.06 | 0.54 +/- 0.05 | 1.73 +/- 0.07 |
| Sprite (HBC Srbija) | 2.71 +/- 0.04 | 1.87 +/- 0.03 | 2.85 +/- 0.06 |
| Schweppes Bitter Lemon | 2.54 +/- 0.03 | 2.72 +/- 0.05 | 4.37 +/- 0.05 |

Derived (DERIVED from the table, not reported by the paper): Sprite TA to pH 5.5 = 1.87 x 0.9613 / 50 mL = 35.9 mmol/L; to pH 7.0 = 2.85 x 0.9613 / 50 = 54.8 mmol/L (mmol of base per litre).

## Comparability caveat (carried into the record)
- The TA of 54.8 mmol/L is a total, which includes whatever carbonic acid was still in the beaker. Sprite at 3.8 gas volumes (Ali et al. 2022) holds about 170 mmol/L of dissolved CO2, which alone would need about 138 mmol/L of base to reach pH 7. The measured total is far lower, so most of the CO2 had left during the titration (INFERENCE). The basis for the cube comparison is stated in rework/71.
