## 1. Question

(a) What are the published speeds of a 330 mL aluminium can filling and seaming line, in cans per minute? (b) What are the published speeds of a pouch or sachet filling and sealing line for dry or powder products, in units per minute? Give the range and the source for each line type. Say whether the sources support the assumptions of 60 pouches a minute with 70 per cent efficiency, and of 20 to 120 pouches a minute.

## 2. Answer in brief

**Tier rule used in this record.** P is a maker's own specification page or data sheet on the maker's own domain (SOURCING_POLICY section 1), or a regulator or official source. S is a trade-press report, a directory, or any third-party copy of a maker document. A copy of a maker document on another site is S, whoever wrote the document. Every speed that a maker states about its own machine is also a COMPANY CLAIM (a maker's rating, not a measured rate), and is marked so in the tables.

**(a) 330 mL can line.** About 100 to 300 cans per minute from the maker table for mid-size filler and seamer lines (Youngsun, rated at 330 mL, page dated 2006 to 2009, P, COMPANY CLAIM). Installed lines reported in trade press are at 200 (CFT, West Berkshire, 2017, S) and about 483 to 550 (Sidel-supplied line, 2020, S, COMPANY CLAIM, with two figures that conflict inside one article). The Krones Craftmate low-output can filler is reported at 100 to 300 (trade press 2016, S, 330 mL not named), which matches the maker table's band. A Youngsun model rated at 600 is shown only as a label-conflicted row (its main table says 300 mL, its description says 330 mL), so 600 is not the top of the range. The large blocks (Krones Modulfill Bloc FS-C, KHS Innofill Can DVD) are rated up to about 2,200 to 2,250 cans per minute across all can formats. Those are not 330 mL speeds, and the Krones figure is a third-party copy of a brochure that calls its values examples from customer facilities. A bench-scale semi-automatic unit runs at about 8 cans per minute.

**(b) Pouch or sachet line.** Two line types need to be kept apart, because the sources support them differently.

- Pre-made pouch filling and sealing, dry or powder: about 15 to 80 pouches per minute across the maker pages read. The bottom is the start of Zonesun's stated range (15 to 60). The top is the Viking Masek 8SU-180, a two-lane (duplex) machine that names powders, at 80 on a dry-cycle basis. Single-lane and rotary models read run 40 to 60: Triangle/JDD 60 (dry cycle), Viking Masek 8S-285 60 (dry cycle), Viking Masek 8S-320 50 (dry cycle), PER-FIL Micro-14 40 maximum (S). Two secondary figures sit inside the range: WeighPack 70 (S, trade press, COMPANY CLAIM) and a 2019 trade-press report of 50 for the Triangle/JDD machine. The 10 per minute seen in the PER-FIL datasheet is only the bottom of that micro-filler's single-lane range (10 to 40); it is not used as the start of the range.
- 60 pouches per minute is a mid-range maker figure. It is not a ceiling: the 8SU-180 reaches 80 and the WeighPack claim is 70. The highest maker page read is not a market ceiling either; an unopened dealer-listing lead gives up to 90 for a PSG LEE duplex machine (not used, see the search log).
- No figure in the pre-made group is a with-product powder rate. The Triangle and Viking figures are dry-cycle figures. Zonesun does not separate dry and with-product rates. WeighPack and PER-FIL give no basis.
- Pillow or sachet form-fill-seal (bags formed from roll film), dry or powder: maker figures run about 20 to 160 packs per minute (Suzuki 20 to 160; Komack 20 to 200; Rovema BVK 260 up to 120; Rovema VPK 260 Speed row 120 and 80 for gusseted, with 180 labelled dry running; Rovema BV Core 260 nominal 160). The Syntegon SVX series reaches 300, but the SVX figure is not for the SVE 3822 LR page that was requested.
- No speed was found for a Hosokawa Pillow Pack machine, or for a Sidel or Ilapak pouch or sachet filler.

**Assumptions.**

- 60 pouches a minute: inside the pre-made maker range of about 15 to 80, in its middle to upper part (PROXY: a maker figure for other machines stands in for the team's line). It is a mid-range maker figure, not a ceiling and not a with-product rate. No source gives 60 with powder product.
- 70 per cent efficiency: not supported by any pouch or sachet source (ASSUMPTION). The only benchmarks are two vendor blogs on packaging OEE, which give a typical band of about 55 to 72 per cent and a world-class band of about 78 to 85 per cent. 70 per cent sits inside one blog's typical band and above the other's. OEE is also not the same measure as line efficiency.
- 20 to 120 pouches a minute: partly supported. The range is supported for pillow or bag form-fill-seal machines (20 from Suzuki and Komack; 120 from Rovema). It is not supported as the range for a pre-made pouch line, whose maker figures start at about 15 and top out at 80 on the pages read, so 120 is above every pre-made figure read.

## 6. Options and recommendation (the choice is the team's)

The line type decides which range applies. Three options follow from the sources.

- Option A, pre-made pouch: use the maker range of about 15 to 80 pouches per minute (P, COMPANY CLAIM, dry-cycle basis at the top end, no with-product figure). Treat 60 as a mid-range maker figure and not a ceiling. Keep 70 per cent as an ASSUMPTION until a trial or a primary efficiency source.
- Option B, formed pillow or three-side-seal sachet: the sources support 20 to 120 packs per minute (P, Rovema BVK 260 at up to 120, condition not stated; P, Suzuki at 20 to 160). Note that the 120 is a maker figure whose condition is not stated, so it is not shown as a powder rate with product.
- Option C, keep one range for both: label 20 to 120 as partly supported and do not present it as sourced for a pre-made pouch.

Recommendation: confirm the pack format first. The repo fixes the unit as one cube per pouch (`CLAUDE.md` section 5) and does not say whether the pouch is pre-made or formed, so the pack records are the place to check it. If the pack is a pre-made pouch, replace 20 to 120 with about 15 to 80 (P) and carry 42 per minute (60 times 70 per cent) only as a DERIVED planning point with ASSUMPTION inputs. If the pack is a formed sachet, keep 20 to 120 as the maker range (P). Either way, the efficiency stays an ASSUMPTION until a fill trial or a primary OEE source replaces it.
