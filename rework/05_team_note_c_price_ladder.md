# Team note: test price ladder for finalist C, and the unit-cost question

Date: 18 October 2026. For the team meeting. Nothing here is decided until the team agrees.

## What we are asking the team to agree

1. **The test price ladder:** 15, 25 and 35 THB per 250 ml can, shown in random order in the consumer test.
2. **The stop threshold:** a proposed 30 percent "yes" on "would you buy this instead of an alcoholic drink on the occasion you described", in either segment. The team sets the final number before the test.
3. **Who requests the unit-cost quote,** and from which co-packer. No cost data exists for this can yet.

## Why this ladder

The earlier test point of 50 to 70 THB per 250 ml is 200 to 280 THB per litre. The desk price audit ([rework/04](04_price_audit.md)) found:

- Mass-market zero-sugar cola and sparkling drinks read 26 to 86 THB per litre. Single cans at Tops read 16 to 17 THB per can, which is about 49 to 52 THB per litre.
- Thai herbal drinks read 26 to 133 THB per litre.
- Beer in 320 ml cans reads 96 to 139 THB per litre. The Makro prices are for licensed business buyers.
- Only imported flavoured sparkling water reaches the test point: Perrier at 198 THB per litre for a 30-can pack and 248 for a 10-can pack.

So 50 to 70 THB per 250 ml matches imported sparkling water, not the drinks price-led switchers would compare against.

The ladder covers the range the audit found:
- **15 THB (60 THB per litre):** inside the mass-market zero-sugar and herbal range.
- **25 THB (100 THB per litre):** the same per litre as the cheaper beers.
- **35 THB (140 THB per litre):** near non-alcoholic beer at Makro.

## The unit-cost question (the one that decides viability)

The ladder is a shelf price. The producer receives less than the shelf price, after Thai VAT (7 percent) and after retailer and distributor margins. The audit did not measure those margins, and the team needs them before any ladder point can be judged.

**Questions for the co-packer quote:**
1. Cost per 250 ml can, ex-works, for a carbonated botanical base (lemongrass, butterfly pea, kaffir lime, pandan or ginger) with a zero-sugar sweetener, at each volume the team sets.
2. Minimum order quantity, and set-up or tooling charges.
3. Whether the co-packer can fill 250 ml cans at all, and at what line speed.
4. Thai food registration (เลขสารบบอาหาร) cost and lead time for the product.
5. Retailer and distributor margins for non-alcoholic RTDs in Thailand, and listing fees.

**The rule for the team:** if the cheapest test point (15 THB) is below the net price the producer needs after VAT and margins, the concept is not viable at that price, however strong the demand. A passing demand test does not fix a cost gap.

## Evidence limits

- The price audit used online listings, not shelf prices. Single-can shelf prices at 7-Eleven and Makro are open.
- Several listings were out of stock, branch-dependent, or sold by a seller marked on vacation.
- The consumer segments have no direct Thai data. The demand evidence is a 2023 vendor survey (n = 508), a Lent-period campaign evaluation with no method given, and an advocacy survey of 2,200 Bangkok respondents.
