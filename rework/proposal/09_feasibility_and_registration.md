# Feasibility and Thai registration route

Status at 20 October 2026: desk study only, with no bench test, supplier quote or submission. The product is Lemon Soda. One cube per single-serve pouch is the team's setting for the sold unit; its owner is the marketing lead, with finance, by M2.

## Technical feasibility (desk)

The design dose is 0.55 volumes of CO2 per 330 mL serve (ASSUMPTION) at a working pH of 3.5 (ASSUMPTION); the desk model gives a pH of about 3.4 to 3.5 (DERIVED). Taste is untested. The 0.55 volume is the carbon dioxide the reaction releases, expressed at 0 C; how much stays dissolved at serve is not measured. Potassium bicarbonate at 0.81 g releases about 8.1 mmol of CO2 (DERIVED). Citric acid is 0.52 g stoichiometrically but 2.10 g to reach pH 3.5, leaving about 1.58 g free (DERIVED). Sucralose is 55 mg a serve (ASSUMPTION). Basis B adds a binder and filler of 0.80 g mannitol, tableting grade (INS 421), with isomalt as the fallback (ASSUMPTION for the amount; range 0.5 to 1.2 g), and natural lemon flavour of 0.05 g (ASSUMPTION; range 0.02 to 0.10 g). The central dry cube mass is 3.79 g (DERIVED on ASSUMPTION inputs; range 3.46 to 4.24 g), in a 14 mm cube at 1.38 g/cm3 (band 1.25 to 1.60 an ASSUMPTION). Nothing is weighed. The acid and CO2 calculations use citric acid, carbonic acid and water pKa values (S, UNVERIFIED: not read).

CO2 solubility at 35 C is 0.592 volumes (S, SOURCED, Lange-attributed; 35 C page basis ambiguous). The 0.55 dose sits below it at every modelled serve from 5 to 35 C (DERIVED). The NIST table (P, SOURCED) has no 35 C row and is not used.

Dissolution is a model estimate at 25 C, not measured (DERIVED on ASSUMPTION inputs): about 6 minutes stirred and about 11 minutes not stirred for a central set of assumed inputs, and 3 to 22 minutes stirred and 5 to 74 minutes not stirred across the assumed ranges. This holds only if the cube breaks into fine pieces.

Fizz is a model estimate at 20 C, not measured (DERIVED on ASSUMPTION inputs). Surface loss alone keeps the dissolved carbon dioxide above the 0.44 g/L sparkling threshold for about 1.7 to 3.4 hours if the full dose dissolves at serve. Bubble formation could cut this to about 15 minutes (PROXY: champagne). If less than 41 percent of the dose is dissolved at serve, the drink starts below the threshold. The 0.44 g/L threshold is S, SOURCED (abstract only) and a PROXY: a mineral-water value applied to a soft drink.

One serve holds about 317 mg of potassium (DERIVED), which is 9 percent of the Thai reference of 3,500 mg per day (DERIVED from a P reference: Notification 445 Annex 3, row 32, in the English translation published by the Thai FDA; the Thai gazetted text was not read). Per litre the cube holds about 960 mg of potassium (DERIVED). The EFSA adequate intake for adults is also 3,500 mg per day (P, SOURCED). The desk model does not cover people on potassium-retaining medicines or with kidney disease. A medical review of the serve and the precaution wording is required at Gate 2 before sale.

Dry-cube shelf life is not tested. The storage test (35 C for 8 weeks, ASSUMPTION) starts by M2 (Gate 5). The 35 C storage temperature is an ASSUMPTION; no Bangkok climate figure is used here to set it.

Gate 3 tests the dose: bench cubes at 0.55 volume are made at a laboratory by M2, and dissolved CO2 is measured at 5, 20 and 35 C. The R&D lead restates the pass rule before the run (by M1). The 0.55 volume dose is held after one rework, and a dose or acid failure after that stops the project. The pass value is not yet stated, so none is given here. Gate 4 tests taste: a blind test at pH 3.5 and either side, passing at a blind preference of at least 55 percent (ASSUMPTION), with one acid reset and then a stop.

## Thai regulatory route

The limits come from the flavoured-drink rows (category 14.1.4) of Annex 1 to Ministry of Public Health Notification No. 468, amendment No. 4 (P, SOURCED for those rows only). The full annex was not read (UNVERIFIED); the full notification was too large to fetch. A Thai FDA clarification, in force from 12 December 2025, says the 14.1.4 permission also covers sub-categories 14.1.4.1 to 14.1.4.3 (S, SOURCED; read in part, so it is not tiered P).

Condition 127 applies the sucralose limit to the drink as ready to drink, which matches the cube (P, SOURCED). Condition TH3 applies only to electrolyte (mineral) drinks, and the annex text does not read a flavoured sparkling drink as one. This is a regulatory question for Gate 1, not settled.

Notification 195 (Thai mineral-salt beverages, B.E. 2543) sets, in clause 4(1.3), potassium at no more than 195 mg per litre where it is present. Clause 4(1) governs the class that clause 3 defines: a drink with mineral salts as its main ingredient, dry forms included. The text of clauses 3, 4(1) and 7 is unchanged from the Gazette original of 24 January 2001 through the Thai FDA compilation of about 29 March 2023 (P; read from page images). The amendments of 2011 and 2020 did not change clauses 3, 4(1) or 7 (P). No later amending notification was found by title on the Thai FDA list to 20 October 2026 (P, negative finding from page extraction). A post-March-2023 amendment inside a general notification is not excluded, because the Royal Gazette site returned HTTP 403 and its index could not be searched (stated limit). The cube holds about 960 mg of potassium per litre, about 4.9 times the ceiling (DERIVED). Whether the cube is a mineral-salt beverage under clause 3 is the Gate 1 question; no Thai FDA guidance on how "main ingredient" is judged was found (stated limit). If the cube were classed as a mineral-salt beverage, the formula would have to change: clause 6 would require a dry drink to be packed for dissolving in 250 mL, where the cube is made up in 330 mL, and clause 7(2) would require red-bordered label statements (our reading of the Thai text, P; not legal advice). Potassium above the ceiling for the chosen class means no sale until the class or the formula changes.

The team's setting for the class is flavoured drink (category 14.1.4). The Gate 1 written Thai regulatory opinion confirms the class, tests the mineral-salt rule, and covers condition 127, the annex edition, the Notification 195 potassium check and the sucralose level. The owner is the regulatory affairs head, by M1. If no sweetener is permitted for the class, there is no sale.

A Thai food registration step applies as classified; its step, form, fees and timing are UNVERIFIED. The food serial number and label approval are to be obtained before pack print. No registration number or outcome exists.

## Labelling and claims

The ingredient list may be stated as a specification: potassium bicarbonate, citric acid, sucralose, mannitol (binder and filler) and natural lemon flavour. The recipe specifies no sodium salt, uses no sodium carbonate and carries no lubricant. Each ingredient is backed by a supplier declaration before pack print (Gate 9). The binder amount (0.80 g) and the flavour amount are assumptions until a cube is weighed. Net weight on the label is the 3.8 g specification, verified by weighing before print (Gate 9).

Sodium from the recipe is 0 to about 2 mg a serve (DERIVED). The water adds its own sodium: 0 to about 7 mg a serve at the WHO typical level of 20 mg per litre (P), or about 33 to 68 mg at the 100 to 200 mg per litre reported for Thai tap water (S, a news report; not measured). These are calculations, not measurements. No sodium-free, very-low-sodium or low-sodium claim is made. The Notification 445 limits are per serving (sodium-free below 5 mg, very low below 35 mg, low below 140 mg; P, Table 1). A claim would need sodium measured in the made-up drink from three production batches by an accredited laboratory, with the water named, and the Gate 1 opinion.

Barred claims (not made): sugar-free, zero-sugar and no-added-sugar wording; sodium-free, low-sodium and very-low-sodium wording; health, function, electrolyte, hydration and potassium-benefit wording; calorie wording; and certification and award wording, none of which is verified. The drink does not meet the Thai standard for an electrolyte drink (Notification 195) and is not presented as one. Held until Gate 7 or a Thai legal review: "no salt added" or "unsalted" wording (Thai rules allow it only against a comparable food that normally contains added salt); novelty words; fizz, sparkling, bubbles and CO2 release until a measured test exists; lemon fruit imagery, glassware and bottle shape; alcohol cues; and the lion or SINGHA beyond this proposal without written sign-off.

The Thai nutrition panel and GDA panel, with sodium and potassium shown, follow Notification 445 Annex 1 and Notification 394 (P, English texts opened; the Thai gazetted texts were not read). They are declarations, not claims, and their values come from laboratory analysis before pack print (Gate 9). If potassium is declared, the Thai rounding gives 320 mg, about 9 percent of the Thai reference. Where the pouch front is under 65 sq cm, Notification 394 allows the panels on the wholesale package; the pouch front area is not yet set. The 9 percent figure and the fizz estimates are not claims and stay off the pack front. Any warning waits for Gate 2. The Thai sweetener declaration rule was not read for this file (UNVERIFIED), so the wording "Contains an intensive sweetener: sucralose" rests on sucralose as a working choice until the Gate 1 opinion. The labelling notification that Notification 195 clause 7(1) now reaches is not settled (the Thai FDA list shows No. 450 B.E. 2567; DERIVED, to confirm at Gate 1). Label text stays provisional until it is read.

The pack precaution is a draft for the Gate 2 reviewer: "Contains about 320 mg of potassium per cube. Ask a doctor or pharmacist before drinking if you have kidney disease or take medicines that can raise blood potassium. Do not drink more than one cube a day." The daily limit is a team assumption; no guideline sets it. A Thai medical reviewer has not checked the wording.

## Classification and excise

Excise item 02.02 covers sweetened or flavoured drinks, including intensively sweetened ones. The 14% ad valorem rate applies if the value base applies; the base was not read (P, SOURCED from the saved copy of the Royal Gazette notice). Per-litre rates depend on sugar per 100 mL: 0 baht up to 6 g, and 1, 3 and 5 baht above (from 1 October 2566; same status). The desk reading assumes no added sugar, which places the cube in the 0-baht band; classification is open. The binder, mannitol, is a polyol that the Thai nutrition definition does not count as sugar, and its total sugars are at most 1.0 percent (JECFA specification, P), so it does not change the excise tier.

Item 02.01 (no added sweetener, no flavouring) does not fit. Its rate is 0 baht per litre and 14% ad valorem if the value base applies (same status; the base was not read, so the 14% cannot be converted to baht). Later schedules were not searched, and the Thai numerals were read from page images.

Classification (beverage, edible ice or electrolyte drink) is open and is not settled here; it decides the route, the excise line and whether TH3 applies. The team's setting is flavoured drink, and the Gate 1 opinion confirms it. The value case uses an excise of THB 1.31 a cube as an assumption (DERIVED on ASSUMPTION inputs); the cube's excise classification behind it is not verified.
