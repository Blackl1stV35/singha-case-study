# Torrico et al. 2015: bitterness recognition thresholds of caffeine and potassium chloride in solution (abstract record)

- Source: D. D. Torrico, A. Sae-Eaw, S. Sriwattana, C. Boeneke, W. Prinyawiwatkul, "Oil-in-water emulsion exhibits bitterness-suppressing effects in a sensory threshold study", Journal of Food Science 80(6), S1404-S1411 (2015), doi 10.1111/1750-3841.12901
- URL read: https://repository.lsu.edu/animalsciences_pubs/1523 (institutional repository record with the abstract; the full paper was not opened)
- Date read: 2026-10-20 (WebFetch of the record, model-summarised; the number below was returned as printed)
- Tier: S (abstract level). Status: SOURCED at abstract level.

## Extracted facts
- The study measured detection and recognition thresholds of NaCl, caffeine and KCl in water and in two canola oil emulsions (20 percent by weight), by the best estimate threshold method.
- Bitterness recognition thresholds of caffeine and KCl were lower in solution than in the emulsions.
- The abstract prints one combined range for the bitterness recognition threshold (group best estimate threshold geometric means) of 0.0242 to 0.0586 g per 100 mL in solution. The summary of the record states that caffeine and KCl are NOT separated in this range, so it is not a KCl-only figure.
- Gender had a marginal effect on thresholds (as reported in the abstract).

## Use in rework/71
- Context only, next to the Schiffman detection thresholds: 0.0242 to 0.0586 g/100 mL is 0.24 to 0.59 g/L. The cube carries 24.5 mmol/L of potassium, which as potassium chloride mass would be 1.83 g/L (0.183 g/100 mL), about 3 to 8 times the printed combined range. The cube's potassium is bicarbonate and citrate potassium, not chloride, in a sweet and sour matrix, so the comparison is an order of magnitude only (a PROXY).

## Limits
- Abstract only; caffeine and KCl are not separated; no metallic quality threshold is given.
