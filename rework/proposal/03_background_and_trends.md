## Background: the group and its beverage business

Boon Rawd Brewery is the holding company, following a 2000 reorganisation. Singha Corporation was set up in 2001. Singha Corporation is a subsidiary of Boon Rawd Brewery and manages more than 150 group companies (S; current English home page, undated, read 19 October 2026; the count of more than 150 is from the About Us page, Source 1). It reports directly to the Boon Rawd board (Source 1; S; SOURCED).

The current English home page describes Singha Corporation as a subsidiary of Boon Rawd Brewery. A different affiliate wording, on a page with a 2014 footer, is not used (S). The beverages category lists six products: Singha Water, Singha Soda, Singha Lemon Soda, Purra, Purra Vitamin Water and FIJI (Source 2). Treating that category as the nearest unit for a non-alcoholic product is an ASSUMPTION.

The group already sells a lime sparkling water, which is a different product from Lemon Soda. Its launch release, dated 13 March 2026, describes Singha Sparkling Water Lime Flavour, a 330 ml can priced at 15 baht, launched in March 2026 (Source 3; P; SOURCED). A news reprint posted 17 March 2026 gives the same flavour, can size and price (Source 4; S; SOURCED). The release calls the can sugar-free with 0% calories; this is the company's claim, not checked here. It gives no ingredient list, and carbonation and canning capacity are not evidenced here.

The Singha Lemon Soda can overlaps Lemon Soda on flavour. The difference lies in form and pack. Lemon Soda is a dissolvable cube that makes the drink in the glass, one cube per pouch. The company's lemon soda is a ready-to-drink 330 mL can, listed on the company product page (Source 5; S; SOURCED; the page is undated), and the page's sugar-free wording is a COMPANY CLAIM. Lemon Soda is the display name used in this proposal for the Singha Lemon Soda Cube, and any use of the SINGHA name or the lion on a pack needs written sign-off at Gate 7.

The can's launch is reported as August 2020 in a launch release carried by the trade press (Brand Buffet, PR-tagged, 19 August 2020; the article prints no launch day) and as late 2020 in two later articles, so the month is not settled (Source 16; S; SOURCED). The lemon can launched at THB 16 in 2020 (Source 16; S; SOURCED). On 20 October 2026 a single can is listed at THB 17.00 at Lotus's Shop Online. A 24-can pack is listed at THB 330 (THB 13.75 a can) at 7-Eleven ALL Online and Lotus's Shop Online and at THB 325 (THB 13.54 a can) at Makro PRO (Source 17; S; SOURCED for the listed pack prices; the per-can figures are DERIVED). Across six retail rows the band is THB 13.54 to 17.00 a can, with a median of THB 13.75 (S; DERIVED). These are online prices; no store shelf price was read. The assumed THB 15 shelf price for a Lemon Soda serve sits above the can's pack prices and below its single-can price (DERIVED comparison; the THB 15 price is an ASSUMPTION).

Limit: no consolidated accounts, innovation budget or group R&D spend were available, and no group financial ratio is used. Press and company sources describe the group structure differently, so the wording above follows the company's own English pages.

Potassium and class. One serve holds about 317 mg of potassium, which is 9% of the 3,500 mg Thai reference (DERIVED; the reference is P, Source 22), and about 960 mg per litre (DERIVED). A Thai notification for mineral-salt beverages (No. 195, clause 4(1.3)) sets potassium at no more than 195 mg per litre where it is present, so the cube would hold about 4.9 times that ceiling if it falls in that class (DERIVED). The text of the clause is unchanged from the Gazette original through the Thai FDA compilation of about 29 March 2023 (Source 21; P; read from page images). No later amending notification was found by title on the Thai FDA list to 20 October 2026 (P, negative finding), but a post-March-2023 amendment inside a general notification is not excluded. The 2011 and 2020 amendments did not change clauses 3, 4(1) or 7. Whether the cube is a mineral-salt beverage under clause 3 is the Gate 1 question. If it were classed as a mineral-salt beverage, the formula would have to change. A medical review of the potassium is required at Gate 2 before any sale.

Sodium. The recipe specifies no sodium salt, and sodium from the recipe is 0 to about 2 mg a serve (DERIVED; the supplier declarations that would back the specification are an ASSUMPTION until they are in hand, Gate 9). Water adds its own sodium: 0 to about 7 mg a serve at the WHO typical level of 20 mg per litre (Source 23; P; the WHO notes higher levels in some places), or about 33 to 68 mg at the 100 to 200 mg per litre reported for Thai tap water (Source 23; S, a news report; not measured). No sodium-free or low-sodium claim is made. These are calculations, not measurements.

Overall: the format carries the central proposition, but no domestic evidence yet shows a Thai buyer for it. The target is the weakest fit. Conversion figures in this proposal are model results, not consumer findings.

## Limits

Several pages were read through an automated summary, so their figures are not checked against the live page. No domestic study of dissolvable cubes has been found. Thai online retail and price-aggregator listings were searched on 20 October 2026 and no dissolvable drink cube was found (S); that is a not-found result, not proof of absence, and no in-store shelf search has been run. Global market-size estimates are not used. The sources disagree on how the group is structured and on the 2025 direction of the Thai carbonated soft-drink market, and no market value has a stated retail or manufacturer level.

The carbon dioxide, pH, dissolution and fizz figures are desk-model results. No bench or sensory test has been run, and taste is untested. The desk model gives a pH of about 3.4 to 3.5, and the working pH of 3.5 is an ASSUMPTION. At 25 C the model gives a dissolution time of about 6 minutes stirred and about 11 minutes not stirred for a central set of assumed inputs, and 3 to 22 minutes stirred and 5 to 74 minutes not stirred across the assumed ranges; this holds only if the cube breaks into fine pieces. At 20 C, surface loss alone keeps the dissolved carbon dioxide above the 0.44 g/L sparkling threshold for about 1.7 to 3.4 hours if the full dose dissolves at serve; bubble formation could cut this to about 15 minutes (champagne proxy). If less than 41% of the dose is dissolved at serve, the drink starts below the threshold (DERIVED from literature solubility and diffusion values, S, on ASSUMPTION inputs; the 0.44 g/L threshold is a PROXY, Source 24, S, abstract only). The R&D lead restates the pass rule for the 0.55-volume dose before the bench run (section 10, Gate 3, by M1); none is set here.

Only the flavoured-drink rows of the Thai food-additive annex were read, not the full annex. Notification 195 was read from page images, and its status after March 2023 rests on a negative finding by title. Unverifiable figures, including two Thai consumer surveys and two trend percentages, are not used. The Singha Lemon Soda can's national retail reach, the number of outlets that carry it and its in-store shelf price are not verified here; its online prices were read on 20 October 2026.
