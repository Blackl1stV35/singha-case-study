# Problem and pain point

## Tier key

P: a government or regulator text read in full, a company's own filing, official statistics, a peer-reviewed article read in full, or a primary database. S: any other source, including press, trade portals, consultancy pages, company web pages that are not filings, handbooks reproduced online, and search snippets. SOURCED: the page text is saved in rework/raw and was read; this is the repository rule. UNVERIFIED: the page was not saved, or only a snippet or summary was read; a figure stays UNVERIFIED until its page is saved. DERIVED: our arithmetic, with the inputs and their tiers shown. PROXY: a stand-in for what is not measured. ASSUMPTION: a value chosen for the model, not sourced.

## Problem statement

The need is a lime drink made in the glass, quick to make and fair in price per serve, for Thai adults who already drink bottled non-alcoholic drinks. 18.2% of people aged 25 to 44 drink them every day, against 16.0% of those aged 45 to 59 [P, SOURCED; NSO 2021, Table 20]. No Thai source tests a sparkling lime form, so it is a hypothesis.

The named target is men aged 25 to 34, compared with men aged 35 to 44; men aged 45 to 59 are a later volume segment. Men aged 25 to 44 report current alcohol drinking at 68.6%, against 65.4% for men aged 45 to 59 [P, UNVERIFIED; printed page not checked]. This is an occasion proxy, not a sugar measure; no source splits the 25 to 34 and 35 to 44 bands.

The problem is one of form, not flavour. The competition brief asks for new ingredients or production, or a new form of consumption. The group already sells zero-sugar soda lines, including a zero-sugar lime sparkling water launched in March 2026 [S, UNVERIFIED; company pages and launch coverage, not saved], so a new lime flavour alone would not be new. The central claim is a new consumption form: a dissolvable cube, in a pouch, that makes the drink in the glass. Scope is domestic Thailand only.

## What is not known

- **No consumers, sales or category data.** No respondents, panel, Singha sales data or Thai retail audit exists, so nothing shows what Thai buyers would pay, drink, try or repeat.
- **The lead is a model result.** The model puts men aged 25 to 34 ahead in about 94% of draws [DERIVED from the age gradient (P, NSO 2021 age bands) and an ASSUMPTION elasticity, uniform 0.5 to 1.0]. The median gap is 0.5 to 0.7 points across baselines of 20 to 50% [DERIVED, same inputs]; a five-point lead has probability zero [DERIVED, same inputs]. The per-year age odds ratio's 95% interval, 0.991 to 1.001, includes 1.0 [DERIVED from NSO 2021 age bands (P); odds-ratio table not located, so UNVERIFIED].
- **The proxy may not transfer.** The age pattern is a sugar-based proxy; the cube uses an intensive sweetener. Drinks with sugar substitutes run the other way: everyday use is 0.6% at 25 to 44 and 1.0% at 45 to 59 [P, SOURCED; NSO 2021, Table 22].
- **Occasions are unverified.** Of 83 public statements, 35 name a Singha product; 29 of the 35 fall into eight segments; 15 of the 29 (52%) come from one platform [S, UNVERIFIED; DERIVED: 15 ÷ 29, inputs S, UNVERIFIED].
- **Novelty is untested.** The Thai patent register is unread. A European application published 5 March 2025 (EP 4,516,115 A2, pending) claims a sodium-free effervescent carbonate with citric acid, and in dependent claims potassium hydrogen carbonate [P, UNVERIFIED; machine-translated claims; freedom to operate not checked]. Whether the cube is new is unproven.
- **The dose is under review.** The design is 0.9 volume of CO2 per 330 mL, needing at least 0.6 volume dissolved at 10 minutes at serves of 30 °C or below [ASSUMPTION for the gate value]. Solubility at 30 °C and 35 °C is 0.655 and 0.592 volumes [S, UNVERIFIED; Lange-attributed table], so 0.9 volume exceeds the ceiling above about 19 °C [DERIVED from the solubility values above]. The 0.55-volume alternative: gate not set; to be defined by R&D.
- **Cost is a model.** No Thai price was found. The model gives THB 3.90 per cube at pH 3.5 in a 12-cube carton [DERIVED from PROXY inputs]. The chosen THB 1.30 pouch base is near the midpoint (1.34) of a non-Thai listing of USD 0.03 to 0.05 per piece, at 33.55 THB per USD from the Bank of Thailand (1.01 to 1.68 THB) [S, PROXY, UNVERIFIED; ASSUMPTION for the base]. The THB 0.67 to 2.70 pouch range gives THB 3.27 to 5.30 per cube [DERIVED: 2.60 non-pouch cost plus the pouch range]; a THB 3.00 pouch is a labelled stress case (THB 5.60 per cube). A THB 15 shelf price is an ASSUMPTION.
- **Carrying weight is untested.** No can or cube mass is sourced. The 0.9-volume reactants per 330 mL are 1.33 g potassium bicarbonate and 0.85 g citric acid at the stoichiometric minimum; pH totals are 3.42 g (pH 3.5) and 5.97 g (pH 3.0) [DERIVED: 13.25 mmol CO2 from the dose; molar masses; acid model].
- **Permission is open.** Sucralose is the recommended sweetener, not yet chosen. The FDA-hosted annex list for Thai notification No. 468 (B.E. 2568) has no sucralose row under flavoured drinks (14.1.4); the gazetted annex is unread [P, UNVERIFIED]. Until permission is confirmed, the product cannot be sold.
- **Units and components.** One cube per pouch is inferred, not confirmed with the organiser. The design uses one component. The organiser's form wording refers to three; this is an unchecked limit.
- **Dates and intentions.** The launch dates of the group's citrus zero-sugar lines are not established [S, UNVERIFIED]. A Mintel release of 16 February 2026 reports 67% of Thai consumers plan to cut sweets and desserts and 61% ready-to-drink sugary drinks; method not stated [P, UNVERIFIED]. These are intentions, not behaviour.
